# Session 2 — the running order

**أبو بكر الصدیق ؓ's caliphate entire: 11–13 AH / 632–634 CE.** A 45-minute slot; this is about
70 minutes of material, deliberately (`DECISIONS.md` #20).

> **THIS FILE IS THE SINGLE SOURCE OF THE RUNNING ORDER.** The deck, the briefing, the cue sheet
> and the worksheet are all built from it, so they cannot disagree. To change the order, edit
> `tools/extract_spine.py` and re-run it — never edit this file by hand.

**Why this cut.** The room said session 1 felt like a speed-run. Twelve years across two caliphates
in forty-five minutes is exactly that. أبو بكر ؓ was caliph for two years and three months, and that
is a complete arc: an army he refused to recall, Arabia breaking apart, the Qur'an bound as a
consequence, and a death that hands the whole thing on. عمر ؓ's 130 researched cards are not lost —
they become sessions 3 and 4, already page-cited and waiting in `CONTENT.md`.

**Interaction (`DECISIONS.md` #24):** two hands-up beats inside the story, one at the close, and a
90-second silent worksheet fill at the end of ACT 5. No live Q&A.

## ACT 1 · The army he would not call back

*11 AH · the first ten days*


### 1. TMW/E-TRN1 · The order of battle at Uḥud

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** شوال ۳ھ `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** n/a — this is a picture, not a place. Put the two wings on a diagram.
**What happened:** At Uḥud, Quraysh brought three thousand men and two hundred horses. Ibn Isḥāq records exactly who led that cavalry. The right wing was under Khālid b. al-Walīd. The left wing was under ʿIkrima, the son of Abū Jahl. The whole army was commanded by Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb. Hold those three names. Every one of them is a Muslim commander before this course reaches the year twenty-three.
**The statement:**
> <div dir="rtl">وَتَعَبَّأَتْ قُرَيْشٌ، وَهُمْ ثَلَاثَةُ آلَافٍ، وَمَعَهُمْ مِئَتَا فَرَسٍ قَدْ جَنَّبُوهَا، فَجَعَلُوا عَلَى مَيْمَنَةِ الْخَيْلِ خَالِدَ بْنَ الْوَلِيدِ، وَعَلَى مَيْسَرَتِهَا عِكْرِمَةَ بْنَ أَبِي جَهْلِ بْنِ هِشَامٍ</div>
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۴ ص۱۸۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/1669
> *English:* "Quraysh drew up, three thousand of them, with two hundred horses they had led along; and they put Khālid b. al-Walīd over the right wing of the cavalry, and ʿIkrima b. Abī Jahl b. Hishām over its left."
**عبرت:** A man's place in one line of battle is not his place in the next.
**Hands-up?** Yes — before naming them: "Who commanded the Meccan army at Uḥud?" Let the room answer, then give all three names.


> 🖐 **[HANDS]** Hands up — how long do you think أبو بكر ؓ was caliph, in total? (Two years and three months. Everything tonight happens inside that.)


### 2. ★ ABU/E-U1 · The appointment nobody liked

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** in the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime, ۱۱ھ `[SOURCED]` (no page read gives the
month) · **Map:** a pin at
**المدینہ**, and an arrow drawn north-west toward **البلقاء / أُبنى**, well past تبوك — the first
time the map points at Byzantine ground.
**What happened:** In his last weeks the Prophet ﷺ raised an army for the Syrian frontier. He put
أسامة بن زيد ؓ over it. أسامة ؓ was eighteen. In the ranks under him stood عمر بن الخطاب ؓ and the
senior men of the مهاجرون and the أنصار. People said so out loud. The Prophet ﷺ heard it, stood up,
and answered them himself.
**The statement:**
> إن تَطْعُنوا في إمارَتِهِ فَقَدْ كُنْتُمْ تَطْعُنون في إمارةِ أبيه منْ قَبْلُ، وايْمُ اللَّهِ إن كان لخليقًا للإمارة، وإن كان لمنْ أحَبِّ النّاس إليَّ، وإنَّ هذا لمِنْ أحبِّ الناسِ إليَّ بَعْدَه
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۵ ص۳۱۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/2495
> *English:* "If you find fault with his command, you found fault with his father's command before
> him. And by Allah, he was fit for command, and he was among the dearest of people to me — and this
> one is among the dearest of people to me after him."
**عبرت:** A man is fit for a task because of what he can do, not because of how long he has been
alive.
**Hands-up?** Ask the room: *how old do you have to be before people will take an order from you?*
Then give the number.

### 3. ★ ABU/E-U3 · "I will not untie a knot"

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ربيع الأول ۱۱ھ `[SOURCED]` (the connected narrative runs through
سيف بن عمر; the wording at ج۷ ص۱۱ comes by a separate route) · **Map:** المدینہ ringed — the
apostatising clans around it — and the army's arrow still pointing away, northward.
**What happened:** The advice came from the best men he had. Tribes around المدینہ had broken away.
Others were refusing the زکاۃ. The state's only field army was pointed at the Roman frontier, and
it was outside the walls. Keep it here, they said. Among those who said it was عمر بن الخطاب ؓ. He
refused, and he refused hard.
**The statement:**
> والذي نفس أبي بكر بيده لو ظننت أن السباعَ تَخْطفني لأنفذتُ بعثَ أسامةَ كما أمرَ به رسولُ الله ﷺ؛ ولو لم يبقَ في القرى غيري لأنفذته
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3156
> *English:* "By Him in whose hand is أبو بكر's soul — if I thought the wild beasts would snatch me
> away, I would still send out أسامة's expedition as the Messenger of Allah ﷺ ordered it. And if
> there were no one left in the settlements but me, I would still send it."
**عبرت:** There is a kind of steadiness that is not stubbornness: it is refusing to treat an
emergency as permission.
**Hands-up?** Ask before telling it: *the capital is surrounded and your only army is marching away.
Do you call it back?* Take the show of hands. Then read the line.

### 4. ABU/E-U4 · The beard, and the man who would not be deposed

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ربيع الأول ۱۱ھ `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** الجُرْف, one march outside
المدینہ.
**What happened:** The أنصار in the column sent عمر ؓ back with a second request. Not "cancel the
march" — "give us an older commander." عمر ؓ carried the message. أبو بكر ؓ had been sitting. He
came up off the ground, took عمر ؓ by the beard, and gave his answer in one sentence. Then he walked
out to الجُرف himself, reviewed the army and sent it off.
**The statement:**
> ثَكِلَتْكَ أُمُّكَ يَا ابْنَ الْخَطَّابِ! اسْتَعْمَلَهُ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ - صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ - وَتَأْمُرُنِي أَنْ أَعْزِلَهُ؟!
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۱۹۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/886
> *English:* "May your mother lose you, son of الخطاب! The Messenger of Allah ﷺ appointed him — and
> you order me to depose him?"
**عبرت:** Authority that can be renegotiated the moment its author is gone was never authority.
**Hands-up?** no

### 5. ABU/E-U5 · The caliph on foot

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** آخر ربيع الأول ۱۱ھ `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** the arrow finally leaves
المدینہ — الجُرْف → ذو خُشُب → the marches of البلقاء.
**What happened:** He walked the army out. The caliph of the Muslims on foot in the dust; the
eighteen-year-old commander in the saddle; عبد الرحمن بن عوف ؓ leading the caliph's riding-beast
behind them, empty. أسامة ؓ could not bear it and offered to dismount. He was refused. Only at the
end did أبو بكر ؓ ask a favour — and he asked it of أسامة ؓ, because أسامة ؓ was the commander and
عمر ؓ was in his army: *would you release عمر to me?*
**The statement:**
> فقال أسامةُ: يا خليفةَ رسولِ الله: إما أن تركبَ وإما أن أنزلَ، فقال: والله لستَ بنازلٍ ولستُ براكب
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3157
> *English:* "أسامة said: 'O successor of the Messenger of Allah, either you ride or I dismount.' He
> said: 'By Allah, you shall not dismount, and I shall not ride.'"
**عبرت:** Rank is a place in an order, not a claim about who is the better man.
**Hands-up?** no

### 6. ★ ABU/E-U6 · Forty days, and what the tribes concluded

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** marched **آخر ربيع الأول ۱۱ھ** `[SOURCED]`, away forty days — said
seventy — `[SOURCED]`. (That puts the return in جمادى; the month of the return is **our arithmetic**,
`[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]`, not a dated report on any page read.) · **Map:** the arrow goes out, strikes,
and comes back — and the ring of hostile clans around المدینہ **thins** rather than closes.
**What happened:** The column marched north through country that was ready to rise. It did not
fight its way through. It simply passed. And the men watching it pass drew the obvious conclusion:
nobody sends an army *away* unless he has more behind him. Forty days later — some say seventy — it
came back whole and carrying spoil. The blow it struck is described differently in different books:
a raid on أُبنى in the البلقاء, or on قُضاعة clans that had broken away, or a clash with Roman
forces. The two books that comment on the *effect* — البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۱ and الکامل ج۲ ص۱۹۶ — agree on it.
(سیر ج۲ ص۴۹۷ names the target and says nothing about the effect; do not enlist it for that.)
**The statement:**
> فجعل لا يمرُّ بقبيل يريدون الارتداد إلَّا قالوا: لولا أن لهؤلاء قوةً ما خرجَ مثلُ هؤلاء من عندهم
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3157
> *English:* "He passed no tribe intending to break away but they said: 'Were it not that these
> people have strength, men like these would not have gone out from among them.'"
**عبرت:** What people believe about your strength does as much work as your strength does.
**Hands-up?** no

## ACT 2 · Arabia comes apart

*11 AH*


### 7. ★ RCT/E-RC01 · The night raid on Medina

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ۱۱ھ, within days of the delegation's return `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** المدينة
lights up. Short red arrows onto the city's passes from the north-east. A second red block halts at
**ذو حُسى** — a valley in the country of عبس and غطفان (البدایہ ed. fn. ج۷ ص۲۰); **no page gives its
distance, so do not label one.** A blue arrow goes out from المدينة, reaches ذو حُسى, and
recoils back to المدينة.
**What happened:** The delegation that had offered the prayer and refused the zakāt went home. On the
way out they counted the men in the city. Three days later the raiders came at night. Half of them
stayed back at a valley called Dhū Ḥusā as a reserve. Abū Bakr ؓ came out with whoever was in the
mosque, on the watering-camels, and drove the raiders off. Then he pursued them — straight into the
reserve, which rolled inflated skins down at the Muslims' camels. The camels bolted and carried
their riders all the way home.
**The statement:**
> فَخَرَجَ عَلَيْهِمُ الرِّدْءُ بِأَنْحَاءَ قَدْ نَفَخُوهَا … فَنَفَرَتْ إِبِلُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَهُمْ عَلَيْهَا، وَرَجَعَتْ بِهِمْ إِلَى الْمَدِينَةِ، وَلَمْ يُصْرَعْ مُسْلِمٌ.
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۰۲ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/893
> *English:* "The reserve came out at them with skins they had inflated … The Muslims' camels bolted
> with them on their backs and carried them back to Medina — and not one Muslim was thrown."

**عبرت:** The first engagement of the war was a setback, and the book records it without
embarrassment.
**Hands-up?** Before telling it: *"A delegation comes to negotiate, and goes home again. What have
they taken with them besides your answer?"*

### 8. RCT/E-RC02 · The counter-attack before dawn

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ۱۱ھ, the same night as the reverse `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** One blue arrow
from المدينة to **ذو القصّة**, one day's ride out. ذو القصّة changes from red to blue and gets a
garrison marker.
**What happened:** They thought the Muslims were finished. Abū Bakr ؓ spent that night forming his men
up — the three brothers of Muqarrin on the right, the left and the rear. He marched in the dark. At
first light the two forces were standing on the same ground, and the enemy had not heard them come.
The swords were among them before the sun's rim showed. By sunrise they were running. He pursued and
camped at Dhū al-Qaṣṣa, left a detachment there under al-Nuʿmān b. Muqarrin ؓ, and went home.
**The statement:**
> فَمَا طَلَعَ الْفَجْرُ إِلَّا وَهُمْ وَالْعَدُوُّ عَلَى صَعِيدٍ وَاحِدٍ … فَمَا ذَرَّ قَرْنُ الشَّمْسِ حَتَّى وَلَّوْهُمُ الْأَدْبَارَ … وَكَانَ أَوَّلَ الْفَتْحِ.
> — الکامل ج۲ ص۲۰۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/894
> *English:* "Dawn did not break until they and the enemy were on one ground … and the sun's rim had
> not risen before they turned their backs … and that was the first victory."

**عبرت:** The recovery came out of the very night the reverse happened, spent preparing.
**Hands-up?** no

### 9. ★ RCT/E-RC05 · Eleven banners at ذو القصّة

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ۱۱ھ, after Usāma's ؓ army had rested `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** **The evening's
biggest map moment.** One dot at ذو القصّة, one day out from المدينة. Eleven arrows leave it at once —
north-east to نجد, east to اليمامة, north to الشام and to قُضاعة, south-east to البحرين, far east to
عمان, south-east to مَهْرة, south to اليمن and to تِهامة, south to حضرموت. Leave them as outlines;
they fill in one by one across the rest of the evening.
**What happened:** He rode out a third time with his sword drawn, and ʿAlī ؓ walked at his camel's
head. This time the Companions would not let him go on. He agreed, tied eleven banners on that
ground, gave each commander his objective and his written orders, and went back to Medina. Then the
eleven separated — each with his own troops joining him — and rode off in eleven directions from one
spot of desert.
**The statement:**
> فَفَصَلَتِ الْأُمَرَاءُ مِنْ ذِي الْقَصَّةِ، وَلَحِقَ بِكُلِّ أَمِيرٍ جُنْدُهُ، وَعَهِدَ إِلَى كُلِّ أَمِيرٍ.
> — الکامل ج۲ ص۲۰۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/895
> *English:* "The commanders separated from Dhū al-Qaṣṣa, and each commander's troops joined him, and
> he gave each commander his commission."

**عبرت:** The whole war was set moving from one camp, in one sitting, by a man who had just been told
to go home.
**Hands-up?** Ask the room to count with you as each arrow goes up.
⚠ Ibn Kathīr announces eleven and prints ten; **الکامل prints all eleven.** If a listener has counted
ten in some other book, that is why. **Do not print the ninth commander's name — three sources give
three forms of it.**

### 10. RCT/E-RC06 · The letter that went ahead of the armies

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ۱۱ھ, with the eleven `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** Thin dotted lines running ahead
of each of the eleven arrows — messengers arriving before soldiers.
**What happened:** Before the armies came, the letters came. One wording, copied out many times, sent
with messengers who rode ahead of the columns. The messenger was to read it aloud in every assembly
of the tribe. And the letter set a single test that any man could apply from a distance, with no
lawyer and no court. Call the adhān when you halt. If they answer it, stop. If they do not, ask them
what they owe.
**The statement:**
> وقد أمرتُ رسولي أن يقرأ كتابه في كلِّ مَجْمعٍ لكم، والداعية الأذان، فإذا إذَّنَ المسلمون فأذنوا فكُفُّوا عنهم، وإن لم يُؤَذِّنوا فسلوهم ما عليهم.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۲۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3170
> *English:* "I have ordered my messenger to read his letter in every assembly of yours. The summons
> is the adhān: when the Muslims call it and they call it too, hold off from them; and if they do not
> call it, ask them what they owe."

**عبرت:** The test was something the other side could pass, and passing it stopped the war where they
stood.
**Hands-up?** no
⚠ The same letter contains a harsh clause. **Do not quote it and do not deny it.** If raised: it is
on that page, transmitted by Sayf b. ʿUmar; the operative test on the same page is the adhān; and the
question is for the fuqahāʾ. Logged for `QA_BANK.md`.

### 11. ★ TMW/E-TRN21 · The ḥadīth about the Sword of Allah, and who carries it

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ۱۱ھ, at the ridda appointment `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** Medina / Dhū al-Qaṣṣa
**What happened:** When Abū Bakr ؓ gave Khālid ؓ the command over the ridda campaign, he quoted something he had heard the Prophet ﷺ say about him. Ibn Kathīr puts the ḥadīth on the page at exactly that moment. The man who transmits it is Waḥshī b. Ḥarb ؓ. Twelve years earlier, at Uḥud, the same two men had been on the same side of the field — and it was the other side.
**The statement:**
> <div dir="rtl">"نِعْمَ عَبْدُ اللَّهِ وَأَخُو الْعَشِيرَةِ، خَالِدُ بْنُ الْوَلِيدِ، سَيْفٌ مِنْ سُيُوفِ اللَّهِ سَلَّهُ اللَّهُ عَلَى الْكُفَّارِ وَالْمُنَافِقِينَ"</div>
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۲۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3170
> *English:* "What an excellent servant of Allah and brother of the clan — Khālid b. al-Walīd; a sword among the swords of Allah, which Allah has drawn against the disbelievers and the hypocrites."
**عبرت:** The praise of one of them reaches us through the other.
**Hands-up?** no
⚠ Grading: the matn is graded ḥasan/ṣaḥīḥ by its routes; this particular chain, through Ḥarb b. Waḥshī, is weak. See §6. Do not assert a grade from the platform beyond "Imām Aḥmad records it."


### 12. ★ RCT/E-RC09 · بُزاخة — the man who walked off the field

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ۱۱ھ `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** Khālid's ؓ arrow moves from **أجَأ وسَلْمى** to
**بُزاخة**. A separate red arrow shows Ṭulayḥa moving up from **سُمَيْراء** to بُزاخة. A third block
sits nearby in amber, labelled **بنو عامر — watching**. After the battle: a thin red line runs from
بُزاخة north-west, all the way to **الشام**.
**What happened:** Ṭulayḥa sat wrapped in a cloak, waiting for revelation, while ʿUyayna b. Ḥiṣn did
the fighting with seven hundred men of Fazāra. Nearby, Banū ʿĀmir sat armed and waited to see which
way it would go. Three times ʿUyayna broke off the fighting, walked back to the cloak, and asked
whether Jibrīl had come. Twice the answer was no. The third time it was yes — and then Ṭulayḥa told
him what had been said.
**The statement:**
> قَالَ لِي: إِنَّ لَكَ رَحًا كَرَحَاهُ، وَحَدِيثًا لَا تَنْسَاهُ. فَقَالَ عُيَيْنَةُ: قَدْ عَلِمَ اللَّهُ أَنَّهُ سَيَكُونُ حَدِيثٌ لَا تَنْسَاهُ، انْصَرِفُوا يَا بَنِي فَزَارَةَ فَإِنَّهُ كَذَّابٌ.
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۰۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/896
> *English:* "He said to me: you have a mill like his mill, and a matter you will not forget. ʿUyayna
> said: Allah knows there will be a matter you will not forget. Withdraw, Banū Fazāra — the man is a
> liar."

**عبرت:** The claim collapsed the moment somebody asked it for something specific.
**Hands-up?** Before telling it: *"He had followed this man, and was fighting for him that morning.
What was the question that ended it?"*
**Ṭulayḥa's own exit, same page:** he had a horse ready and a mount for his wife, got away with her,
and called back over his shoulder — «يَا مَعْشَرَ فَزَارَةَ، مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ أَنْ يَفْعَلَ هَكَذَا وَيَنْجُوَ
بِامْرَأَتِهِ فَلْيَفْعَلْ»: *"whoever can do this and get away with his wife, let him do it."*

### 13. TMW/E-TRN13 · Ṭulayḥa at Buzākha — the question, and the answer he got

**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** ۱۱ھ `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** Buzākha, Najd — the map already carries this pin
**What happened:** At Buzākha, Ṭulayḥa's men were breaking. He asked one of them, plainly, what was defeating them. The answer he received explains the whole year better than any figure does.
**The statement:**

### 42. ★ ZIA/E-ZY17 · The number nobody can give

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ۱۲ھ, after the battle `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** n/a — this is the last slide
of the al-Yamāma act.
**What happened:** After al-Yamāma, Abū Bakr ؓ ordered the Qurʾān collected, and the reason the books
give is that the killing had run hot among the reciters. That is the phrase all three of them use.
What none of them gives is a number. Ibn Kathīr's own summary of the Muslim dead is four hundred and
fifty — and he says in the same breath that he is counting bearers of the Qurʾān, Companions and
others together in that one figure. So we know why the Qurʾān was collected. We do not know how many
reciters died. We only know that it was enough.
**The statement:**
> وذلك بعد ما استَحَرّ القتلُ في القُرّاء يوم اليمامة كما ثبت به الحديث في صحيح البخاري.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۷۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3225 — **صحيح البخاري ٤٩٨٦**
> *English:* "…and that was after the killing had run hot among the reciters on the day of
> al-Yamāma, as the ḥadīth in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī establishes."

**عبرت:** They did not wait to find out how much had been lost before they secured what was left.
**Hands-up?** Before telling it: *"How many men who had the whole Qurʾān by heart died that day? What
would you guess?"* — then give the honest answer: the books do not say.
⚠ **This is the card that stops a wrong number being said. The figures the safe list does give run
from 58 to more than 1,080 (§Y), and none of them is a count of reciters.** ⚠ **Anyone who says "450
reciters" has misread البدایہ ج۷ ص۵۵.**

### 43. ABU/E-Q1 · The killing ran hot among the reciters

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** اليمامة — ابن كثير: begun ۱۱ھ, finished ۱۲ھ; «المشهور» ربيع ۱۲ھ;
ابن الاثیر places it in ۱۱ھ `[SOURCED]` for the dispute · **Map:** **اليمامة** in central نجد, and
then the map goes quiet and the scene returns to المدینہ.
**What happened:** At اليمامة the ردة was broken and مسيلمة was killed, and the cost is the one
Ibn Kathīr stops to count: the dead in the garden and in the battle at close to ten thousand —
though he adds that some say twenty-one thousand — and the Muslim dead at six hundred, or five
hundred, and then he writes *and Allah knows best*. Among the
fallen were men who carried the Qur'an in their memories. Ibn Kathīr's phrase for it is
**استحرَّ القتل في القرّاء** — the killing *ran hot* among the reciters (the editor glosses
استحرَّ as اشتدَّ, grew fierce).
**The statement:**
> وقتل من المسلمين ستمئة، وقيل: خمسمئة، فالله أعلم، وفيهم من سادات الصحابة، وأعيان الناس من يُذكر بعد
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۳۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3181
> *English:* "Six hundred of the Muslims were killed — and it is said five hundred — and Allah knows
> best. Among them were leaders of the Companions and men of standing, who will be named later."
**عبرت:** A generation can carry something priceless and never notice how thin the carrying is until
some of it is dropped.
**Hands-up?** Ask: *if every copy of a book you loved lived only in people's memories, how many
funerals would it take to lose it?*

### 45. ★ ABU/E-Q2 · "How can we do a thing the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did not do?"

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** سنة ۱۲ھ `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** المدینہ; no movement — this is the
evening's still point.
**What happened:** After اليمامة, أبو بكر ؓ sent for زيد بن ثابت ؓ — a young man of بنو النجار who
had been eleven years old when the Prophet ﷺ arrived in المدینہ, who had memorised seventeen سور
before he was introduced, who had learned the Jews' script in a fortnight because the Prophet ﷺ
asked him to, and who had written the revelation down as it came. أبو بكر ؓ told him to go and find
the whole Qur'an and gather it. زيد ؓ balked — and his objection was the most serious objection
there is.
**The statement:**
> فَقُلْتُ: كَيْفَ تَفْعَلُوْنَ شَيْئاً لَمْ يَفْعَلْهُ رَسُوْلُ اللهِ -صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ-. قَالَ: هُوَ -وَاللهِ- خَيْرٌ. فَلَمْ يَزَلْ أَبُو بَكْرٍ يُرَاجِعُنِي حَتَّى شَرَحَ اللهُ صَدْرِي لِلَّذِي شَرَحَ لَهُ صَدْرَ أَبِي بَكْرٍ وَعُمَر
> — سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۲ ص۴۳۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/2411
> *English:* "I said: 'How will you do a thing the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did not do?' He said: 'By
> Allah, it is good.' And أبو بكر did not stop coming back to me until Allah opened my breast to
> that to which He had opened the breasts of أبو بكر and عمر."
**عبرت:** The men closest to the Prophet ﷺ were the most afraid of adding to what he ﷺ left — which
is exactly why what they did add can be trusted.
**Hands-up?** no

### 46. ★ ABU/E-Q3 · Parchment, shoulder-blades, palm-stalks, and the breasts of men

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** سنة ۱۲ھ `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** none. Show the four materials instead —
they *are* the picture.
**What happened:** There was no volume to copy from. زيد ؓ had to go and track it down — the verb in
his own account is تتبَّع, to follow a thing up piece by piece — and gather it off whatever the
Companions had written it on: strips of parchment, the
flat shoulder-blades of animals, the bare stalks of palm branches, thin white stones — and out of
the memories of the men who had it by heart. Then it was one set of صحف, in one place, for the
first time.
**The statement:**
> فَكُنْتُ أَتَتَبَّعُ القُرْآنَ، أَجْمَعُهُ مِنَ الرِّقَاعِ، وَالأَكْتَافِ، وَالعُسُبِ، وَصُدُوْرِ الرِّجَالِ.
> — سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۲ ص۴۳۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/2411
> *English:* "So I began to track down the Qur'an, gathering it from the parchments, the
> shoulder-blades, the palm-stalks, and the breasts of men."
**عبرت:** Preservation is not one heroic act; it is somebody doing a careful, unglamorous job
properly.
**Hands-up?** Ask the room to name the four things after you have said them once. It is the one line
in the evening everybody will remember.
*(Ibn Kathīr's own wording at البدایہ ج۷ ص۷۹ names اللِّخاف — the thin white stones — where الذہبی
names الرقاع. If both are wanted, say so and cite both.)*

### 47. RCT/E-RC27 · دارين — into the water

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ۱۱ھ→۱۲ھ `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** The survivors take ship from the البحرين
coast to **دارين** — a crossing of a day and a night by sea. Blue lines seal every land road first.
Then a blue arrow goes **straight across the water** and comes straight back the same day.
**What happened:** The beaten took ship for Dārīn, an anchorage a day and a night out from the shore.
Al-ʿAlāʾ ؓ did not chase them straight away. He first wrote to the Muslims of Bakr b. Wāʾil to sit on
every road and close the land behind him. Then he brought his men to the water's edge — where the

**The statement:**
> قَدْ أَرَاكُمُ اللَّهُ مِنْ آيَاتِهِ فِي الْبَرِّ لِتَعْتَبِرُوا بِهَا فِي الْبَحْرِ، فَانْهَضُوا إِلَى عَدُوِّكُمْ وَاسْتَعْرِضُوا الْبَحْرَ.

> *English:* "Allah has shown you of His signs on land so that you may take heed of them at sea. Rise
> against your enemy, and go straight across the sea."

**عبرت:** He closed the roads behind the enemy before he opened one in front of himself.
**Hands-up?** no

### 51. ★ RCT/E-RC36 · What Ibn Kathīr says the whole war was

**Tier:** CORE — **this is the closing slide** · **When:** «أواخر سنة إحدى عشرة وأوائل سنة ثنتي عشرة»
`[SOURCED]` · **Map:** The completed map. All eleven arrows filled in, every front resolved, نجران in
its own colour as a covenanted community, and the date bracket across the bottom: **۱۱ھ → ۱۲ھ**.
**What happened:** When Ibn Kathīr finishes the whole account, he stops and sums it up himself. He
does not describe a conquest. He says there was no region of Arabia in which some people did not turn
— and that Abū Bakr ؓ sent armies and commanders to be a support to whichever believers were already
in that region. The fifth of the spoils came back to Medina and was spent among the people. And it
went on that way until there was nobody left in the peninsula except people in obedience, and people
under a covenant of protection — like the people of Najrān, who had sent a delegation to renew theirs
while Muslim tribes were breaking theirs.

### 52. ★ IKO/E-IKR1 · ⭐ The call does not add numbers; it removes rivalry

### 55. ISA/E-C5 · The march across the waterless desert

**Tier:** CORE · **When:** 13 AH `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۷; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۵۳; سیر ج۱ ص۳۶۶]` ·
**Map:** THE map moment of the evening. A line strikes north-west out of Iraq at Qurāqir, crosses
blank desert with no road on it, and comes out at Suwā and Tadmur — behind the Roman armies.
**What happened:** The commanders in Syria wrote to Abū Bakr ؓ asking for reinforcement and telling
him what the Romans had gathered at the Yarmūk. He wrote back to Khālid ؓ in Iraq: put a deputy over
Iraq, bring the men you have to Syria, and when you reach them you are the commander over them. The
letter reached Iraq and Khālid ؓ set off at once — but not by any road. He chose the
Samāwa, five days of waterless country. His guide was Rāfiʿ b. ʿUmayra al-Ṭāʾī, and Rāfiʿ told him
plainly it could not be done with cavalry and baggage: a lone rider fears that road. Khālid ؓ said he
had no choice; he had to come out behind the Romans. So they prepared. The strongest camels were made
thirsty, then watered twice over, their lips cut and their mouths bound so they could not chew and
lose the water. Every day they opened the bellies of ten of them and watered the horses from what was
inside. On the last stretch Rāfiʿ, whose eyes were bad, asked the men whether they could see a
box-thorn bush the height of a seated man. They said no. He said: then by God you are dead, and I am
dead with you. They looked again. The bush was there, cut down to a stump. They dug at its root and
found the spring.
**The statement:**
> وَاللَّهِ مَا وَرَدْتُ هَذَا الْمَاءَ قَطُّ إِلَّا مَرَّةً وَاحِدَةً مَعَ أَبِي وَأَنَا غُلَامٌ
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۵۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/944
> *English:* "By God, I have come to this water only once in my life — with my father, when I was a

**عبرت:** The march was survived on preparation and on one man's memory; neither was an accident.
**Hands-up?** Ask: how do you carry water for an army across five days of desert, with no wells? Take
two answers. Then tell them.
