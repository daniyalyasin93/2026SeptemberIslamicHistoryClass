### 3.1 The cliffhanger, and why it is honest chronology

Ibn Kathīr opens his year-12 chapter in his own voice, **unhedged**:

> ⁨استُهلَّت هذه السنة، وجيوشُ الصديق وأُمراؤه الذين بعثهم لقتال أهل الرّدّة جَوّالون في البلاد يمينًا وشمالًا، لتمهيد قواعد الإسلام⁩

⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۱⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3207 · cached
*"This year began while al-Ṣiddīq's armies and his commanders, whom he had sent to fight the people of the
ridda, were roving the land right and left, to lay down the foundations of Islam."*

**So the turn outward did not wait for the Ridda to finish — the two overlap, and the page says so.** On the
very next page the march to Iraq is dated into that same month:

> ⁨وقد ذكر المدائني بإسناده أن خالدًا توجّه إلى العراق في المحرم سنة اثنتي عشرة⁩

⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۲⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3208 · cached

⚠⚠ **That dating is al-Madāʾinī's, by isnād, as Ibn Kathīr transmits it — not Ibn Kathīr's own assertion.**
Say *"al-Madāʾinī dates it to Muḥarram 12"*, never *"it was Muḥarram 12"*. **And on the same page Ibn Kathīr
records that the route itself was disputed** — al-Wāqidī: one party says Khālid ؓ went straight on from
al-Yamāma to Iraq, another that he went back to Medina first and then took the Kufa road to al-Ḥīra; and
Ibn Kathīr gives his own verdict, «⁨قلت: والمشهور الأول⁩» — *"I say: the first is the better known."*
**Say that aloud: it is the honest version, and it is a better beat than a bare date.** al-Madāʾinī's own
line adds the route — by way of Basra, leaving Quṭba b. Qatāda there and **al-Muthannā b. Ḥāritha
al-Shaybānī** over Kufa, which is the same man evening 6's `RC27` names sealing the roads at Dārīn.
The same chapter also places the four kings of Kinda in
year 12 unhedged (⁨ص۶۱⁩), and hedges Juwāthā, Oman and Mahra into year 12 with «⁨وقد قيل⁩» — report that as
one view. ⚠ The words «⁨فقتلوا يوم النُّجير⁩» on ⁨ص۶۱⁩ are the **editor's footnote**, not Ibn Kathīr's body
text: the al-Nujayr link is sound from ⁨الکامل⁩, but do not cite ⁨ص۶۱⁩ for it.

**At the level of the year the overlap is page-cited and unhedged. Do not claim a month-by-month overlap** —
no page fixes the months of the southern and eastern fronts.

### 3.2 The correction `ISA/E-C2` needs before it can close the evening

The card's body opens *"Khālid ؓ had taken al-Ḥīra"*. **That is past the threshold and it is not what the
cited page supports** — ⁨ج۷ ص۶۴⁩ *precedes* al-Ḥīra (⁨ص۶۹–۷۱⁩) in Ibn Kathīr's own order, and the al-Ḥīra
connection in the source is only that al-Shaʿbī was read the letter by Banū Buqayla **of** al-Ḥīra. Rewrite
that opening sentence and the card is a clean threshold: *a letter went to the Persian court, before an
army did.* The quotation and the ⁨عبرت⁩ stand as they are, and its hands-up beat is already written for this
exact moment.

**If no rewrite is wanted:** close on `ISA/E-C7` instead — four provinces given before they are taken — and
put `C2`'s letter on the «Next week» slide.

## 4. The one research gap, and it is evening 7's only one

**The condition of the two empires in 11–12 AH is nowhere in this repo.** Verified against
`docs/research/INDEX.md` (all rows), `docs/catalogue/`, and both pools.

What exists and is **not** this: `docs/PRIMER.md` has one unsourced sentence — *"two empires that had
exhausted each other in a long war"* — which is Daniyal's crash course, not a page-cited note; and
`maps/world_632_kandg.png` is a Kings & Generals video frame, which `MAPS.md` itself says is **for slide
drama only**.

**What the note must establish, page-cited:** Sasanian succession after Khosrau II and the run of short
reigns down to Yazdegerd III · Heraclius's war of 602–628, his counter-offensive and his recovery of the
Cross to Jerusalem · the exhaustion of both treasuries and field armies · al-Ḥīra and the Lakhmids as the
Persian buffer that had already been dismantled · how either empire looked to Arabia in 11–12 AH, and
whether either had noticed Medina · and, if it is to be used, a sourced treatment of ⁨سورة الروم⁩ 1–4.

**Three things make it cheaper than it looks:**

1. **The Persian court-killing is already spoken.** Evening 3 told `AAA/E-AW01` and `AW02` — the Prophet's ﷺ
   letter torn unread, and Kisrā killed **by his own sons** that same night, ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۳⁩. So Persia's
   instability is a **callback**, not new material: *"you already know how the Persian king died."*
2. **A contemporaneous Muslim reading of Persia's condition is already on a CORE card and already cached** —
   Khālid's ؓ own line on `ISA/E-C2`'s page: «⁨فالحمد لله الذي فض خدمتكم وسلبَ مُلْككم ووهنَ كيدكَم⁩»,
   ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۴⁩. That is the cheapest honest way to put *an empire in trouble* on a slide.
3. **The TOC is already cached** and names the chapters to start from: ⁨ذكر بعثه ﷺ إلى كسرى ملك الفرس⁩
   (30097 index 2000), ⁨قدوم رسول قيصر إلى رسول الله ﷺ بتبوك⁩ (2158). ⚠ Both are **pre-11 AH** — they give
   the Prophet's ﷺ embassies, not the empires' state in 11–12 AH. Expect to fetch.

⚠ **Whatever the note cannot establish from the safe list stays off the slide, or carries
`[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]` on the slide face** (`CLAUDE.md` §0.1). Byzantine and Sasanian dynastic detail is
exactly the kind of thing general scholarship fixes and our four books do not — **so it is labelled, or it
is not said.** The honest fallback, if the note comes back thin, is to build Part V out of what Muslims said
about the two empires rather than out of what the two empires were: the callback, Khālid's ؓ line, and
Ibn Khaldūn's slide.
