## 1. What changed, and why

**Old session 1** — «ایک نظر میں»: the whole 1400 years narrated in 45 minutes.
**New session 1** — orientation in time and space, then one event understood properly.

> A 45-minute race through fourteen centuries is the most praised and least remembered lecture
> format there is. Nothing sticks, because nothing is concrete.

The orientation job is not dropped — it is done **better**, with three nested timelines and three
maps that the audience takes home on paper. The grand timeline carries the **whole arc as pegs**,
so a one-off attendee still leaves with the full shape (`DECISIONS.md` #13). Then the evening spends
its second half making one event genuinely comprehensible.

**The event is سقیفہ بنی ساعدہ** — narrated everywhere and understood almost nowhere, because nobody
first explains who الأوس and الخزرج were, or why the Anṣār had a claim at all. Teaching **Medina's
clan structure before the event** is the load-bearing idea of this session.

**Nothing renumbers.** The evening stops the night before the ردہ, and L02 «بارہ سال» already opens
on the ردہ.

## 2. What the listener leaves with

Not "that was interesting." Specifically:

1. A sheet of paper with **three nested timelines**, and the felt sense that ۱۴۰۰ years is both very
   long and — against the prophets — very short.
2. A **map** with Arabia placed between two empires, and the six anchor cities marked.
3. The ability to name **four men who were in that courtyard**, which clan each came from, and what
   each one actually said.
4. One sentence: **a تاریخ report is not a ruling and not a creed** — the thing that will keep them
   safe for the next nine weeks.
5. A question they cannot answer yet: *this map is the state on the night the Prophet ﷺ died. Next
   week, most of it breaks away. Why?*

## 3. The 45 minutes

The fixed shape in `CLAUDE.md` §2 governs sessions 2–10. **Session 1 is the orientation evening and
runs its own shape.** Its most visible difference: **no separate تعارف block** — the people are
introduced inside the story, where each becomes load-bearing (`DECISIONS.md` #15).

| Start | Block | Min | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **آغاز — one image, one question** | 2 | The destination is named *first*, so the orientation half never feels like throat-clearing |
| 0:02 | **تاریخ کیا ہے** — حدیث / تاریخ / فقہ | 4 | The definition, the four Arabic quotations, **and the guardrail** |
| 0:06 | **Timeline 1 — Pakistan** | 3 | ~10 pegs the room lived through |
| 0:09 | **Timeline 2 — the Ummah** | 5 | Timeline 1 shrinks to a sliver at the right edge. **The whole arc as pegs** |
| 0:14 | **Timeline 3 — the prophets** | 3 | Timeline 2 shrinks in turn. Ordered; mostly undated, and honestly so |
| 0:17 | **Map 1 — Arabia in the world** | 4 | Rome and Persia; where Arabia sits between them |
| 0:21 | **Map 2 — Arabia itself** | 3 | Tribes, settlements, the six anchor cities |
| 0:24 | **Map 3 → مدینہ کی ساخت** | 6 | **The clan chart.** The worksheet comes out here |
| 0:30 | **سقیفہ بنی ساعدہ** | 8 | The event, told plainly, with the chart still on screen |
| 0:38 | **۱۱ھ کی رات — the state** | 3 | The governors map. The territorial extent |
| 0:41 | **آج کا سبق** | 2 | One sentence, written by hand |
| 0:43 | **اگلے ہفتے** | 2 | End on the question, not a summary |

**Total 45.** The two blocks to cut if running long, in this order: Timeline 1 → 90 seconds
(name the pegs, don't discuss them); Map 2 → merged into Map 1.
**Never cut the guardrail block and never cut the clan chart.** The first is the safety net; the
second is what makes سقیفہ land.

## 4. Block by block

### 0:00 · آغاز — one image, one question (2 min)

One slide. A plain statement, no map, no timeline yet:

> On a Monday in **۱۱ھ**, in a covered courtyard in Medina, a small number of men decided who would
> lead the Muslims after the Prophet ﷺ.
> Most of us have heard the name **سقیفہ بنی ساعدہ**.
> Almost none of us could name three men who were in that courtyard.

Then: *"Tonight we will fix that. But not yet — first we have to know **when** we are, and
**where**."*

**Why this is here:** the orientation half is the right pedagogy but it is a hard 24-minute opening
if the audience does not know what it is preparing them for. Naming the destination first converts
preamble into build-up.

Also in this block: the Q&A policy, said once and applied all ten weeks — **no questions during the
45 minutes**, slips into the box at the door, speaker stays 15 minutes afterwards.

### 0:02 · تاریخ کیا ہے — and the guardrail (4 min)

Three moves, in order:

1. **The definition.** تاریخ is the recording of events *in their order in time*. Its distinguishing
   question is not "what happened" — every discipline asks that — but **"when, and in what order."**
   From the muqaddima pp.۳۲–۳۳ and السخاوي's own wording in الإعلان بالتوبيخ.
2. **Why order is a weapon.** The four Arabic quotations, on screen in Naskh with English beneath:
   سفيان الثوري · حفص بن غياث · حماد بن زيد. Then the forged Khaybar document — الخطيب البغدادي
   destroys it *on dates alone*, because a man named in it accepted Islam after Khaybar and another
   had died before it. **Chronology is how forgery is caught.** That is why this course is built on
   a timeline.
3. **THE GUARDRAIL** — the reason this block exists at all:

> **حدیث** — a report must be authentic before it can bind. It carries دین.
> **فقہ** — a report is weighed for what it obliges. It carries حکم.
> **تاریخ** — a report is recorded to establish *what happened and in what order*.
>
> So **a تاریخ report is not a ruling and it is not a creed.**
> **تاریخِ اسلام = تاریخِ مسلمین** — this is the history of Muslims, not a source of the دین.
> We narrate what the books narrate. **We do not judge between the Companions ؓ.**

Sixty seconds of that, said calmly and once, is what makes it safe to narrate سقیفہ to this room
(`DECISIONS.md` #14). Session 3 still carries the fuller fiqh treatment of the مشاجرات.

### 0:06–0:17 · The three nested timelines (11 min)

The device: **each timeline shrinks to a shaded sliver at the right-hand edge of the next.** The
audience physically watches the scale change. This is the single best orientation tool available and
it costs one well-drawn graphic per step.

| | Span | ~Pegs | Point made |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Pakistan** | 1947 → today | ~10 | "You remember some of this. Your parents remember all of it." |
| **2 · The Ummah** | ۱ھ → today | ~20 | **The whole arc.** Timeline 1 is the last centimetre |
| **3 · The prophets** | آدم ؑ → today | ordered, mostly undated | Timeline 2 is itself the last centimetre |

**Timeline 3 is epistemically constrained and the slide must say so.** Our sources do **not** fix
dates for آدم، نوح، ابراہیم، موسیٰ، داؤد ؑ. Approximate centuries appear only from general
scholarship, labelled **`[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]`** on the slide itself; آدم ؑ and نوح ؑ go
**off-scale** with no number invented. The caption carries the point:

> **ترتیب معلوم ہے، تاریخیں نہیں** — *the order is known; the dates are not.*

That is not a hedge. It is the method message from the previous block, arriving again with teeth
(`DECISIONS.md` #16).

### 0:17–0:24 · The maps (7 min)

Time, then space. Three, in zoom order:

1. **The world, c. ۱۱ھ / 632 CE** — the Roman (Byzantine) and Sasanid empires, and where Arabia sits
   between them. Europe for scale. The point: *this was the edge of the known world, not its centre.*
2. **Arabia** — the major tribal blocs and the settlements: مکہ · مدینہ · الطائف · اليمامة · نجران ·
   صنعاء · البحرين · عُمان · حضرموت. The six course anchor cities appear here for the first time.
3. Hands off to the Medina block.

### 0:24 · مدینہ کی ساخت — the clan chart (6 min)

**The load-bearing block.** One infographic, on screen and in the workbook:

```
                    THE ANṢĀR  (from الأزد — قحطان, southern)
        ┌──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┐
      الأوس                                        الخزرج
   بنو عبد الأشهل                              بنو ساعدة
   بنو عمرو بن عوف                              بنو النجار
   بنو حارثة …                                  بنو الحارث · بنو سلمة …

                THE MUHĀJIRŪN  (قريش — عدنان, northern)
   بنو هاشم · بنو تيم · بنو عدي · بنو أمية · بنو مخزوم · بنو زهرة ·
   بنو أسد · بنو الحارث بن فهر · بنو عامر بن لؤي
```

Rules for this block:

- **The chart is shown; it is not read aloud.** Eight sub-clans narrated will lose the room.
- **The speaker names four men out loud**, no more, each with his clan — and each is a man who will
  speak in the next block.
- **يوم بُعاث** gets thirty seconds: الأوس and الخزرج had been at war shortly before the Hijra. That
  is why "who leads" was a live question in that courtyard and not an abstract one.
- **عدنان / قحطان** gets thirty seconds: the Anṣār are southern, قريش are northern. An old fault
  line, and it is in the room.
- **The worksheet comes out here.** The chart is printed with the chiefs' names **blank**; the
  audience writes them in. This is session 1's hand-writing ritual, replacing the تعارف panels
  (`DECISIONS.md` #15). If time is short, one name is written in the room and the rest goes home
  (`DECISIONS.md` #17).

### 0:30 · سقیفہ بنی ساعدہ (8 min)

Told plainly, in sequence, **with the clan chart still visible** so every name lands somewhere. From
البدایہ والنہایہ, page-cited, in Ibn Kathir's own order.

**Register rules, absolute in this block:**

- Say **«شرکاء»** or "who was present" — **never** "actors", "cast" or "characters" (`CLAUDE.md` §1.2).
- Honorifics on every name, every time.
- **Narrate; do not adjudicate.** Where the sources record a difference, say who reports what and
  move on. The speaker does not draw the inference and does not let the room push him to.
- What ابن كثير says about سعد بن عبادہ ؓ afterwards is reported **exactly as he reports it** —
  neither softened nor sharpened.

The four men the audience should be able to name at the end. Each is introduced *here*, at the
moment he becomes load-bearing, in a short تراجم notice — نسب and clan · one sentence on what he
did · what he said that night:

| | Who | Clan | Why he is one of the four | Page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ۱ | **أبو بكر الصديق ؓ** | بنو تيم · قريش | What he argued, and that he put **two other men** forward instead of himself | ج۵ ص۳۴۶ |
| ۲ | **الحباب بن المنذر ؓ** | الخزرج | «منا أمير ومنكم أمير» — the proposal that was refused | ج۵ ص۳۴۶ |
| ۳ | **بشير بن سعد ؓ** | الخزرج | An Anṣārī, and the first hand extended. The moment it turned | ج۵ ص۳۴۷ |
| ۴ | **عمر الفاروق ؓ** | بنو عدي · قريش | The whole account is **his own**, given later from the minbar, against himself | ج۵ ص۳۴۵ |

Present, named in one clause each, **not** profiled: **سعد بن عبادہ ؓ** (بنو ساعدة · الخزرج — the
courtyard was his clan's) and **أبو عبيدة بن الجراح ؓ** *أمين الأمة* (one of the two men أبو بكر ؓ
put forward).

> **Three corrections forced by the sourcing** — `docs/research/saqifah-bani-saida.md` §7:
>
> 1. **سعد بن عبادہ ؓ gives no speech in البدایہ.** He is present but **ill and wrapped up** —
>    «رجلٌ مُزَمَّلٌ … وجعٌ» (ج۵ ص۳۴۵). The long speech everyone has heard is from
>    **سیرت ابن ہشام / طبری, outside the safe list**, and is not used. Say so if asked — it is the
>    course's method made visible (`QA_BANK.md` 1.9).
> 2. **أسيد بن حضير ؓ and ثابت بن قيس ؓ are not mentioned at the سقيفة at all** in ج۵ ص۳۴۳–۳۵۷ or
>    ج۷ ص۵–۸. They stay in the clan chart as chiefs; they are **not** placed in that courtyard.
> 3. **«الأئمة من قريش» is not carried verbatim on these pages.** Do not quote it. Use what
>    ابن کثیر does give at ج۵ ص۳۴۶–۳۴۸ instead.

### 0:38 · ۱۱ھ کی رات — the state (3 min)

The closing map: Arabia at the death of the Prophet ﷺ. Territorial extent, and **the governors named
on the map where they sat** — مکہ · الطائف · اليمن · حضرموت · البحرين · عُمان. Plus جيش أسامہ ؓ,
outside Medina, already gone.

Delivered flatly, as a state of affairs. No commentary.

### 0:41 · آج کا سبق (2 min)

One sentence, on screen, and everyone writes it into the workbook by hand.

*(Final Urdu wording set from `docs/catalogue/LESSONS.md` once the research is promoted. The
sense: **the order in which things happened is the first thing to know and the last thing to
guess** — and: **an institution's first test is the day its founder is gone.**)*

### 0:43 · اگلے ہفتے (2 min)

The governors map goes back up. The speaker says: *this is the state on the night the Prophet ﷺ died.
Within months, most of this map has broken away.* Then the question, and stop:

> **کیوں؟ اور یہ واپس کیسے آیا؟**

No summary. End on the question (`CLAUDE.md` §2).

## 6. Risks specific to this session

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **سقیفہ draws a hostile question** on علی ؓ's bayʿah, فدک, or the Anṣār being wronged | The guardrail at 0:02; no live questions; pre-written answers in `QA_BANK.md` **before** delivery; rehearsed deferral to session 3 |
| **The orientation half feels like preamble** | The آغاز at 0:00 names the destination first |
| **The clan chart overwhelms** | Chart shown, not read. Four names spoken. The rest is a worksheet |
| **An invented date on the prophets timeline** | `[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]` labels on the slide; آدم ؑ and نوح ؑ off-scale |
| **75 minutes of material in a 45-minute slot** | Cut order fixed in §3; worksheets go home (`DECISIONS.md` #17) |
