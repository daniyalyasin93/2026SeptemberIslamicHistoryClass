# ⁨صفين⁩ — the year between ⁨الجمل⁩ and the raised ⁨مصاحف⁩ (36–37 AH)

**Question this note answers.** What happened in the twelve months between ⁨الجمل⁩ and the
morning the fighting stopped at ⁨صفين⁩: the letters that passed between ⁨علي ؓ⁩ and ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ and
what each is reported to have said; the embassy of ⁨جرير بن عبد الله ؓ⁩; the march up the
⁨الفرات⁩ and the two armies facing each other; the quarrel over the water; the weeks of daily
skirmishing and the men named in them; the killing of ⁨عمار بن ياسر ؓ⁩ and what it did to the
men who saw it; ⁨هاشم بن عتبة ؓ⁩ and the other dead who are named; ⁨ليلة الهرير⁩; and the raising
of the ⁨مصاحف⁩ on the spear points — who is reported to have proposed it, how each camp
reacted, and what our two books refuse to settle.

## 1. ⁨الكوفة⁩ becomes the seat, and two governors are called in

After ⁨الجمل علي ؓ⁩ saw ⁨أم المؤمنين عائشة ؓ⁩ on her way back toward ⁨مكة⁩ and then went from ⁨البصرة⁩
to ⁨الكوفة⁩. ⁨ابن كثير⁩ gives the date on the authority of ⁨أبو الكنود عبد الرحمن بن عبيد⁩, and with
it a small refusal that says a great deal about the man: they offered him the White Palace and
he would not take it, because ⁨عمر ؓ⁩ had disliked living in it.

> فدخلها علي يوم الإثنين لثنتي عشرة ليلةً خَلَتْ من رجب سنةَ ستٍّ وثلاثين فقيل له: انزل بالقصر الأبيض، فقال: لا! إن عمر (بن الخطاب) كان يكره نزوله فأنا أكرهه كذلك، فنزل في الرحبة وصلَّى في الجامع الأعظم ركعتين
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٣٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3583
> *English:* “He entered it on Monday, twelve nights having passed of ⁨رجب⁩ in the year thirty-six. He was told: lodge in the White Palace. He said: no — ⁨عمر⁩ disliked lodging in it, so I dislike it likewise. So he lodged in ⁨الرحبة⁩ and prayed two rakʿahs in the great mosque.”

Then he called in the two men who were still holding ⁨عثمان ؓ⁩'s provinces in the east —
⁨جرير بن عبد الله البجلي ؓ⁩ at ⁨همذان⁩ and ⁨الأشعث بن قيس ؓ⁩ at ⁨أذربيجان⁩ — to take the ⁨بيعة⁩ there and
come to him. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ says the same thing in almost the same words (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۲۹⁩). Both men
came. Both matter later: ⁨جرير ؓ⁩ carries the letter to ⁨الشام⁩, and ⁨الأشعث ؓ⁩ is the man who
negotiates the arbitration at the end of this note.

## 2. The embassy of ⁨جرير بن عبد الله ؓ⁩

When ⁨علي ؓ⁩ proposed to send to ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩, ⁨جرير ؓ⁩ asked for the errand himself, on the ground of
an old friendship. ⁨الأشتر⁩ objected in front of everyone; ⁨علي ؓ⁩ overruled him. Both books carry
the exchange, and both carry ⁨الأشتر⁩'s objection in the same plain form — that ⁨جرير ؓ⁩'s sympathy
lay with the other side.

> قال جرير بن عبد الله: أنا أذهب إليه (يا أمير المؤمنين) فإن بيني وبينه ودًا، فآخذ لك منه البيعة (٤)، فقال الأشتر: لا تبعثه يا أمير المؤمنين فإني أخشى أن يكون هواه معه.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٣٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3583
> *English:* “⁨جرير بن عبد الله⁩ said: I will go to him (O ⁨أمير المؤمنين⁩), for there is friendship between me and him, and I will take the ⁨بيعة⁩ from him for you. ⁨الأشتر⁩ said: do not send him, O ⁨أمير المؤمنين⁩, for I fear that his inclination is with him.”

The letter told ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ that ⁨المهاجرون⁩ and ⁨الأنصار⁩ had agreed on the ⁨بيعة⁩, gave him the news of
⁨الجمل⁩, and called him to enter what the people had entered. ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ called in ⁨عمرو بن العاص ؓ⁩
and the heads of ⁨الشام⁩, and their answer was the one condition that never changed all year.

> فأبوا أن يبايعوا حتى يقتل
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٣٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3583
> *English:* “They refused to give the ⁨بيعة⁩ until the killers of ⁨عثمان⁩ were killed, or until they were handed over to them; and if he would not do it, they would fight him and not give the ⁨بيعة⁩ until he killed…”

⁨جرير ؓ⁩ came back with that answer, and the quarrel between him and ⁨الأشتر⁩ broke out in front of
⁨علي ؓ⁩. It is worth giving because it is the one moment where two of ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s own men say to each
other, out loud, what each thinks the other has cost the ummah.

> فقال الأشتر: يا أمير المؤمنين ألم أنهك (٢) أن تبعث جريرًا؟ فلو كنت بعثتني لما فتح معاوية بابًا إلا أغلقته. فقال له جرير: لو كنت ثمَّ لقتلوكَ بدم عثمان. فقال الأشتر: والله لو بعثني لم يعنني جواب معاوية ولأعجلنه عن الفكرة، ولو أطاعني قبل لحبسك وأمثالك حتى يستقيم أمر هذه الأمة
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٣٨⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3584
> *English:* “⁨الأشتر⁩ said: O ⁨أمير المؤمنين⁩, did I not forbid you to send ⁨جرير⁩? Had you sent me, ⁨معاوية⁩ would not have opened a door but I would have shut it. ⁨جرير⁩ said to him: had you been there they would have killed you for the blood of ⁨عثمان⁩. ⁨الأشتر⁩ said: by God, had he sent me, their answer would not have baffled me, and I would have hurried him past his own calculation; and had he obeyed me before, he would have jailed you and your like until the affair of this ummah stood straight.”

⁨جرير ؓ⁩ left in anger and settled at ⁨قرقيسياء⁩. In ⁨ابن كثير⁩ he then writes to ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ and is
summoned to him (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۳۸⁩); ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ says the same (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۲۹⁩–۶۳۰).

**⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ adds a second explanation ⁨ابن كثير⁩ does not have.** He says it may not have been
⁨جرير ؓ⁩'s errand that failed at all, but ⁨شرحبيل بن السمط الكندي⁩ who caused ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ to send him
back empty-handed — and he traces it to a grudge from ⁨عمر ؓ⁩'s day between ⁨شرحبيل⁩ and
⁨الأشعث بن قيس⁩, worked out through ⁨جرير ؓ⁩ (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۳۰⁩). **The two books therefore give two
different reasons for the same refusal. Do not merge them.**

## 3. What was on the ⁨منبر⁩ in ⁨الشام⁩ — a scene only ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ carries

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ explains why ⁨الشام⁩ was already immovable before any letter arrived. This is not in
⁨ابن كثير⁩'s ⁨صفين⁩ chapter at all, and it is the single most vivid thing on these forty-four pages.

> وَكَانَ أَهْلُ الشَّامِ لَمَّا قَدِمَ عَلَيْهِمُ النُّعْمَانُ بْنُ بَشِيرٍ بِقَمِيصِ عُثْمَانَ الَّذِي قُتِلَ فِيهِ مَخْضُوبًا بِالدَّمِ بِأَصَابِعِ زَوْجَتِهِ نَائِلَةَ، إِصْبَعَانِ مِنْهَا وَشَيْءٌ مِنَ الْكَفِّ وَإِصْبَعَانِ مَقْطُوعَتَانِ مِنْ أُصُولِهِمَا، وَنِصْفُ الْإِبْهَامِ، وَضَعَ مُعَاوِيَةُ الْقَمِيصَ عَلَى الْمِنْبَرِ، وَجَمَعَ الْأَجْنَادَ إِلَيْهِ، فَبَكَوْا عَلَى الْقَمِيصِ مُدَّةً وَهُوَ عَلَى الْمِنْبَرِ، وَالْأَصَابِعُ مُعَلَّقَةٌ فِيه
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٢٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1318
> *English:* “When ⁨النعمان بن بشير⁩ came to the people of ⁨الشام⁩ with the shirt of ⁨عثمان⁩, the one he was killed in, dyed with blood, together with the fingers of his wife ⁨نائلة⁩ — two of them and part of the palm, and two fingers cut off at the root, and half the thumb — ⁨معاوية⁩ put the shirt on the ⁨منبر⁩ and gathered the troops to him, and they wept over the shirt for a time while it was on the ⁨منبر⁩, and the fingers hanging on it.”

And then the oath that made a negotiated settlement almost impossible:

> وَأَقْسَمَ رِجَالٌ مِنْ أَهْلِ الشَّامِ أَنْ لَا يَمَسَّهُمُ الْمَاءُ إِلَّا لِلْغُسْلِ مِنَ الْجَنَابَةِ، وَأَنْ لَا يَنَامُوا عَلَى الْفُرُشِ حَتَّى يَقْتُلُوا قَتَلَةَ عُثْمَانَ، وَمَنْ قَامَ دُونَهُمْ قَتَلُوهُ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٢٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1318
> *English:* “And men of the people of ⁨الشام⁩ swore that water should not touch them except for the ⁨غسل⁩ of ⁨جنابة⁩, and that they would not sleep on bedding, until they had killed the killers of ⁨عثمان⁩; and whoever stood in their way, they would kill him.”

`[SOURCED — ⁨الکامل⁩ only]`. The Kufan side of the same year has no equivalent scene in either
book. That asymmetry is itself a finding: our two books preserve the Syrian motive in an image
and the Iraqi motive only in speeches.

## 4. ⁨عمرو بن العاص ؓ⁩ decides — and the two books tell it differently

⁨ابن كثير⁩ disposes of it in three lines at the end of the previous chapter: ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩ had left
⁨المدينة⁩ before ⁨عثمان ؓ⁩ was besieged so as not to witness his killing, he had a grievance of his
own about being removed from ⁨مصر⁩, he settled near ⁨الأردن⁩, and when ⁨عثمان ؓ⁩ was killed he went to
⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ and gave him the ⁨بيعة⁩ on the undertaking to demand the blood (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۳۶⁩).

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ tells it as a conversation, and it is much harder. He has ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩ consult his two sons
⁨عبد الله ؓ⁩ and ⁨محمد⁩. ⁨عبد الله⁩'s counsel:

> تُوُفِّيَ النَّبِيُّ - صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ - وَأَبُو بَكْرٍ وَعُمَرَ وَهُمْ عَنْكَ رَاضُونَ، فَأَرَى أَنْ تَكُفَّ يَدَكَ وَتَجْلِسَ فِي بَيْتِكَ حَتَّى يَجْتَمِعَ النَّاس
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٢٨⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1317
> *English:* “The Prophet ﷺ died, and ⁨أبو بكر⁩ and ⁨عمر⁩, and they were pleased with you. So I hold that you should withhold your hand and sit in your house until the people gather…”

And the father's verdict on his two sons' advice, which ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ lets stand without comment:

> أَمَّا أَنْتَ يَا عَبْدَ اللَّهِ فَأَمَرْتَنِي بِمَا هُوَ خَيْرٌ لِي فِي آخِرَتِي وَأَسْلَمُ لِي فِي دِينِي، وَأَمَّا أَنْتَ يَا مُحَمَّدُ فَأَمَرْتَنِي بِمَا هُوَ خَيْرٌ لِي فِي دُنْيَايَ، وَشَرٌّ لِي فِي آخِرَتِي.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٢٨⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1317
> *English:* “As for you, O ⁨عبد الله⁩, you have ordered me to what is better for me in my ⁨آخرة⁩ and safer for me in my ⁨دين⁩; and as for you, O ⁨محمد⁩, you have ordered me to what is better for me in my ⁨دنيا⁩ and worse for me in my ⁨آخرة⁩.”

**A warning about the next passage.** On the same page ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ also carries a reported
sentence of ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩ to ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ that ends «⁨ولكنا أردنا هذه الدنيا⁩» — we wanted this world. It is a
severe thing to say of a Companion, ⁨ابن كثير⁩ does not carry it, and it rests on the same
⁨سيف⁩/⁨أبو مخنف⁩-type material that ⁨ابن كثير⁩ rejects elsewhere in this very chapter. **It is
recorded here for completeness and it does not go on a slide or into the hall.** See
§ *Not for the hall*.

## 5. The march: ⁨النخيلة⁩, ⁨الرقة⁩, and a vanguard that arrives from behind

⁨علي ؓ⁩ camped at ⁨النخيلة⁩ and left ⁨أبو مسعود عقبة بن عمرو البدري الأنصاري ؓ⁩ over ⁨الكوفة⁩. Some of
his people advised him to stay in ⁨الكوفة⁩ and send armies; others told him to go himself. He went
himself. ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩, when the news reached him, asked ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩, who said: then you go yourself too
(البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۳۸ · الکامل ج۲ ص۶۳۱).

Two travelling details are worth the telling. The first is ⁨الأشتر⁩ at ⁨الرقة⁩, which only
⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ has: the townsmen had pulled their boats over to their own bank and refused a bridge.

> أُقْسِمُ بِاللَّهِ لَئِنْ لَمْ تَعْمَلُوا جِسْرًا يَعْبُرُ عَلَيْهِ أَمِيرُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ لِأُجَرِّدَنَّ فِيكُمُ السَّيْفَ، وَلَأَقْتُلَنَّ الرِّجَالَ، وَلَآخُذَنَّ الْأَمْوَالَ!
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٣٣⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1322
> *English:* “I swear by God, if you do not build a bridge for ⁨أمير المؤمنين⁩ to cross on, I will bare the sword among you, and I will kill the men, and I will take the property!”

They built it. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩'s comment on why is the drily funny line of the whole march: they said
to one another, it is ⁨الأشتر⁩, and he is likely to keep his oath, or bring more than it
(الکامل ج۲ ص۶۳۳).

The second is the vanguard. ⁨زياد بن النضر الحارثي⁩ went out in front with eight thousand and
⁨شريح بن هانئ⁩ with four thousand. Hearing that ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ was already moving, they judged it wrong
to march with the river between themselves and their own army, were refused a crossing at ⁨عانات⁩,
crossed at ⁨هيت⁩ instead — and came up behind ⁨علي ؓ⁩, who had passed them.

> فقال علي: مقدمتي تأتي من ورائي؟ فاعتذروا إليه بما جرى لهم، فعذرهم
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٠⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3586
> *English:* “⁨علي⁩ said: my vanguard comes to me from my rear? They excused themselves to him for what had happened, and he excused them.”

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ has the same sentence and adds ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s one-word verdict on their judgement —
**⁨سُدِّدْتُمَا⁩**, you two were rightly guided (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۳۳⁩). `[SOURCED]`

## 6. The monk on the ⁨البليخ⁩ — a story, and how ⁨ابن كثير⁩ treats it

On the way, near ⁨الرقة⁩, at a place called ⁨البليخ⁩ on the bank of the ⁨الفرات⁩, a monk is reported to
have come down from his cell with a book his forefathers had handed down, describing an
unlettered prophet and then a man of his ummah who would pass by the bank of this very ⁨الفرات⁩.

> فمضى الراهب معه وأسلم فكان مع عليّ حتى أصيب يوم صفين.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٣٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3585
> *English:* “So the monk went with him and accepted Islam, and he was with ⁨علي⁩ until he was struck down on the day of ⁨صفين⁩.”

And ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s reported reaction on hearing the book read:

> الحمد لله الذي لم يجعلني عنده نسيًا منسيًا، والحمد لله الذي ذكرني عنده في كتب الأبرار.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٣٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3585
> *English:* “Praise be to God, who did not make me with Him a thing forgotten; and praise be to God, who mentioned me with Him in the books of the righteous.”

**This is exactly the kind of report that has to be handled out loud, not quietly dropped.**
⁨ابن كثير⁩ gives it with its full chain, which runs through ⁨نصر بن مزاحم⁩, and the printed edition's
editor stops the reader at that name in a footnote on the page where the chain first appears:

> نصر بن مزاحم هذا كذاب، كما في الميزان (٤/ ٢٥٤)، فلا يشك عاقل بأن هذا الخبر كذت محض.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٣٨⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3584
> *English:* “This ⁨نصر بن مزاحم⁩ is a liar, as in ⁨الميزان⁩ (4/254), so no sensible man doubts that this report is a pure falsehood.”

That is the **editor's** footnote, not ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s sentence — say so if it is used. But the same
chain, and the same objection, will come back twice more in this note (§10 and §14), and by the
third time the audience should have learned the habit: **a story in a history book is not a
ruling, and even the books themselves say so on the page.** `[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۳۸⁩ (the
footnote), ۴۳۹ (the story)

## 7. The water

⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s army got to the ground first and chose it: open, level, and holding the only watering
place on that reach of the river.

> وَأَخَذَ شَرِيعَةَ الْفُرَاتِ، وَلَيْسَ فِي ذَلِكَ الصُّقْعِ شَرِيعَةٌ غَيْرَهَا، وَجَعَلَهَا فِي حَيِّزِهِ، وَبَعَثَ عَلَيْهَا أَبَا الْأَعْوَرِ السُّلَمِيَّ يَحْمِيهَا وَيَمْنُعُهَا
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٣٤⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1323
> *English:* “And he took the watering place of the ⁨الفرات⁩ — and there is no watering place in that tract but it — and made it inside his own lines, and posted ⁨أبو الأعور السلمي⁩ on it to guard it and keep others off.”

⁨علي ؓ⁩ came up and camped away from the water. His men went down to drink and were driven off.
⁨ابن كثير⁩ gives what was said to them as they were driven off, and it is the sentence that
explains the whole year in eight words:

> فمنعهم أولئك وقال: موتوا عطشًا كما منعتم عثمان الماء
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤١⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3587
> *English:* “Those men kept them off and said: die of thirst as you kept the water from ⁨عثمان⁩.”

⁨علي ؓ⁩ sent ⁨صعصعة بن صوحان⁩ with a formal protest: we came unwilling to fight you before putting
the argument to you; your vanguard attacked us before we attacked you; and now this second thing,
you have kept the people from the water. ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ put it to his council, and the council split.
⁨ابن كثير⁩ and ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ agree on who said what, and both put ⁨عمرو بن العاص ؓ⁩ on the side of
letting them drink.

> فَقَالَ عَمْرُو بْنُ الْعَاصِ: خَلِّ بَيْنَ الْقَوْمِ وَبَيْنَ الْمَاءِ، وَإِنَّهُمْ لَنْ يَعْطَشُوا وَأَنْتَ رَيَّانٌ، وَلَكِنْ بِغَيْرِ الْمَاءِ، فَانْظُرْ فِيمَا بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَ اللَّهِ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٣٥⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1324
> *English:* “⁨عمرو بن العاص⁩ said: leave the people and the water alone. They will not go thirsty while you are watered — but by something other than water. So look to what is between you and God.”

⁨ابن كثير⁩'s wording of the same intervention is shorter and sharper:

> فقال عمرو (٨): خل بينهم وبينه، فليس من النصف أن نكون ريَّانين وهم عطاش
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤١⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3587
> *English:* “⁨عمرو⁩ said: leave them and it alone; it is not fair dealing that we should be watered and they thirsty.”

Against him, ⁨الوليد بن عقبة⁩ and ⁨عبد الله بن سعد بن أبي سرح⁩ argued for keeping the water. ⁨ابن كثير⁩
gives ⁨الوليد⁩'s argument in full — let them taste of thirst what they made ⁨أمير المؤمنين عثمان⁩
taste when they besieged him and kept good water and food from him forty mornings — and
⁨ابن أبي سرح⁩'s — keep it from them till nightfall and perhaps they will go home
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۱⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۳۵⁩). ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ himself said nothing except that his opinion would
come later.

**And then ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩, on the very same page, records the objection that both those men were not
at ⁨صفين⁩ at all:**

> وَقَدْ قِيلَ: إِنَّ الْوَلِيدَ وَابْنَ أَبِي سَرْحٍ لَمْ يَشْهَدَا صِفِّينَ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٣٥⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1324
> *English:* “And it has been said: ⁨الوليد⁩ and ⁨ابن أبي سرح⁩ were not present at ⁨صفين⁩.”

That single line is the most useful thing on the page for our purposes. It is a historian
recording a scene and, in the next breath, recording that the two men who speak the worst lines
in it may not have been there. **Narrate the scene and narrate the objection together, or narrate
neither.** `[SOURCED — disputed within ⁨الکامل⁩ itself]`

The Iraqis then took the water by force. The rajaz shouted at the Syrians as they came on is in
both books:

> خَلُّوا لنا ماءَ الفُراتِ الجاري … أو اثبتوا لِجَحْفَلٍ جَرّار
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤١⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3587
> *English:* “Leave us the running water of the ⁨الفرات⁩ — or stand your ground before a heavy host.”

**What happened next is the sharpest disagreement between our two books in this whole strand.**

⁨ابن كثير⁩: the Iraqis pushed the Syrians off the water and then the two armies simply shared it.

> ثم اصطلحوا على الورود حتى صاروا يزدحمون في تلك الشريعة لا يكلم أحدٌ أحدًا، ولا يؤذي إنسانٌ (٤) إنسانًا.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤١⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3587
> *English:* “Then they agreed on coming down to drink, until they were crowding one another at that watering place, no man speaking a hard word to another, and no man harming another.”

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩: the Iraqis, having taken it, swore they would not let the Syrians drink, and ⁨علي ؓ⁩
had to overrule his own army.

> فَقَالُوا: وَاللَّهِ لَا نُسْقِيهِ أَهْلَ الشَّامِ! فَأَرْسَلَ عَلِيٌّ إِلَى أَصْحَابِهِ: أَنْ خُذُوا مِنَ الْمَاءِ حَاجَتَكُمْ وَخَلُّوا عَنْهُمْ، فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ نَصَرَكُمْ بِبَغْيِهِمْ وَظُلْمِهِمْ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٣٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1325
> *English:* “They said: by God, we will not give the people of ⁨الشام⁩ to drink of it! So ⁨علي⁩ sent to his companions: take from the water what you need and leave them to it, for God gave you the victory by their transgression and wrongdoing.”

**Do not merge these.** In ⁨ابن كثير⁩ the decency is the armies'; in ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ it is ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s
order against his armies' wish. The second is the better story and the first is the older
telling; both are on the page. If it is told in the hall, tell ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩'s and say whose it is.

## 8. ⁨ذو الحجة⁩ 36 AH — a month of single days

For the whole of ⁨ذو الحجة⁩ the two armies fought a limited war: a named man went out each day with
a force, and a named man came out to meet him, and at evening they drew apart. Sometimes twice in
one day. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ explains the restraint outright — they were unwilling to throw the whole of
⁨العراق⁩ against the whole of ⁨الشام⁩ for fear of annihilation (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۳۷⁩).

**⁨علي ؓ⁩'s day-commanders**, as both books list them: ⁨الأشتر النخعي⁩ (most often of all),
حجر بن عدي الكندي, شبث بن ربعي, خالد بن المعمر, زياد بن النضر الحارثي, زياد بن خصفة التيمي,
سعيد بن قيس الهمداني, معقل بن قيس, قيس بن سعد بن عبادة ؓ.

**⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s day-commanders:** ⁨عبد الرحمن بن خالد بن الوليد⁩, ⁨أبو الأعور السلمي⁩,
حبيب بن مسلمة الفهري, ذو الكلاع الحميري, عبيد الله بن عمر بن الخطاب, شرحبيل بن السمط,
⁨حمزة⁩/⁨حُمرة بن مالك الهمداني⁩. `[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۲⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۳۷⁩

Before that month, a formal embassy had already been sent and refused: ⁨بشير بن عمرو الأنصاري ؓ⁩,
⁨سعيد بن قيس الهمداني⁩ and ⁨شبث بن ربعي⁩. ⁨بشير ؓ⁩'s opening is the most quotable thing said to
⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ all year:

> يا معاوية! إن الدنيا عنك زائلة، وإنك راجع إلى الآخرة، والله محاسبك بعملك، ومجازيك بما قدمت يداك، وإني أنشدك الله أن تفرق جماعة هذه الأمة، وأن (٣) تسفك (دماءها) بينها.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٢⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3588
> *English:* “O ⁨معاوية⁩, the world is passing away from you, and you are returning to the ⁨آخرة⁩, and God is reckoning with you for your deed and requiting you for what your hands have sent forward; and I adjure you by God that you not split the body of this ummah, and not spill its blood among itself.”

The answer was the same four words it had been since the first letter:

> فقال معاوية: ويُطلُّ دمُ عثمان؟ لا والله لا أفعل ذلك أبدًا
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٢⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3588
> *English:* “⁨معاوية⁩ said: and is the blood of ⁨عثمان⁩ to be let go for nothing? No, by God, I will never do that.”

## 9. ⁨المحرم⁩ 37 AH — the truce, and the embassies that failed

At the turn of the year the two armies stopped fighting for the month, hoping something could be
settled. ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s summary of why is worth having as the neutral sentence, because it is the one
sentence in the chapter with no blame in it at all:

> فلما انسلخ ذو الحجة ودخل المحرم تداعى الناس للمتاركة، لعل الله أن يصلح بينهم على أمر يكون فيه حقن دمائهم
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٣⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3589
> *English:* “When ⁨ذو الحجة⁩ passed and ⁨المحرم⁩ came in, the people called one another to leave off, hoping that God might set matters right between them in a way that would spare their blood.”

Four men went from ⁨علي ؓ⁩: ⁨عدي بن حاتم الطائي ؓ⁩, ⁨يزيد بن قيس الأرحبي⁩, ⁨شبث بن ربعي⁩, ⁨زياد بن خصفة⁩.
⁨عدي ؓ⁩'s speech asked for a thing that would gather the ummah's word and stop the blood, and ended
with a warning about ⁨الجمل⁩. ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s reply refused the errand and the man:

> فقال له معاوية: كأنك إنما جئت متهددًا (٦) ولم تأت مصلحًا، هيهات والله (٧) يا عدي، كلا والله إني لابن حرب، لا يقعقع (٨) لي بالشِّنان
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٣⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3589
> *English:* “⁨معاوية⁩ said to him: it is as if you came threatening and did not come reconciling. Far from it, by God, O ⁨عدي⁩! Never — I am the son of ⁨حرب⁩; dry skins are not rattled at me.”

Then the exchange that decides the shape of this whole strand, because it puts ⁨عمار بن ياسر ؓ⁩'s
name into the room five weeks before he is killed. **The two books give it differently and the
difference matters.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩:

> أنشدك الله يا معاوية، لو تمكنت من عمار أكنت قاتله بعثمان؟ قال معاوية: [والله] لو تمكنت من ابن سُمَيَّة ما قتلته بعثمان، ولكني كنت قتلته بغلام عثمان.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٤⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3590
> *English:* “[⁨شبث⁩ said:] I adjure you by God, O ⁨معاوية⁩ — if you had ⁨عمار⁩ in your power, would you kill him for ⁨عثمان⁩? ⁨معاوية⁩ said: [by God] if I had ⁨ابن سمية⁩ in my power I would not kill him for ⁨عثمان⁩ — but I would kill him for ⁨عثمان⁩'s servant.”

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ has ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ answer with a question instead of a denial:

> فَقَالَ: وَمَا يَمْنَعُنِي مِنْ ذَلِكَ؟ لَوْ تَمَكَّنْتُ (مِنِ ابْنِ سُمَيَّةَ) لَقَتَلْتُهُ بِمَوْلَى عُثْمَانَ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٤٢⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1331
> *English:* “He said: and what would stop me from that? If I had (⁨ابن سمية⁩) in my power I would kill him for ⁨عثمان⁩'s client.”

⁨شبث⁩'s reply is the same in both, and it is a threat: you will not reach ⁨عمار⁩ until heads fly from
their shoulders and the wide earth is narrow on you. `[SOURCED — the two books differ on
⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s words; give both or give neither]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۴⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۲⁩

⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s embassy in return — ⁨حبيب بن مسلمة الفهري⁩, ⁨شرحبيل بن السمط⁩, ⁨معن بن يزيد بن الأخنس⁩ —
asked ⁨علي ؓ⁩ for two things: hand over the killers, then step aside and let it be ⁨شورى⁩. It went
worse than the other. Both books record the demand and both record ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s flat refusal to
entertain it from that man (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۴⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۲⁩).

## 10. The question ⁨علي ؓ⁩ would not answer — and what ⁨ابن كثير⁩ refuses to certify

The envoys pressed him for a verdict on ⁨عثمان ؓ⁩, and got the one answer that keeps a historian
honest and satisfies nobody:

> وأنه قال في غبون ذلك: لا أقول إن عثمان قُتل مظلومًا
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٤⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3590
> *English:* “And that he said in the course of it: I do not say that ⁨عثمان⁩ was killed wronged, nor [that he was killed] wronging.”

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ gives the same refusal in the same words and adds the envoys' response — whoever does
not hold that he was killed wronged, we are quit of him — and their walking out (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۳⁩).
Both books then have ⁨علي ؓ⁩ recite ⁨النمل ۸۰⁩–۸۱ at their backs.

**And now the most valuable material in this note.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩, having reported the long speech,
refuses to certify it:

> ثم ذكر أهل السير كلامًا طويلًا جرى بينهم وبين علي، وفي صحة ذلك عنهم وعنه نظر
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٤⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3590
> *English:* “Then the writers of ⁨سير⁩ mention a long speech that passed between them and ⁨علي⁩, and the soundness of that from them and from him is open to question.”

And on the following page, of the sentence attributed to ⁨علي ؓ⁩ that closes it, he says flatly:

> وهذا عندي لا يصح عن علي ﵁.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٥⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3591
> *English:* “And this, in my view, is not soundly established from ⁨علي ؓ⁩.”

**This is the guardrail in the source's own voice, and it is the single most useful finding on
these seventy-eight pages.** When the room wants a verdict on who was right in a reported speech,
the answer from the platform is not the speaker's opinion: it is that ⁨ابن كثير⁩, reading the same
report, wrote *this is not soundly established*, and stopped. `[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۴⁩–۴۴۵

The same page also records what the shuttling reciters got out of the two men, question by
question — **did you kill him? / no, and you know I did not. / then you ordered it. / by God I
neither killed nor ordered nor sided. / then give us his killers, they are in your camp. / the
people construed the Qur'an against him in a fitna and killed him under his own authority, and I
have no way against them.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ runs the whole shuttle and it ends nowhere
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۵⁩). `[SOURCED]`

## 11. The men who came, looked, and went home

Two Companions crossed the lines to try, and then withdrew and fought for neither side. They are
the most useful people in the chapter for a room that wants to know what an ordinary Muslim was
supposed to do that year.

> وخرج أبو الدرداء وأبو أمامة فدخلا على معاوية فقالا له: يا معاوية على م تقاتل هذا الرجل؟ فوالله إنه أقدم (٧) منك ومن أبيك إسلامًا، وأقرب منك إلى رسول الله ﷺ وأحق بهذا الأمر منك.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٥⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3591
> *English:* “⁨أبو الدرداء⁩ and ⁨أبو أمامة⁩ went out and came in to ⁨معاوية⁩ and said to him: O ⁨معاوية⁩, on what do you fight this man? By God, he is earlier in Islam than you and than your father, and nearer than you to the Messenger of God ﷺ, and worthier of this affair than you.”

⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ sent them on to ⁨علي ؓ⁩ with the same one condition. What they found there, ⁨ابن كثير⁩
reports without comment: a great crowd came out and said, *all of us killed ⁨عثمان⁩.*

> فمن شاء فليرمنا [ويكدنا]. قال: فرجع أبو الدرداء وأبو أمامة فلم يشهدا لهم قتالًا بل لزما بيوتهما.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3592
> *English:* “…so let whoever wishes shoot at us [and plot against us]. He said: so ⁨أبو الدرداء⁩ and ⁨أبو أمامة⁩ went back and did not witness any fighting for them, but kept to their houses.”

⁨ابن كثير⁩ reinforces the point twice more in the chapter: ⁨ابن بطة⁩'s report from ⁨بكير بن الأشج⁩ that
men of ⁨بدر⁩ shut their doors after ⁨عثمان ؓ⁩ was killed and came out only to their graves
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۳۷⁩); and ⁨محمد بن سيرين⁩'s:

> قال: هاجت الفتنة وأصحاب رسول الله ﷺ عشرات الألوف فلم يحضرها منهم مئة، بل لم يبلغوا ثلاثين.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٣٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3583
> *English:* “He said: the fitna broke out while the Companions of the Messenger of God ﷺ were tens of thousands, and not a hundred of them were present at it — indeed they did not reach thirty.”

**But the numbers are disputed inside the chapter itself.** On the same page ⁨ابن كثير⁩ gives the
Imam ⁨أحمد⁩ tradition in which ⁨شعبة⁩ calls ⁨أبو شيبة⁩ a liar for saying seventy ⁨بدريون⁩ were at ⁨صفين⁩,
and says only ⁨خزيمة بن ثابت ؓ⁩ was found — with ⁨سهل بن حنيف ؓ⁩ and ⁨أبو أيوب الأنصاري ؓ⁩ added on
⁨ابن تيمية⁩'s authority:

> شهد صفين من أهل بدر سبعون رجلًا، فقال: كذب أبو شيبة، والله لقد ذاكرنا الحكم في ذلك فما وجدناه شهد صفين من أهل بدر غير خُزَيْمة بن ثابت؟
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٣٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3583
> *English:* “Seventy men of the people of ⁨بدر⁩ were present at ⁨صفين⁩. He said: ⁨أبو شيبة⁩ lied. By God, we put that to ⁨الحكم⁩ and did not find that anyone of the people of ⁨بدر⁩ was present at ⁨صفين⁩ except ⁨خزيمة بن ثابت⁩.”

Against which, a page later, ⁨ابن كثير⁩ reports from ⁨الحكم بن عتيبة⁩ that ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s army had **eighty
⁨بدريون⁩ and a hundred and fifty of those who gave the ⁨بيعة⁩ under the tree** (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۳۸⁩).
**The chapter contains both. Neither number may be given on a slide as the number.**
`[SOURCED — disputed]`

## 12. The rules ⁨علي ؓ⁩ gave before the lines closed

When ⁨المحرم⁩ ran out, ⁨علي ؓ⁩ had it cried to the Syrian camp at sunset that the truce was over and
he had thrown their pledge back to them on equal terms. Then the orders. ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s version:

> وأنه لا يُذَفَّفُ على جريحٍ ولا يتبع مدبرٌ ولا يُكْشفُ سترُ امرأةٍ ولا تُهان، وإن شتمت أمراء الناس وصلحاءهم
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3593
> *English:* “…and that no wounded man is to be finished off, and no man who turns his back is to be pursued, and no woman's covering is to be stripped and she is not to be humiliated — even if she abuses the people's commanders and their righteous men.”

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩'s is longer and adds four more prohibitions — no mutilation of the dead, no tearing of
a curtain, no entering a house, no taking of their property:

> فَإِذَا هَزَمْتُمُوهُمْ فَلَا تَقْتُلُوا مُدْبِرًا، وَلَا تُجْهِزُوا عَلَى جَرِيحٍ، وَلَا تَكْشِفُوا عَوْرَةً، وَلَا تُمَثِّلُوا بِقَتِيلٍ، وَإِذَا وَصَلْتُمْ إِلَى رِحَالِ الْقَوْمِ فَلَا تَهْتِكُوا سِتْرًا وَلَا تَدْخُلُوا دَارًا، وَلَا تَأْخُذُوا شَيْئًا مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ، وَلَا تُهَيِّجُوا امْرَأَة
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٤٥⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1334
> *English:* “And when you have routed them, do not kill a man who turns his back, and do not finish off a wounded man, and do not uncover a nakedness, and do not mutilate a slain man; and when you reach the people's quarters, do not tear a curtain, and do not enter a house, and do not take anything of their property, and do not stir up a woman.”

And ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ adds the sentence that makes the restraint an argument rather than a courtesy:
**you are, by God's praise, upon a proof, and your leaving off fighting them is a second proof**
(⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۵⁩). `[SOURCED]`

## 13. ⁨صفر⁩ 37 AH — seven days of named champions, and men tied together so they cannot run

⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s line went out on the first day of ⁨صفر⁩ with five ranks of men who had given him the
⁨بيعة⁩ on death and had **tied their own legs together with their turbans** so that flight was
physically impossible.

> وَبَايَعَ رِجَالٌ مِنْ أَهْلِ الشَّامِ عَلَى الْمَوْتِ، فَعَقَلُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ بِالْعَمَائِمِ، وَكَانُوا خَمْسَةَ صُفُوف
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٤٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1335
> *English:* “And men of the people of ⁨الشام⁩ gave the ⁨بيعة⁩ on death, and hobbled themselves with their turbans, and they were five ranks.”

The seven days, as ⁨ابن كثير⁩ dates them (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۸⁩–۴۴۹; ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ agrees on the pairings,
الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۶–۶۴۷):

| Day | ⁨العراق⁩ | ⁨الشام⁩ |
|---|---|---|
| Wednesday 1 ⁨صفر⁩ | ⁨الأشتر النخعي⁩ | ⁨حبيب بن مسلمة⁩ |
| Thursday | ⁨هاشم بن عتبة ؓ⁩ | ⁨أبو الأعور السلمي⁩ |
| Friday | ⁨عمار بن ياسر ؓ⁩ | ⁨عمرو بن العاص ؓ⁩ |
| Saturday | ⁨محمد ابن الحنفية⁩ | ⁨عبيد الله بن عمر⁩ |
| Sunday | ⁨عبد الله بن عباس ؓ⁩ | ⁨الوليد بن عقبة⁩ |
| Monday | ⁨قيس بن سعد ؓ⁩ | ⁨ابن ذي الكلاع الحميري⁩ |
| Tuesday | ⁨الأشتر النخعي⁩ | ⁨حبيب بن مسلمة⁩ |

⁨ابن كثير⁩'s verdict on the week: **⁨ولم يغلب أحد أحدًا في هذه الأيام كلها⁩** — no one beat anyone in
any of those days (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۹⁩). `[SOURCED]`

Two things from that week are worth a card each. The first, on the Friday, is a duel that did not
happen: ⁨زياد بن النضر الحارثي⁩ and the man who came out against him recognised each other as
brothers by the same mother, and each turned back to his own people and left the other alone.

> وَبَارَزَ يَوْمَئِذٍ زِيَادُ بْنُ النَّضْرِ أَخَاهُ لِأُمِّهِ، وَاسْمُهُ عَمْرُو بْنُ مُعَاوِيَةَ مِنْ بَنِي الْمُنْتَفِقِ، فَلَمَّا الْتَقَيَا تَعَارَفَا، فَانْصَرَفَ كُلُّ وَاحِدٍ مِنْهُمَا عَنْ صَاحِبِهِ وَتَرَاجَعَ النَّاسُ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٤٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1335
> *English:* “And that day ⁨زياد بن النضر⁩ fought his brother by his mother, whose name was ⁨عمرو بن معاوية⁩ of ⁨بنو المنتفق⁩; when they met they recognised one another, and each turned away from his fellow, and the people drew back.”

The second is the Saturday. ⁨عبيد الله بن عمر⁩ challenged ⁨محمد ابن الحنفية⁩ to single combat and the
son went out — and his father stopped him, moved his own mount forward, and offered himself
instead. ⁨عبيد الله⁩ declined. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ ends the scene with a father's correction that belongs in
the hall:

> فَقَالَ عَلِيٌّ: يَا بُنَيَّ لَا تَقُلْ فِي أَبِيهِ إِلَّا خَيْرًا.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٤٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1336
> *English:* “⁨علي⁩ said: O my son, say nothing of his father but good.”

(The son had just called ⁨عبيد الله⁩ a ⁨فاسق⁩; the father let the word about the son pass and
corrected only the word about the father — and the father is ⁨عمر بن الخطاب ؓ⁩.) `[SOURCED —
⁨الکامل⁩ only]` ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۷⁩

## 14. The night before the general engagement

⁨علي ؓ⁩ decided the week of champions was getting nowhere — **⁨حتى متى لا نناهض هؤلاء القوم⁩
⁨بأجمعنا؟⁩** how long do we not face these people with all of us? — and preached on the Tuesday
evening. The khutba is in both books almost identically. Its close:

> ألا وإنكم لاقوا القومِ غدًا فأطيلوا الليلةَ القيامَ، وأكثروا تلاوةَ القرآن، واسألوا الله النصر والصبر والقَوْهم (٦) بالجدّ والحزم وكونوا صادقين.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3595
> *English:* “Know that you will meet the people tomorrow: so make the standing long tonight, and read much of the Qur'an, and ask God for victory and for endurance, and meet them with earnestness and resolve, and be truthful.”

They went to their weapons. And a Syrian Christian Arab poet of ⁨تغلب⁩, ⁨كعب بن جعيل⁩, walking past
and seeing what they were doing, said the two lines that are the whole of ⁨صفين⁩ in twenty words:

> أَصْبَحَتِ الأمَّةُ في أمْرٍ عَجَبْ … والمُلْكُ مَجْموعٌ غدًا لمنْ غَلَبْ فَقُلتُ قَوْلًا صادقًا غيرَ كَذِبْ … إن غدًا تهلك أعْلامُ العَرَب
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٤٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3595
> *English:* “The ummah has woken into a strange affair, and the kingship is gathered tomorrow to whoever wins. I have spoken a true word, no lie in it: tomorrow the standards of the Arabs perish.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۹⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۷⁩ — ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ has the same two lines from the same
man on the same evening.

Then a thing that only ⁨ابن كثير⁩ has, and that no other sentence in the chapter matches: ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s
prayer as the lines faced each other, which asks for opposite things in the two possible outcomes.

> إن أظهرتنا على عدونا فجنبنا البغي والفساد وسددنا للحق، وإن أظهرتهم علينا فارزقني (٤) الشهادة وجنب بقية أصحابي من الفتنة.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٥٠⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3596
> *English:* “If You give us the upper hand over our enemy, then keep us far from transgression and corruption and set us straight for the truth; and if You give them the upper hand over us, then grant me martyrdom, and keep the rest of my companions far from the fitna.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۰⁩. Of everything on these pages, this is the line a room will
remember — because a man about to fight asks first not to be made a tyrant by winning.

## 15. Thursday: ⁨عبد الله بن بديل ؓ⁩, and the right wing that broke

⁨علي ؓ⁩'s right was ⁨عبد الله بن بديل بن ورقاء الخزاعي ؓ⁩; his left ⁨ابن عباس ؓ⁩; the ⁨قراء⁩ with ⁨عمار ؓ⁩,
⁨قيس بن سعد ؓ⁩ and ⁨ابن بديل ؓ⁩; he himself in the centre with the people of ⁨المدينة⁩. ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ raised
a great domed tent, threw cloths over it, and the horse of ⁨دمشق⁩ stood round it
(الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۸).

⁨ابن بديل ؓ⁩ drove ⁨حبيب بن مسلمة⁩'s left back until he had pushed it into ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s own centre —
⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ says up to the ⁨قبة⁩ itself, by noon. Then ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ sent the men who had sworn on death
against that wing, and the Iraqi right broke, and the rout ran back as far as ⁨علي ؓ⁩ himself
(البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۲ · الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۹).

There follows the scene both books tell and neither embroiders: ⁨علي ؓ⁩ walking, not running, with
the arrows passing between his neck and his shoulders and his sons trying to shield him. ⁨الحسن ؓ⁩
asked him to hurry.

> يا بني إن لأبيك يومًا لن يعدوه ولا يبطئ به عنه (٥) السعي ولا يعجل به إليه المشي إن أباك والله ما يبالي وقع على الموت أو وقع عليه [الموت]
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٥٢⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3598
> *English:* “O my son, your father has a day he will not outrun; hastening will not delay him from it and walking will not bring him to it sooner. By God, your father does not care whether he falls upon death or death falls upon him.”

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ gives it in nearly the same words (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۵۰⁩) and adds what he said when he
reached ⁨ربيعة⁩, who had stood when ⁨مضر⁩ gave way: whose banners are these? — the banners of ⁨ربيعة⁩.
— rather, banners whose people God has preserved. `[SOURCED]` ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۵۰⁩

⁨الأشتر⁩ was sent to turn the rout, and turned it. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ preserves the message ⁨علي ؓ⁩ sent with
him, which is the best single sentence said to running men in the chapter:

> أَيْنَ فِرَارُكُمْ مِنَ الْمَوْتِ الَّذِي لَنْ تُعْجِزُوهُ إِلَى الْحَيَاةِ الَّتِي لَا تَبْقَى لَكُمْ؟
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٥١⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1340
> *English:* “Where is your flight — from the death you will not outrun, to the life that will not last for you?”

⁨ابن بديل ؓ⁩, re-joined by ⁨الأشتر⁩ with some two or three hundred ⁨قراء⁩ still lying to the ground
where they had been left, was ordered by ⁨الأشتر⁩ to hold his place. He refused, and charged
⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s standing-place, where the men round it were like mountains and ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ had a sword in
each hand. He killed everyone who came near him, reached him, was surrounded, and was killed
(البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۳ · الکامل ج۲ ص۶۵۳).

Then the moment that gives a Companion his epitaph out of the mouth of the man whose army killed
him. ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ sent to have the body identified; nobody knew it; he went himself.

> هَذَا عَبْدُ اللَّهِ بْنُ بُدَيْلٍ، وَاللَّهِ لَوِ اسْتَطَاعَتْ نِسَاءُ خُزَاعَةَ لَقَاتَلَتْنَا فَضْلًا عَنْ رِجَالِهَا!
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٥٣⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1342
> *English:* “This is ⁨عبد الله بن بديل⁩. By God, if the women of ⁨خزاعة⁩ were able they would fight us, let alone her men!”

⁨ابن كثير⁩ has the same identification and then ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ reciting ⁨حاتم الطائي⁩ over him — *he is
war's brother: if war bites him he bites her* (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۳⁩). `[SOURCED — both books, the
wording differs]`

⁨الأشتر⁩ then took the right wing and drove it back through **four** of the five turban-tied ranks,
to the fifth, which was ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s own (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۳⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۵۳⁩).

**A disagreement to keep.** ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ says plainly that ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ called for his horse and that
he afterwards said the verses of ⁨ابن الإطنابة الأنصاري⁩ were what stopped him running
(⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۵۳⁩–۶۵۴). ⁨ابن كثير⁩ gives the *same* verses twice: first as what ⁨الأشتر⁩ said steadied
**him** when he nearly fled, and then — introduced with **⁨والعجب أن ابن ديزيل روى في كتابه⁩** —
as ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۳⁩–۴۵۴). ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s own word for the second version is **⁨العجب⁩**, the
strange thing. **Both attributions are on the page; report the doubt, not one of them.**
`[SOURCED — disputed]`

## 16. ⁨عبيد الله بن عمر⁩, and ⁨ذو الكلاع⁩

⁨عبيد الله بن عمر بن الخطاب⁩ was killed at ⁨صفين⁩, fighting for ⁨الشام⁩. ⁨ابن كثير⁩ keeps a domestic
detail that makes it human and then a brutal one that makes it war: he had brought his two wives,
⁨أسماء بنت عطارد⁩ and ⁨بحرية بنت هانئ⁩, out on two mounts behind him to watch him fight; when he was
killed the tribe that killed him pitched their commander's tent and, short of a peg for one rope,

> فبقي طنب (٧) منها لم يجدوا له وتدًا فشدوه برجل عبيد الله
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٥٤⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3600
> *English:* “…one rope of it was left for which they found no peg, so they tied it to ⁨عبيد الله⁩'s leg.”

The two women came and stood over him weeping, and his wife ⁨بحرية⁩ interceded with the commander,
who released the body to them, and they carried it away in their howdah
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۴⁩). `[SOURCED]`

**Who killed him is disputed.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ says the Iraqis differed over it, and reports that the
⁨ربيعة⁩ of ⁨الكوفة⁩ under ⁨زياد بن خصفة التيمي⁩ charged him as one man. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ names three separate
candidates:

> قَتَلَهُ مُحْرِزُ بْنُ الصَّحْصَحِ مِنْ تَيْمِ اللَّهِ بْنِ ثَعْلَبَةَ مِنْ أَهْلِ الْبَصْرَةِ، وَأَخَذَ سَيْفَهُ ذُو الْوِشَاحِ، وَكَانَ لِعُمَرَ، فَلَمَّا مَلَكَ مُعَاوِيَةُ الْعِرَاقَ أَخَذَهُ مِنْهُ، وَقِيلَ: بَلْ قَتَلَهُ هَانِئُ بْنُ خَطَّابٍ الْأَرْحَبِيُّ، (وَقِيلَ: قَتَلَهُ مَالِكُ بْنُ عَمْرٍو التِّنْعِيُّ الْحَضْرَمِي
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٠⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1349
> *English:* “⁨محرز بن الصحصح⁩ of ⁨تيم الله بن ثعلبة⁩, of the people of ⁨البصرة⁩, killed him, and ⁨ذو الوشاح⁩ took his sword — it had been ⁨عمر⁩'s; and when ⁨معاوية⁩ ruled ⁨العراق⁩ he took it from him. And it is said: rather ⁨هانئ بن خطاب الأرحبي⁩ killed him; and it is said: ⁨مالك بن عمرو التنعي الحضرمي⁩ killed him.”

`[SOURCED — disputed, three names]` ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۰⁩. The sword ⁨ذو الوشاح⁩ having been ⁨عمر ؓ⁩'s, and
⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ later taking it back, is a detail only ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ carries.

⁨ذو الكلاع الحميري⁩, one of ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s principal commanders, was killed in the same fighting — and
both books stress that he was killed **before** ⁨عمار ؓ⁩, because that ordering is what the next
section turns on (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۴⁩, ۴۵۷ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۰⁩, ۶۶۲).

## 17. مقتل عمار بن ياسر ؓ

This is the hinge of ⁨صفين⁩ and ⁨ابن كثير⁩ gives it a chapter heading of its own. He also states his
own conclusion in the first two lines of it, before any report, and it must be quoted as his
conclusion and attributed to him by name:

> وبان وظهر بذلك سرُّ ما أخبر به الرسول ﷺ من أنه تقتله الفئة الباغية، وبان بذلك أن عليًا محق وأن معاوية باغ، وما في ذلك من دلائل (٢) النبوة.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٥٥⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3601
> *English:* “And by that was made plain and manifest the secret of what the Messenger ﷺ had told — that the transgressing party would kill him; and by that it was made plain that ⁨علي⁩ was in the right and ⁨معاوية⁩ was transgressing; and what is in that of the proofs of prophethood.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۵⁩. **This sentence is ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s, it rests on a hadith in ⁨الصحيحين⁩, and
it is the settled Sunni position: ⁨علي ؓ⁩ was the nearer to the truth, and that does not make
⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ anything but a Companion who erred in ⁨اجتهاد⁩.** Attribute it to ⁨ابن كثير⁩ and stop
there — see § *Not for the hall* and the guardrail in `CLAUDE.md` §1.6.

**⁨عمار ؓ⁩ on that day.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ gives an eyewitness description of him — an old man, tall, dark,
holding a spear, his hand shaking — and then what he said:

> والذي نفسي بيده لقد قاتلت بهذه الراية مع رسول الله ﷺ مرات وهذه الرابعة، والذي نفسي بيده لو ضربونا حتى يبلغوا بنا سعفات (٤) هجر لعرفت أن مُصْلِحينا على الحق، وأنهم على الضلالة.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٥٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3602
> *English:* “By Him in whose hand is my soul, I have fought under this banner with the Messenger of God ﷺ three times, and this is the fourth. By Him in whose hand is my soul, if they struck us until they drove us to the palm-fronds of ⁨هجر⁩, I would know that those of us who seek to set things right are upon the truth, and that they are upon error.”

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩'s wording of the ⁨هجر⁩ line is the crisper one:

> وَايْمُ اللَّهِ لَوْ ضَرَبُونَا حَتَّى يَبْلُغُوا بِنَا سَعَفَاتِ هَجَرَ، لَعَلِمْتُ أَنَّا عَلَى الْحَقِّ، وَأَنَّهُمْ عَلَى الْبَاطِلِ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٠⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1349
> *English:* “And by God, if they struck us until they drove us to the palm-fronds of ⁨هجر⁩, I would know that we are upon the truth and that they are upon falsehood.”

**The milk.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ has it twice from ⁨أبو البختري⁩ through the Imam ⁨أحمد⁩: ⁨عمار ؓ⁩ called for a
drink of milk, and laughed, and said the Messenger of God ﷺ had told him it would be the last
thing he drank in this world (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۷⁩). ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ has the sharper version, where
⁨حذيفة بن اليمان ؓ⁩ had told a man years before both the killing **and** the milk, and the witness
watched both come true in an hour:

> عَلَيْكُمْ بِالْفِئَةِ الَّتِي فِيهَا ابْنُ سُمَيَّةَ، فَإِنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ - صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ - قَالَ: " تَقْتُلُهُ الْفِئَةُ الْبَاغِيَةُ النَّاكِبَةُ عَنِ الطَّرِيقِ، وَإِنَّ آخِرَ رِزْقِهِ ضَيَاحٌ مِنْ لَبَن
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٢⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1351
> *English:* “Keep to the party that has ⁨ابن سمية⁩ in it, for the Messenger of God ﷺ said: the transgressing party, swerving from the road, will kill him; and his last provision will be milk thinned with water.”

And the witness's own comment: **⁨فَمَا أَخْطَأَ حُذَيْفَةُ مِقْيَاسَ شَعْرَةٍ⁩** — ⁨حذيفة⁩ was not out by
the measure of a hair (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۲⁩). `[SOURCED]`

**⁨عمار ؓ⁩ and ⁨هاشم ؓ⁩.** The last thing he is reported to have done was to go and get ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s
one-eyed standard-bearer moving:

> يا هاشم تقدم (٤)! الجنة تحت ظلال السيوف، والموت في أطراف الأسنة، وفد فتحت أبواب الجنة (٥) وتزينت الحور العين.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٥٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3605
> *English:* “O ⁨هاشم⁩, go forward! Paradise is under the shade of the swords, and death is at the points of the spears; the gates of Paradise have been opened and the ⁨حور العين⁩ have adorned themselves.”

And then the two lines that everyone in both camps knew by the next morning:

> اليوم ألْقَى الأحبَّة … مُحمَّدًا وحِزْبه
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٥٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3605
> *English:* “Today I meet the loved ones — ⁨محمد⁩ and his company.”

⁨ابن كثير⁩'s source for this — ⁨أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي⁩, through ⁨ابن جرير⁩ — ends it in one line:
**⁨ثم حملا هو وهاشم فقتلا رحمهما الله تعالى⁩** (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۹⁩). `[SOURCED]`

**Who killed him.** Both books agree:

> فأما أبو الغادية فطعنه، وأما ابن حوى فاحتز رأسه.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٥٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3603
> *English:* “As for ⁨أبو الغادية⁩, he speared him; and as for ⁨ابن حوى⁩, he cut off his head.”

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ adds the doubt — **⁨وقيل قتله غيره⁩**, and it is said someone else killed him
(⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۲⁩) — and then the detail that settled the claim: men came to ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ one after
another each saying *I killed ⁨عمار⁩*, and ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩ tested each by asking what he had heard him say,
until one repeated **⁨اليوم ألقى الأحبة محمدًا وحزبه⁩**. ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩'s reply to that man is in both books:

> رُوَيْدًا، وَاللَّهِ مَا ظَفِرَتْ يَدَاكَ، وَلَقَدْ أَسْخَطْتَ رَبَّكَ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٢⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1351
> *English:* “Softly. By God, your two hands have won nothing, and you have angered your Lord.”

**⁨ذو الكلاع⁩ and the hadith.** Both books preserve the same thing, and it is the most humanly
exact detail in the chapter. ⁨ذو الكلاع⁩ had heard ⁨عمرو بن العاص ؓ⁩ himself narrate the hadith, and
kept asking him about it; ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩ kept answering that ⁨عمار ؓ⁩ would come over to their side. Then
⁨ذو الكلاع⁩ was killed, and ⁨عمار ؓ⁩ after him.

> مَا أَدْرِي بِقَتْلِ أَيِّهِمَا أَنَا أَشَدُّ فَرَحًا، بِقَتْلِ عَمَّارٍ أَوْ بِقَتْلِ ذِي الْكَلَاعِ، وَاللَّهِ لَوْ بَقِيَ ذُو الْكَلَاعِ بَعْدَ قَتْلِ عَمَّارٍ (لَمَالَ بِعَامَّةِ) أَهْلِ الشَّامِ إِلَى عَلِيٍّ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٢⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1351
> *English:* “I do not know at which of the two killings I am the more glad — the killing of ⁨عمار⁩ or the killing of ⁨ذي الكلاع⁩. By God, had ⁨ذو الكلاع⁩ lived after ⁨عمار⁩ was killed, he would have swung the general body of the people of ⁨الشام⁩ over to ⁨علي⁩.”

⁨ابن كثير⁩ has it in the same words and adds **⁨ولأفسد علينا جندنا⁩**, and he would have ruined our
army for us (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۷⁩). `[SOURCED — both books]`

**The construction ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ put on it, and ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s judgement of that construction.** When
⁨عبد الله بن عمرو ؓ⁩ pressed his father with the hadith in ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s hearing, the answer was:

> أو نحن قتلنا عمارًا؟ إنما قتل عمارًا من جاء به؟
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٠⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3606
> *English:* “Was it we who killed ⁨عمار⁩? Rather ⁨عمار⁩ was killed by those who brought him out.”

⁨ابن كثير⁩ does not let it pass:

> وهذا التأويل الذي سلكه معاوية ﵁ بعيد
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٠⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3606
> *English:* “And this construction which ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ followed is far-fetched.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۰⁩.

**And what ⁨ابن كثير⁩ will NOT allow to be added to the hadith.** This is the second half of the
guardrail and it is as important as the first:

> وما زاده الروافض (٢) في هذا الحديث بعد قوله "الباغية": لا أنالها والله شفاعتي يوم القيامة، فهو كذب وبهت على رسول الله ﷺ فإنه قد ثبتت الأحاديث عنه صلوات الله عليه وسلامه بتسمية الفريقين مسلمين
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٢⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3608
> *English:* “And what the ⁨روافض⁩ added to this hadith after his words “the transgressing party” — *by God she shall not attain my intercession on the Day of Resurrection* — is a lie and a slander against the Messenger of God ﷺ, for the ahadith are firmly established from him, the blessings and peace of God be upon him, naming **both parties Muslims**.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۲⁩. **Those last four words — naming both parties Muslims — are the
sentence that keeps this evening inside the Sunni frame, and they are ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s own.**

⁨عبد الله بن عمرو ؓ⁩'s own position is recorded too, and it is a third way that neither camp liked:
the Messenger ﷺ had commanded him to obey his father while he lived, **⁨وأنا معكم ولست أقاتل⁩** — I
am with you and I do not fight (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۸⁩). `[SOURCED]`

**One report that disagrees with the whole sequence.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ relays, from ⁨الهيثم بن عدي⁩'s lost
book on ⁨الخوارج⁩, a version in which ⁨عمار ؓ⁩ was still alive when the ⁨مصاحف⁩ were raised, hated it,
said something about ⁨علي ؓ⁩ that ⁨ابن كثير⁩ declines to repeat, and then charged and was killed
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۵⁩). ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s own narrative has ⁨عمار ؓ⁩ killed a day earlier. **Two sequences, both
on the page; the note records the conflict and settles nothing.** `[SOURCED — disputed]`

## 18. ⁨هاشم بن عتبة المرقال ؓ⁩, and the other dead who are named

⁨هاشم بن عتبة بن أبي وقاص ؓ⁩, nephew of ⁨سعد ؓ⁩, one-eyed since ⁨اليرموك⁩, carried ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s banner. What
⁨عمار ؓ⁩ said to get him moving is in ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ in a blunter form than in ⁨ابن كثير⁩:

> وَجُبْنًا؟ لَا خَيْرَ فِي أَعْوَرَ لَا (يَغْشَى الْبَأْسَ) ، ارْكَبْ يَا هَاشِم
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦١⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1350
> *English:* “…and a coward too? There is no good in a one-eyed man who does not go into the thick of it. Mount, ⁨هاشم⁩.”

(The opening words — **⁨فقال⁩: ⁨يا هاشم أعورًا⁩** — stand at the foot of the previous page,
⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۰⁩; the rest, quoted above, is at the head of ⁨ص۶۶۱⁩.)


He mounted. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ then gives him the best scene any single man gets at ⁨صفين⁩: a young Syrian
of ⁨غسان⁩ kept charging out chanting that ⁨علي ؓ⁩ had killed ⁨ابن عفان⁩, and cursing. ⁨هاشم ؓ⁩ did not
fight him; he argued with him.

> يَا هَذَا، إِنَّ هَذَا الْكَلَامَ بَعْدَهُ الْخِصَامُ، وَإِنَّ هَذَا الْقِتَالَ بَعْدَهُ الْحِسَابُ، فَاتَّقِ اللَّهَ، فَإِنَّهُ سَائِلُكَ عَنْ هَذَا الْمَوْقِفِ، وَمَا أَرَدْتَ بِهِ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٥⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1354
> *English:* “O you — this speech has a reckoning after it, and this fighting has an accounting after it. So fear God, for He will ask you about this stand of yours and what you intended by it.”

The young man argued back: your man does not pray and you do not pray, and your man killed our
caliph. ⁨هاشم ؓ⁩ answered him point by point, and the young man asked whether there was a ⁨توبة⁩ for
him, and was told yes, and went back — and when the Syrians said *the Iraqi tricked you*, he
answered: **⁨كَلَّا، وَلَكِنْ نَصَحَ لِي⁩** — no; he gave me sincere counsel
(⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۵⁩). `[SOURCED — ⁨الکامل⁩ only]`

⁨هاشم ؓ⁩ was speared soon after by ⁨الحارث بن المنذر التنوخي⁩. ⁨علي ؓ⁩ sent to him to bring his banner
forward, and the answer is the last line he speaks in either book:

> انْظُرْ إِلَى بَطْنِي، فَإِذَا هُوَ [قَدْ] انْشَقَّ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٥⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1354
> *English:* “Look at my belly — and it had been split open.”

`[SOURCED — ⁨الکامل⁩ only]` ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۵⁩.

**Other dead our two books name.** ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ is the register here; ⁨ابن كثير⁩ names far fewer.

  `[SOURCED — ⁨الکامل⁩ only]` ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۵۱⁩–۶۵۲
- **⁨عقبة بن حديد النميري⁩**, who announced that he had sold this house for the one in front of it —

## 19. ليلة الهرير

After ⁨عمار ؓ⁩ fell, ⁨علي ؓ⁩ called on ⁨ربيعة⁩ and ⁨همدان⁩ — **⁨أنتم درعي ورمحي⁩**, you are my shield and my
spear — and some twelve thousand answered him, and he went in front of them on a mule
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۲⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۳⁩). The fighting did not stop at dark. ⁨ابن كثير⁩:

> ثم حانت صلاة المغرب فما صلَّى بالناس إلَّا إيماءً صلاتي العشاءَ واستمرّ القتال في هذه الليلة كلها وهي من أعظم الليالي شرًا بين المسلمين، وتسمى (هذه الليلة) ليلة الهرير
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٢⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3608
> *English:* “Then the time of the ⁨مغرب⁩ prayer came, and he prayed the two prayers with the people only by gesture — ⁨العشاء⁩ [as well] — and the fighting went on through that whole night, and it is among the worst of nights ever between Muslims, and it is called ⁨ليلة الهرير⁩.”

And what the fighting had become by then:

> أنهم اقتتلوا بالرماح حتى تقصفت (١)، وبالنبال حتى فنيت، وبالسيوف حتى تحطمت ثم صاروا إلى أن تقاتلوا بالأيدي والرمي بالحجارة والتراب في الوجوه، وتعاضُّوا بالأسنان
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٣⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3609
> *English:* “…that they fought with spears until they were shattered, and with arrows until they ran out, and with swords until they were broken to pieces, and then they came to fighting with their hands and throwing stones and dust in the faces, and they bit one another with their teeth.”

⁨ابن كثير⁩ adds the detail that fixes the horror better than any of that: **two men would fight
until both were badly wounded, then sit down and rest together, each cursing the other, then get
up and fight again** (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۳⁩). ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ names the night in one clause and describes
⁨علي ؓ⁩ moving all night between the two wings ordering each squadron forward past the one beside
it, until by morning **the whole battlefield was behind his back** (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۶⁩). `[SOURCED]`

⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ also keeps the piece of advice ⁨علي ؓ⁩ was acting on: ⁨عبد الله بن كعب المرادي⁩, found
dying on the ground, sent him a message by ⁨الأسود بن قيس المرادي⁩:

> قَاتِلْ عَلَى الْمَعْرَكَةِ حَتَّى تَجْعَلَهَا خَلْفَ ظَهْرِكَ، فَإِنَّهُ مَنْ أَصْبَحَ غَدًا وَالْمَعْرَكَةُ خَلْفَ ظَهْرِهِ كَانَ الْعَالِي.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1355
> *English:* “Fight over the battlefield until you put it behind your back; for whoever wakes tomorrow with the battlefield behind his back is the one on top.”

⁨علي ؓ⁩'s word over him when the message was delivered: **⁨جاهد عدونا في الحياة ونصح لنا في الوفاة⁩**
— he struggled against our enemy in life and gave us sincere counsel in death
(⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۶⁩). `[SOURCED — ⁨الکامل⁩ only]`

By the forenoon of Friday the Syrian ranks were coming apart. ⁨ابن كثير⁩, ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۳⁩:
**⁨فحمل بمن معه على أهل الشام وتبعه علي فتنقضت غالب صفوفهم وكادوا ينهزمون⁩** — ⁨الأشتر⁩ charged with
those who were with him, and ⁨علي ؓ⁩ followed, and most of their ranks came apart, and they were on
the point of rout. `[SOURCED]`

## 20. The ⁨مصاحف⁩ go up


> فعند ذلك رفع أهل الشام المصاحف فوق الرماح: وقالوا: هذا بيننا وبينكم قد فني الناس فمن للثغور؟ ومن لجهاد المشركين والكفار (٦).
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٣⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3609
> *English:* “At that the people of ⁨الشام⁩ raised the ⁨مصاحف⁩ above the spears and said: this is between us and you; the people are finished — who is left for the frontiers? and who for the ⁨جهاد⁩ against the ⁨مشركون⁩ and the ⁨كفار⁩?”

**Who proposed it.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ attributes it, from ⁨ابن جرير⁩ and other historians, to
⁨عمرو بن العاص ؓ⁩, and gives his reasoning in his own words, which are perfectly explicit about the
purpose:

> إني قد رأيت أمرًا لا يزيدنا هذه الساعة إلا اجتماعًا ولا يزيدهم (١٠) إلا فرقة، أرى أن نرفع المصاحف وندعوهم إليها
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٣⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3609
> *English:* “I have seen a course that will add to us, this hour, nothing but unity, and add to them nothing but division: I hold that we should raise the ⁨مصاحف⁩ and call them to them.”

And he spells out what would happen either way: if they all accept, the fighting stops this hour;
and if they split over it — some saying *we answer them* and some *we do not* — **⁨فشلوا وذهب⁩
⁨ريحهم⁩**, they fail and their wind goes (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۳⁩). ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ gives the identical
calculation in three sentences (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۷⁩). `[SOURCED — both books agree on ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩]`

**A second account of the mechanics** — this one through ⁨أبو وائل⁩, in the Imam ⁨أحمد⁩'s ⁨مسند⁩, which
⁨ابن كثير⁩ calls **⁨حديث صحيح⁩** in the printed edition's footnote — has a single ⁨مصحف⁩ sent across by
a man who called out ⁨آل عمران ۲۳⁩, and ⁨علي ؓ⁩ answering:

> فقال علي: نعم! أنا أولى بذلك بيننا وبينكم كتاب الله
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٤⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3610
> *English:* “⁨علي⁩ said: yes — I have the better right to that; between us and you is the Book of God.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۴⁩.

**⁨علي ؓ⁩'s reading of it**, which he said out loud to his own army at once:

> ويحكم والله إنهم ما رفعوها أنهم يقرؤونها ولا يعملون بما فيها وما رفعوها إلا خديعة ودهاءً ومكيدة.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٤⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3610
> *English:* “Woe to you — by God, they did not raise them because they recite them and act by what is in them; they raised them for nothing but deception and cunning and stratagem.”

The army refused him. Their answer is the sentence that makes the whole thing tragic rather than
merely clever: **⁨ما يسعنا أن نُدعى إلى كتاب الله فنأبى أن نقبله⁩** — it is not open to us to be
called to the Book of God and refuse to accept it (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۴⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۸⁩).
`[SOURCED — both books, same words]`

**Then the threat from his own side.** ⁨مسعر بن فدكي التميمي⁩ and ⁨زيد بن حصين الطائي⁩, with a body of
the ⁨قراء⁩ — whom both books identify as the men **who afterwards became ⁨الخوارج⁩** — said to him:

> يا علي أجب إلى كتاب الله إذ دعيتَ إليه وإلا دفعناك برمتك إلى القوم أو نفعل بك ما فعلنا بابن عفان، إنه غلبنا أن يعمل بكتاب الله فقتلناه، والله لتفعلنها أو لنفعلنها بك.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٤⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3610
> *English:* “O ⁨علي⁩, answer the Book of God when you are called to it, or we will deliver you up bound to the people, or do to you what we did to ⁨ابن عفان⁩. He refused to let us act by the Book of God, so we killed him. By God we will do it, or you will do it.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۴⁩; ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ has it in nearly identical words, ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۸⁩. **This
is the sentence that connects ⁨صفين⁩ to ⁨النہروان⁩ and it should be said exactly once, plainly, and
without comment.**

**⁨الأشتر⁩ recalled.** ⁨علي ؓ⁩ was forced to send for him. The first message came back refused — *this
is not an hour in which you should move me from my position; I have hoped God would open for me*
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۵⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۸⁩). The second came with *the fitna has fallen*, and he came. His
speech to the men who had stopped him is the angriest thing in either book:

> خُدِعْتُمْ فَانْخَدَعْتُمْ، وَدُعِيتُمْ إِلَى وَضْعِ الْحَرْبِ فَأَجَبْتُم
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1358
> *English:* “You were deceived and you let yourselves be deceived; you were called to lay down the war and you answered.”

⁨ابن كثير⁩ has it in the same words and keeps the rest — *we used to think your prayer was
renunciation of the world and longing to meet God; I see your aim was nothing but the world* —
and the end of the quarrel, in which they beat his mount's face with their whips and he struck at
theirs, until ⁨علي ؓ⁩ shouted at both and they stopped (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۶⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۹⁩).
`[SOURCED]`

**And the one argument nobody answered.** ⁨الأشتر⁩ put it to them as a dilemma and it stands
unanswered on the page: if the first fighting was right, go on with it; and if it was wrong, then
bear witness that your own dead are in the Fire (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۶⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۹⁩). Their reply
was: **⁨قاتلناهم لله، وندع قتالهم لله⁩** — we fought them for God and we leave off fighting them for
God (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۹⁩). `[SOURCED]`

**⁨سهل بن حنيف ؓ⁩ — the Companion answer.** Twice in this chapter ⁨ابن كثير⁩ produces the same man
with the same argument, and it is in ⁨البخاري⁩ and ⁨مسلم⁩:

> يا أيها الناس! اتهموا الرأي على الدين، فلقد رأيتني يوم أبي جندل ولو أقدر لرددتُ على رسول الله ﷺ أمره
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٥٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3603
> *English:* “O people, distrust your own opinion where the ⁨دين⁩ is concerned. I saw myself on the day of ⁨أبو جندل⁩, and had I been able I would have turned back the Messenger of God ﷺ from his decision.”

`[SOURCED — ⁨الصحيحين⁩]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۷⁩, and again at ۴۶۴ in the ⁨أبو وائل⁩ version, where he is
answering the ⁨قراء⁩ who wanted to walk up the hill with their swords. **This is the licensed
Companion line for the whole evening: a ⁨بدري⁩, standing in the middle of ⁨صفين⁩, saying that a
man's own judgement is the thing to be suspected first.**

## 21. The numbers — and why no single number may go on a slide

⁨ابن كثير⁩ gathers the figures in one paragraph and they do not agree with each other. First, the
figure he relays from several, including ⁨محمد بن سيرين⁩ and ⁨سيف⁩:

> بل صبروا حتى قتل من الفريقين فيما ذكره غير واحد سبعون ألفًا: خمسة وأربعون ألفًا من أهل الشام، وخمسة وعشرون ألفًا من أهل العراق.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3612
> *English:* “…rather they held on until there were killed of the two sides, as more than one has mentioned, seventy thousand: forty-five thousand of the people of ⁨الشام⁩, and twenty-five thousand of the people of ⁨العراق⁩.”

Immediately after, he reports **twenty-five ⁨بدريون⁩** among the Iraqi dead on ⁨أبو الحسن بن البراء⁩'s
authority — which cannot be reconciled with the tradition on ⁨ص۴۳۷⁩ that only ⁨خزيمة بن ثابت ؓ⁩ of
⁨بدر⁩ was there at all. Both are in the same chapter.

Then a wholly different set of figures, which ⁨ابن كثير⁩ gives from ⁨البيهقي⁩ through
⁨صفوان بن عمرو⁩ on the same page:

> كان أهل الشام ستين ألفًا فقتل منهم عثرون ألفًا، وكان أهل العراق مئة وعشرين ألفًا فقتل منهم أربعون ألفًا.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3612
> *English:* “The people of ⁨الشام⁩ were sixty thousand and twenty thousand of them were killed; and the people of ⁨العراق⁩ were one hundred and twenty thousand and forty thousand of them were killed.”

So on the same page: 45,000 Syrian dead, or 20,000; 25,000 Iraqi dead, or 40,000.
**`[SOURCED — irreconcilably disputed]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۶⁩. Any number on a slide must be labelled
`[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]` and shown as a range with the disagreement named, or left off.**

**How long the armies lay there is disputed in the same paragraph.** ⁨سيف⁩: seven months, or nine.
⁨أبو الحسن بن البراء⁩: a hundred and ten days. And then ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s own arithmetic, which he offers
tentatively and closes with *God knows best*:

> قلت: ومقتضى كلام أبي مخنف أنه كان من مستهلّ ذي الحجة في يوم الجمعة لثلاث عشرة خلت من صفر وذلك سبعة وسبعون يومًا، فالله سبحانه أعلم.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3612
> *English:* “I say: what ⁨أبو مخنف⁩'s account requires is that it was from the first of ⁨ذو الحجة⁩ to Friday, thirteen nights having passed of ⁨صفر⁩ — and that is seventy-seven days. And God, glory be to Him, knows best.”

**Ninety engagements** is the figure ⁨ابن كثير⁩ gives for the number of times the two sides closed
(**⁨تسعون زحفًا⁩**), and eighty-five in the three months of shuttling before that
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۵⁩, ۴۶۶). And ⁨الزهري⁩'s report of the burials:

> وقال الزُّهري: بلغني أنه كان يدفن في القبر الواحد خمسون نفسًا.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٦⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3612
> *English:* “⁨الزهري⁩ said: it reached me that fifty persons were buried in a single grave.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۶⁩.

## 22. What the Prophet ﷺ is reported to have said about this, in the books' own arrangement

⁨ابن كثير⁩ closes the battle not with a verdict but with a set of ahadith, and this is how the
chapter is meant to be read. The first is ⁨البيهقي⁩'s application of a hadith of the ⁨الصحيحين⁩ to
this battle specifically:

> لا تقوم الساعة حتى تقتتل فئتان عظيمتان يقتل بينهما مقتلة عظيمة ودعواهما واحدة
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3613
> *English:* “The Hour will not come until two great parties fight one another, and there is a great slaughter between them, and their claim is one.”

`[SOURCED — ⁨البخاري ومسلم⁩, cited at]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۷⁩. **⁨ودعواهما واحدة⁩ — their claim is one.**
That clause is the whole guardrail in four words and it is in the ⁨صحيح⁩.

Then the hadith that fixes the date. ⁨ابن مسعود ؓ⁩, in the Imam ⁨أحمد⁩'s ⁨مسند⁩:

> إن رحى الإسلام ستزول لخمس وثلاثين أو ست وثلاثين، فإن يهلكوا فسبيل (٦) من هلك، وإن يقم لهم دينهم يقم لهم سبعين عامًا
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٧⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3613
> *English:* “The millstone of Islam will shift at thirty-five or thirty-six; and if they perish, then the way of those who perished; and if their ⁨دين⁩ stands for them, it stands for them seventy years.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۷⁩. And a second wording of it on the same page: **if they reconcile
among themselves they will eat of the world for seventy years in ease; and if they fight one
another they will take the road of those who were before them.**

And last, the one that explains why none of this could be prayed away:

> سألت ربي أن لا يهلك أمتي بسنة عامة فأعطانيها، وسألته أن لا يسلط عليهم عدوًا من غيرهم (٢) فيستبيح بيضتهم فأعطانيها، وسألته أن لا يسلط بعضهم على بعض فمنعنيها
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3615
> *English:* “I asked my Lord that He not destroy my ummah by a general famine, and He granted it to me; and I asked Him that He not set an enemy from outside them over them to violate them wholesale, and He granted it to me; and I asked Him that He not set them one upon another, and He withheld that from me.”

`[SOURCED — ⁨مسلم⁩, cited at]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۹⁩. **This is the licensed closing sentence for this
strand.** It says what happened without blaming anyone in the room's hearing, and the ﷺ says it
himself.

⁨ابن كثير⁩ also keeps two reports that a Sunni platform should handle as *reports*, not as doctrine:
a hadith through ⁨ابن ديزيل⁩ in which the Prophet ﷺ is asked about two caliphs in Islam and tells
⁨عثمان ؓ⁩ **⁨بك ينشبون الحرب⁩** — through you they will fall to war (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۸⁩); and ⁨عمر ؓ⁩'s
question to ⁨ابن عباس ؓ⁩ about how a single ummah could divide, answered that a people would come
who did not understand the Qur'an as they understood it, and would differ over it, and having
differed would fight (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۸⁩). `[SOURCED]`

## 23. The document, the name that was erased, and the man who would not sign

The two sides then negotiated the terms. ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s answer to ⁨الأشعث بن قيس ؓ⁩, when he came to ask
what exactly the raised ⁨مصاحف⁩ were meant to mean, is the clearest statement of the arbitration in
either book:

> لِنَرْجِعَ نَحْنُ وَأَنْتُمْ إِلَى مَا أَمَرَ اللَّهُ بِهِ فِي كِتَابِهِ، تَبْعَثُونَ رَجُلًا تَرْضَوْنَ بِهِ، وَنَبْعَثُ نَحْنُ رَجُلًا نَرْضَى بِهِ، نَأْخُذُ عَلَيْهِمَا أَنْ يَعْمَلَا بِمَا فِي كِتَابِ اللَّهِ لَا يَعْدُوَانِهِ، ثُمَّ نَتَّبِعُ مَا اتَّفَقْنَا عَلَيْهِ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1358
> *English:* “That we and you should return to what God commanded in His Book: you send a man you are content with, and we send a man we are content with, and we bind the two of them to act by what is in the Book of God and not go beyond it; then we follow what we have agreed on.”

**Who would represent ⁨العراق⁩.** ⁨الشام⁩ named ⁨عمرو بن العاص ؓ⁩ at once. ⁨علي ؓ⁩ wanted ⁨ابن عباس ؓ⁩ and
was refused; he proposed ⁨الأشتر⁩ and was refused; the same body of ⁨قراء⁩ insisted on
⁨أبو موسى الأشعري ؓ⁩. ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s own comment on the first refusal is an aside of one clause and it
tells you exactly where he stands:

> وأراد عليّ أن يوكل عبد الله بن عباس - وليته فعل - ولكنه منعه القُرّاء [الخوارج] ممن ذكرنا وقالوا: لا نرضى إلا بأبي موسى الأشعري.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3615
> *English:* “And ⁨علي⁩ wanted to appoint ⁨عبد الله بن عباس⁩ — and would that he had done it — but the ⁨قراء⁩ [⁨الخوارج⁩] whom we mentioned prevented him, and said: we are content with none but ⁨أبو موسى الأشعري⁩.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۹⁩. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ gives ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s objection to the man in his own words — he
is not to be trusted; he parted from me and discouraged the people from me, then fled from me —
and the ⁨قراء⁩'s reply, which is the sentence that shows what they actually wanted:

> لَا نُرِيدُ إِلَّا رَجُلًا هُوَ مِنْكَ وَمِنْ مُعَاوِيَةَ سَوَاءٌ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1358
> *English:* “We want none but a man who is the same distance from you and from ⁨معاوية⁩.”

`[SOURCED — ⁨الکامل⁩ only]` ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۹⁩. ⁨علي ؓ⁩'s last word on it, in ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩, is
**⁨فاصنعوا ما أردتم⁩** — do what you wish.

**⁨أبو موسى ؓ⁩'s own reaction** when the news reached him where he had withdrawn from the fighting
is two sentences and needs no commentary:

> فلما قيل له إن الناس قد اصطلحوا قال: الحمد لله، قيل له: وقد جُعلت حكمًا، فقال: إنا لله وإنا إليه راجعون
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج٧ ص٤٦٩⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3615
> *English:* “When it was said to him, the people have made peace, he said: praise be to God. It was said to him, and you have been made an arbiter: he said, ⁨إنا لله وإنا إليه راجعون⁩.”

`[SOURCED]` ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۹⁩. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ has it identically (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۷۰⁩).

**The name that was erased.** The scribe began *this is what ⁨أمير المؤمنين⁩ has agreed to*, and
⁨عمرو ؓ⁩ objected: he is your commander; ours he is not. ⁨الأحنف بن قيس⁩ warned against giving it up:

> لَا تَمْحُ اسْمَ إِمَارَةِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ فَإِنِّي أَخَافُ إِنْ مَحَوْتَهَا أَنْ لَا تَرْجِعَ إِلَيْكَ أَبَدًا، لَا تَمْحُهَا وَإِنْ قَتَلَ النَّاسُ بَعْضُهُمْ بَعْضًا.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٧٠⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1359
> *English:* “Do not erase the title of the commandership of the believers, for I fear that if you erase it, it will never come back to you. Do not erase it, even if the people kill one another.”

⁨علي ؓ⁩ held out most of a day. ⁨الأشعث بن قيس ؓ⁩ said erase it, and it was erased. And then ⁨علي ؓ⁩
said the thing that makes this the most quotable moment of the whole strand, because it is the
one moment where the man has a precedent from the Prophet ﷺ for his own humiliation:

> فَقَالَ عَلِيٌّ: اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ! سُنَّةٌ بِسُنَّةٍ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٧٠⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1359
> *English:* “⁨علي⁩ said: God is greatest! A precedent matching a precedent.”

He then told them that he had been the Prophet ﷺ's own scribe at ⁨الحديبية⁩, had written
*⁨محمد رسول الله⁩*, had been told *you are not the Messenger of God, write your name and your
father's name*, had refused to erase it himself, and the Prophet ﷺ had erased it with his own
hand and said to him:

> إِنَّكَ سَتُدْعَى إِلَى مِثْلِهَا فَتُجِيبُ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٧٠⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1359
> *English:* “You will be called to the like of it, and you will answer.”

`[SOURCED — ⁨الکامل⁩ only for this scene]` ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۷۰⁩. ⁨ابن كثير⁩ does not carry the erasure
scene in these pages; he refers the text of the document to ⁨الطبری⁩ in a footnote and moves on
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۹⁩). **Say whose it is.**

**The document itself** (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۷۱⁩) binds both to the Book of God from its opening to its
close, then to **⁨السنة العادلة الجامعة غير المفرقة⁩** for whatever the two do not find in the Book;
gives the two arbiters safe conduct; and sets the deadline: **⁨وأجل القضاء إلى رمضان⁩** — the term of
the judgement is until ⁨رمضان⁩, with leave to postpone. Witnesses are named on both sides —
⁨الأشعث بن قيس ؓ⁩, ⁨سعيد بن قيس⁩, ⁨حجر بن عدي الكندي⁩ and others for ⁨العراق⁩; ⁨أبو الأعور السلمي⁩,
⁨حبيب بن مسلمة⁩, ⁨عبد الرحمن بن خالد المخزومي⁩, ⁨عتبة بن أبي سفيان⁩ and others for ⁨الشام⁩. `[SOURCED]`

**And one man refused to be written into it:**

> لَا صَحِبَتْنِي يَمِينِي، وَلَا نَفَعَتْنِي بَعْدَهَا شِمَالِي، إِنْ خُطَّ لِي فِي هَذِهِ الصَّحِيفَة
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٧١⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1360
> *English:* “May my right hand never keep me company, and may my left never profit me after it, if a name of mine is written in this ⁨صحيفة⁩.”

`[SOURCED — ⁨الکامل⁩ only]` ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۷۱⁩. **That is where this note stops.** What the two
arbiters did afterwards belongs to the next strand.

## 24. Two small things both armies did that the room should hear

**They released each other's prisoners.** ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ records ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ taking prisoners and
⁨عمرو ؓ⁩ advising him to kill them, and one prisoner — ⁨عمرو بن أوس الأودي⁩ — talking his way out by
pointing out that ⁨أم حبيبة ؓ⁩, mother of the believers, was ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩'s sister and therefore his own
aunt; and then ⁨علي ؓ⁩ releasing a large number of prisoners, at which ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ said to ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩:

> يَا عَمْرُو لَوْ أَطَعْنَاكَ فِي هَؤُلَاءِ الْأَسَارَى لَوَقَعْنَا فِي قَبِيحٍ مِنَ الْأَمْرِ، وَخَلَّى سَبِيلَ مَنْ عِنْدَهُ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج٢ ص٦٦٤⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1353
> *English:* “O ⁨عمرو⁩, had we obeyed you concerning these prisoners we would have fallen into an ugly business — and he set free those he had.”

`[SOURCED — ⁨الکامل⁩ only]` ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۴⁩.

**And when they were not fighting they talked.** ⁨أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي⁩, explaining how he came to
overhear ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ and ⁨عمرو ؓ⁩ on the night ⁨عمار ؓ⁩ was killed, says it in one clause that both books
keep: **⁨وكنا إذا توادعنا من القتال تحدثوا إلينا وتحدثنا إليهم⁩** — when we left off fighting they
would talk to us and we would talk to them (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۹⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۳⁩). `[SOURCED]`

## What could NOT be established

- **The numbers of the dead.** Not by a long way. ⁨البدایہ⁩ gives two irreconcilable sets on one
  page (⁨ص۴۶۶⁩) and neither book prefers one. Nor can the total strength of either army be fixed:
  ⁨ابن كثير⁩ reports 150,000 Iraqis and about as many Syrians from one chain, and 100,000-odd
  against 130,000 from another, both on ⁨ص۴۴۸⁩.
- **How long the two armies were at ⁨صفين⁩.** Seven months, nine months, 110 days, or ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s
  own 77 days — ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۶⁩, ending in **⁨فالله سبحانه أعلم⁩**.
- **How many Companions were present.** The chapter contains "fewer than thirty", "only
  ⁨خزيمة بن ثابت ؓ⁩ of ⁨بدر⁩", "eighty ⁨بدريون⁩ and a hundred and fifty of the people of the tree", and
  "twenty-five ⁨بدريون⁩ among the dead". These cannot all be true and neither book reconciles them.
- **Who killed ⁨عبيد الله بن عمر⁩.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ says the Iraqis themselves differed; ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ names
  three men.
- **Whether ⁨الوليد بن عقبة⁩ and ⁨عبد الله بن سعد بن أبي سرح⁩ were at ⁨صفين⁩ at all** — and therefore
  whether the harshest lines in the water council were spoken. ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ records the denial
  (الکامل ج۲ ص۶۳۵).
- **Whether ⁨عمار ؓ⁩ was killed before or after the ⁨مصاحف⁩ were raised.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s own narrative
  says before, by a day; the report he relays from ⁨الهيثم بن عدي⁩ says after (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۶۵⁩).
- **Whether the verses of ⁨ابن الإطنابة⁩ steadied ⁨الأشتر⁩ or ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩.** ⁨البدایہ⁩ gives both, the
  second flagged by ⁨ابن كثير⁩ with **⁨والعجب⁩** (⁨ج۷ ص۴۵۳⁩–۴۵۴); ⁨الکامل⁩ gives only ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩.
- **Whether ⁨علي ؓ⁩ killed five hundred men at ⁨صفين⁩ with his own hand.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ reports the claim
  from ⁨ابن ديزيل⁩ and then says of the chain carrying it: **⁨وهذا إسناد ضعيف وحديث منكر⁩** — this is
  a weak chain and a rejected hadith (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۱⁩). **Do not use the figure.**
- **The report that blood rained at ⁨صفين⁩** (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۱⁩–۴۵۲, through ⁨ابن لهيعة⁩). Recorded
  without endorsement; unusable.
- **The wording of the long speeches.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ says so himself about the biggest of them:
  **وفي صحة ذلك عنهم وعنه نظر** (ج۷ ص۴۴۴).
- **What ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ himself decided about the water.** He gave no answer on the page; both books say
  only that he said his opinion would come later, and then the cavalry went to the ford.
- **Why ⁨جرير ؓ⁩'s embassy failed.** ⁨البدایہ⁩: ⁨الشام⁩'s blanket condition. ⁨الکامل⁩: a personal grudge
  worked through ⁨شرحبيل بن السمط⁩. Neither book chooses.
- **A map of the ground.** Neither book gives a description of the site beyond *by the ⁨الفرات⁩,
  east of the land of ⁨الشام⁩*, *level and wide*, and *one watering place*. Any detailed battle map
  would be invention. `[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]` only, and labelled as such.

## Not for the hall

Everything in this section is genuinely on the pages read, and none of it may be narrated from
the platform. Each line says why.

1. **The sentence attributed to ⁨عمرو بن العاص ؓ⁩ ending «⁨ولكنا أردنا هذه الدنيا⁩»** (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۲۸⁩).
   A Companion made to confess he fought for the world. ⁨ابن كثير⁩ does not carry it.
2. **⁨علي ؓ⁩'s reported words about ⁨معاوية ؓ⁩ and ⁨أبو سفيان ؓ⁩ — «⁨طليق ابن طليق⁩»، «⁨حزب من الأحزاب⁩»,
   and that the two entered Islam unwillingly** (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۳⁩). ⁨ابن كثير⁩ reports that such
   material exists and then rejects it: **⁨وفي صحة ذلك عنهم وعنه نظر⁩** (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۴⁩). This is
   the ⁨مشاجرات⁩ at its rawest and it is exactly what the guardrail exists to keep off the platform.
3. **⁨عمار ؓ⁩'s long speech calling the other side men with no ⁨سابقة⁩ who deceived their followers to
   become tyrant kings** (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۵۵⁩ · ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۰⁩). It is a Companion's words about
   Companions; quoting it in a room is taking a side in the ⁨مشاجرات⁩.
4. **⁨عمار ؓ⁩'s words to ⁨عمرو بن العاص ؓ⁩ — «⁨بعت دينك بمصر، تبًا لك⁩»** (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۶۱⁩). Same reason.
5. **⁨يزيد بن قيس الأرحبي⁩'s speech naming ⁨سعيد⁩ and ⁨الوليد⁩ and ⁨ابن عامر⁩ as what ⁨الشام⁩'s victory
   would impose** (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۶۴۸⁩). Named Companions and governors attacked by name.
6. **The charge in ⁨الوليد بن عقبة⁩'s mouth in the water council, and the taunt «⁨موتوا عطشًا كما⁩
   ⁨منعتم عثمان الماء⁩»** (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۴۴۱⁩). Narratable only as *what the Syrians shouted*, never as
   a Companion's considered position — and never without §7's note that ⁨ابن الأثیر⁩ records the

### E-SF51 · Seventy thousand, and nobody agrees
