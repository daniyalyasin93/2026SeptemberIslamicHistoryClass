# The two empires at the hinge — what our four books say of Persia and Rome, 12–13 AH

**The question.** Evening 6/7 closes the Ridda and turns the map outward (`DECISIONS.md` #70). What can be said of
the two empires *at that moment* — from the safe-list books only, with a page under every sentence — so that
Parts IX–X of `S06_hadramawt_bahrayn/` can be built without a line of general-knowledge history on a slide?

**How it was done.** No page was fetched. Every page below was already in `sources/shamela/`, read for the
Iraq/Syria note (`iraq-syria-and-yarmuk-12-15ah.md`) or for evening 3. The cache was searched for the names of
the Persian house and for Heraclius; the pages that answered were read whole.

**The short answer.** The books do not describe the two empires. They tell four things in passing, and those four
are enough: **who Khālid's ؓ first letter was sent on to; what the Persian house had done to itself; that the
Romans were afraid; and what their emperor told them.** That is Part IX and the close of Part X. Dynastic dates,
the long Roman–Persian war, and the size of either state are **not on these pages** and stay off the slides.

---

## 1. Persia

### 1.1 The room already knows how the Persian king died

Evening 3 told `AAA/E-AW02`: the Prophet ﷺ in Medina, the night Kisrā was killed by his own son
(⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۳⁩, index 3159). Part IX opens on that as a callback. Nothing new is needed for it.

### 1.2 Who the letter was sent on to

`F1`

> فكتب إليه خالدٌ فبعث هرمزُ بكتابِ خالدٍ إلى شيرَى بن كسرى، وأردشير بن شيرَى
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3210
> *English:* “So Khālid wrote to him; and Hurmuz sent Khālid's letter on to Shīrā son of Kisrā, and Ardashīr son of Shīrā.”

`F2`

> وكان سببها أنّ هرمز (٣) كان قد كتب إلى أردشير وشيرَى بقدومِ خالدٍ نحوهُ من اليمامة، فبعث إليه كسرى بمدد
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3212
> *English:* “Its cause was that Hurmuz had written to Ardashīr and Shīrā of Khālid's coming toward him from al-Yamāma; and Kisrā sent him reinforcements.”

So Ibn Kathīr's page, following Sayf's telling, has two names at the head of Persia when Khālid ؓ arrives:
**Shīrā son of Kisrā, and Ardashīr son of Shīrā** — the son who killed his father, and that son's son. And on the
next page the same telling says, in one breath, "Ardashīr and Shīrā" and "Kisrā sent him reinforcements": *Kisrā*
there is the title, not the dead king.

### 1.3 What the house had done to itself

`Y1`

> لَمْ يَبْقَ إِلَّا غُلَامٌ يُدْعَى يَزْدَجِرْدَ مِنْ وَلَدِ شَهْرَيَارَ بْنِ كِسْرَى، وَأُمُّهُ مِنْ أَهْلِ بَادُورَيَا. فَأَرْسَلُوا إِلَيْهَا وَطَلَبُوهُ مِنْهَا، وَكَانَتْ قَدْ أَنْزَلَتْهُ أَيَّامَ شِيرَى حِينَ جَمَعَهُنَّ فَقَتَلَ الذُّكُورَ، وَأَرْسَلَتْهُ إِلَى أَخْوَالِهِ، فَلَمَّا سَأَلُوهَا عَنْهُ دَلَّتْهُمْ عَلَيْهِ، فَجَاءُوا بِهِ فَمَلَّكُوهُ وَهُوَ ابْنُ إِحْدَى وَعِشْرِينَ سَنَة
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۸۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/975
> *English:* “None is left but a boy called Yazdagird, of the line of Shahriyār son of Kisrā; his mother is of the people of Bādūrayā.” So they sent to her and asked her for him. She had got him away in the days of Shīrā — when he gathered them and killed the males — and had sent him to his mother's brothers. When they asked her about him she led them to him; and they brought him and made him king, at twenty-one.

This is Ibn al-Athīr under the year 13, telling how the great men of Persia — he names Rustam and al-Fayrūzān —
were forced to stop quarrelling and find a king. It is told as a search among the women of Kisrā's house, because
**in Shīrā's days the males of the house had been gathered and killed**. One boy had been got out.

### 1.4 How a Muslim commander read it, at the time

`K1`

> فالحمد لله الذي فض خدمتكم (٤) وسلبَ مُلْككم ووهنَ كيدكَم
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3210
> *English:* “Praise be to Allah, who has broken up your company, taken away your kingship and weakened your scheming.”

Khālid's ؓ letter to the marzubāns, already on `ISA/E-C2`. It is the only sentence on these pages in which
someone *of that year* says what Persia's condition was.

## 2. Rome

### 2.1 They were afraid, and they wrote to their emperor

`R1`

> ولما توجهت هذه الجيوش نحو الشام أفزع ذلك الروم وخافوا خوفًا شديدًا، وكتبوا إلى هرقل
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3231
> *English:* “When these armies set out toward Syria it alarmed the Romans, and they feared greatly; and they wrote to Heraclius.”

### 2.2 Where he was — the books give it two ways

`R2`

> فيقال: إنه كان يومئذ بحمص، ويقال: [بل] كان حج عامه ذلك إلى بيت المقدس
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3232
> *English:* “It is said that he was at Ḥimṣ that day; and it is said: rather, he had made pilgrimage that year to Bayt al-Maqdis.”

Ibn al-Athīr says only: he was in Jerusalem (`R4`).

### 2.3 What he said

`R3`

> ويْحكم إن (هؤلاء) أهلُ دينٍ جديد، وإنهم لا قبلَ لأحدٍ بهم، فأطيعوني وصالحوهم بما تصالحونهم على نصفِ خراج الشام ويبقى لكم جبالُ الروم، وإن أنتم أبيتم ذلك أخذوا منكم الشام وضيقوا عليكم جبال الروم
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3232
> *English:* “Woe to you — these are the people of a new religion, and no one has the strength to face them. So obey me, and make peace with them — on half the revenue of Syria, and the mountains of the Romans remain yours. If you refuse that, they will take Syria from you, and press you hard in the mountains of the Romans.”

`R3b`

> فنخروا من ذلك نخرةَ حُمُرِ الوَحْش
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3232
> *English:* “And they snorted at that, the snort of wild asses.”

Ibn Kathīr marks whose telling this is on the page before — it is Sayf's account, with others, as al-Ṭabarī and
others carry it. Then: he went to Ḥimṣ and ordered the armies out, one against each of the four commanders. **The
deck stops before that sentence.**

### 2.4 The same, in Ibn al-Athīr's words

`R4`

> فَبَلَغَ الرُّومُ ذَلِكَ فَكَتَبُوا إِلَى هِرَقْلَ، وَكَانَ بِالْقُدْسِ، فَقَالَ: أَرَى أَنْ تُصَالِحُوا الْمُسْلِمِينَ، فَوَاللَّهِ لَأَنْ تُصَالِحُوهُمْ عَلَى نِصْفِ مَا يَحْصُلُ مِنَ الشَّامِ وَيَبْقَى لَكُمْ نَصِفُهُ مَعَ بِلَادِ الرُّومِ - أَحَبُّ إِلَيْكُمْ مِنْ أَنْ يَغْلِبُوكُمْ عَلَى الشَّامِ وَنِصْفِ بِلَادِ الرُّومِ. فَتَفَرَّقُوا عَنْهُ وَعَصَوْه
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۵۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/942
> *English:* “Word of that reached the Romans, and they wrote to Heraclius — he was in Jerusalem. He said: I think you should make peace with the Muslims. By God, to make peace with them for half of what Syria yields, and keep half of it along with the land of the Romans, is dearer to you than that they overcome you for Syria and half the land of the Romans. And they scattered from him, and disobeyed him.”

The two books agree on the substance: **the emperor advised peace for half of Syria; his people refused.** They
differ in the wording of the offer and in where he was.

## 3. The date of the order to Iraq

`M1`

> وقد ذكر المدائني بإسناده أن خالدًا توجّه إلى العراق في المحرم سنة اثنتي عشرة
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۲ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3208
> *English:* “al-Madāʾinī has mentioned, by his chain, that Khālid set out for Iraq in al-Muḥarram of the year twelve.”

`ISA/E-C1` gives "Muḥarram 12 AH". It is al-Madāʾinī's date, by his chain, as Ibn Kathīr reports it — say so.

# X. WHAT COULD **NOT** BE ESTABLISHED

- **Any date for Shīrā, Ardashīr or Yazdagird.** The pages give none. General histories put Shīrā's death within
  months of his father's, years before 12 AH `[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]`; Ibn Kathīr's page has the letter sent on to
  him in year 12. **Do not reconcile them on a slide or from the lectern.** Say the names as the page's.
- **How many rulers Persia had between Kisrā and Yazdagird**, or how long each lasted. Būrān daughter of Kisrā is
  named on `Y1`'s page as the one the grandees speak to; nothing else about her is used.
- **The Roman–Persian war before Islam**, its length, or its outcome. Not on any page read here. (Sūrat al-Rūm and
  its tafsīr are a separate question, not opened.)
- **Heraclius's dates, his title in Greek, or why he was in Jerusalem.** The pages say "it is said … he had made
  pilgrimage that year" and no more.
- **The extent of either empire.** The map's two zones are outlines for the eye, and its face says so.
- **Whether the words in `R3` were spoken as given.** It is a Muslim historian's report of a Roman council, through
  Sayf b. ʿUmar. It is narrated as what the book carries.
- **Yazīd b. Abī Sufyān's ؓ dates.** No ⁨تراجم⁩ page was read for him in this pass.
