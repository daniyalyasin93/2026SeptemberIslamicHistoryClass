# ابن خلدون on the fitna — framing only

**Question this note answers:** How ابن خلدون reads the window from the conquests under عثمان ؓ to the
handover of the امر to معاوية ؓ — the beginning of the agitation against عثمان ؓ, the siege and the
killing, the بيعة of علي ؓ, الجمل, صفين, the arbitration, the خوارج, the killing of علي ؓ, and the
abdication of الحسن ؓ. Above all it records his **analysis of causes**: what he says عصبية did to the
Arabs once the conquests were finished, why he thinks the agitation broke out in the امصار and not
in المدینہ, how he reads the change from خلافة to ملك, and what he says about the Companions and
about the reports transmitted concerning them. **Every finding below is recorded as how ابن خلدون
reads it, attributed to him by name — never as what happened.**

## 0. The sentences this note licenses

Each of these may go on a slide **only in the form "ابن خلدون reads it this way"**, with the page given.

1. **ابن خلدون opens the agitation against عثمان ؓ not with a grievance but with a structural
   change: the conquests were finished and the state was complete** — «لما استكمل الفتح واستكمل
   للملّة الملك ونزل العرب بالأمصار» `[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۸۶. **This is the single most useful sentence
   in the whole strand**: he makes the trouble a consequence of success, not of a scandal.
2. **He distinguishes two classes of Arab: those who had صحبة — المهاجرون والأنصار من قريش وأهل
   الحجاز — and the great tribal bulk (بكر بن وائل, عبد القيس, ربيعة, الأزد, كندة, تميم, قضاعة) who
   had no share in صحبة but a very large share in the conquests** `[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۸۶.
3. **His causal sentence, and the one the whole strand exists for: once the flood receded and the
   enemy was humbled and the ملك grew great, "the veins of the جاهلية began to stir"** —
   «كانت عروق الجاهلية تنفض» `[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۸۷. He reads the resentment as reactivated tribal
   عصبية, not as a doctrinal dispute.
4. **He says plainly what they resented: that leadership over them belonged to المهاجرون والأنصار
   من قريش — «فأنفت نفوسهم منه»** `[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۸۷.
5. **He dates the outbreak to عثمان ؓ's reign as a coincidence of timing, not of cause —
   «ووافق أيام عثمان»** `[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۸۷. On his own reading the pressure had built regardless
   of who held the office.
6. **عثمان ؓ sent investigators to the امصار and they came back reporting nothing wrong: محمد بن
   مسلمة ؓ to الکوفہ, أسامة بن زيد ؓ to البصرہ, عبد الله بن عمر ؓ to الشام, عمّار بن ياسر ؓ to مصر**
   `[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۸۷ and again ج۲ ص۵۹۳. ⚠ The list of envoys is given twice by ابن خلدون with a
   difference (see §3.3) — **corroborate before use.**
7. **ابن خلدون himself corrects a charge laid against عثمان ؓ over the خمس of إفريقية: he says the
   sound position is that مروان bought it, and states in his own voice that the version in which it
   was given outright «لا يصح»** `[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۷۴ («وبعض الناس يقول أعطاه إياه ولا يصح») and
   ج۲ ص۵۸۸. **This is a case of ابن خلدون refusing a report, and it belongs in the speaker's hand.**
8. **He records عثمان ؓ's own stated reason for each disputed act in عثمان ؓ's own mouth — the
   completing of the prayer at منى, the third call on the Friday — and records that the Companions
   divided over it: «فمن الصحابة من تبعه على ذلك ومنهم من خالفه»** `[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۸۸. **That
   clause is the guardrail in ابن خلدون's own words and should be on the slide.**
9. **He assembles a consultation scene in which عثمان ؓ asks his governors what to do and each gives
   a different answer — ابن عامر: occupy them with jihad · سعيد: when their leaders perish they
   scatter · معاوية: put them in the hands of their own commanders · عبد الله: buy their goodwill
   with money** `[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۹۲. ⚠ Framing only; the wording is his.
10. **He names عبد الله بن سبأ (ابن السوداء) as an agitator and gives his doctrines** `[SOURCED]` —
    ج۲ ص۵۸۷, ص۵۹۱, ص۵۹۳. ⚠⚠ **This is the single most contested item in the strand and it is listed
    in §Not for the hall.** ابن خلدون is a late compiler here and is not a permitted sole authority.
11. **He states in his own voice that عثمان ؓ was killed wrongfully and that he was a martyr, and his
    own chapter heading carries «رضي الله عنه وأتابه ورفع درجته»** `[SOURCED]` — chapter title at
    ج۲ ص۵۹۳ (heading `حصار عثمان ومقتله رضي الله عنه وأتابه ورفع درجته`).
12. **On the Companions who fought each other, his repeated method is to name the motive each side
    gave and stop.** Recorded through §7–§10 below.
13. **His long-range verdict on the whole window is the transition from خلافة to ملك — the chapter
    after بيعة الحسن opens the دولة بني أمية as a دولة** `[SOURCED]` — ج۳ ص۴, heading

## 1. The causal frame — ابن خلدون starts the fitna with success, not with a grievance

This is the reason the strand exists. Every other historian in our safe list opens the trouble with a
list of complaints. ابن خلدون opens it with a **structural change in the community**, and only then
lists the complaints. The chapter is headed `بدء الانتقاض على عثمان رضي الله عنه` and its first
sentence is (ج۲ ص۵۸۶):

> لما استكمل الفتح واستكمل للملّة الملك ونزل العرب بالأمصار في حدود ما بينهم وبين الأمم من البصرة والكوفة والشام ومصر
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1383
> *English:* "When the conquest was completed, and the kingship of the community was completed, and the Arabs settled in the garrison cities along the frontiers between themselves and the nations — Basra, Kufa, Syria and Egypt …"

He then draws the line his whole analysis turns on: **there are two kinds of Arab now.** One kind had
صحبة; the other had conquests (ج۲ ص۵۸۶):

> وكان المختصون بصحابة الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم والاقتداء بهداه وآدابه المهاجرين والأنصار من قريش وأهل الحجاز ومن ظفر بمثل ذلك من غيرهم. وأما سائر العرب من بني بكر بن وائل وعبد القيس وسائر ربيعة والأزد وكندة وتميم وقضاعة وغيرهم، فلم يكونوا من تلك الصحبة بمكان إلّا قليلا منهم وكان لهم في الفتوحات قدم
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1383
> *English:* "Those singled out by the companionship of the Messenger ﷺ and by following his guidance and his manners were the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār, of Quraysh and the people of the Ḥijāz, and whoever else attained the like of that. But as for the rest of the Arabs — Bakr b. Wāʾil, ʿAbd al-Qays and the rest of Rabīʿa, and Azd, Kinda, Tamīm, Quḍāʿa and others — they had no standing in that companionship, save a few of them; but they had a foremost part in the conquests."

**The causal sentence itself is on the following page (ج۲ ص۵۸۷), and it is the one to build a slide
around.** ابن خلدون says that while prophecy was still living memory the older loyalties were held
down by awe; when that receded, they returned:

> فلما انحسر ذلك العباب وتنوسي الحال بعض الشيء وذلّ العدو واستفحل الملك، كانت عروق الجاهلية تنفض ووجدوا الرئاسة عليهم للمهاجرين والأنصار من قريش وسواهم، فأنفت نفوسهم منه، ووافق أيام عثمان
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1384
> *English:* "Then, when that flood-tide receded, and the condition was somewhat forgotten, and the enemy was humbled, and the kingship grew great — the veins of the Jāhiliyya began to stir. They found that leadership over them belonged to the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār, of Quraysh and others; and their souls recoiled from that. And it fell out in the days of ʿUthmān."

`[SOURCED]` — the three quotations above, ج۲ ص۵۸۶–۵۸۷.

**What the speaker may take from this, and what he may not.** He may say: *ابن خلدون reads the
troubles of this period as what happens to a community when the emergency ends* — the enemy is
beaten, the wealth arrives, and older loyalties that had been submerged surface again. He may not
say that this is *the* cause, and he must not extend «عروق الجاهلية تنفض» to any named person.
ابن خلدون applies it to a general condition of the tribes in the امصار, never to an individual.

Note the precise force of «ووافق أيام عثمان» — *it coincided with the days of ʿUthmān* `[SOURCED]`.
On his own reading the timing is the pressure reaching its limit, not a verdict on عثمان ؓ. **That
clause, quoted as his, is one of the most useful things in this note for a room that wants a verdict.**

## 2. How the pressure showed itself, on his reading

ابن خلدون lists the symptoms in one long sentence, and each item is a behaviour, not a charge
(ج۲ ص۵۸۷):

> فكانوا يظهرون الطعن في ولاته بالأمصار، والمؤاخذة لهم باللحظات والخطرات، والاستبطاء [١] عليهم في الطاعات والتجني بسؤال الاستبدال منهم والعزل، ويفيضون في النكير على عثمان
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1384
> *English:* "So they began openly to attack his governors in the garrison cities; to hold them to account for glances and passing thoughts; to drag their feet in obedience to them; to seek grievances by demanding their replacement and dismissal; and to pour out condemnation of ʿUthmān."

`[SOURCED]`. **The bracketed [١] is the printed edition's own footnote marker**; that footnote reads
`وفي النسخة الباريسية: والاشتضاط` — the Paris manuscript has a different word there. The clause is
therefore textually unstable and **must not be quoted on a slide from this edition alone**.

**And then he records that the complaints did not survive investigation.** عثمان ؓ sent men to look
(ج۲ ص۵۸۷):

> وبعث الى الأمصار من يأتيه بصحيح الخبر: محمد بن مسلمة إلى الكوفة، وأسامة بن زيد إلى البصرة، وعبد الله بن عمر إلى الشام، وعمّار بن ياسر إلى مصر وغيرهم إلى سوى هذه، فرجعوا إليه فقالوا: ما أنكرنا شيئا ولا أنكره أعيان المسلمين ولا عوامهم
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1384
> *English:* "And he sent to the garrison cities men who would bring him the sound report: Muḥammad b. Maslama to Kufa, Usāma b. Zayd to Basra, ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿUmar to Syria, ʿAmmār b. Yāsir to Egypt, and others to places besides these. They returned to him and said: we found nothing objectionable, and neither the notables of the Muslims nor their common people found anything objectionable."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۵۸۷. ⚠ **He gives the same scene twice, and not identically.** At ج۲ ص۵۹۳ the same
errands appear but phrased «وغيرهم إلى سواها» and the finding reads «ولا أنكره علماء المسلمين ولا
عوامهم» — **علماء**, not **أعيان**. That is a real divergence *inside the same book*, and a slide must
not quote a composite of the two. **Corroborate from البدایہ / الکامل before any artifact uses this
scene.**

## 3. The specific charges — and the two ابن خلدون himself refuses

Three of these belong in the speaker's hand, because ابن خلدون handles each by naming the charge and
then giving عثمان ؓ's own stated reason — and in one case by declaring the charge unsound outright.

### 3.1 The خمس of إفريقية — ابن خلدون says the story «لا يصح»

Narrating the إفريقية campaign he is unambiguous (ج۲ ص۵۷۴):

> وأرسل ابن الزبير بالفتح والخمس فاشتراه مروان بن الحكم بخمسمائة ألف دينار، وبعض الناس يقول أعطاه إياه ولا يصح، وإنما أعطى ابن أبي سرح خمس الخمس من الغزوة الأولى.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۷۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1371
> *English:* "And Ibn al-Zubayr sent the news of the victory and the fifth; and Marwān b. al-Ḥakam purchased it for five hundred thousand dīnārs. Some people say that he gave it to him outright, and that is not sound. What he did give was to Ibn Abī Sarḥ — the fifth of the fifth, from the first campaign."

He repeats the correction when he reaches the list of grievances (ج۲ ص۵۸۸):

> مع ما كان من إعطاء مروان خمس مغانم إفريقية والصحيح أنه اشتراه بخمسمائة ألف فوضعها عنه.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1385
> *English:* "…together with the matter of giving Marwān the fifth of the spoils of Ifrīqiya — though the sound position is that he bought it for five hundred thousand, and [ʿUthmān] remitted that sum to him."

`[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۵۷۴ and ج۲ ص۵۸۸. **This is ابن خلدون grading a report, in his own voice, twice,
against a charge made about a Companion.** The words «ولا يصح» and «والصحيح أنه اشتراه» are exactly
what the guardrail is built on: the speaker can say that even the historian relied on for framing
declines to accept this charge as it circulated. ⚠ The two passages are not quite the same claim —
ص۵۷۴ says مروان paid; ص۵۸۸ adds that عثمان ؓ then remitted the sum. **Do not merge them; if only one
goes on a slide, use ص۵۷۴.**

### 3.2 The prayer at منى, and the third call to the Friday prayer

Here he adjudicates nothing. He states the charge, states عثمان ؓ's answer in his own mouth, states
عبد الرحمن ؓ's rebuttal of that answer, and closes with the sentence that is the guardrail in
ابن خلدون's own words (ج۲ ص۵۸۸):

> فقال عثمان: هذا رأي رأيته. فمن الصحابة من تبعه على ذلك ومنهم من خالفه.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1385
> *English:* "ʿUthmān said: this is a judgement I formed. So among the Companions there were those who followed him in it, and among them those who differed with him."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۵۸۸. **That last clause should be on a slide, attributed to ابن خلدون, because it
models exactly the posture the course takes: a matter on which the Companions themselves differed is
a matter on which we report the difference and stop.**

The charge as he states it: `زيادة النداء الثالث على الزوراء يوم الجمعة، وإتمامه الصلاة في منى وعرفة
مع أن الأمر في حياة رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم والشيخين بعده كان على القصر` `[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۵۸۸.
⚠ **needs corroboration from البدایہ / سیر before any artifact uses it** — this is precisely a
fiqh-adjacent matter on which ابن خلدون may not be the sole authority.

### 3.3 The going of أبو ذر ؓ to الرَّبَذَة

ابن خلدون's reading is that the cause was أبو ذر ؓ's own severity in زهد (ج۲ ص۵۸۷):

> وكان الّذي دعا إلى ذلك شدّة الورع من أبي ذر وحمله الناس على شدائد الأمور والزهد في الدنيا وانه لا ينبغي لأحد أن يكون عنده أكثر من قوت يومه
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1384
> *English:* "What led to that was the intensity of Abū Dharr's scrupulousness, and his pressing people to severities and to renunciation of the world, and [his holding] that no one ought to keep by him more than a single day's sustenance."

And he records that the departure was **asked for by أبو ذر ؓ himself**, and that عثمان ؓ provided for
him afterwards (ج۲ ص۵۸۸):

> ثم استأذن أبو ذرّ عثمان في الخروج من المدينة وقال: إنّ رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم أمرني بالخروج منها إذا بلغ البناء سلعا، فأذن له، ونزل الرَّبَذَة وبنى بها مسجدا وأقطعه عثمان صرمة من الإبل وأعطاه مملوكين وأجرى عليه رزقا، وكان يتعاهد المدينة
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1385
> *English:* "Then Abū Dharr asked ʿUthmān's leave to go out from Medina, and said: the Messenger of God ﷺ commanded me to go out from it when the building reached Salʿ. So he gave him leave. He settled at al-Rabadha and built a mosque there, and ʿUthmān granted him a herd of camels and gave him two slaves and assigned him a stipend; and he used to keep up his visits to Medina."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۵۸۷–۵۸۸. ⚠ This is one version of a contested episode and **ابن خلدون may not carry
it alone**; see §Not for the hall.

## 4. عثمان ؓ's consultation with his governors — four answers, none of them about the grievance

ابن خلدون stages a scene a room will remember, because it is four men giving four different answers
to the same question (ج۲ ص۵۹۲). عثمان ؓ summons معاوية ؓ, عبد الله بن أبي سرح, سعيد بن العاص,
عبد الله بن عامر and عمرو بن العاص ؓ and says:

> إنكم وزرائي ونصحائي وأهل ثقتي وقد صنع الناس ما رأيتم فطلبوا أن أعزل عمالي وأرجع إلى ما يحبون فاجتهدوا رأيكم.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۹۲ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1389
> *English:* "You are my ministers, my counsellors and the people of my trust. The people have done what you have seen, and have demanded that I dismiss my governors and return to what they like. So exert your judgement."

The four answers, exactly as he gives them (ج۲ ص۵۹۲):

> فقال ابن عامر: أرى أن تشغلهم بالجهاد، وقال سعيد: متى تهلك قادتهم يتفرّقوا، وقال معاوية: اجعل كفالتهم إلى أمرائهم وأنا أكفيك الشام، وقال عبد الله: استصلحهم بالمال.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۹۲ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1389
> *English:* "Ibn ʿĀmir said: I hold that you should occupy them with jihād. Saʿīd said: when their leaders perish, they will scatter. Muʿāwiya said: put them into the charge of their own commanders, and I will take care of Syria for you. ʿAbd Allāh said: win them over with money."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۵۹۲. ⚠ **Framing only.** The arrangement and the wording are ابن خلدون's, and this
needs corroboration before any artifact treats it as a record of what was said. **As framing it is
excellent**: four experienced administrators, and none of the four proposes addressing the grievance
itself. That observation is the speaker's and must be offered as an observation, not as a judgement
on any of the five men.

The outcome, in one clause: `فردّهم عثمان إلى أعمالهم وأمرهم بتجهيز الناس في البعوث ليكون لهم فيها
شغل` — *"so ʿUthmān sent them back to their posts and ordered them to equip the people for
expeditions, so that they would be occupied in them"* `[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۵۹۲.

## 5. معاوية ؓ and the Kufan dissidents — عصبية argued out loud

The longest speech ابن خلدون gives in this window, and it is a speech about exactly what he has just
theorised: who owes what to whom, now that the conquests are done (ج۲ ص۵۸۹):

> أنتم قوم من العرب لكم أسنان وألسنة وقد أدركتم بالإسلام شرفا وغلبتم الأمم وحويتم مواريثهم وقد بلغني أنكم نقمتم قريشا ولو لم تكن قريش كنتم أذلة
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1386
> *English:* "You are a people of the Arabs who have years and tongues. Through Islam you attained honour; you overcame the nations and took possession of their inheritances. It has reached me that you hold a grievance against Quraysh — yet had there been no Quraysh, you would have been lowly."

And the answer he is given, by صعصعة بن صوحان (ج۲ ص۵۸۹):

> أمّا ما ذكرت من قريش فإنّها لم تكن أكثر الناس ولا أمنعها في الجاهلية فتخوّفنا
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1386
> *English:* "As for what you mentioned of Quraysh — they were neither the most numerous of people nor the most inviolable in the Jāhiliyya, that you should frighten us with them."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۵۸۹. ⚠ **needs corroboration before any artifact uses the wording**, and the exchange
runs on into mutual rebuke — see §Not for the hall.

**Why it matters for the framing.** Both men are arguing about pre-Islamic precedence. ابن خلدون has
told the reader in advance that this is what the argument would be about. The speaker's usable point
is that *ابن خلدون predicts the shape of the quarrel before he narrates it* — and then stops.

## 6. The siege as ابن خلدون narrates it — and the place where he refuses to narrate

### 6.1 عثمان ؓ declines to leave, and declines an army

معاوية ؓ offers twice; عثمان ؓ refuses twice (ج۲ ص۵۹۴):

> وقال له معاوية: أخرج معي إلى الشام قبل أن يهجم عليك ما لا تطيقه، قال: لا أبتغي بجوار رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم بدلا. قال: فأبعث إليك جندا يقيمون معك، قال: لا أضيق على جيران رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۹۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1391
> *English:* "Muʿāwiya said to him: come out with me to Syria, before there falls upon you what you cannot withstand. He said: I will take no substitute for the neighbourhood of the Messenger of God ﷺ. He said: then let me send you troops to be stationed with you. He said: I will not crowd the neighbours of the Messenger of God ﷺ."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۵۹۴. The next clause in the printed text is `فقال معاوية: لتغتالن ولتعرين [٢]` —
**[٢] is the edition's own footnote marker, and the footnote reads `وفي نسخة ثانية: لتعيّرن`.** That
wording is unstable across manuscripts; **do not project it.** What is stable and carries the scene
is عثمان ؓ's reply, `حسبي الله ونعم الوكيل`. ⚠ needs corroboration from البدایہ / سیر before use.

### 6.2 «لا أنزع ما ألبسني الله»

When abdication is demanded (ج۲ ص۵۹۹):

> فاخلع نفسك فقال: لا أنزع ما ألبسني الله ولكن أتوب وأرجع.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۹۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1396
> *English:* "[They said:] then depose yourself. He said: I will not strip off what God has clothed me in; but I will repent and turn back."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۵۹۹. ⚠ This saying is well known and البدایہ carries a version; **an artifact must
cite البدایہ for it, not ابن خلدون.**

### 6.3 The Qurʾān he was reciting

ابن خلدون places him at سورة طه when the door is forced, then at 3:173 (ج۲ ص۶۰۱). The sentence of
narrative is quotable:

> ودخلوا وعثمان يصلّي وقد افتتح سورة طه، وقد سار أهل الدار فما شغله شيء من أمرهم حتى فرغ وجلس إلى المصحف يقرأ
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۰۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1398
> *English:* "They entered, and ʿUthmān was praying, having begun Sūrat Ṭā Hā. The people of the house had gone out, and nothing of their affair distracted him until he had finished; then he sat to the muṣḥaf, reading."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۰۱. ⚠⚠ **The Qurʾānic text that follows it in this cached file is NOT to be
projected from here.** It carries the Shamela in-line verse reference `٣: ١٧٣` inside the closing
quotation mark and prints the divine name unvowelled in a text otherwise vowelled — both are
apparatus of the digital edition, not the muṣḥaf. **Any Qurʾānic text on a slide is set from a
muṣḥaf, never from a history file.** That ابن خلدون places 3:173 here is the finding; the text is not.

### 6.4 ابن خلدون refuses to report an exchange — the most valuable four words in this note

In the last minutes he narrates a series of men entering, and then he stops (ج۲ ص۶۰۱):

> ودخل عليه محمد بن أبي بكر فحاوره طويلا بما لا حاجة إلى ذكره ثم استحيا وخرج.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۰۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1398
> *English:* "And Muḥammad b. Abī Bakr came in to him and disputed with him at length, with what there is no need to mention; then he felt shame, and went out."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۰۱. **«بما لا حاجة إلى ذكره» — "with what there is no need to mention".** ابن خلدون,
who is not a cautious writer, deliberately declines to transmit words spoken by a Companion's son to
the caliph under siege, on the stated ground that there is no need. **The speaker may quote this, in
Arabic, as his licence to do the same from the platform.** It is not an argument he invented; it is
the practice of the historian he is reading.

### 6.5 The dates — and the number that must not be flattened

`وكان قتله لثمان عشرة خلت من ذي الحجة وبقي في بيته ثلاثة أيام` — *"his killing was on the eighteenth
of Dhū al-Ḥijja, and he remained in his house three days"* `[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۰۱. ⚠ **needs
corroboration from البدایہ / سیر / الکامل before any artifact uses either figure.**

⚠ **The siege length is given twice on the same page-run and the two are not the same measurement.**
ج۲ ص۵۹۶ has `وصلّى عثمان بالناس وهو محصور ثلاثين يوما` — thirty days during which he still led the
prayer — and then, in the same paragraph, `وبقي الحصار أربعين يوما`. ج۲ ص۶۰۰ repeats
`وكانت مدّة انحصاره أربعين يوما`, and adds a detail that makes the distinction intelligible:
`ولثمان عشرة منها وصل الخبر بمسير الجنود من الأمصار فاشتد الانحصار ومنعوه من لقاء الناس ومن الماء` —
at the eighteenth day the siege tightened. **A slide giving "thirty" or "forty" without saying which
is being counted would be wrong.** `[SOURCED, disputed]` ج۲ ص۵۹۶, ص۶۰۰.

**His chapter heading is itself a finding.** The section is titled
`حصار عثمان ومقتله رضي الله عنه وأتابه ورفع درجته` — *"the siege of ʿUthmān and his killing, may God
be pleased with him, and reward him, and raise his degree"* `[SOURCED]` — heading at ج۲ ص۵۹۳ ·
https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1390. ابن خلدون's own posture toward the killing stands in the title of
the chapter, before a word of narrative.

## 7. ابن خلدون names his own source for this window — and this is why he is framing only

**This is the most important methodological finding in the note, and it must be read before any other
section is used.** Twice ابن خلدون stops narrating and tells the reader where his account came from.

At the close of الجمل (ج۲ ص۶۲۲):

> هذا أمر الجمل ملخص من كتاب أبي جعفر الطبريّ اعتمدناه للوثوق به ولسلامته من الأهواء الموجودة في كتب ابن قتيبة وغيره من المؤرخين.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۲۲ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1419
> *English:* "This account of the Camel is summarised from the book of Abū Jaʿfar al-Ṭabarī. We relied on it for its trustworthiness, and for its freedom from the partisan leanings found in the books of Ibn Qutayba and other historians."

And at the very end of the whole caliphal section (ج۲ ص۶۵۰):

> وهذا آخر الكلام في الخلافة الإسلامية وما كان فيها من الردّة والفتوحات والحروب ثم الاتفاق والجماعة أوردتها ملخصة عيونها ومجامعها من كتاب محمد بن جرير الطبريّ وهو تاريخه الكبير فإنه أوثق ما رأيناه في ذلك، وأبعد من المطاعن عن الشبه في كبار الأمّة من خيارهم وعدولهم عن الصحابة رضي الله عنهم والتابعين
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۵۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1447
> *English:* "This is the end of the discourse on the Islamic caliphate, and what occurred in it of the Ridda, the conquests and the wars, and then the agreement and the coming-together. I have set it out in summary — its choicest and most comprehensive parts — from the book of Muḥammad b. Jarīr al-Ṭabarī, which is his great history; for it is the most reliable we have seen on this, and the furthest from slanders and from doubts cast on the great ones of the community, its best and most upright, of the Companions — may God be pleased with them — and of the Successors."

He continues immediately with a principle (ج۲ ص۶۵۰):

> فكثيرا ما يوجد في كلام المؤرخين أخبار فيها مطاعن وشبه في حقهم أكثرها من أهل الأهواء فلا ينبغي أن تسوّد بها الصحف
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۵۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1447
> *English:* "For there is much to be found in the words of the historians — reports carrying slanders and doubts against them, most of which come from the people of partisan passions. It is not fitting that pages be blackened with them."

`[SOURCED]` — ج۲ ص۶۲۲ and ج۲ ص۶۵۰.

**Two consequences, and both are load-bearing.**

**(a) The guardrail gains an authority.** «فلا ينبغي أن تسوّد بها الصحف» is a fourteenth-century
historian stating, as a rule of his craft, that reports impugning the Companions are mostly the
product of partisan passion and should not be written down. The speaker may put that sentence on a
slide, attributed to ابن خلدون, as the reason the evening will not adjudicate. It sits beside his
refusal at ج۲ ص۶۰۱ («بما لا حاجة إلى ذكره», §6.4) and his refusals at §8.1 and §11.3.

**(b) It confirms, from his own pen, that he may not carry a fact in this window.** `CLAUDE.md` §4
admits ابن خلدون for framing only, and rules that **الطبری is never used alone and never at all for
the مشاجرات.** ابن خلدون has just declared that his الجمل chapter, and the caliphal section as a
whole, **is a summary of الطبری.** Therefore every narrative detail in §§6, 8, 9, 10 and 11 below is,
by his own statement, الطبری at one remove. It is recorded here so that it is never re-researched —
and it is barred from every artifact unless البدایہ, سیر or الکامل carries it independently. **No
exception.** This finding is the single strongest vindication of `DECISIONS.md` #29 and should be
cited whenever anyone asks why ابن خلدون is fenced off.

## 8. Where ابن خلدون refuses or corrects a report — the collected list

This is the section the speaker should read before the evening. Four refusals, each in his own voice,
each with a page.

### 8.1 «وفي هذا الخبر بعض الهون» — he corrects a chronology

On the report that عمرو ؓ took مصر before صفين (ج۲ ص۶۲۳):

> وفي هذا الخبر بعض الهون لأنّ الصحيح أنّ عمرا ملك مصر بعد صفّين، وقيس ولاه عليّ لأول بيعته
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۲۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1420
> *English:* "In this report there is some weakness, because the sound position is that ʿAmr took possession of Egypt after Ṣiffīn, and that ʿAlī appointed Qays over it at the beginning of his bayʿa."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۲۲.

### 8.2 «وهذا بعيد» — he refuses a poisoning story about الأشتر

On the death of الأشتر on the road to مصر (ج۲ ص۶۴۱):

> وقيل إنّ معاوية بعث إلى صاحب القلزم فسمّه على أن يسقط عنه الخراج وهذا بعيد.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۴۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1438
> *English:* "It is said that Muʿāwiya sent to the governor of al-Qulzum that he should poison him, in exchange for remitting his land-tax — and this is far-fetched."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۴۱. **He states the report and then, in two words, sets it aside.**

### 8.3 «فهو من أحاديث الشيعة» — he refuses the poisoning of الحسن ؓ

On the death of الحسن ؓ at المدينہ (ج۲ ص۶۴۹):

> وما ينقل من أنّ معاوية دس إليهم السمّ مع زوجه جعدة بنت الأشعث فهو من أحاديث الشيعة وحاشا لمعاوية من ذلك.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۴۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1446
> *English:* "And what is transmitted, that Muʿāwiya slipped poison to them by means of his wife Jaʿda bint al-Ashʿath — that is from the reports of the Shīʿa, and God forbid that Muʿāwiya should have done such a thing."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۴۹. ⚠ **This is his position, attributed to him, and nothing more.** The course does
not adjudicate the question; it records that ابن خلدون rejects the report and names where he thinks
it comes from. **He also gives the date of الحسن ؓ's death twice and disagrees with himself's source**
— `إلى أن هلك سنة تسع وأربعين. وقال أبو الفرج الأصبهاني سنة احدى وخمسين وعلى فراشه بالمدينة`
`[SOURCED, disputed]` ج۲ ص۶۴۹. ⚠ **needs corroboration from سیر / البدایہ; do not put a year for
الحسن ؓ's death on any artifact on ابن خلدون's authority.**

### 8.4 The editor's footnote quoting ابن كثير — and how to handle it

On the page where ابن خلدون reports mutual cursing in the قنوت after the arbitration (ج۲ ص۶۳۷), the
**printed edition's own footnote [٢]** reads:

> [٢] قال ابن كثير في تاريخه: ان هذا لم يصحّ أهـ ولعلّ الدعاء كان لغير اللعن. قاله نصر.
> — the printed edition's footnote at تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۳۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1434
> *English:* "Footnote [2]: Ibn Kathīr said in his history: this is not sound. — end of quotation. And perhaps the supplication was for something other than cursing. So said Naṣr."

`[SOURCED, as the edition's apparatus]` ج۲ ص۶۳۷. ⚠⚠ **Read this correctly and do not over-claim it.**
This is **the modern editor** (signing himself نصر) quoting ابن كثير against the text he is printing.
It is not ابن كثير speaking to us directly, and **this note does not license a citation of
البدایہ والنہایہ.** What it does license is a research action: **go to البدایہ (30097) and find
ابن كثير's own words.** If البدایہ carries «لم يصح» on this report, that is a first-rank guardrail
quotation and belongs in the catalogue. Until that page is read, **the cursing report may not be
narrated at all** — see §Not for the hall.

## 9. The one sentence at صفين that the speaker most needs — «لا أقول مظلوما ولا ظالما»

Delegates from معاوية ؓ come to علي ؓ before the fighting and press him to endorse a verdict on the
killing of عثمان ؓ. ابن خلدون gives the exchange in three lines (ج۲ ص۶۲۸):

> فقالوا: نشهد أن عثمان قتل مظلوما، فقال: لا أقول مظلوما ولا ظالما. قالوا: فمن لم يقل ذلك فنحن منه برآء وانصرفوا.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۲۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1425
> *English:* "They said: we bear witness that ʿUthmān was killed wrongfully. He said: I say neither wrongfully nor wrongdoing. They said: whoever will not say that, we disown him — and they withdrew."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۲۸. ⚠ **needs corroboration from البدایہ / الکامل before any artifact uses it** — by
§7 this is الطبری at one remove, and it touches the مشاجرات directly.

**Why it is worth the corroboration effort.** If it can be anchored in البدایہ or الکامل, the speaker
has the strongest possible answer to the hostile question of the evening, and it is not his own
answer: a refusal to be forced into a public verdict, from within the narrative itself. The
guardrail stops being a modern scruple and becomes a reported position. **Put this at the top of the
corroboration list for session 3.**

Two more from the same run, both usable and both needing the same corroboration:

- **علي ؓ's rules of engagement before the battle** (ج۲ ص۶۲۹) — and note that he had given nearly the
  same order at الجمل (ج۲ ص۶۱۸, `لا تتبعوا مدبرا ولا تجهزوا على جريح ولا تدخلوا الدور`):

> لا تقاتلوهم حتى يقاتلوكم فإذا هزمتموهم فلا تقتلوا مدبرا ولا تجهزوا على جريح ولا تكشفوا عورة ولا تمثّلوا ولا تأخذوا مالا ولا تهيّجوا امرأة وإن شتمتكم فإنّهنّ ضعاف الأنفس والقوى
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۲۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1426
> *English:* "Do not fight them until they fight you. And when you have routed them, do not kill one who turns his back, do not finish off a wounded man, do not uncover nakedness, do not mutilate, do not take property, and do not provoke a woman even if she reviles you — for they are weak in spirit and in strength."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۲۹. ⚠ needs corroboration. **This is the single best non-partisan slide in the
strand: it is about conduct, not about who was right.**

- **His judgement on the dead of both armies, spoken at الجمل before the fighting** (ج۲ ص۶۱۶):

> فقال: أرجو أن لا يقتل منّا ومنهم أحد نقي قلبه للَّه إلّا أدخله الله الجنة
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۱۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1413
> *English:* "He said: I hope that no one is killed, from among us or from among them, whose heart is pure toward God, but that God admits him to the Garden."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۱۶. ⚠ needs corroboration. **If it anchors, this is the closing عبرت line of the
evening** — the man leading one side declines to consign the other side.

## 10. الجمل — what ابن خلدون's arrangement makes visible, and what must not be taken from it

**The whole chapter is a summary of الطبری by his own statement (§7), so nothing here is a fact for
an artifact.** What survives as framing is the *shape* he gives the event, and it is worth recording
because it is not the shape a modern listener expects.

**(a) He narrates both sides as seeking to avoid the battle, and the battle beginning against both.**
He records the negotiation through القعقاع بن عمرو and the sentence that carried it (ج۲ ص۶۱۵):

> قال هذا الأمر دواؤه التسكين وإذا سكن اختلجوا فآثروا العافية ترزقوها
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۱۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1412
> *English:* "He said: the remedy of this affair is quieting it; and when it is quiet, they will be drawn apart. So prefer well-being, and you will be granted it."

and then `فقالوا قد أصبت وأحسنت فارجع فان قدم عليّ وهو على مثل رأيك صلح هذا الأمر، فرجع وأخبر عليا
فأعجبه وأشرف القوم على الصلح` — *"they said: you have hit the mark and done well… he returned and
told ʿAlī, and it pleased him, and the people were on the verge of peace"* `[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۱۵.

**(b) He attributes the outbreak to a third party acting at night.** ج۲ ص۶۱۷: `وبات الذين أثاروا أمر
عثمان بشّر ليلة يتشاورون، واتفقوا على إنشاب الحرب بين الناس` `[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۱۷. ⚠⚠ **This is the
most contested single claim in the whole window** and it rests on the السبئية material — see
§Not for the hall. **Do not narrate it.** It is recorded so it is never re-researched.

**(c) الأحنف بن قيس's third way is the usable material.** He had given بيعة and would not break it,
and would not fight أم المؤمنين ؓ, so he withdrew with his people (ج۲ ص۶۱۶):

> فقلت لا أنقض بيعتي ولا أقاتل أمّ المؤمنين، ولكن أعتزل
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۱۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1413
> *English:* "So I said: I will not break my bayʿa, and I will not fight the Mother of the Believers — but I will withdraw."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۱۶. ⚠ needs corroboration. **He also records that البصرہ split three ways, not two**
— `وكان أهل البصرة على ثلاث فرق مفترقين مع هؤلاء وهؤلاء وثالثة اعتزلت كالأحنف ابن قيس وعمران بن حصين`
`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۱۷. **That a third of a city stood aside is itself the framing: withdrawal was a
recognised option taken by named Companions, not a modern invention.**

**(d) The aftermath, which is where ابن خلدون is at his most useful and least partisan.** After the
battle: `وأمر عليّ فنودي لا تتبعوا مدبرا ولا تجهزوا على جريح ولا تدخلوا الدور` ج۲ ص۶۱۹; he ordered a
canopy set over the هودج and asked whether she was wounded, ج۲ ص۶۱۹; he prayed over the dead **of
both sides** — `ثم صلّى على القتلى من الجانبين` ج۲ ص۶۲۰; and the exchange (ج۲ ص۶۱۹):

> وأتاها عليّ فقال: كيف أنت يا أمّه؟ قالت: بخير قال: يغفر الله لك. قالت: ولك.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۱۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1416
> *English:* "ʿAlī came to her and said: how are you, mother? She said: well. He said: may God forgive you. She said: and you."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۱۹. ⚠ needs corroboration. And عائشة ؓ's own word to القعقاع, on the same page:
`وددت اني مت قبل هذا اليوم بعشرين سنة` — *"I wish I had died twenty years before this day"*
`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۱۹. ⚠ needs corroboration.

And his account of the escort home (ج۲ ص۶۲۰): `ثم جهّزها عليّ إلى المدينة بما احتاجت إليه وبعثها مع
أخيها محمد مع أربعين من نسوة البصرة اختارهن لمرافقتها… ثم جاء يوم ارتحالها فودّعها واستعتبت له
واستعتب لها، ومشى معها أميالا وشيّعها بنوه مسافة يوم` `[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۲۰. ⚠ needs corroboration.
**This is the ending the evening needs and it is in the book: the two principals part with mutual
apology and an escort of forty women.**

**(e) A number, given plainly, and not to be used on his authority.** `وأحصى القتلى من الجانبين
فكانوا عشرة الاف منهم من ضبّة ألف رجل` `[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۲۰. ⚠⚠ **Ten thousand is a large, memorable
number and therefore exactly the kind of figure a deck will reach for. It may not be used unless
البدایہ or الکامل gives it.**

**(f) علي ؓ's own remark at طلحة ؓ's body, which corrects the "rabble" framing from inside the
account** (ج۲ ص۶۲۰): `ورأى كعب بن سور وعبد الرحمن بن عتّاب وطلحة بن عبيد الله وهو يقول: زعموا أنه لم
يخرج إلينا إلا الغوغاء مع أن هؤلاء فيهم` — *"…and he was saying: they claimed that none but the rabble
came out against us, yet these men are among them"* `[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۲۰. ⚠ needs corroboration.
**Note carefully what it does: the commander of one side publicly refuses his own side's
characterisation of the other.**

## 11. From خلافة to ملك — ابن خلدون's own long analysis, and the one place he must be quoted by name

Everything before this section is narrative he took from someone else. **This section is ابن خلدون
himself**, and it is the reason the course admits him at all. It stands on ج۲ ص۶۵۰–۶۵۱ and ج۳ ص۳–۵.

### 11.1 عام الجماعة, with the year

> واستقرّ الأمر لمعاوية واتفق الجماعة على بيعته وذلك في منتصف سنة احدى وأربعين وسمّي ذلك العام عام الجماعة من أجل ذلك.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۵۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1447
> *English:* "The affair settled for Muʿāwiya, and the community agreed upon his bayʿa; and that was in the middle of the year forty-one. And that year was named 'the year of the community' on account of it."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۵۰, and repeated at ج۳ ص۵ — `واتفقت الجماعة على بيعة معاوية في منتصف سنة إحدى
وأربعين`. ⚠ **needs corroboration from البدایہ / الکامل before the year goes on any artifact** — but
the *name* عام الجماعة, given as ابن خلدون's explanation of the name, is safe as framing.

### 11.2 His two reasons for placing معاوية ؓ with the Umayyads rather than the Rāshidūn

**The first reason is the whole analysis in one sentence** (ج۲ ص۶۵۰):

> الأوّل أنّ الخلافة لعهده كانت مغالبة لأجل ما قدّمناه من العصبية التي حدثت لعصره، وأمّا قبل ذلك كانت اختيارا واجتماع فميّزوا بين الحالتين، فكان معاوية أوّل خلفاء المغالبة والعصبيّة الذين يعبّر عنهم أهل الأهواء بالملوك
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۵۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1447
> *English:* "The first is that the caliphate in his time was [held] by contest, on account of what we have set out of the ʿaṣabiyya that arose in his age; whereas before that it was by choice and by coming-together. So they distinguished between the two conditions. Muʿāwiya was thus the first of the caliphs of contest and of ʿaṣabiyya — those whom the people of partisan passions call kings."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۵۰. **This is the خلافت → ملوکیت hinge of the whole course, in the words of the man
the course borrows the concept from.** Note three things a careless slide would lose:
1. He says the change is in **how the office is obtained** — اختيار واجتماع before, مغالبة after.
2. He traces the change to **العصبية that arose in that age**, which is the same cause he gave for
   the agitation at ج۲ ص۵۸۷ (§1). **His account is one causal arc from ص۵۸۶ to ص۶۵۰.**
3. **He explicitly refuses the word "kings" for معاوية ؓ**, calling it the usage of أهل الأهواء, and
   in the next breath `فهو من الخلفاء الراشدين`. ⚠⚠ **A slide that says «خلافت سے بادشاہت» and cites
   ابن خلدون for it would be putting on his lips a word he declines in this very passage.** He
   describes the *mechanism* as مغالبة and عصبية; he withholds the *label*.

**The second reason is prosaic and he says so** (ج۲ ص۶۵۱): the Umayyad caliphs share one lineage and
معاوية ؓ is its greatest, so compilers grouped him with his kin, while the first four were of
differing lineages — `الأمر الثاني في ذكر معاوية مع خلفاء بني أمية دون الخفاء الأربعة أنهم كانوا أهل
نسب واحد وعظيمهم معاوية` `[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۵۱. (The printed text reads `الخفاء` for `الخلفاء` twice on
this page — **a typographical defect in the edition; do not reproduce it.**)

### 11.3 His distinction between two kinds of ملك — the part most often mis-cited

> واعلم أنّ الملك الّذي يخالف بل ينافي الخلافة هو الجبروتيّة المعبر عنها بالكسرويّة التي أنكرها عمر على معاوية حين رأى ظواهرها، وأما الملك الّذي هو الغلبة والقهر بالعصبية والشوكة فلا ينافي الخلافة ولا النبوّة
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۵۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1447
> *English:* "Know that the kingship which opposes — indeed contradicts — the caliphate is tyranny, called 'Chosroes-like', which ʿUmar reproved in Muʿāwiya when he saw its outward marks. But the kingship which is dominance and mastery by ʿaṣabiyya and armed strength — that contradicts neither the caliphate nor prophethood."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۵۰. He grounds the second kind in سليمان ؑ and داود ؑ (ج۲ ص۶۵۱): `فقد كان سليمان بن
داود وأبوه صلوات الله عليهما نبيّين وملكين وكانا على غاية الاستقامة`. And he closes with a criterion
that is itself worth a slide (ج۲ ص۶۵۱):

> والقانون في ذلك عرض أفعالهم على الصحيح من الأخبار لا بالواهي، فمن جرت أفعاله عليها فهو خليفة النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم في المسلمين، ومن خرجت أفعاله عن ذلك فهو من ملوك الدنيا وإنما سمّي خليفة بالمجاز.
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۵۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1448
> *English:* "The rule in this is to measure their deeds against the sound reports, not the weak ones. Whoever's deeds run according to them is the successor of the Prophet ﷺ among the Muslims; and whoever's deeds depart from that is one of the kings of this world, and is called caliph only by a figure of speech."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۵۱. **«عرض أفعالهم على الصحيح من الأخبار لا بالواهي» is a method statement, and the
course's own method in one clause: measure against what is sound, not against what is weak.**

**His defence of معاوية ؓ's motive, in his own voice** (ج۲ ص۶۵۱):

> ومعاوية لم بطلب الملك ولا أبّهته للاستكثار من الدنيا، وانما ساقه أمر العصبيّة بطبعها لمّا استولى المسلمون على الدول كلها وكان هو خليفتهم
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۶۵۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1448
> *English:* "Muʿāwiya did not seek kingship, nor its pomp, for the sake of amassing the world. Rather the matter of ʿaṣabiyya drove him by its own nature, once the Muslims had taken possession of all the states and he was their caliph."

`[SOURCED]` ج۲ ص۶۵۱. ⚠⚠ **This is a judgement about a Companion's inner motive, offered by a later
historian. It is recorded here because it is exactly the kind of thing an audience member will
quote, and the speaker must know where it comes from. It is not narrated from the platform as a
finding.** What is usable is the structure of his argument, not the verdict.

⚠⚠⚠ **ONE ITEM ON THIS PAGE IS BARRED ABSOLUTELY.** In the same sentence-run at ج۲ ص۶۵۰ ابن خلدون
grades a well-known hadith: `ولا ينظر في ذلك إلى حديث الخلافة بعدي ثلاثون سنة فإنه لم يصحّ`. **A
historian's grading of a hadith is not a hadith ruling, our course does not adjudicate hadith, and
this particular hadith is accepted by many scholars.** It is recorded here only so that it is never
re-derived and never wandered into by accident. **See §Not for the hall. It must not appear on any
slide, in any script, or in any answer from the floor.**

### 11.4 The عصبية account carried back to before Islam — ج۳ ص۳–۵

The opening of ج۳ is ابن خلدون's own essay, and it completes the arc. He argues that Islam did not
abolish عصبية but suspended the *boastful* kind while leaving the *natural* kind (ج۳ ص۴):

> ولم يبق إلّا العصبية الطبيعية التي لا تفارق وهي بعزّة الرجل على أخيه وجاره في القتل والعدوان عليه، فهذه لا يذهبها شيء ولا هي محظورة بل هي مطلوبة ونافعة في الجهاد والدعاء إلى الدين
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۳ ص۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1450
> *English:* "There remained only the natural ʿaṣabiyya, which does not depart — the man's protective pride over his brother and his neighbour against being killed or wronged. Nothing removes this; nor is it forbidden. Rather it is sought after, and useful in jihād and in the call to the religion."

`[SOURCED]` ج۳ ص۴. **This is the distinction that keeps the whole analysis from being an accusation.**
He is not saying the men of this window relapsed into جاهلية; he is saying a natural solidarity,
which Islam does not forbid, reasserted its political weight once the extraordinary conditions
ended. **A slide that renders «عروق الجاهلية تنفض» (§1) without this qualification from ج۳ ص۴ would
misrepresent him.** The two passages belong together.

And his statement of the mechanism at the moment of the handover (ج۳ ص۵):

> واتفقت الجماعة على بيعة معاوية في منتصف سنة إحدى وأربعين عند ما نسي الناس شأن النبوّة والخوارق ورجعوا إلى أمر العصبية والتغالب
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۳ ص۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1451
> *English:* "And the community agreed on the bayʿa of Muʿāwiya in the middle of the year forty-one, when people had forgotten the matter of prophethood and the extraordinary events, and had returned to the affair of ʿaṣabiyya and mutual contest."

`[SOURCED]` ج۳ ص۵.

**His comparison of the two armies is the clearest statement of his thesis as applied to the
fighting** (ج۳ ص۵):

> كانت عساكر عليّ أكثر عددا لمكان الخلافة والفضل إلّا أنها من سائر القبائل من ربيعة ويمن وغيرهم، وجموع معاوية إنما هي جند الشام من قريش شوكة مضر وبأسهم نزلوا بثغور الشام منذ الفتح فكانت عصبيته أشدّ وأمضى شوكه
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۳ ص۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1451
> *English:* "ʿAlī's armies were more numerous, by virtue of the caliphate and of precedence — but they were from all the various tribes, from Rabīʿa and Yemen and others. Muʿāwiya's assemblies were the army of Syria, of Quraysh, the armed strength of Muḍar and their valour, who had settled in the Syrian frontier posts since the conquest. So his ʿaṣabiyya was harder and his armed strength more decisive."

`[SOURCED]` ج۳ ص۵. **This is a structural explanation with no moral content at all, and that is
exactly why it is usable**: the difference he names is cohesion, not virtue — he explicitly grants
الخلافة والفضل to the other side in the same sentence.

### E-IK1 · The conquests finish and the state is complete
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** the close of the conquest era, no year given on the page `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** the four garrison cities light at once — البصرہ, الکوفہ, الشام, مصر.

**What happened:** ابن خلدون does not open the troubles of this period with a complaint about anyone. He opens them with a change in the shape of the community itself. The conquests are finished, the state is complete, and the Arabs have come to rest in garrison cities along the frontier with the nations they have just defeated. Everything he says afterwards about the agitation is built on that one sentence.

**The statement:**
> لما استكمل الفتح واستكمل للملّة الملك ونزل العرب بالأمصار في حدود ما بينهم وبين الأمم من البصرة والكوفة والشام ومصر
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1383
> *English:* "When the conquest was completed, and the kingship of the community was completed, and the Arabs settled in the garrison cities along the frontiers between themselves and the nations — Basra, Kufa, Syria and Egypt …"

**عبرت:** A community is tested by the end of its emergency as surely as by the emergency itself.

**Hands-up?** Before the card: "When an army stops fighting and settles in a new city, what is the first thing that changes?"

### E-IK2 · Two kinds of Arab, one with صحبة and one with conquests
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** the same passage, no year given `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** الحجاز against the frontier cities — the line runs between them.

**What happened:** ابن خلدون now draws the distinction his whole analysis turns on. On one side stand those who had the companionship of the Messenger ﷺ and were formed by his guidance — the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār, of Quraysh and of the people of the Ḥijāz. On the other side stand the great tribal bulk of the Arabs: Bakr b. Wāʾil, ʿAbd al-Qays and the rest of Rabīʿa, Azd, Kinda, Tamīm, Quḍāʿa. Very few of the second group had any standing in that companionship, but they had a foremost part in the conquests.

**The statement:**
> وكان المختصون بصحابة الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم والاقتداء بهداه وآدابه المهاجرين والأنصار من قريش وأهل الحجاز ومن ظفر بمثل ذلك من غيرهم. وأما سائر العرب من بني بكر بن وائل وعبد القيس وسائر ربيعة والأزد وكندة وتميم وقضاعة وغيرهم، فلم يكونوا من تلك الصحبة بمكان إلّا قليلا منهم وكان لهم في الفتوحات قدم
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1383
> *English:* "Those singled out by the companionship of the Messenger ﷺ and by following his guidance and his manners were the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār, of Quraysh and the people of the Ḥijāz, and whoever else attained the like of that. But as for the rest of the Arabs — Bakr b. Wāʾil, ʿAbd al-Qays and the rest of Rabīʿa, and Azd, Kinda, Tamīm, Quḍāʿa and others — they had no standing in that companionship, save a few of them; but they had a foremost part in the conquests."

**عبرت:** Two honours can grow in one community and not be the same honour.

### E-IK3 · The veins of the جاهلية begin to stir
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** the reign of عثمان ؓ, ۲۳–۳۵ھ `[STANDARD]` (to verify) · **Map:** the frontier cities stay lit; nothing moves.

**What happened:** While the memory of prophecy was still fresh, ابن خلدون says, the older loyalties were held down by awe. Then the flood-tide receded, the condition was somewhat forgotten, the enemy was beaten and the kingship grew great. At that point, on his reading, the old tribal solidarities began to stir again. What the tribes found when they looked around was that leadership over them belonged to the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār — and their souls recoiled from it.

**The statement:**
> فلما انحسر ذلك العباب وتنوسي الحال بعض الشيء وذلّ العدو واستفحل الملك، كانت عروق الجاهلية تنفض ووجدوا الرئاسة عليهم للمهاجرين والأنصار من قريش وسواهم، فأنفت نفوسهم منه، ووافق أيام عثمان
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1384
> *English:* "Then, when that flood-tide receded, and the condition was somewhat forgotten, and the enemy was humbled, and the kingship grew great — the veins of the Jāhiliyya began to stir. They found that leadership over them belonged to the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār, of Quraysh and others; and their souls recoiled from that. And it fell out in the days of ʿUthmān."

**عبرت:** What an extraordinary time suppresses, an ordinary time brings back.

**Hands-up?** no

### E-IK4 · And it fell out in the days of عثمان ؓ
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** ۲۳–۳۵ھ `[STANDARD]` (to verify) · **Map:** No map.

### E-IK5 · How the pressure showed itself in the cities
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** ۲۳–۳۵ھ `[STANDARD]` (to verify) · **Map:** the four garrison cities, all four at once.

**What happened:** ابن خلدون lists what the agitation actually looked like on the ground, and every item on his list is a behaviour rather than a charge. Men began to attack the governors openly. They held them to account for a glance or a passing thought, dragged their feet in obedience, and demanded that this governor be replaced and that one dismissed. And they poured out condemnation of عثمان ؓ himself.

**The statement:**
> فكانوا يظهرون الطعن في ولاته بالأمصار، والمؤاخذة لهم باللحظات والخطرات، والاستبطاء [١] عليهم في الطاعات والتجني بسؤال الاستبدال منهم والعزل، ويفيضون في النكير على عثمان
> — تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۸۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1384
> *English:* "So they began openly to attack his governors in the garrison cities; to hold them to account for glances and passing thoughts; to drag their feet in obedience to them; to seek grievances by demanding their replacement and dismissal; and to pour out condemnation of ʿUthmān."

**عبرت:** When the will to find fault comes first, the faults are never hard to find.

### E-IK6 · Four Companions are sent to investigate and find nothing
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** ۲۳–۳۵ھ `[STANDARD]` (to verify) · **Map:** four arrows out of المدینہ — to الکوفہ, البصرہ, الشام, مصر — and four back.

**What happened:** عثمان ؓ did not answer the complaints from المدینہ alone. He sent men to the cities to bring him back the sound report of what was happening there: محمد بن مسلمة ؓ to الکوفہ, أسامة بن زيد ؓ to البصرہ, عبد الله بن عمر ؓ to الشام, عمّار بن ياسر ؓ to مصر, and others to places besides. They came back and told him that they had found nothing objectionable, and that neither the notables of the Muslims nor the common people had found anything objectionable either.

**The statement:**
