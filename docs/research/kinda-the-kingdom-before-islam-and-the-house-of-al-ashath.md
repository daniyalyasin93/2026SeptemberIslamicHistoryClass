# Kinda — the kingdom before Islam, and the house of al-Ashʿath

**Question this note answers:** Lecture 3 ends in Yemen with al-Ashʿath b. Qays ؓ brought down from
the fort of al-Nujayr, sent to Medina and pardoned. The speaker wants a few cards of **backstory**:
(1) did Kinda have a kingship before Islam, and what do the books say of it; (2) did that royal past
show itself when Kinda came to the Prophet ﷺ and when it broke away in the ridda; (3) what became of
al-Ashʿath ؓ afterwards, in one line each; and (4) the later chapter of the same house — the revolt of
his grandson against al-Ḥajjāj — as a forward pointer for a future Umayyad evening.

**Scope and sister notes.** The ridda of Kinda itself (the she-camel, the *maḥājir*, al-Nujayr, the
*amān* for seventy, the pardon, the marriage) belongs to the Yemen sister notes and to
`ridda-campaign-the-conduct-of-the-wars.md` §6.4. **This note is backstory and aftermath only.** It
touches the ridda at one point — the word "kings" in the ridda narrative (§3) — and stops there.

# 1. KINDA BEFORE ISLAM

## 1.1 Who Kinda were

Kinda is a Qaḥṭānī tribe; al-Dhahabī gives Ibn Saʿd's chain from Kinda (whose name was Thawr) up to
Kahlān b. Sabaʾ and Qaḥṭān, and a folk-etymology for the name. [SOURCED] ⁨سیر ج۲ ص۳۸⁩. The tribal
map (ʿAdnān / Qaḥṭān) is already in `arabian-tribes-and-the-ridda-setup.md` — not repeated here.

The point that matters for this note: **Kinda were southerners, and the tribes they ruled — Bakr,
Asad, Ghaṭafān, Taghlib, Qays — were northerners.** That distance is named by Banū Asad themselves
when they killed their Kindī king (§1.3).

## 1.2 How the kingship began — two accounts

**Ibn al-Athīr's account.** The chapter opens by saying it will first give "the reason for their
rule over the Arabs of Najd" (⁨الکامل ج۱ ص۴۶۱⁩). Then:

> كَانَ سُفَهَاءُ بَكْرٍ قَدْ غَلَبُوا عَلَى عُقَلَائِهَا وَغَلَبُوهُمْ عَلَى الْأَمْرِ وَأَكَلَ الْقَوِيُّ الضَّعِيفَ، فَنَظَرَ الْعُقَلَاءُ فِي أَمْرِهِمْ فَرَأَوْا أَنْ يُمَلِّكُوا عَلَيْهِمْ مَلِكًا يَأْخُذُ لِلضَّعِيفِ مِنَ الْقَوِيِّ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۱ ص۴۶۲⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/458
> *English:* "The foolish men of Bakr had got the better of its wise men and taken the matter out of
> their hands, and the strong devoured the weak. So the wise considered their affair and decided to
> set a king over themselves who would take the weak man's due from the strong."

The Arabs warned them that a king from among themselves would not work — one party would obey him and
another defy him — so they went south:

> فَسَارُوا إِلَى بَعْضِ تَبَابِعَةِ الْيَمَنِ، وَكَانُوا لِلْعَرَبِ بِمَنْزِلَةِ الْخُلَفَاءِ لِلْمُسْلِمِينَ، وَطَلَبُوا مِنْهُ أَنْ يُمَلِّكَ عَلَيْهِمْ مَلِكًا، فَمَلَّكَ عَلَيْهِمْ حُجْرَ بْنَ عَمْرٍو آكِلَ الْمُرَارِ
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۱ ص۴۶۲⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/458
> *English:* "So they went to one of the Tubbaʿs of Yemen — who were to the Arabs what the caliphs
> are to the Muslims — and asked him to set a king over them; and he made Ḥujr b. ʿAmr, Ākil al-Murār,
> king over them."

Ḥujr settled at **Baṭn ʿĀqil**, took back most of Bakr's land from the Lakhmids of al-Ḥīra, ruled
until he died, and was buried at Baṭn ʿĀqil. His son **ʿAmr, "al-Maqṣūr"**, succeeded him — so named,
Ibn al-Athīr says, because he was confined to his father's kingdom — while ʿAmr's brother Muʿāwiya
"al-Jawn" held al-Yamāma. [SOURCED] ⁨الکامل ج۱ ص۴۶۲⁩.

**Ibn Khaldūn's account — framing only, attributed.** Ibn Khaldūn gives Kinda a chapter of its own
among the Arab royal houses, beside the Lakhmids of al-Ḥīra and Ghassān: «⁨الخبر عن ملوك كندة من هذه⁩
⁨الطبقة ومبدإ أمرهم⁩». His account is taken from al-Ṭabarī, from Hishām al-Kalbī, and differs from
Ibn al-Athīr's. In it the Kindī chief ʿAmr b. Ḥujr served the Ḥimyarite king Ḥassān b. Tubbaʿ, and
Ḥassān, returning from the north, set over **all of Maʿadd** a man the page names as Ḥujr b. ʿAmr,
Ākil al-Murār. **The wording of the page is not clean on who was whose brother** — render it loosely:

> ولّى على معدّ بن عدنان كلّها أخا حجر بن عمرو هذا وهو آكل المرار، فدانوا له وسار فيهم أحسن سيرة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۳۲۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1134
> *English:* "He set over the whole of Maʿadd b. ʿAdnān [the one the text calls] the brother, this Ḥujr
> b. ʿAmr — he is Ākil al-Murār — and they submitted to him, and he governed them in the best way."

**How to use the two.** Both put the origin of the Kindī kingship in **an appointment by a Yemeni
king over northern tribes**. They differ on the occasion (a request from Bakr / a grant by Ḥassān) and
on how Ḥujr ruled. Per `DECISIONS.md` #29 the Ibn Khaldūn version rests on al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Khaldūn
together, so it is **framing, never the account**: if it is spoken at all, say "Ibn Khaldūn,
following an older genealogist, tells it differently."

## 1.3 al-Ḥārith b. ʿAmr — al-Ḥīra won and lost

> فَلَمَّا مَاتَ عَمْرٌو مَلَكَ بَعْدَهُ ابْنُهُ الْحَارِثُ، وَكَانَ شَدِيدَ الْمُلْكِ بَعِيدَ الصَّوْتِ
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۱ ص۴۶۲⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/458
> *English:* "When ʿAmr died his son al-Ḥārith became king after him, and he was a king of great
> power and far-reaching name."

When Qubādh b. Fīrūz of Persia accepted the teaching of Mazdak, al-Mundhir b. Māʾ al-Samāʾ — Persia's
governor at al-Ḥīra — refused to follow him; al-Ḥārith accepted, and Qubādh gave him al-Ḥīra and
drove al-Mundhir out. Ibn al-Athīr adds that other accounts of how he came to rule exist. When
Anūshirwān succeeded, he killed Mazdak, restored al-Mundhir, and sought al-Ḥārith at al-Anbār, his
seat. al-Ḥārith fled with his children and wealth to the land of Kalb; al-Mundhir's horsemen from
Taghlib, Iyād and Bahrāʾ plundered his herds, and:

> وَأَخَذَتْ تَغْلِبُ ثَمَانِيَةً وَأَرْبَعِينَ نَفْسًا مِنْ بَنِي آكِلِ الْمُرَارِ، فِيهِمْ عَمْرٌو وَمَالِكٌ ابْنَا الْحَارِثِ، فَقَدِمُوا بِهِمْ عَلَى الْمُنْذِرِ، فَقَتَلَهُمْ فِي دِيَارِ بَنِي مَرِينَا
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۱ ص۴۶۲⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/458
> *English:* "Taghlib took forty-eight persons of Banū Ākil al-Murār, among them ʿAmr and Mālik, the
> two sons of al-Ḥārith, and brought them to al-Mundhir, and he killed them in the lands of Banū
> Marīnā."

How al-Ḥārith himself died is disputed inside the same page: **Kalb claim they killed him; "the
learned men of Kinda" say he died after a hunt** (⁨الکامل ج۱ ص۴۶۳⁩). Ibn al-Athīr prints both.

**A date peg.** Ibn al-Athīr, arguing against another report, puts **"about a hundred and thirty
years" between the reign of Qubādh and the Hijra**. [SOURCED] ⁨الکامل ج۱ ص۴۶۱⁩. Any Common-Era date
for al-Ḥārith is `[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]` (early 6th century CE) and must be labelled as such.

## 1.4 A source remark the speaker can make inside the story

On the same page Ibn al-Athīr rejects a report that a Kindī king ruled al-Ḥīra again, on the ground
that the Lakhmids' rule there was only broken under Qubādh — and says where he thinks the report
came from:

> وَيُشْبِهُ أَنْ يَكُونَ بَعْضُ الْكِنْدِيِّينَ قَدْ ذَكَرَ هَذَا تَعَصُّبًا، وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ.
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۱ ص۴۶۱⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/457
> *English:* "It looks as though some of the Kindīs mentioned this out of partisanship — and Allah
> knows best."

This is the one "how do we know?" remark this backstory needs: **a later Muslim historian checking a tribe's own stories about itself against the sequence of kings, and saying so.**

## 1.5 The sons over the tribes, and the killing of Ḥujr

While al-Ḥārith held al-Ḥīra, the chiefs of several tribes of Nizār came to him:

> إِنَّا فِي طَاعَتِكَ وَقَدْ وَقَعَ بَيْنَنَا مِنَ الشَّرِّ بِالْقَتْلِ مَا تَعْلَمُ وَنَخَافُ الْفَنَاءَ فَوَجِّهْ مَعَنَا بَنِيكَ يَنْزِلُونَ فِينَا فَيَكُفُّونَ بَعْضَنَا عَنْ بَعْضٍ
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۱ ص۴۶۳⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/459
> *English:* "We are in obedience to you. You know what bloodshed has fallen between us, and we fear
> being wiped out. Send your sons with us to live among us and hold us back from one another."

So he divided his sons among the tribes (⁨الکامل ج۱ ص۴۶۳⁩):

| Son | Set as king over |
|---|---|
| **Ḥujr** | Banū Asad b. Khuzayma and Ghaṭafān |
| **Shuraḥbīl** (later killed on the Day of al-Kulāb) | all of Bakr b. Wāʾil, and others |
| **Maʿdī Karib, "Ghalfāʾ"** | Qays ʿAylān and other groups |
| **Salama** | Taghlib, al-Namir b. Qāsiṭ, and Banū Saʿd b. Zayd Manāt of Tamīm |

Ḥujr took a yearly levy from Banū Asad. When they drove out his collectors he came against them with
troops of Rabīʿa and of Qays and Kināna, seized their leading men, **"began killing them with the
staff"**, let their property be taken and drove them to Tihāma; he then relented at a poem from
ʿAbīd b. al-Abraṣ and sent to bring them back. At a day's distance from him Banū Asad turned, fell on
his camp and killed him in his tent — **ʿIlbāʾ b. al-Ḥārith al-Kāhilī, whose father Ḥujr had killed,
struck the blow.** [SOURCED] ⁨الکامل ج۱ ص۴۶۴⁩. Then Banū Asad turned to the men of Qays and Kināna in
Ḥujr's own army:

> يَا مَعْشَرَ كِنَانَةَ وَقَيْسٍ أَنْتُمْ إِخْوَانُنَا وَبَنُو عَمِّنَا، وَالرَّجُلُ بَعِيدُ النَّسَبِ مِنَّا وَمِنْكُمْ، وَقَدْ رَأَيْتُمْ سِيرَتَهُ وَمَا كَانَ يَصْنَعُ بِكُمْ هُوَ وَقَوْمُهُ
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۱ ص۴۶۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/460
> *English:* "Men of Kināna and Qays — you are our brothers and our cousins. This man is far from us
> and from you in lineage, and you have seen how he conducted himself, and what he and his people used
> to do to you."

Qays and Kināna plundered his baggage. ⁨الکامل⁩ gives a second version introduced with «⁨وَقِيلَ⁩» —
Ḥujr withdrew, returned with a great army, and Banū Asad resolved to fight him — which continues onto
a page not fetched (§8). **Tell the first version, and say "the books give more than one account."**

⚠ The same page carries a soothsayer's rhymed prophecy of Ḥujr's death. **Do not use it.**

## 1.6 Imruʾ al-Qays — one card at most

⁨الکامل⁩'s chapter is titled «⁨ذِكْرُ مَقْتَلِ حُجْرٍ أَبِي امْرِئِ الْقَيْسِ⁩ …» — Imruʾ al-Qays is Ḥujr's son
(⁨ج۱ ص۴۶۱⁩). Ibn Kathīr gives him an entry as the author of the foremost of the *Muʿallaqāt*:

> امرئ القيس بن حجر الكندي صاحب إحدَى المعلّقات … وهي أفخرهنّ وأشهرهن التي أولها: قِفا نبكِ مِن ذكرى حَبيبٍ ومنزل
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۲ ص۵۰۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/935
> *English:* "Imruʾ al-Qays b. Ḥujr al-Kindī, author of one of the *Muʿallaqāt* — the proudest and
> most famous of them — the one that begins: *Halt, you two, and let us weep for the memory of a
> beloved and a dwelling.*"

⚠ **The ḥadīth Ibn Kathīr prints under this entry is not usable.** The editor grades its chain
«⁨ضعيف جدًا⁩» and notes Ibn al-Jawzī placed it among the baseless reports (⁨ج۲ ص۵۰۷⁩ fn. 8). Do not quote
it, do not allude to it.

The one thing from Imruʾ al-Qays that belongs to *this* story is his lament for the forty-eight of his
house killed by al-Mundhir (§1.3), which ⁨الکامل⁩ quotes:

> فَلَوْ فِي يَوْمِ مَعْرَكَةٍ أُصِيبُوا ... وَلَكِنْ فِي دِيَارِ بَنِي مَرِينَا
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۱ ص۴۶۳⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/459
> *English:* "Had they fallen on a day of battle — but it was in the lands of Banū Marīnā."

**Not in our pages:** the famous saying attributed to him on hearing of his father's death, his
journey to Byzantium, and the manner of his death. None of it is on a fetched page; do not tell it
from memory.

## 1.7 Which king was "Ākil al-Murār"? — the books differ

| Book | Who bore the name |
|---|---|
| ⁨الکامل ج۱ ص۴۶۲⁩ | **Ḥujr b. ʿAmr** — "Ḥujr b. ʿAmr, Ākil al-Murār"; and "ʿAmr b. Ḥujr Ākil al-Murār" for his son |
| ⁨ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۳۲۷⁩ (framing) | **Ḥujr** — «⁨وأبوه حجر هو الّذي تسمّيه العرب آكل المرار⁩» |
| ⁨البدایہ ج۵ ص۵۶⁩ (the gloss in Ibn Isḥāq's report) | **al-Ḥārith b. ʿAmr** — «⁨وهو الحارث بن عمرو⁩» with a chain up to Kinda |

Different recorded views, both in approved books. **On a slide, say "the house of Ākil al-Murār" and
do not pin the name on one man.**

# 2. THE DELEGATION — "WE ARE THE SONS OF ĀKIL AL-MURĀR"

## 2.1 Ibn Isḥāq's report, in Ibn Kathīr

> فدخلوا على رسول الله ﷺ مسجدَه، قد رجَّلوا جُمَمَهم وتَكَحَّلوا، عليهم جُبَب الحِبَرَة (٢)، قد كفَّفُوها بالحرير
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۵ ص۵۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/2240
> *English:* "They came in to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in his mosque, their long hair combed and
> their eyes lined with kohl, wearing Yemeni striped cloaks edged with silk."

The Prophet ﷺ asked them, "Have you not become Muslims?" — "Yes." — "Then what is this silk doing on
your necks?" — and they tore it off and threw it down. Then al-Ashʿath spoke:

> ثم قال له الأشعثُ بن قيس: يا رسول الله! نحن بنو آكل المُرار، وأنت ابن آكل المُرار. قال: فتبسم رسول الله ﷺ
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۵ ص۵۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/2240
> *English:* "Then al-Ashʿath b. Qays said to him: Messenger of Allah — we are the sons of Ākil
> al-Murār, and you are the son of Ākil al-Murār. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ smiled."

The report explains why he said it: al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib ؓ and Rabīʿa b. al-Ḥārith ؓ, trading
far off among the Arabs, used to say "we are the sons of Ākil al-Murār" to be honoured there —

> ليَعِزَّا في تلك البلاد، لأنّ كِنْدة كانوا ملوكًا، فاعتقدَتْ كِنْدة أن قريشًا منهم
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۵ ص۵۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/2240
> *English:* "…to be held in honour in those lands, **because Kinda had been kings**; and so Kinda came
> to believe that Quraysh were of them."

— and the Prophet ﷺ answered:

> ثم قال رسول الله ﷺ لهم: "لا، نحن بنو النَّضْر بن كنانة، لا نَقْفُو أمَّنا، ولا ننتفي من أبينا".
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۵ ص۵۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/2240
> *English:* "No. We are the sons of al-Naḍr b. Kināna. We do not trace ourselves through our mother,
> and we do not disown our father."

al-Ashʿath's reply closes the report: «⁨والله يا معشر كندة لا أسمع رجلًا يقولها إلا ضربته ثمانين⁩» — "By
Allah, men of Kinda, I will not hear a man say it but I will give him eighty lashes."

**Grading.** Ibn Isḥāq's version is from al-Zuhrī without a connected chain. Ibn Kathīr then gives it
**connected**, from Aḥmad, through al-Ashʿath himself, with the reply «⁨نحن بنو النَّضْر بن كنانة، لا نقفو⁩
⁨أمَّنَا، ولا نَنْتَفي من أَبينا⁩» — the editor: «⁨وإسناده حسن⁩»; and from Ibn Mājah, no. 2612 —
«⁨وهو حديث حسن⁩» (⁨ج۵ ص۵۶⁩ fnn. 5, 8). ⚠ **The connected version does not carry the words "Ākil
al-Murār"** — al-Ashʿath says only «⁨إنا نَزْعُمْ أنَّكمْ منّا⁩», "we hold that you are from us". So:
**the exchange is ḥasan; the royal wording belongs to Ibn Isḥāq's version.** Say so if the royal
wording goes on a slide.

# 3. THE BRIDGE — WHAT THE BOOKS DO AND DO NOT CONNECT

## 3.1 "The four kings" in the ridda narrative

The Kinda chiefs who led the refusal in Ḥaḍramawt are called kings by Ibn al-Athīr:

> فَنَزَلَ جَمَدٌ مَحْجَرًا، وَمِخْوَصٌ مَحْجَرًا، وَمِشْرَحٌ مَحْجَرًا، وَأَبْضَعَةُ مَحْجَرًا، وَأُخْتُهُمُ الْعَمَرَّدَةُ مَحْجَرًا، وَهُمُ الْمُلُوكُ الْأَرْبَعَةُ رُؤَسَاءُ عَمْرٍو … وَنَزَلَتْ بَنُو الْحَارِثِ بْنِ مُعَاوِيَةَ مَحَاجِرَهَا، فَنَزَلَ الْأَشْعَثُ بْنُ قَيْسٍ مَحْجَرًا
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۳۱⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/922
> *English:* "Jamad took a stronghold, and Mikhwaṣ a stronghold, and Mishraḥ a stronghold, and
> Abḍaʿa a stronghold, and their sister al-ʿAmarrada a stronghold — these are the four kings, the
> chiefs of ʿAmr … And Banū al-Ḥārith b. Muʿāwiya took their strongholds, and al-Ashʿath b. Qays took
> a stronghold."

Two facts follow from that page and nothing more:

- **The "four kings" were of Banū ʿAmr b. Muʿāwiya; al-Ashʿath was of Banū al-Ḥārith b. Muʿāwiya** — a
  different branch of Kinda. [SOURCED] ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۲۳۱⁩.
- When al-Nujayr was opened, **"they brought down the kings who were in it and killed them"**, and
  al-Ashʿath was sent bound to Abū Bakr ؓ — «⁨فَاسْتَنْزَلُوا مَنْ فِيهِ مِنَ الْمُلُوكِ فَقَتَلُوهُمْ⁩» (⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۲۳۲⁩).
  The detail of al-Nujayr belongs to the sister note.

The editor of ⁨البدایہ⁩, in a footnote citing al-Fīrūzābādī's *al-Qāmūs*, adds that the four were sons
of Maʿdī Karib, **"came in the delegation with al-Ashʿath, became Muslims, then apostatised, and were
killed on the day of al-Nujayr"** (⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۱⁩ fn.). ⚠ **That is a lexicographer quoted in an
editor's footnote, not the historian.** It may be mentioned as such; it is not the authority for
putting the four kings in the delegation.

## 3.2 The cause the book actually gives

Ibn al-Athīr states the cause of Kinda's ridda in his own words, and it is **not** the kingship:

> وَكَانَ سَبَبُ رِدَّةِ كِنْدَةَ وَإِجَابَتِهِمُ الْأَسْوَدَ الْكَذَّابَ حَتَّى لَعَنَ النَّبِيُّ - صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ - الْمُلُوكَ الْأَرْبَعَةَ مِنْهُمْ - أَنَّهُمْ لَمَّا أَسْلَمُوا أَمَرَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ - صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ - أَنْ يُوضَعَ بَعْضُ صَدَقَةِ حَضْرَمَوْتَ فِي كِنْدَةَ، وَبَعْضُ صَدَقَةِ كِنْدَةَ فِي حَضْرَمَوْتَ
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۳۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/921
> *English:* "The cause of Kinda's apostasy and of their answering al-Aswad the liar — to the point that
> the Prophet ﷺ cursed the four kings among them — was that when they became Muslims the Messenger of
> Allah ﷺ ordered that part of the zakāt of Ḥaḍramawt be placed among Kinda, and part of Kinda's zakāt
> in Ḥaḍramawt…"

⚠ This passage is in ⁨الکامل⁩'s ridda section, which the standing caution in
`ridda-campaign-the-conduct-of-the-wars.md` flags as running heavily through ⁨سيف بن عمر⁩. Use the
fact that a zakāt arrangement is named as the cause; do not build a slide on the cursing clause.

## 3.3 What may be said to the room

**Side by side, with no arrow between them:**

1. Kinda had been kings over the northern tribes (§1).
2. When they came to the Prophet ﷺ they came in silk, and their leader claimed the royal lineage for
   both parties; the Prophet ﷺ declined it, and Kinda's own report says why the lineage was prized:
   *they had been kings* (§2).
3. In the ridda, Ibn al-Athīr calls the chiefs of one Kinda clan "the four kings" (§3.1).
4. The cause he gives for the ridda is a zakāt arrangement (§3.2).

**Do not say** that the memory of the kingdom caused or shaped Kinda's ridda. No source we hold says
it — not Ibn al-Athīr, not Ibn Kathīr, not al-Dhahabī, and not Ibn Khaldūn (his Kinda chapter and his
ridda pages ⁨ج۲ ص۴۹۴⁩–۵۰۶ were both checked for it). If the room draws the inference, the speaker lets
it stand and does not endorse it.

# 5. THE GRANDSON — ʿABD AL-RAḤMĀN B. MUḤAMMAD B. AL-ASHʿATH

⚠ **SPEAKER'S DISCRETION — this whole section.** A revolt against a ruler, with scholars on it: the
room (Tanzeem-e-Islami) will hear it with its own questions. Narrate what the books narrate; give
both books' framings together; adjudicate nothing; draw no line to the present.

## 5.1 The name

**ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Muḥammad b. al-Ashʿath b. Qays al-Kindī**, known as **Ibn al-Ashʿath**:

> الأَمِيْرُ، مُتَوَلِّي سِجِسْتَانَ، عَبْدُ الرَّحْمَنِ بنُ مُحَمَّدِ بنِ الأَشْعَثِ بنِ قَيْسٍ الكِنْدِيُّ.
> — ⁨سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۴ ص۱۸۳⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/3336
> *English:* "The commander, governor of Sijistān, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Muḥammad b. al-Ashʿath b. Qays
> al-Kindī."

**The speaker's "Mālik b. al-Ashʿath" is not a person in this story.** The man is ʿAbd al-Raḥmān,
al-Ashʿath's **grandson** through his son Muḥammad, whom al-Dhahabī calls «⁨مِنْ كِبَارِ الأُمَرَاءِ⁩
⁨وَأَشْرَافِهِمْ⁩» (⁨سیر ج۲ ص۴۲⁩). The only Mālik of this house on our pages is **Mālik b. al-Ḥārith**, one of the
forty-eight killed by al-Mundhir before Islam (§1.3). Do not confuse either with the later Abbasid
commander Muḥammad b. al-Ashʿath al-Khuzāʿī, a different man of a different tribe (Ibn Khaldūn's TOC).

al-Dhahabī joins grandfather and grandson in one passage:

> وَهُوَ وَالِدُ الأَمِيْرِ عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ بنِ مُحَمَّدِ بنِ الأَشْعَثِ الَّذِي خَرَجَ مَعَهُ النَّاسُ، وَعَمِلَ مَعَ الحَجَّاجِ تِلْكَ الحُرُوْبَ المَشْهُوْرَةَ الَّتِي لَمْ يُسْمَعْ بِمِثْلِهَا. بِحَيْثُ يُقَالُ: إِنَّهُ عَمِلَ مَعَهُ أَحَداً وَثَمَانِيْنَ مَصَافّاً (١) ، مُعْظَمُهَا عَلَى الحَجَّاجِ. ثُمَّ فِي الآخِرِ خُذِلَ ابْنُ الأَشْعَثِ، وَانْهَزَمَ، ثُمَّ ظَفِرُوا بِهِ، وَهَلَكَ.
> — ⁨سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۲ ص۴۲⁩–۴۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/2023
> *English:* "[Muḥammad] is the father of the commander ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Muḥammad b. al-Ashʿath, with
> whom the people went out, and who fought those famous wars with al-Ḥajjāj, the like of which had not
> been heard — so that it is said he fought eighty-one pitched battles with him, most of them against
> al-Ḥajjāj. Then at the last Ibn al-Ashʿath was abandoned, and was routed; then they took him, and he
> perished."

## 5.2 Two framings — give both, always together

**al-Dhahabī:**

> بَعَثَهُ الحَجَّاجُ عَلَى سِجِسْتَانَ، فَثَارَ هُنَاكَ، وَأَقْبَلَ فِي جَمْعٍ كَبِيْرٍ، وَقَامَ مَعَهُ عُلَمَاءُ وَصُلَحَاءُ للهِ -تَعَالَى- لِمَا انْتَهَكَ الحَجَّاجُ مِنْ إِمَاتَةِ وَقْتِ الصَّلاَةِ، وَلِجَوْرِهِ وَجَبَرُوْتِهِ.
> — ⁨سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۴ ص۱۸۳⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/3336
> *English:* "al-Ḥajjāj sent him over Sijistān; he rose there and came on with a great host, and there
> rose with him scholars and righteous men, for Allah the Exalted, because of al-Ḥajjāj's violation in
> letting the time of the prayer die, and because of his injustice and his tyranny."

**Ibn Kathīr:**

> وكان سبب هذه الفتنة أن ابن الأشعث كان الحجاج يبغضه وكان هو يفهم ذلك ويضمر له السوء وزوال الملك عنه
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۹ ص۱۷۸⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/4301
> *English:* "The cause of this *fitna* was that al-Ḥajjāj hated Ibn al-Ashʿath, and he knew it, and
> harboured ill towards him and [wished for] the end of his rule."

The first names the grievance of the men who joined; the second names the quarrel of the two
principals and calls the whole affair a *fitna*. **Neither book withdraws the other's point, and the
speaker does not choose between them.** al-Dhahabī's own closing word is «⁨خُذِلَ⁩» — *he was abandoned*
(⁨سیر ج۲ ص۴۳⁩).

## 5.3 The sequence

**The army and the order to push on.** Ibn al-Ashʿath commanded al-Ḥajjāj's army against Rutbīl —
"king of the Turks" in Ibn Kathīr's words — whose country the army was to enter from Sijistān; he took some of the country and proposed to his men to hold what
they had through the winter and advance the next year. al-Ḥajjāj wrote back with contempt, ordering
him to press on at once, and wrote again, and a third time. [SOURCED] ⁨البدایہ ج۹ ص۱۷۸⁩–۱۷۹. Ibn Khaldūn
adds that the third letter threatened to give the command to his brother Isḥāq (⁨ج۳ ص۶۱⁩ — framing,
attributed).

**The start date is disputed.** «⁨قال أبو مخنف: كان ابتداؤها في هذه السنة، وقال الواقدي: في سنة ثنتين⁩
⁨وثمانين⁩» — Ibn Kathīr places it in **81 AH**, following Ibn Jarīr. [SOURCED] ⁨البدایہ ج۹ ص۱۷۸⁩.

**The break — first the governor, then the caliph.** Ibn al-Ashʿath put the letters before the army;
it refused al-Ḥajjāj; they gave allegiance to Ibn al-Ashʿath in his place — at first without renouncing
ʿAbd al-Malik. He made terms with Rutbīl and marched on Iraq. Then:

> ووثبوا إلى عبد الرحمن بن محمد بن الأشعث فبايعوه عوضًا عن الحجاج، ولم يذكروا خلع عبد الملك بن مروان
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۹ ص۱۷۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/4302
> *English:* "They rushed to ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Muḥammad b. al-Ashʿath and gave him allegiance in place of
> al-Ḥajjāj — and they made no mention of renouncing ʿAbd al-Malik b. Marwān."

> فلما توسطوا الطريق قالوا: إن خَلْعَنا للحجاج خلع لابن مروان فخلعوهما وجددوا البيعة لابن الأشعث
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۹ ص۱۷۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/4302
> *English:* "When they were half-way along the road they said: our renouncing al-Ḥajjāj is a renouncing
> of Ibn Marwān — so they renounced them both and renewed the allegiance to Ibn al-Ashʿath."

Ibn Khaldūn records the same turn at Fārs (⁨ج۳ ص۶۱⁩ — corroboration, attributed).

⚠ **Not for the room:** the text of al-Ḥajjāj's third letter and his words about the family — which
include the line «⁨وجده الأشعث ارتد عن الإسلام⁩», "and his grandfather al-Ashʿath apostatised from Islam"
(⁨البدایہ ج۹ ص۱۷۹⁩) — and Ibn al-Ashʿath's reply about al-Ḥajjāj. Both are exchanges of insult; both sit
in a passage the editor marks as supplied from one manuscript («⁨زيادة من ط⁩»); and the insult also
names his father in connection with ʿUthmān ؓ and Muslim b. ʿAqīl, which this note does not open.
**It is recorded here only because it is the single place in our pages where the ridda and the
grandson are spoken of together — and it is spoken by his enemy, as abuse, not by a historian as an
explanation.**

**Kūfa, and Dayr al-Jamājim.** Kūfa went over to him. al-Ḥajjāj came up with the Syrian army and camped
at Dayr Qurra; Ibn al-Ashʿath camped at Dayr al-Jamājim, and the battle there was **in Shaʿbān 82 AH**.
[SOURCED] ⁨البدایہ ج۹ ص۱۸۶⁩. With him:

> وفيهم القراء من المصرَيْن وخلق من الصالحين
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۹ ص۱۸۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/4309
> *English:* "Among them were the Qurʾān-readers of the two garrison cities, and a great number of
> righteous men."

Ibn Kathīr gives the host as **a hundred thousand men on the stipend, with as many again of their
clients** (⁨ج۹ ص۱۸۶⁩) — a transmitted figure, to be said as such. Both sides entrenched; single combats
went on daily "for a long time".

**The offer.** ʿAbd al-Malik's counsellors put it to him plainly:

> إن كان أهل العراق يرضيهم منك أن تعزل عنهم الحجاج فهو أيسر من قتالهم وسفك دمائهم
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۹ ص۱۸۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/4309
> *English:* "If what will satisfy the people of Iraq is that you remove al-Ḥajjāj from them, that is
> easier than fighting them and shedding their blood."

He sent his brother Muḥammad b. Marwān and his son ʿAbdallāh with written terms: al-Ḥajjāj removed;
the Iraqis' stipends made equal to the Syrians'; Ibn al-Ashʿath to govern any city he chose for life;
Muḥammad b. Marwān over Iraq. al-Ḥajjāj wrote against it; ʿAbd al-Malik sent it anyway, "wanting
relief from war" (⁨ج۹ ص۱۸۷⁩). And the answer:

> فقام فيهم خطيبًا وندبهم الى قبول ما عرضوا عليهم من عزل الحجاج عنهم وبيعة عبد الملك بن مروان
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۹ ص۱۸۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/4310
> *English:* "[Ibn al-Ashʿath] stood and addressed them, and urged them to accept what had been offered
> them — the removal of al-Ḥajjāj and allegiance to ʿAbd al-Malik b. Marwān."

> فنفر الناس من كل جانب وقالوا: لا والله لا نقبل ذلك، نحن أكثر عَدَدًا وعُدَدًا
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۹ ص۱۸۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/4310
> *English:* "The people broke out from every side and said: No, by Allah, we will not accept it — we
> are more in number and in equipment."

They renewed the renunciation of ʿAbd al-Malik and his governor, and the two Umayyad envoys handed the
war back to al-Ḥajjāj.

**The end.** al-Dhahabī: the war lasted months, many were killed on both sides, and at the last Ibn
al-Ashʿath's host was routed and he fled to Rutbīl:

> وَفِي آخِرِ الأَمْرِ انْهَزَمَ جَمْعُ ابْنِ الأَشْعَثِ، وَفَرَّ هُوَ إِلَى المَلِكِ رُتْبِيْلَ مُلْتَجِئاً إِلَيْهِ.
> — ⁨سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۴ ص۱۸۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/3337
> *English:* "At the end of the affair Ibn al-Ashʿath's host was routed, and he fled to King Rutbīl,
> seeking refuge with him."

al-Dhahabī then gives **more than one account** of his death, each introduced as reported: Rutbīl,
pressed by al-Ḥajjāj's letters and offered seven years' remission, handed him over with thirty of his
family in fetters; **«⁨وَقِيْلَ⁩»** he died of consumption and his head was sent on; and on the road towards
Iraq he fell from a ruined palace where they had lodged him, and died — **"in the year 84"**.
[SOURCED] ⁨سیر ج۴ ص۱۸۴⁩. **Give the year and "the books differ on how he died"; do not narrate the manner
on stage.**

One more thing on that page is worth keeping for a later evening: a band of five hundred of his men
refused to go to Rutbīl, held a town until they were granted *amān* by al-Ḥajjāj's commander ʿUmāra
b. Tamīm — **"and he kept faith with them"** («⁨وَوَفَى لَهُم⁩», ⁨سیر ج۴ ص۱۸۴⁩).

# 6. HOW TO NARRATE §5 IN THIS ROOM

1. **Two sentences, both said:** al-Dhahabī records that scholars and righteous men rose with him
   because of al-Ḥajjāj's injustice and the neglect of the prayer's time; Ibn Kathīr records the same
   events as a *fitna* rooted in the two men's quarrel.
2. **The book's own facts carry the weight:** the escalation from the governor to the caliph on the
   road; the offer that would have removed al-Ḥajjāj; its refusal by the army against the counsel of
   its own commander; the rout.
3. **No verdict** on whether the rising was right, on the scholars who joined, or on al-Ḥajjāj beyond
   what the quoted pages say. **No line to the present.**
4. If a slip asks "were the scholars right to revolt?": *"The books record scholars who went out with
   him, and they record how it ended, and the great scholars wrote about it at length. I am reading a
   book, not issuing a ruling — that question belongs to the ʿulamāʾ."*

# 7. WHAT COULD NOT BE ESTABLISHED

- **Any source saying the old kingship caused or shaped Kinda's ridda.** None of ⁨الکامل⁩, ⁨البدایہ⁩,
  ⁨سیر⁩ or ⁨ابن خلدون⁩ (Kinda chapter ⁨ج۲ ص۳۲۷⁩; ridda pages ⁨ج۲ ص۴۹۴⁩–۵۰۶) says it. ⁨الکامل⁩ gives a zakāt
  arrangement as the cause (⁨ج۲ ص۲۳۰⁩). The only textual links are (a) the delegation report's gloss
  "Kinda had been kings", (b) ⁨الکامل⁩'s calling the ridda chiefs "kings", and (c) al-Ḥajjāj's insult
  naming the grandfather's apostasy. None of these is a causal claim.
- **Whether al-Ashʿath descended from Ākil al-Murār in the direct line.** The two printed chains — ⁨البدایہ⁩
  ⁨ج۵ ص۵۶⁩ for Ākil al-Murār and ⁨سیر ج۲ ص۳۷⁩ for al-Ashʿath — both run back through a Muʿāwiya b. al-Ḥārith
  b. Muʿāwiya b. Thawr, but they are not written to be compared and the names do not align cleanly.
  **Do not say he was Ākil al-Murār's descendant, and do not say he was not.** Say "of the royal house
  of Kinda" only if a page is found that says so; until then, "a chief of Kinda".
- **The year of the Kinda delegation.** No fetched page gives it; `[STANDARD]` 10 AH (to verify).
- **Any Common-Era date for Ḥujr Ākil al-Murār, al-Ḥārith, Ḥujr's killing or Imruʾ al-Qays.** Our
  sources give only "about 130 years between Qubādh's reign and the Hijra" (⁨الکامل ج۱ ص۴۶۱⁩). Everything
  else is `[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]` and must be labelled on the slide.
- **The name "the Army of the Peacocks"** (*jaysh al-ṭawāwīs*) for the army sent to Sijistān. It is on
  **Ibn Khaldūn's page only** («⁨وكان يسمى جيش الطواويس لحسن زيهم⁩», ⁨ج۳ ص۶۱⁩). Under `DECISIONS.md` #29 it
  cannot be the sole authority for a name. **Not for a slide** until a safe-list page carries it — the
  likely place is ⁨البدایہ⁩'s year 80 (index 4296–4297), not fetched.
- **Named scholars who joined Ibn al-Ashʿath, and named scholars who counselled against the revolt.**
  Neither list is on a fetched page. Do not name any from memory.
- **Ibn Kathīr's own date for Ibn al-Ashʿath's death**, and ⁨الکامل⁩'s account of the whole revolt
  (years 81–84, indices 1924–1955) — not fetched. The death year here is al-Dhahabī's, attached to one
  of his versions.
- **The outcome of the battle at Dayr al-Jamājim in Ibn Kathīr's words.** ⁨البدایہ ج۹ ص۱۸۷⁩ stops as the
  lines are drawn up; the rout is here from ⁨سیر ج۴ ص۱۸۴⁩ and ⁨سیر ج۲ ص۴۳⁩.
- **The rest of Imruʾ al-Qays's story** (§1.6) and the second version of Ḥujr's death, which runs onto
  ⁨الکامل⁩ index 461, not fetched.

### E-KD01 · A Yemeni house made kings over the northern tribes
**Tier:** GOOD · ≈1.5 min · **When:** before Islam, not dated in our sources; `[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]`
late 5th century CE — label it on the slide · **Map:** Arabia with Yemen shaded. An arrow from Yemen
north into Najd, ending at **Baṭn ʿĀqil**, which takes a plain place marker. The tribal names
Bakr, Asad, Ghaṭafān, Taghlib appear in the north.
**What happened:** Kinda was a tribe of the south, of Qaḥṭān. The books of the Arabs' past report that
among the northern tribe of Bakr the reckless men had overpowered the wise, and the strong were eating
the weak. The wise men wanted a king who would take the weak man's due from the strong — but not one of
their own, whom half would obey and half defy. So they went to a Tubbaʿ of Yemen, and he gave them a
man of Kinda: Ḥujr b. ʿAmr, remembered as Ākil al-Murār. He settled at Baṭn ʿĀqil, took back Bakr's
lands from the Lakhmids of al-Ḥīra, and was buried there. Ibn Khaldūn, following an older genealogist,
tells it differently: a Ḥimyarite king set Ḥujr over all of Maʿadd.
**Beats:**
1. Kinda was a southern tribe — Of Qaḥṭān, not of the northern Arabs it came to rule.
2. The north had a problem — Among Bakr the reckless had overpowered the wise; the strong ate the weak.
3. They wanted a king from outside — Not one of their own, whom half would obey and half defy.
4. So they went to a Tubbaʿ of Yemen — And he gave them a man of Kinda: Ḥujr b. ʿAmr, Ākil al-Murār.
5. What he did with it — He settled at Baṭn ʿĀqil and took back Bakr's lands from al-Ḥīra.
6. Ibn Khaldūn tells it differently — A Ḥimyarite king set Ḥujr over all of Maʿadd. Say both; resolve neither.
**Quote after beat:** 4
**The statement:**
> فَرَأَوْا أَنْ يُمَلِّكُوا عَلَيْهِمْ مَلِكًا يَأْخُذُ لِلضَّعِيفِ مِنَ الْقَوِيِّ
> — ⁨الکامل فی التاریخ ج۱ ص۴۶۲⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/458
> *English:* "They decided to set a king over themselves who would take the weak man's due from the

**⁨عبرت⁩:** A people that could not keep the strong from the weak went looking for a ruler from outside.
**Hands-up?** *"If your own tribe cannot agree on a chief, where do you look?"*

### E-KD07 · al-Ashʿath ؓ after the pardon — "and I did"
**Tier:** GOOD · ≈1.5 min · **When:** 11–40 AH; died 40 AH `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** a single line of
points for his later life: **al-Qādisiyya** → **al-Yarmūk** → **Ādharbayjān** → **Kūfa**. No battle markers.
**What happened:** The man brought to Medina in fetters lived nearly thirty more years as a Muslim. Abū
Bakr ؓ married him to his own sister. He was among the notables sent to call Rustam before
al-Qādisiyya; he lost an eye at al-Yarmūk; ʿUthmān ؓ made him governor of Ādharbayjān; he was the
senior commander with ʿAlī ؓ at Ṣiffīn; and he died at Kūfa in 40 AH, and al-Ḥasan ؓ prayed over him.
At a funeral where he and Jarīr b. ʿAbdallāh ؓ were both present, he

### E-KD08 · The grandson in Sijistān — from the governor to the caliph
**Tier:** GOOD · ⚠ SPEAKER'S DISCRETION · ≈2 min · **When:** `[SOURCED, disputed]` 81 AH (Abū Mikhnaf) or 82 AH
(al-Wāqidī) · **Map:** a far-eastern inset: **Sijistān** and Rutbīl's country beyond it. A long
arrow west from Sijistān through **Fārs** towards **al-Baṣra** and **Kūfa**. A break-mark on the arrow at
Fārs.
**What happened:** Seventy years after the ridda, al-Ashʿath's grandson ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Muḥammad commanded
al-Ḥajjāj's army against Rutbīl, beyond Sijistān. He took ground, and proposed to hold it through the
winter. al-Ḥajjāj wrote back with contempt ordering him forward, then again, then a third time. He put
the letters before the army, and the army refused al-Ḥajjāj and gave its allegiance to him instead — at
first saying nothing of the caliph ʿAbd al-Malik. Half-way to Iraq they said: renouncing the governor
is renouncing the caliph, and renounced both. al-Dhahabī records that scholars and righteous men rose
with him because of al-Ḥajjāj's injustice and his letting the prayer's time die. Ibn Kathīr records
it as a fitna whose cause was the hatred between the two men. Both are given; neither is chosen.
**The statement:**
> فلما توسطوا الطريق قالوا: إن خَلْعَنا للحجاج خلع لابن مروان فخلعوهما
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۹ ص۱۷۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/4302
> *English:* "When they were half-way along the road they said: our renouncing al-Ḥajjāj is a
> renouncing of Ibn Marwān — so they renounced them both."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** A refusal of one man's order became, on the road, a refusal of the state.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-KD09 · Dayr al-Jamājim — the offer refused, and the end
**Tier:** GOOD · ⚠ SPEAKER'S DISCRETION · ≈2 min · **When:** Dayr al-Jamājim, Shaʿbān 82 AH `[SOURCED]`;
Ibn al-Ashʿath's death 84 AH `[SOURCED]` (al-Dhahabī, one of several accounts) · **Map:** two camps close
together near **Kūfa**: **Dayr Qurra** (al-Ḥajjāj, blue from Syria) and **Dayr al-Jamājim** (Ibn al-Ashʿath).
No distance labelled. After the rout, one thin arrow back east to **Rutbīl's country**.
**What happened:** Kūfa went over to him, and his host camped at Dayr al-Jamājim — among them, Ibn
Kathīr says, the Qurʾān-readers of both garrison cities and many righteous men. The two armies
entrenched and fought daily for a long time. ʿAbd al-Malik's counsellors told him that removing
al-Ḥajjāj would be easier than shedding the Iraqis' blood, and he sent his brother and his son with
terms: al-Ḥajjāj removed, Iraqi stipends equal to Syria's, a city for Ibn al-Ashʿath for life.
Ibn al-Ashʿath stood and urged his men to accept. They refused: we are more in number. The war went
back to al-Ḥajjāj. After months the host was routed; Ibn al-Ashʿath fled to King Rutbīl, and in 84 AH
he died — the books give more than one account of how. al-Dhahabī's word for his end is that he was abandoned.
**The statement:**
> فقام فيهم خطيبًا وندبهم الى قبول ما عرضوا عليهم من عزل الحجاج عنهم وبيعة عبد الملك بن مروان
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۹ ص۱۸۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/4310
> *English:* "He stood and addressed them, and urged them to accept what had been offered them — the
> removal of al-Ḥajjāj and allegiance to ʿAbd al-Malik b. Marwān."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** The terms that would have ended it were on the table, and it was the army, not its commander,
that refused them.
