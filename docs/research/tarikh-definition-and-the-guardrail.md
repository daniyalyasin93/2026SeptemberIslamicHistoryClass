## 1. The question this note answers

The lecture's first five minutes have to do three things at once, and this strand supplies all
three from the course's own two books:

1. Say what ⁨تاریخ⁩ **is** — and say it in a way that is not a dictionary definition read aloud.
2. Land the single idea the whole series rests on: **⁨تاریخ⁩'s distinguishing question is
   "when, and in what order".** Not "what happened" — every discipline reports what happened.
   *When.*
3. Install the guardrail — the fiqh ruling on reading the ⁨مشاجرات⁩, and the principle
   ⁨تاریخِ اسلام⁩ = ⁨تاریخِ مسلمین⁩ — **before** anything sensitive is narrated.

## 2. THE DEFINITION

### 2.1 لغوی — الإعلام بالوقت

Both books say the same thing, and the Arabic source says it in three words.

> فَالتَّارِيخُ فِي اللُّغَةِ: الإِعْلَامُ بِالْوَقْتِ.
> يُقَالُ: أَرَّخْتُ الْكِتَابَ وَوَرَّخْتُهُ (بِمَعْنًى) أَي: بَيَّنْتُ وَقْتَ كِتَابَتِهِ.
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٨٩** (باب: تعريف التاريخ)

*(working translation — ours: "⁨تاریخ⁩, in the language, is: **the announcing of the time.** One says
'I dated the letter' — that is, I made plain the time of its writing.")*

Same page, ⁨السخاوي⁩ quotes ⁨الجوهري رحمہ اللہ⁩:

> قَالَ الْجَوْهَرِيُّ: "التَّارِيخُ: تَعْرِيفُ الْوَقْتِ. وَالتَّورِيخُ مِثْلُهُ، يُقَالُ: أَرَّخْتُ وَوَرَّخْتُ ..."
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٨٩**

And the tribal split of the two forms — a nice, harmless detail if a light touch is wanted:

> قَالَ الأَصْمَعِيُّ: "بَنُو تَمِيمٍ يَقُولُونَ: وَرَّخْتُ الْكِتَابَ تَوْرِيخًا. وَقَيْسٌ تَقُولُ: أَرَّخْتُهُ تَأْرِيخًا"
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٨٩**

**The Urdu book carries exactly this** at **muqaddima p.۳۲** under the heading «⁨تاریخ، لغت میں⁩»:
it gives «⁨الاعلام بالوقت⁩» and the same ⁨بنو تمیم⁩ / ⁨قیس⁩ split. So the Arabic above is not an import —
it is the source of the sentence the course's own book already prints.

### 2.2 ⁨اصطلاحی⁩ — ⁨السخاوي⁩'s own definition

This is the one that should go on the slide. ⁨السخاوي⁩ gives it in two movements: first the full
technical definition, then a one-line summary.

**The full definition:**

> وَفِي الإِصْطِلاحِ: التَّعْرِيفُ بِالْوَقْتِ الَّذِي تُضْبَطُ بهِ الأَحْوَالُ؛ مِنْ مَوْلِدِ الرُّوَاةِ وَالْأَئِمَّةِ، وَوَفَاةٍ، وَصِحَّةٍ، وَعَقْلٍ، وَبَدَنٍ، وَرِحْلَةٍ، وَحَجٍّ، وَحِفْظٍ، وَضَبْطٍ، وَتَوْثِيقٍ، وتَجرِيحٍ ومَا أَشْبَهَ هَذَا مِمَّا مَرْجِعُهُ الفَحْصُ عَنْ أَحْوَالِهِمْ في ابتِدَائِهِمْ وَحَالِهِمْ وَاسْتِقْبَالِهِمْ.
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩١**

*(working translation — ours: "And in the technical usage: **the fixing of the time by which
circumstances are pinned down** — the birth of the transmitters and the imams, and death, and
health, and mind, and body, and travel, and Hajj, and memory, and precision, and being declared
reliable or unreliable, and whatever resembles this, all of which comes back to examining their
condition at their beginning, their middle and their end.")*

He then widens it to public events, and the list is worth having in full because it is what a
history course actually covers:

> وَيَلْتَحِقُ بِهِ مَا يَتَّفِقُ مِنَ الْحَوَادِثِ وَالْوَقَائِعِ الْجَلِيلَةِ، مِنْ ظُهُورِ مِلَّةٍ، وَتَجْدِيدِ فَرْضٍ، وَخَلِيفَةٍ، وَوَزِيرٍ، وَغَزْوَةٍ، وَمَلْحَمَةٍ، وَحَرْبٍ، وَفَتْحِ بَلَدٍ، وَانْتِزَاعِهِ مِنْ مُتَغَلِّبٍ عَلَيْهِ، وَانْتِقَالِ دَوْلَةٍ.
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩١**

*(working translation — ours: "And joined to it is whatever occurs of great events and
happenings: the appearance of a religion, the renewal of an obligation, a caliph, a vizier, a
campaign, a great battle, a war, the conquest of a city and its recovery from one who had seized
it, and the passing of a state from one hand to another.")*

**The one-line summary — THIS IS THE SLIDE LINE:**

> وَالْحَاصِلُ أنَّهُ فَنٌّ يُبْحَثُ فِيهِ عَنْ وَقَائِعِ الزَّمَانِ مِنْ حَيْثِيَّةِ التَّعْيِينِ وَالتَّوْقِيتِ، بَلْ عَمَّا كَانَ فِي الْعَالَمِ.
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩٢**

*(working translation — ours: "The upshot is that it is **a discipline in which the events of time
are investigated from the standpoint of fixing them and dating them** — indeed, of whatever has
been in the world.")*

⚠ **A discrepancy you must know about.** The Urdu muqaddima, at **p.۳۳**, attributes to
⁨السخاوي⁩ a *shortened* form: `⁨الْبَحْث عَن وَقَائِعِ الزَّمَانِ بِالتوقيت⁩`, and glosses it
«⁨وقت کی ترتیب و تعیین کے ساتھ زمانے کے واقعات کی جستجو کرنا⁩». That is a faithful **summary** of
⁨ص٩٢⁩ above, but it is **not** what ⁨السخاوي⁩'s printed text says. **Put ⁨ص٩٢⁩'s wording on the slide,
not the Urdu book's compressed form.** The Urdu book's own footnote for it reads «⁨الاعلان⁩
⁨بالتوبيخ، ص ١٧⁩» — a different edition; do not print that page number.

### 2.3 موضوع — الإنسان والزمان

> وَأَمَّا مَوْضُوعُهُ فَالْإِنْسَانُ وَالزَّمَانُ.
> وَمَسَائِلُهُ: أَحْوَالُهُمَا الْمُفَصِّلَةُ لِلجُزْئِيَّاتِ، تَحْتَ دَائِرَةِ الْأَحْوَالِ الْعَارِضَةِ الْمَوْجُودَةِ لِلْإِنْسَانِ وَفِي الزَّمَانِ.
> وَأَمَّا فَائِدَتُهُ فَمَعْرِفَةُ الْأُمُورِ عَلَى وَجْهِهَا.
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩٣** (باب: موضوع التاريخ وفائدته)

*(working translation — ours: "As for its subject-matter: **man and time.** Its questions are the
states of the two, set out in their particulars… And as for its benefit: **knowing matters as they
actually were.**")*

**Two words. ⁨انسان اور زمانہ⁩.** This is the most projectable line in the whole strand, and the
Urdu muqaddima prints it too, at **p.۳۳**, as «⁨مَوْضُوعُهُ الْإِنْسَانُ وَالزَّمَان⁩» / «⁨تاریخ کا⁩
⁨موضوع انسان اور زمانہ ہے⁩» — so using it is staying inside the course's own frame.

⁨السخاوي⁩ uses the same pairing in his opening ⁨خطبة⁩, which is a nicer, warmer form of the same idea:

> وَمُفَهِّمِ الأَلِبَّاءِ فِي التَّعْرِيفِ بِالإِنْسَانِ وَالزَّمَانِ الطَّرِيقَ المُسْنَدَ المُدْرَجَ فِي العَوَالِي
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٨٦** (مقدمة المصنف)

### 2.4 ⁨غایت⁩ — what it is finally for

> وَأَمَّا غَايَتُهُ فَالتَّرَجِّي لِرِضَا اللهِ؛ فَإِنَّهُ لَا يُضِيعُ أَجْرَ مَنْ أَحْسَنَ عَمَلًا، وَالأَعْمَالُ بِالنِّيَّاتِ.
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص١٧٨**

*(working translation — ours: "As for its goal: hoping for the pleasure of Allah — for He does not
waste the reward of one who does good, and deeds are by intentions.")*

## 3. THE CORE POINT — ⁨تاریخ⁩ is the ordering of events in TIME

### 3.1 The muqaddima's own paragraph — the cleanest statement we hold

**muqaddima p.۳۳**, under the heading «⁨تاریخ اور دوسرے فنون میں فرق⁩», says in substance
*(paraphrased from the Urdu — the OCR is not quotable):*

> Events are related in works of ⁨ادب⁩ too, and a great many events occur in the ⁨احادیث⁩ — yet
> nobody calls those books of history. **Because in ⁨حدیث⁩ and in ⁨ادب⁩ the events do not come in the
> order of time.** In most places the month, even the year, of the event is not stated at all.
> - In **⁨ادب⁩** the aim is that an event be set before the reader so that he take a lesson from it.
> - In **⁨حدیث⁩** events are gathered according to ⁨فقہی احکام⁩ and ⁨نکات⁩, the standing of the ⁨سند⁩, or
>   the names of the ⁨رواۃ⁩ — each compiler of a ⁨حدیث⁩ collection having his own objectives.
> - **⁨تاریخ⁩ is a separate discipline.** In it, attention is given to gathering and presenting
>   events *in such an order* that what happened first comes first and what happened later comes
>   later. Most histories run: the events of ⁨محرم⁩ of year 1, then ⁨صفر⁩, then ⁨ربیع الاول⁩; the whole
>   year finished, the events of year 2 are begun — and so the ⁨مؤرخ⁩ goes on to his own day.
>
> — **⁨تاریخِ امت ج۱، مقدمہ،⁩ p.۳۳**

**This single paragraph is the lecture's opening.** It is the course's own book saying that what
separates history from every neighbouring discipline is *sequence in time*.

### 3.2 ⁨السخاوي⁩ says the same thing when he separates ⁨تاریخ⁩ from ⁨طبقات⁩

The Arabic backing for §3.1, and it is sharper:

> قُلْتُ: بَيْنَهُمَا عُمُومٌ وَخُصُوصٌ وَجْهِيٌّ، فَيَجْتَمِعَانِ فِي التَّعْرِيفِ بِالرُّوَاةِ، وَيَنْفَرِدُ التَّارِيخُ بِالحَوَادِثِ ...
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص١٧٧** (باب: الفرق بين التاريخ والطبقات)

> وَقَدْ فَرَّقَ بَيْنَهُمَا بَعْضُ الْمُتَأَخِّرِينَ بِأَنَّ التَّارِيخَ يُنْظَرُ فِيهِ بِالذَّاتِ إِلَى الْمَوَالِيدِ وَالْوَفَيَاتِ، وَبِالْعَرَضِ إِلَى الأَحْوَالِ، وَالطَّبَقَاتِ يُنْظَرُ فِيهَا بِالذَّاتِ إِلَى الْأَحْوَالِ، وَبِالْعَرَضِ إِلَى الْمَوَالِيدِ وَالْوَفَيَاتِ. وَلَكِنَّ الأَوَّلَ أَشْبَهُ.
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص١٧٨**

*(working translation — ours: "Some of the later scholars distinguished the two thus: **in ⁨تاریخ⁩
one looks essentially at births and deaths, and only incidentally at circumstances**; in ⁨طبقات⁩ one
looks essentially at circumstances and only incidentally at births and deaths. And the first is the
apter.")*

> **Slide idea (English-first, per §1.3 of CLAUDE.md):** two columns, *⁨حدیث⁩ / ⁨تاریخ⁩*, one row each
> for "what it collects" and "how it orders". Then the one-line Arabic from ⁨ص٩٢⁩ underneath.

### 3.3 The four sayings — verbatim, WITH the corrections

The Urdu muqaddima gathers three of these at **p.۵۷** under «⁨تاریخ کے ذریعے جھوٹے راویوں کی پکڑ⁩»,
and the fourth at **p.۵۳**. **All three of the first group have been re-verified against the Arabic
and all three differ from the Urdu book's rendering.** Use the Arabic column.

#### (a) ⁨سفيان الثوري رحمہ اللہ⁩ — the headline quotation

> قَالَ سُفْيَانُ الثَّوْرِيُّ:
> "لَمَّا اسْتَعْمَلَ الرّوَاةُ الْكَذِبَ، اسْتَعْمَلْنَا لَهُمُ التَّارِيخَ".
>
> — **⁨الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩٧⁩** · edition's takhrīj: ⁨أخرجه الخطيب في⁩ "⁨الكفاية⁩" ⁨ص ١٩٣⁩
> — also at **⁨تاریخِ امت ج۱، مقدمہ،⁩ p.۵۷**

*(working translation — ours: "When the transmitters started using lies against us, **we used
chronology against them.**")*

⚠ **Wording:** ⁨السخاوي⁩ prints `⁨اسْتَعْمَلْنَا لَهُمُ التَّارِيخَ⁩`. The Urdu book prints
`⁨اِسْتَعْمَلْنَاهُم التاريخ⁩`. **Set the ⁨ص٩٧⁩ form.**

#### (b) ⁨حفص بن غياث القاضي رحمہ اللہ⁩ — and a correction that matters

> وَعَنْ حَفْصِ بْنِ غِيَاثٍ الْقَاضِي قَالَ:
> "إِذَا اتَّهَمْتُمُ الشَّيْخَ فَحَاسِبُوهُ بِالسِّنَّيْنَ".
> بِفَتْحِ النُّونِ الْمُشَدَّدَةِ، تَثْنِيةُ "سِنٍّ" وَهُوَ الْعُمْرُ. يُرِيدُ: احْسِبُوا سِنَّهُ وَسِنَّ مَنْ كَتَبَ عَنْهُ.
>
> — **⁨الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩٧⁩** · edition's takhrīj: ⁨أخرجه الخطيب في⁩ "⁨الكفاية⁩" ⁨ص ١٩٣، ومن طريقه ابن⁩
> عساكر في "تاريخه" ١/ ٥٤؛ وقال الجديع: "وإسناده لا بأس به"
> — also at **⁨تاریخِ امت ج۱، مقدمہ،⁩ p.۵۷**

*(working translation — ours: "When you suspect the shaykh, **reckon him by the two ages.**" And
⁨السخاوي⁩ explains it himself: with a fatha on the doubled nūn — it is the **dual of ⁨سِنّ⁩, meaning
age**. He means: **compute his age and the age of the man he claims to have written from.**)*

⚠ **This is the most valuable correction in the note.** The Urdu book prints
`⁨إذا اتَّهَمُتُم فَحَاسِبُوهُ بِالسِّنِين⁩` and renders it loosely as "check him by means of
⁨تاریخ⁩". ⁨السخاوي⁩'s printed text has **⁨الشَّيْخَ⁩** in it and reads **⁨بِالسِّنَّيْنَ⁩** — *the two
ages*, not *the years* — **and he vowels and glosses the word himself on the same page** precisely
so that it is not misread. Set the ⁨ص٩٧⁩ form, and use ⁨السخاوي⁩'s own gloss as the slide's
translation. It is also far more concrete for the audience: *do the arithmetic on two birthdates.*

#### (c) «⁨لم يُسْتَعَنْ على الكذابين بمثل التاريخ⁩» — and who said it

> وعن حَسَّانِ بْنِ زَيْدٍ قَالَ:
> "لم يُسْتَعَنْ عَلَى الْكَذَّابِينَ بِمِثْلِ التَّارِيخِ، يُقَالُ لِلشَّيْخِ: سَنَةَ كَمْ وُلِدْتَ؟ فَإِذَا أَقَرَّ بِمَوْلِدِهِ، مَعَ مَعْرِفَتِنَا بِوَفَاةِ الَّذِي انْتَمَى إِلَيْهِ، عَرَفْنَا صِدْقَهُ مِنْ كِذْبِهِ".
>
> — **⁨الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩٧⁩** · edition's takhrīj: ⁨أخرجه ابن عساكر في⁩ "⁨تاريخه⁩" ١/ ⁨٥٥، وقال⁩:
> "... **وأظنه حماد بن زيد** ..."
> — also at **⁨تاریخِ امت ج۱، مقدمہ،⁩ p.۵۷**, where it is attributed to **⁨حماد بن زید رحمہ اللہ⁩**

*(working translation — ours: "Nothing has ever been of such help against the liars as chronology.
The shaykh is asked: **in what year were you born?** Then, once he has admitted his birth-year,
and we know the death-date of the man he has claimed to transmit from, **we know his truth from his
lying.**")*

⚠ **Attribution.** ⁨ط الظفيري⁩ prints **⁨حَسَّان بن زيد⁩**; the editor notes that ⁨ابن عساكر⁩, who
records it, said "and I think it is **⁨حماد⁩** ⁨بن زيد⁩". The Urdu book follows the ⁨حماد⁩ reading.
**Recommendation for the deck:** either give the saying **without the name**, or write
«⁨رُوِي عن حسان بن زيد⁩ — ⁨وقيل⁩: ⁨حماد بن زيد⁩». Do not print a bare "⁨حماد بن زید رحمہ اللہ⁩" as
certain. The saying itself is not in doubt; only whose it is.

#### (d) «⁨ما أشبه الليلة بالبارحة⁩» — ⚠ NOT ESTABLISHED IN ARABIC

The muqaddima uses this at **p.۵۳**, in its list of the ⁨فوائد⁩ of history, as a **⁨کہاوت⁩** (a
proverb) that a historian repeats to keep his nerve when a new situation arises — "this night is
much like last night" — glossing the European "history repeats itself".

**What is established:** the muqaddima quotes such a proverb at **p.۵۳**. That page citation is solid.

**What is NOT established:** the exact Arabic wording. The Urdu OCR at that point reads
`⁨مالشبة الليل البَارِحَة⁩`, which is damaged and unusable. And **the phrase does not occur anywhere
in ⁨الإعلان بالتوبيخ⁩ pp.٨٥–٢٦٩**, all of which were fetched and grepped for `⁨بارح⁩` / `⁨بَارِح⁩`.

**Therefore:** do **not** set this one in Arabic on a slide from this note. Either (a) eyeball

### 3.4 The worked examples — chronology catching a liar, three times

These are in ⁨السخاوي⁩'s chapter **⁨أغاليط المؤرخين⁩** and they are better slide material than any
maxim, because the audience can do the subtraction in their heads.

**(i) The man who heard from a dead teacher.**

> وَسَأَلَ إِسْمَاعِيلُ بْنُ عَيَّاشٍ رَجُلًا اخْتِبَارًا: "أَيَّ سَنَةٍ كَتَبْتَ عَنْ خَالِدِ بْنِ مَعْدَانَ؟ فَقَالَ: سَنَةَ ثَلَاثَ عَشْرَةَ وَمِائَةٍ. فَقَالَ: أَنْتَ تَزْعُمُ أَنَّكَ سَمِعْتَ مِنْهُ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهِ بِسَبْعِ سِنِينَ! "
>
> — **⁨الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩٧⁩** · edition's takhrīj: ⁨أخرجه ابن حبان في⁩ "⁨المجروحين⁩" ١/ ⁨٧١؛ وقال⁩
> الجديع: "إسناده جيد"

*(working translation — ours: "⁨إسماعيل بن عياش⁩ asked a man, testing him: 'In what year did you
write from ⁨خالد بن معدان⁩?' He said: 'The year 113.' He said: **'Then you are claiming you heard
from him seven years after he died.'**")*

**(ii) The man who met ⁨أم المؤمنين عائشہ ؓ⁩ in a city that did not yet exist.**

> "وَرَوَى سُهَيْلُ بْنُ ذَكْوَانَ أَبُو السِّنْدِيِّ عَنْ عَائِشَةَ، وَزَعَمَ أَنَّهُ لَقِيَهَا بوَاسِطٍ! وَهَكَذَا يَكُونُ الْكَذِبُ؛ فَمَوْتُ عَائِشَةَ كَانَ قَبْلَ أَنْ يَخُطَّ الْحَجَّاجُ مَدِينَةَ وَاسِطٍ بِدَهْرٍ".
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩٨**

*(working translation — ours: "…and he claimed he met her at ⁨واسط⁩! **And this is what lying looks
like** — for ⁨عائشہ ؓ⁩ died a long age before ⁨الحجاج⁩ ever laid out the city of ⁨واسط⁩.")*

**(iii) The man who held the stirrup of someone who died before he was born.**

> " (إِنَّهُ) غَلَطٌ فَاحِشٌ؛ لَأَنَّ الْأَعْمَشَ وُلِدَ إِمَّا فِي سَنَةِ إِحْدَى وَسِتِّينَ، أَوْ تِسْعٍ وَخَمْسِينَ، وَأَبُو بَكْرَةَ مَاتَ سَنَةَ إِحْدَى أَوِ اثْنَتَيْنِ وَخَمْسِينَ، فَكَيْفَ يَتَهَيَّأُ أَنْ يَأْخُذَ بِرِكَابِ مَنْ مَاتَ قَبْلَ مَوْلِدِهِ بِعَشْرِ سِنِينَ أَوْ نَحْوِهَا! "
>
> — **⁨الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص٩٨⁩** (⁨السخاوي⁩ quoting ⁨شيخنا⁩, i.e. ⁨الحافظ ابن حجر رحمہ اللہ⁩)

*(working translation — ours: "That is a gross error — for ⁨الأعمش⁩ was born either in 61 or 59, and
⁨أبو بكرة⁩ died in 51 or 52. **So how could he have taken hold of the stirrup of a man who died some
ten years before he was born?**")*

### 3.5 The opening scene — a letter that said only "⁨شعبان⁩"

**muqaddima pp.۳۶–۳۷** *(the page marked `PRINTED p.21` in the extraction — see §9.3; it is really
p.۳۷)*. Paraphrased from the Urdu; the muqaddima footnotes it to **⁨السيوطي، الشماريخ في علم⁩
⁨التاريخ⁩** (and to ⁨تاریخ الطبری⁩ for one strand):

- As letters, receipts and documents multiplied, it became hard to tell which writing belonged to
  which year. Sayyidunā ⁨ابو موسیٰ اشعری ؓ⁩ wrote to Sayyidunā ⁨عمر ؓ⁩: *letters reach us from you with
  no date on them — fix us a calendar.*
- Another report: a letter reached Sayyidunā ⁨عمر ؓ⁩ **marked only «⁨شعبان⁩»**. He said: **«⁨یہ کیسے⁩
  ⁨معلوم ہوگا کہ یہ کون سے سال کا شعبان ہے؟⁩»** — *how is anyone to know which year's ⁨شعبان⁩ this is?*
- He put it to the ⁨صحابہ ؓ⁩. Rome's era was rejected as running back too far (to Alexander); Persia's
  was rejected because it restarted with every king. A calendar of their own, then — but from when?
  Three views: the birth ﷺ, the ⁨هجرة⁩, the ⁨وفاة⁩.
- Sayyidunā ⁨عمر ؓ⁩ ruled: **from the ⁨هجرة⁩ — «⁨کیوں کہ اس سے حق و باطل کے درمیان فرق ہوا⁩»** (because by
  it truth and falsehood were separated).
- The month: ⁨ربیع الاول⁩ was proposed (the ⁨هجرة⁩ itself), ⁨رمضان⁩ was proposed (its merit); the decision
  went to Sayyidunā ⁨عثمان ؓ⁩'s view — **⁨محرم⁩**: a sacred month, the first month of the year, and the
  month in which people return from Hajj.
- The muqaddima dates the decision to **۱۷ or ⁨۱۸ھ⁩** and adds, on ⁨السيوطي⁩'s authority, that the
  Prophet ﷺ himself had directed years to be counted from the ⁨هجرة⁩ after reaching Medina, and that
  a letter of his ﷺ is established bearing "year 5 ⁨هجری⁩" — so ⁨عمر ؓ⁩ was following a ⁨سنت⁩, not
  innovating.

**Why this is the right opening.** It is the ummah discovering, in the middle of running a state,
that *an event without a date is not usable information*. That is §3.1 dramatised, it is a
Companion-led decision, it needs no argument, and it hands the audience the ⁨ھجری⁩ calendar — the
axis of every timeline they will see for the next ten weeks.

⚠ Cite it as **muqaddima pp.۳۶–۳۷**. The exact wording of ⁨عمر ؓ⁩'s «⁨فرق بین الحق والباطل⁩» line and
of ⁨عثمان ؓ⁩'s reasoning must be eyeballed against the page images before being set as a quotation;
the sense above is safe, the Urdu wording is OCR.

## 4. THE DISTINCTION — ⁨حدیث⁩ / ⁨تاریخ⁩ / ⁨فقہ⁩

This is the analytical heart of the strand: **the same report is handled by three different
disciplines under three different sets of conditions.**

### 4.1 The three disciplines, by what they are *for* — muqaddima p.۳۳

See §3.1. In one line each:

| | What it collects the event for | How it orders |
|---|---|---|
| **⁨ادب⁩** | so that the reader takes a lesson from it | not by time |
| **⁨حدیث⁩** | for ⁨فقہی احکام⁩ and ⁨نکات⁩, the standing of the ⁨سند⁩, the names of the ⁨رواۃ⁩ | by topic / by sanad / by transmitter — not by time |
| **⁨تاریخ⁩** | so that the sequence itself is preserved | strictly earlier-before-later, year by year, month by month |

### 4.2 The ⁨شرائط⁩ differ — muqaddima p.۶۶, «⁨روایت تاریخ اور روایت حدیث میں فرق⁩»

**This is the page the whole guardrail argument runs on.** Paraphrased from the Urdu (OCR — not
quotable, but the structure is unambiguous):

> Some conditions are **shared** between ⁨روایتِ حدیث⁩ and ⁨روایتِ تاریخ⁩: that the ⁨راوی⁩ be ⁨عاقل⁩, that
> his memory be sound, that he be honest. But in some things ⁨روایتِ تاریخ⁩ **differs**.
>
> In ⁨روایتِ حدیث⁩, investigation of the ⁨سند⁩ is given very great weight. **In ⁨روایتِ تاریخ⁩,
> investigation of the ⁨سند⁩ is obligatory only on certain specific occasions, and not otherwise.**
> Those occasions are:
>
> 1. Reports touching the **character and qualities of the Prophet ﷺ and the ⁨صحابہ کرام ؓ⁩**.
> 2. Reports from which an **objection against Islamic personalities, the ⁨سلف صالحین⁩ or the
>    ⁨مشاہیرِ اسلام⁩** could be drawn.
> 3. Reports which, on account of a weak ⁨سند⁩, could **bear on ⁨عقیدہ⁩ or on ⁨مسائلِ حلال و حرام⁩**.
>
> Apart from those three, breadth may be exercised in collecting historical reports, and **even
> ⁨ضعیف روایات⁩ may be accepted**, so that as much detail of events as possible is preserved. A
> ⁨مؤرخ⁩ may also cite weak reports to draw people toward good and warn them off evil — but he
> should make the weakness plain.
>
> This is why the verifying scholars do **not** accept the reports of historical transmitters such
> as ⁨الواقدی⁩ and ⁨محمد بن اسحاق⁩ **in a discussion of ⁨شرعی⁩ matters**, while their reports **may** be
> drawn on **for the particulars of ⁨سیر و مغازی⁩**.
>
> — **⁨تاریخِ امت ج۱، مقدمہ،⁩ p.۶۶**

**That is the classical ⁨تساهل⁩ principle stated with its own boundary drawn around it.** For the
lecture, the useful compression is:

> **Loose where nothing turns on it. Strict the moment a Companion's honour, an ʿaqīda, or a
> ḥalāl/ḥarām ruling is on the line.**

The same page adds, on the authority of a ⁨حدیث⁩ in ⁨صحیح البخاری⁩, that in mere historical
particulars (⁨معلوماتِ عامہ⁩) even a non-Muslim source may be reported from —
`⁨حَدِّثُوا عَنْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ وَلَا حَرَجَ⁩`. ⚠ The muqaddima gives the ⁨البخاری⁩ number as
**۳۴۶۱** in the OCR (`۱۶۴۳` reversed by the bidi algorithm); the digits are exactly the kind the
CLAUDE.md digit-bug warning covers. **Do not print that number without eyeballing p.۶۶.** Marked
**(to verify)**.

### 4.3 ⁨روایہ⁩ vs ⁨درایہ⁩ — muqaddima pp.۶۳–۶۴

The muqaddima classifies ⁨تاریخ نویسی⁩ into three modes. Paraphrased:

- **⁨تاریخ بالروایہ⁩** — reports transmitted as received, the ⁨مؤرخ⁩ adding no comment. Strength: little
  room for the ⁨مؤرخ⁩'s own distortion; the ⁨ماضی⁩ reaches later generations intact. **Weakness:
  things contrary to reason and far-fetched get carried in too.** Named as examples:
  **⁨طبقات ابن سعد، تاریخ طبری، انساب الاشراف، فتوح البلدان⁩** — books in which reports are simply
  set down with their chains, and **the judgement of sound-or-unsound is left to the reader.**
  > The muqaddima says explicitly, **p.۶۳**: dozens of ⁨ضعیف⁩ reports in ⁨تاریخ طبری⁩, ⁨انساب الاشراف⁩
  > and ⁨طبقات ابن سعد⁩ will, if read without comparison against other reports, breed severe
  > misunderstanding — **which is why the ⁨علماء⁩ forbid ordinary people from reading such large
  > histories: they are for the ⁨اہلِ فن⁩, not for the ⁨عوام⁩.** He draws the exact parallel: a layman
  > reading a translation of ⁨صحیح بخاری⁩ or ⁨مسلم⁩ and extracting rulings at will is in the same danger.

  ⚠ **This paragraph is the single most useful sentence in the muqaddima for the L03 guardrail.** It
  is the course's own book saying, in advance, why the speaker is reading these books *for* the room
  rather than handing the room the books.

- **⁨تاریخ بالدرایہ⁩** — the ⁨مؤرخ⁩ links a few reports and indications into a construction. Strength: no
  room for the far-fetched. **Weakness: no firm foundation; it all rests on the ⁨مؤرخ⁩'s own estimate,
  and he takes and leaves what he likes.** The muqaddima names, as an example of this mode, the
  European reconstructions from archaeology that place man hundreds of thousands of years back.
  It then sets limits on ⁨درایت⁩ (**p.۶۴**): ⁨قیاس⁩ and ⁨درایت⁩ are **not** to be given unrestricted
  priority over ⁨روایت⁩; ⁨متواتر⁩ reports are not to be dropped on ⁨درایت⁩; a ⁨ضعیف⁩ report is rejected on
  ⁨درایت⁩ **only** when it contains something impossible or wholly unreasonable; and
  **a thing being strange, unusual or contrary to habit is not a ground for rejecting a report —
  because it is precisely the out-of-the-ordinary that history records.**

- **⁨تاریخ بالروایہ والدرایہ⁩** — both together: the weight kept on the transmitted reports, but odds
  and ends not swept up; a doubtful report flagged to the reader as doubtful; every report weighed
  in the balance of reason; interpretation kept close to the facts and impartial. **The muqaddima
  calls this the best mode of history-writing** and names as largely following it:
  **البدایہ والنہایہ (ابن کثیر رحمہ اللہ)، تاریخ الاسلام (الذہبی رحمہ اللہ)، تاریخ ابن خلدون.**
  — **muqaddima p.۶۴**

  > This is the paragraph that licenses the course's own source policy. **⁨البدایہ⁩ is the primary
  > narrative because the course's own ⁨مقدمہ⁩ ranks its method highest; ⁨طبری⁩ is corroboration
  > because the same ⁨مقدمہ⁩ classes it as bare ⁨روایہ⁩ that leaves the sifting to the reader.**

### 4.4 The ⁨شرائط⁩ for reproducing a historical report — muqaddima p.۶۵

The muqaddima lists conditions on the ⁨مؤرخ⁩ reproducing a report. Paraphrased; two of them matter
for us:

- If something among the historical reports **collides with ⁨قرآن و سنت⁩, the ⁨قرآن و سنت⁩ is believed
  and the report is dropped** — because the investigation and painstaking sifting that has been done
  in ⁨قرآن و حدیث⁩ cannot be done in ⁨تاریخ⁩.
- **Where a report carries a taunt, an accusation or sarcasm against a reliable figure of the ⁨سلف⁩,
  hold back from accepting it,** and decide only after reflection and investigation — because people
  whose piety and reliability are already established cannot be held blameworthy on one man's
  accusation.

— **⁨تاریخِ امت ج۱، مقدمہ،⁩ p.۶۵** (the muqaddima's footnote there: ⁨قاعدة في المؤرخين للعلامة تاج⁩
الدين السبكي، ط دار البشائر بيروت)

### 4.5 ⁨السخاوي⁩'s parallel — what is HARAM in ⁨تاریخ⁩

The Arabic backing for §4.2 and §4.4, and it names the ⁨مشاجرات⁩ directly. This is inside ⁨السخاوي⁩'s
own chapter on the five ⁨احکام⁩:

> وَمِنْهُ مَا هُوَ حَرَامٌ كالْمَذْكُورِ مِمَّا وَقَعَ لِكَثِيرٍ مِنْ جُهَّالِ الْمُؤَرِّخِينَ الَّذِينَ مُعَوَّلُهُمْ غَالِبًا عَلَى النَّاقِلِينَ عَنْ كُتُبِ الأوَّلِينَ ... وَكَذَا غَيْرُهُ مِنَ الأَخْبَارِ الَّتِي تَجْرِي مَجْرَى الْخُرَافَاتِ، حَيْثُ أَوْرَدَهُ بِالْجَزْمِ مِنْ غَيْرِ بَيَانٍ لِبُطْلَانِهِ، وَلَا أَنَّهُ مِمَّا نُقِلَ عَنْ كُتُبِ الْأَوَائِلِ، سِيَّمَا الْمُضَافُ لِسِيَرِ الْأَنْبِيَاءِ، وَالْمَحْكِيُّ عَمَّا شَجَرَ بَيْنَ الصَّحَابَةِ مِنَ الْأَخْبَارِيِّينَ [إِذْ] الْغَالِبُ **عَلَيْهِمُ الْإِكْثَارُ وَالتَّخْلِيطُ.**
>
> — **⁨الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص١٨١⁩–١٨٢** (the sentence breaks across the two pages; the bolded clause

*(working translation — ours: "And among it is **what is ḥarām** — of the sort mentioned, that has

## 5. THE GUARDRAIL

### 5.1 The five ⁨أحكام⁩ applied to ⁨تاریخ⁩ — the frame

**⁨السخاوي⁩ devotes a chapter to it:** `[⁨تَمَشِّي الأَحْكَامِ الخَمْسَةِ فِي التَّارِيخِ⁩]`, printed
**⁨ص١٧٩⁩–⁨ص١٨٤⁩**. He opens:

> وَأَمَّا حُكْمُهُ فَلَيْسَ بِمُطَّرِدٍ فِي وَاحِدٍ، بَلْ مِنْهُ مَا هُوَ وَاجِبٌ إِذَا تَعَيَّنَ طَرِيقًا لِلْوُقُوفِ عَلَى اتِّصَالِ الْخَبَرِ وَشِبْهِهِ، وَلِمَعْرِفَةِ النَّسْخِ، وَلِلْأَنْسَابِ الَّتِي يَنْشَأُ عَنْهَا التَّوَارُثُ وَالْكَفَاءَةُ.
>
> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص١٧٩**

*(working translation — ours: "As for its ruling, it is not uniform under a single heading. Rather,
part of it is **obligatory** — when it is the only road to establishing whether a report's chain is
connected and the like, and to knowing abrogation, and for the lineages from which inheritance and
⁨كفاءة⁩ follow.")*

He had already flagged this in his own preface, listing as the sixth of his ten chapters
«⁨وَحُكْمِهِ مِنَ الوُجُوبِ أَوِ الإِستِحبَابِ أَوِ المُبَاحَاتِ⁩» (**⁨ص٨٧⁩**), and a chapter earlier
argues **⁨التَّارِيخُ فَرْضَ كِفَايَةٍ أَفْضَلُ مِن فَرْضِ عَيْنٍ⁩** (**⁨ص١٧٤⁩**).

He then works down the scale — **⁨مكروه⁩** at **⁨ص١٨٤⁩** (the historians who blacken pages with trivia
"the turning away from which is better"), **⁨مباح⁩** at **⁨ص١٨٤⁩** (quoting ⁨الغزالي رحمہ اللہ⁩ from
⁨الإحياء⁩: histories and non-obscene poetry are among the *permissible* sciences), **⁨مستحب⁩** at
**⁨ص١٨٣⁩**, **⁨واجب⁩ / ⁨فرض كفاية⁩** at **⁨ص١٧٩⁩–١٨٠**, **⁨حرام⁩** at **⁨ص١٨١⁩–١٨٣** (§4.5 above).

### 5.2 The Urdu book's version of the same ladder — muqaddima p.۵۲

lecture will actually use. It footnotes itself «⁨ملخص از الاعلان بالتوبيخ⁩» — i.e. **it is an

### 5.3 ⭐ THE SENTENCE — the ⁨مشاجرات⁩ ruling

This is the sentence the whole session-3 guardrail is built on. It sits at the foot of
**muqaddima p.۵۲** and runs over onto **p.۵۳**. Paraphrased from the Urdu — the OCR is damaged and
**this sentence in particular must be eyeballed against the page images of p.۵۲ and p.۵۳ before it
is set on a slide or in a script.** The sense is unambiguous:

> **Studying or discussing the ⁨مشاجرات⁩ of the ⁨صحابہ کرام ؓ⁩ without a specific need is ⁨مکروہ⁩** —
> because there is in it no benefit, worldly or otherworldly; rather there is the possibility of a
> falling-off in the ⁨ادب⁩ and ⁨احترام⁩ owed to those personages.
> **If there is a danger of a defect entering one's ⁨عقیدہ⁩, then it is ⁨حرام⁩.**
> **However** — studying the subject, and debating on it, **in order to know the answer to the
> propaganda of the ⁨اہلِ باطل⁩ against the ⁨صحابہ ؓ⁩, and to acquaint others with the reality, is
> ⁨جائز⁩ — indeed, at a time of need, it becomes ⁨واجب⁩.**
>
> — **⁨تاریخِ امت ج۱، مقدمہ،⁩ pp.۵۲–۵۳**

**Read the structure, because the structure is what makes the evening safe:**

> **Default: ⁨مکروہ⁩. With ⁨عقیدہ⁩ at risk: ⁨حرام⁩. For the defence of the ⁨صحابہ ؓ⁩ against propaganda:
> ⁨جائز⁩ — and at need, ⁨واجب⁩.**

That is what licenses this course to narrate ⁨سقيفة⁩, the ⁨ردة⁩, and (in session 3) the approach to
⁨عثمان ؓ⁩ → ⁨علی ؓ⁩ **at all**: the course exists in the third category. The speaker says so out loud
and then does exactly that and no more.

**The supporting footnote on p.۵۳** — the muqaddima cites **⁨علامہ ابن حجر الہیتمی رحمہ اللہ⁩**
(⁨تنویر الایمان، ترجمهٔ تطهير الجنان⁩), who himself narrated many reports of the ⁨مشاجرات⁩, to this
effect *(paraphrased; OCR damaged)*: our aʾimma stated plainly in the ⁨اصول⁩ that the fighting that
occurred among the ⁨صحابہ ؓ⁩ should not be mentioned — *but let no one object to us that we have
related these events, for our purpose is to state the sound events and draw the sound conclusions
from them*; and: *what I have mentioned, I have transmitted in accordance with the reality, for the
sake of making the truth plain. Such mention is among the important obligations and the gravest
necessities — because through it the purity and the innocence of the ⁨صحابہ ؓ⁩ becomes manifest.*

> **This is the single best thing to say from the platform when the room pushes.** It is not our
> reasoning; it is ⁨ابن حجر الہیتمی رحمہ اللہ⁩'s, quoted by the course's own book, and it is exactly
> the position the course occupies. See also the Arabic in §4.5.

**Why the room needs it — muqaddima pp.۴۹–۵۰.** The muqaddima's own diagnosis of why this is live:
history slipped from the hands of the ⁨علماء⁩ into those of court secretaries and poets, then into
the hands of outsiders; the ⁨مستشرقین⁩ made a plaything of Islamic history; then their students, of
secular mind, took the field; **and these people strain every nerve to distort history, to put false
constructions on the ⁨مشاجرات⁩ of the ⁨صحابہ ؓ⁩, to defame the ⁨سلف⁩, and to declare the Muslim
conquerors brigands and plunderers.** At **p.۵۰** it names the Western writers behind the impression
its own readers have absorbed — **⁨گولڈزیہر، پادری زویمر، جوزف شاخت، ولیم میور⁩** — and observes that
in the colleges the subject is taught by teachers to whom ⁨سلطان محمود غزنوی⁩ and ⁨عالمگیر⁩ can only be
tyrants. ⚠ The four names are given as spelled here from the OCR; **verify the spellings against the
page image before printing them.**

### 5.4 ⭐ ⁨تاریخِ اسلام⁩ = ⁨تاریخِ مسلمین⁩ — muqaddima p.۶۱

The second half of the guardrail, and the muqaddima gives it a heading of its own:
**«⁨تاریخ اسلام یا تاریخ مسلمین⁩»**. Paraphrased:

> Within ⁨تاریخِ خاص⁩, our subject is **⁨تاریخِ اسلام⁩**. **⁨تاریخِ اسلام⁩ is in reality ⁨تاریخِ مسلمین⁩**
> — because it is not the history of a religion purely, as the outward wording "⁨تاریخِ اسلام⁩"
> suggests.
>
> The history of a **religion** is one in which a religion's beginning, its spread, the life of its
> founder and the notable followers who took part in spreading it are recounted; along with people
> turning away from it, its acceptance or rejection, and its sect-formations, examined in the order
> of time. (The muqaddima's examples: ⁨الملل والنحل⁩ of ⁨علامہ شہرستانی رحمہ اللہ⁩; and, in our own day,
> ⁨مفتی تقی عثمانی مدظلہ⁩'s «⁨عیسائیت کیا ہے؟⁩» as a short history of the Christian religion.)
>
> Having understood that, one sees that **⁨سیرۃ النبی ﷺ⁩ and ⁨سیرِ صحابہ ؓ⁩ can without doubt be called
> the history of the religion — but the circumstances of the Muslims in the later period cannot be
> carried on that meaning.** Granted, much was done in later ages in the character of religion —
> efforts for the advance of Islam, ⁨تبلیغ⁩ to non-Muslims, the founding of ⁨مدارس⁩, ⁨مساجد⁩ and ⁨خانقاہیں⁩,
> scholarly labour. **But the remaining affairs of state and politics had merely a worldly
> character, and we too should see them in history in that character.** So **we cannot include the
> Muslims' wars among themselves, their political upheavals, their dynastic quarrels, and the
> circumstances of sinful and tyrannical kings, in the history of the religion.**
>
> The just statement is this: **it is the history of the followers of Islam, in which there is both
> ascent and decline; attachment to the religion, and in places distance from it.**
>
> Therefore the common books of ⁨تاریخِ اسلام⁩ — **⁨تاریخ طبری، البدایہ والنہایہ، الکامل فی التاریخ⁩** —
> **should be read as the history of a people (⁨قوم کی تاریخ⁩), not as the history of a religion.**
>
> — **⁨تاریخِ امت ج۱، مقدمہ،⁩ p.۶۱**

> **The lecture line, in English, on the slide:**
> **"Islamic history is the history of Muslims, not of Islam. It is not a source of ⁨دین⁩."**
> Everything after it — every dynasty, every civil war, every failure — is thereby placed where it
> belongs, and the room is told in advance that a Muslim behaving badly in the year 250⁨ھ⁩ is not
> evidence about Islam.

### 5.5 Reading history ⁨خلافِ اصول⁩ — what it does to the reader, muqaddima pp.۵۸–۶۰

The muqaddima's heading is **«⁨خلاف اصول تاریخ پڑھنے کے نقصانات⁩»**. It opens with an image worth
keeping: *even a good thing, used against its own principles, becomes harmful — honey itself, taken
past a limit, does damage.* Its causes, paraphrased:

1. **Sect-driven transmitters inserted their own home-made reports throughout the historical
   material, and the work of cleansing it has never been fully done.** Only expert ⁨علماء⁩ can gauge
   such material. Those with raw or slight learning **believe those reports and fall into one or
   another intellectual fallacy.** (p.۵۸)
2. **History states the event but does not always throw light on its cause, background or
   justification** — from which many misunderstandings can be born. Where caution, fineness of
   judgement and fairness are not held to, **fictions contrary to fact can come into existence**,
   and a man's own conjecture can make a mountain of a molehill. This happens on two grounds:
   **(a) not knowing the standing of the personality involved; (b) rancour and hostility.** (pp.۵۸–۵۹)
   > **The muqaddima's own worked illustration (p.۵۹) is excellent and completely safe** — a
   > respectable-looking man is seen standing at a bus stop at one o'clock every day. One observer
   > concludes he is a plain-clothes policeman keeping watch. Another, that he is an ordinary man
   > out for a stroll. Another, that he is mentally ill. Someone with a grudge says he is a
   > terrorist looking for an opportunity. **And it is entirely possible that all of it is wrong —
   > he is an employee waiting for the bus to the office.**
   > *One event; four stories.* Then the muqaddima's own conclusion: **history is precisely a
   > collection of events; what conclusion should be drawn from an event, and what the soundest
   > comment on it would be, can be decided only after deep study and the fullest possible
   > acquaintance with the life of the person concerned. That stage of comment is the ⁨مؤرخ⁩'s own
   > to carry — and if he is not cautious and fair, he becomes at such a moment a source of a
   > misleading impression.**
   >
   > ⚠ **Use this on a slide in L01.** It is the muqaddima's own analogy, it is modern, it is
   > entirely free of any figure it would be improper to speculate about, and it teaches the room
   > the reflex the whole course depends on.
3. People read and teach history and philosophy-of-history — much of it imported from Europe —
   **while remaining generally unacquainted with those ⁨اصولِ تاریخ⁩ that the Muslim ⁨مؤرخین⁩ laid
   down.** Any science taken up in violation of its own principles will necessarily breed
   misguidance and crooked thinking. (p.۵۹)
4. **Before ⁨تاریخ⁩ and even before ⁨اصولِ تاریخ⁩, a Muslim ought to have the necessary knowledge of
   ⁨عقائد⁩, ⁨شرعی احکام⁩, ⁨سیرتِ رسول ﷺ⁩, ⁨اصولِ حدیث⁩ and ⁨فنِ رجال⁩.** When these are abandoned, wandering in
   the twisting ravines of history and falling into intellectual misguidance is entirely to be
   expected. (p.۵۹)
5. Most of our historians cannot reach the original sources for want of Arabic and Persian and make
   do with Urdu or English translations. (p.۵۹)
6. From the habit of reading English, students begin from the books of the ⁨مستشرقین⁩, which under
   cover of scholarly research are full of ideological poison. (p.۵۹)
7. Some, from ignorance of ⁨علم اسماء الرجال⁩, **give the reports of lying transmitters the rank of
   ⁨وحی⁩.** (p.۵۹)
8. Some, from ignorance of ⁨فنِ روایت⁩, on seeing disturbing material in a report **form superficial
   opinions about the transmitter** — and of these, some go so far as to declare the very basic
   sources of ⁨سیرت⁩ and ⁨تاریخ⁩ to be the fables of ⁨منافقین⁩ and ⁨مجوسیوں⁩, while some, finding such
   material in the ⁨حدیث⁩ corpus, **arrive at ⁨انکارِ حدیث⁩.** (p.۶۰)

**And then the muqaddima gives the positive standard**, at **p.۶۰**, from **⁨شیخ علی طنطاوی رحمہ⁩
⁨اللہ⁩** (its footnote: ⁨قصص من التاريخ الطنطاوي، مقدمة، ص ١٣⁩–⁨١٤، ط دار المنارة السعودية⁩).
Paraphrased:

> The narration of the ⁨مؤرخین⁩ is of a popular kind; the narration that stands up to the scholarly
> touchstone is that of the ⁨محدثین⁩. **That is why the first source of our history is what the
> ⁨محدثین⁩ transmitted. One who is unacquainted with the terminology and the science of the ⁨محدثین⁩
> cannot be counted a ⁨مؤرخ⁩ at all.**
>
> And: *"Every analyst who thinks it enough to give a page-reference to ⁨الطبری⁩ at the end of a
> discussion is announcing that he is in the habit of groping about in a dark night. He does not
> know what should be taken and what left."* **He alone can become an Islamic ⁨مؤرخ⁩, or a teacher of
> Islamic history, who knows ⁨فنِ رجال⁩ and their circumstances, knows ⁨علمِ حدیث⁩ and its ⁨اصول⁩, is
> expert in Arabic, can distinguish the outward sense of Arab speech from its inner import and
> understand its allusions, is free of prejudice and self-interest, and seeks truth and the pleasure
> of Allah.** If he is void of these qualities then he is ignorant of history — indeed a deceiver —
> though he be a professor of history in some university or the holder of great degrees. For
> government institutions appoint a man a teacher on sight of a degree, and such degrees are got by
> forgery too. **But no government can make a scholar of an ignorant man, make a prejudiced man
> upright, or make a liar truthful.**
>
> — **⁨تاریخِ امت ج۱، مقدمہ،⁩ p.۶۰**

> ⚠ **Register warning.** This passage is powerful and it is also a broadside at university
> historians. For a Tanzeem-e-Islami room it will land hard and it will be enjoyed. **The speaker
> should deliver it as a quotation from ⁨شیخ علی طنطاوی رحمہ اللہ⁩ carried by the course's own book,
> and not adopt it as his own verdict** — consistent with CLAUDE.md §1.5 ("let the quoted
> authorities carry every conclusion, and decline to adjudicate").

## 6. THE FORGED KHAYBAR DOCUMENT — the proof-case

**Both books carry it. The Arabic is fuller and better, and we have the page.**

### 6.1 The principle, stated one page before the story

> وَطَالَما كَانَ طَرِيقًا لِلإطِّلَاعِ عَلَى التَّزْوِيرِ فِي الْمَكَاتِيبِ وَنَحْوِهَا، بِأَنْ يُعْلَمَ أَنَّ الْحَاكِمَ الَّذِي نُسِبَ إِلَيْهِ الثُّبُوتُ، أَوِ الشَّاهِدَ، أَوْ غَيْرُهُمَا مِنَ أَسْبَابِهِ، أَوْ نَحْوُ ذَلِكَ، مَاتَ قَبْلَ تَارِيخِ الْمَكْتُوبِ.

> — **الإعلان بالتوبيخ، ص١٠٠**

*(working translation — ours: "And how often it has been the road to detecting **forgery in written

| Argument 2 | ⁨سعد بن معاذ ؓ⁩ died **⁨يوم بني قريظة⁩, two years before ⁨خیبر⁩** | ⁨سعد بن معاذ ؓ⁩ was martyred at **⁨غزوۂ خندق⁩**, long before ⁨خیبر⁩ |
| Its own citation | ⁨ابن القيم، أحكام أهل الذمة ١⁩/ ٩١ (editor's note) | ⁨المنتظم لابن جوزی⁩ (the muqaddima's footnote) |

⚠ **The one divergence to handle carefully.** ⁨السخاوي⁩ says ⁨سعد بن معاذ ؓ⁩ died **⁨يوم بني قريظة⁩**; the
muqaddima says at **⁨غزوۂ خندق⁩**. These are not really rival claims — ⁨بنو قریظہ⁩ followed immediately
on ⁨الخندق⁩, and ⁨سعد ؓ⁩ was wounded at ⁨الخندق⁩ — but **they are different sentences and the deck must not
merge them.** Recommended slide wording, which is true on both accounts and needs no adjudication:
**"⁨سعد بن معاذ ؓ⁩ had already died — before ⁨خیبر⁩."** If a date is wanted, use ⁨السخاوي⁩'s, since we hold
his printed page: **two years before the conquest of ⁨خیبر⁩.**

⚠ **Also record, and do not print without checking:** the muqaddima adds a **footnote on p.۵۸** giving
a second view on ⁨معاویہ ؓ⁩ — *that he had already accepted Islam secretly but had been unable to
emigrate from Mecca* (its footnote: ⁨طبقات ابن سعد⁩) — and observes that **even on that view the
argument stands unchanged, because ⁨معاویہ ؓ⁩ by agreement was not present at ⁨غزوۂ خیبر⁩; he was in
Mecca at that time.** ⚠ The ⁨طبقات ابن سعد⁩ volume/page in the OCR is garbled; do not print it. Marked
**(to verify)**. Note also that **⁨طبقات ابن سعد⁩ is outside our safe list** — it is being cited here
only as *"the muqaddima's own footnote says"*.

⚠ And a **second footnote on p.۵۸** records a closely parallel case: **⁨امام ابن تیمیہ رحمہ اللہ⁩** in
the eighth century ⁨هجری⁩, as related by his special student **⁨امام ابن قیم الجوزیہ رحمہ اللہ⁩**, was
shown a document bearing Companions' signatures purporting to confirm a settlement with the Jews of
⁨خیبر⁩. Among his grounds for declaring it forged: **the document mentions remission of ⁨جزیہ⁩, whereas
⁨جزیہ⁩ was legislated at the occasion of ⁨غزوۂ تبوک⁩**; and **it pledges not to take forced labour
(⁨بیگار⁩) from the Jews, whereas taking forced labour from anyone, Jew or otherwise, was never a
practice among the Muslims — the Prophet ﷺ and the ⁨صحابہ ؓ⁩ were wholly free of that oppression; the
practice of ⁨بیگار⁩ began in the days of the tyrannical kings of a later age.** The muqaddima's
footnote cites **⁨زاد المعاد⁩**. ⚠ The volume/page is garbled in the OCR; do not print it.
Marked **(to verify)**.

> **Why this belongs in the lecture.** It is the cleanest possible demonstration that the ordering
> of events in time is not antiquarian bookkeeping — it is the tool that stopped a forged instrument
> from being enforced by a state. And it closes the loop opened by §3.5: a caliph asked *"which
> year's ⁨شعبان⁩ is this?"*, and four centuries later a ḥāfiẓ answered a forgery with the same
> question.
