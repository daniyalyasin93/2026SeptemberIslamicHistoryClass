## 0. The sentences this note licenses

Each is page-cited below. **Nothing here is a fact-claim about what happened**; every one of them is
a claim about **how ⁨ابن خلدون رحمہ اللہ⁩ read what happened**.

1. **Two independent classical authorities, a century apart, say the same thing about why history
   goes wrong.** ⁨السخاوي⁩ (d. ⁨۹۰۲ھ⁩ `[STANDARD]` — to verify; no page read in this pass) built the
   session-1 guardrail; ⁨ابن خلدون رحمہ اللہ⁩ (d. ⁨۸۰۸ھ⁩ `[STANDARD]` — to verify; likewise not page-cited
   here) names **seven causes of falsehood in reports** and puts partisanship first (**⁨ج۱ ص۴۶⁩–۴۷**). Two
   authorities on one point is far stronger than one. `[SOURCED]`
2. **He says outright that transmitted army-counts and money-counts are the likeliest place in any
   narrative for a report to be false** — «⁨إذ هي مظنّة الكذب ومطيّة الهذر⁩» (**⁨ج۱ ص۱۳⁩**). That single
   clause licenses every "these figures are transmitted, not audited" caption in the series.
   `[SOURCED]`
3. **He draws the ⁨حدیث⁩ / ⁨تاریخ⁩ distinction the guardrail needs, in classical Arabic:**
   isnād-criticism (⁨التعديل والتجريح⁩) is the measure for **⁨شرعي⁩** reports because they are binding
   obligations; a report **about an event** must first be tested for whether the thing was even
   possible (**⁨ج۱ ص۴۹⁩**). `[SOURCED]`
4. **His own book gives two different sizes for the same Persian army** — 120,000 at ⁨ج۱ ص۱۴⁩ and
   again at ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩, against 60,000 from ⁨عائشہ ؓ⁩ and ⁨الزہری⁩ at ⁨ج۱ ص۱۴⁩. He is the best possible
   demonstration of his own rule. `[SOURCED]`
5. **⁨عصبية⁩, in his definition, is not tribalism as a vice.** It is ordinary human protectiveness over
   kin, doing structural work; without it, he says, *nothing* that must be carried against resistance
   can be carried — «⁨كلّ أمر يحمل النّاس عليه من نبوة أو إقامة ملك أو دعوة⁩» (**⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩**). `[SOURCED]`
6. **Leadership, on his reading, does not leave the lineage it started in** (**⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۴⁩–۱۶۵**). This
   is his structural account of why a kin-ordered society argues about succession the way it does —
   *but he never applies it to ⁨سقيفة⁩ himself; see §7.3 for the boundary.*
   `[SOURCED for the principle; the application is ours]`
7. **The mechanism the whole evening needs:** a religious call, on his reading, **adds strength to a
   dynasty over and above the ⁨عصبية⁩ it already had**, because it removes the mutual jealousy that
   keeps kin-groups apart and points them all one way (**⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩**). He names ⁨صدر الإسلام والفتوحات⁩
   as his own worked example. `[SOURCED]`
8. **And he states the reverse test in the same breath:** when the religious colouring decays, the
   outcome falls back to bare ⁨عصبية⁩ (**⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۹⁩**). `[SOURCED]`
9. **He holds that the Arabs of the peninsula could not have unified on political grounds at all** —
   too proud, too competitive over leadership, «⁨فقلّما تجتمع أهواؤهم⁩» — and that only a ⁨صبغة دينية⁩
   put the restraint *inside* them (**⁨ج۱ ص۱۸۹⁩–۱۹۰**). `[SOURCED]`
10. **His narrative of ۱۱–⁨۲۳ھ⁩ contains almost no analysis.** Ninety-five cached pages, searched for
    every analytical marker he uses elsewhere, yielded **two** hits. The judgement is in volume 1;
    the events are in volume 2; **joining them is our work, not his** (§7.1–§7.2). `[SOURCED —
    negative finding]`

# 1. THE BOUNDARY, STATED OPERATIONALLY

`DECISIONS.md` #29 is not a caveat to be recited and then forgotten. It is a rule about what may
appear on a slide. Restated as a working test:

| | |
|---|---|
| **May be attributed to him** | An explanation. A structural claim. A reading. Always with his name on it — *"Ibn Khaldūn reads it this way."* |
| **May NOT come from him** | A date · a name · a number · a chain · **the wording of any saying**. For the early period his narrative follows ⁨الطبری⁩, so citing him alone for a fact is citing ⁨الطبری⁩ at one remove, which `CLAUDE.md` §4 forbids. |
| **Never** | The ⁨مشاجرات⁩. Same rule as ⁨الطبری⁩, same reason. |

**Three concrete traps this pass actually found:**

### 1.1 He re-uses other men's sentences without attribution

«⁨والمسلمون كالغنم في الليلة الممطرة⁩» at ⁨ج۲ ص۴۸۹⁩ is **⁨ابن إسحاق⁩'s**, and ⁨البدایہ⁩ carries it twice —
see §6.5. **Anything vivid in his narrative should be assumed to be someone else's until checked.**

### 1.2 Sayings he alone carries — check them, and normalise the grep before you trust a negative

Of five superb lines in this note (§6.6, §6.7, §5.5), **one — ⁨عمر ؓ⁩'s «⁨والزموا السنّة تلزمكم⁩
⁨الدولة⁩» — is in ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۳۵۴⁩ almost verbatim and is usable, cited there.** A second is
**misattributed**: «⁨أكل عمر كبدي⁩» is ⁨أبو لؤلؤة⁩'s line in ⁨البدایہ، الکامل⁩ **and in ⁨ابن خلدون⁩'s own
volume 2**, not ⁨رستم⁩'s (§5.5, §7.7). The remaining three are uncorroborated and marked **do not
quote until corroborated.**

> ❗ **The earlier pass got this wrong, and the failure is instructive.** It grepped bare consonantal
> phrases against a **fully vowelled** ⁨الکامل⁩ text, so «⁨تلزمكم الدولة⁩» could not match
> «⁨تَلْزَمْكُمُ الدَّوْلَةُ⁩», and the best line in the strand was written off. **Strip harakat and fold
> ⁨أ⁩/⁨إ⁩/⁨آ، ى⁩/⁨ي، ة⁩/⁨ه⁩ before concluding that anything is absent.** See §7.6 for the corrected table.

### 1.3 The printed text itself is corrupt in places

⁨ج۲ ص۴۹۵⁩ prints, inside ⁨أبو بكر ؓ⁩'s standard commission to the commanders:

> فمن أجاب إلى أمر الله عز وجل وأقرّ له قبل ذلك منه وأعانه عليه **بالمعروب**. **وطنماذ** يقاتل من كفر
> باللَّه على الإقرار بما جاء من عند الله
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۴۹۵⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1292

«⁨بالمعروب⁩» is plainly «⁨بالمعروف⁩»; «⁨وطنماذ⁩» is not a word. **If the edition is unreliable at the level
of the word, he cannot be the authority for a wording.** That is not a criticism of ⁨ابن خلدون⁩
⁨رحمہ اللہ⁩; it is a fact about the text we hold, and it is the single cleanest justification for
`DECISIONS.md` #29's boundary.

# 2. METHOD, PART ONE — THE SEVEN NAMED CAUSES OF ERROR

This is the strand's first deliverable, and it is better than expected: he sets the causes out as a
list, compactly, across a page and a half.

## 2.1 The framing clause — **⁨ج۱ ص۴۶⁩**

> ولمّا كان الكذب متطرّقا للخبر بطبعته وله أسباب تقتضيه.
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/44
> *"Since falsehood finds its way into a report by its very nature, and there are causes that bring
> it about …"*

> ⚠ The edition prints «⁨بطبعته⁩». The sense is «⁨بطبعه⁩» ("by its nature"). **Read this off a page image
> before it reaches a slide.**

## 2.2 The seven, in his order, each in his own words

**Causes 1–5 are wholly on ⁨ج۱ ص۴۶⁩. Cause 6 *begins* on ⁨ج۱ ص۴۶⁩ and runs across the page break onto
⁨ج۱ ص۴۷⁩ — the break falls after «⁨ومنها تقرّب النّاس⁩», so that block spans indices 44 **and** 45.
Cause 7 is wholly on ⁨ج۱ ص۴۷⁩.**

**1 · ⁨التّشيّعات للآراء والمذاهب⁩ — partisanship for opinions and schools** (⁨ج۱ ص۴۶⁩):

> فمنها التّشيّعات للآراء والمذاهب فإنّ النّفس إذا كانت على حال الاعتدال في قبول الخبر أعطته حقّه من
> التّمحيص والنّظر حتّى تتبيّن صدقه من كذبه وإذا خامرها تشيّع لرأي أو نحلة قبلت ما يوافقها من
> الأخبار لأوّل وهلة وكان ذلك الميل والتّشيّع غطاء على عين بصيرتها عن الانتقاد والتّمحيص فتقع في
> قبول الكذب ونقله.
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/44
> *"Among them is partisanship for opinions and schools. For when the soul is in a state of balance
> in receiving a report, it gives it its due of scrutiny and examination until it can tell its truth
> from its falsehood; but when partisanship for some opinion or creed has got into it, it accepts
> whatever agrees with it at first hearing, and that inclination and partisanship become a covering
> over the eye of its insight, keeping it from criticism and scrutiny — so it falls into accepting
> falsehood and passing it on."*

**2 · ⁨الثّقة بالنّاقلين⁩ — trusting the transmitters** (⁨ج۱ ص۴۶⁩):

> ومن الأسباب المقتضية للكذب في الأخبار أيضا الثّقة بالنّاقلين وتمحيص ذلك يرجع إلى التّعديل
> والتّجريح.
> *"Another of the causes that produce falsehood in reports is trust in the transmitters; and the
> sifting of that comes back to declaring them sound or impugning them."*

**3 · ⁨الذّهول عن المقاصد⁩ — missing what was meant** (⁨ج۱ ص۴۶⁩):

> ومنها الذّهول عن المقاصد فكثير من النّاقلين لا يعرف القصد بما عاين أو سمع وينقل الخبر على ما في
> ظنّه وتخمينه فيقع في الكذب.
> *"And among them is inattention to intentions: many a transmitter does not know what was meant by
> what he saw or heard, and passes the report on according to his own supposition and guess — and so
> falls into falsehood."*

**4 · ⁨توهّم الصّدق⁩ — assuming truthfulness** (⁨ج۱ ص۴۶⁩):

> ومنها توهّم الصّدق وهو كثير وإنّما يجيء في الأكثر من جهة الثّقة بالنّاقلين
> *"And among them is presuming truthfulness — which is common, and mostly comes from the side of
> trusting the transmitters."*

**5 · ⁨الجهل بتطبيق الأحوال على الوقائع⁩ — not knowing how the circumstances fit the event**
(ج۱ ص۴۶):

> ومنها الجهل بتطبيق الأحوال على الوقائع لأجل ما يداخلها من التّلبيس والتّصنّع فينقلها المخبر كما
> رآها وهي بالتّصنّع على غير الحقّ في نفسه.
> *"And among them is ignorance of how conditions fit events, because of the dissimulation and
> contrivance that enter into them: the reporter transmits them as he saw them, while by that
> contrivance they are in themselves other than the truth."*

**6 · ⁨التّقرّب لأصحاب التّجلّة والمراتب⁩ — flattering the powerful** (⁨ج۱ ص۴۶⁩–۴۷, the sentence runs
across the page break):

> ومنها تقرّب النّاس في الأكثر لأصحاب التّجلّة والمراتب بالثّناء والمدح وتحسين الأحوال وإشاعة الذّكر
> بذلك فيستفيض الإخبار بها على غير حقيقة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۶⁩–۴۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/44 **and** https://shamela.ws/book/12320/45
> (the first page carries the sentence up to «⁨ومنها تقرّب النّاس⁩», the second the rest of it, from
> «⁨في الأكثر لأصحاب التّجلّة⁩»)
> *"And among them is that people mostly ingratiate themselves with men of eminence and rank by
> praise and eulogy, by making their circumstances look well and spreading their fame — so the
> reports about them become widespread, and not according to the truth."*

**7 · ⁨الجهل بطبائع الأحوال في العمران⁩ — not knowing how societies actually work** (⁨ج۱ ص۴۷⁩):

> ومن الأسباب المقتضية له أيضا **وهي سابقة على جميع ما تقدّم** الجهل بطبائع الأحوال في العمران
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/45
> *"And another of the causes that produce it — **and this one is prior to everything already
> mentioned** — is ignorance of the natures of conditions in human society."*

> ⭐ **The seventh is the one he cares about, and he says so.** It is *prior to all the others*, and
> it is the thesis of his entire book.

## 2.3 Partisanship — why this cause matters to session 1 specifically

The guardrail already installed says *we do not judge between the Companions*. Cause 1 gives the room
a reason drawn from the discipline of history rather than from piety alone: **a report that flatters
the side you already hold is the report you are least equipped to test.** That is a sentence a
religiously committed audience can accept without feeling that anything has been conceded.

## 2.4 Accepting a report without asking whether the thing was even possible — ⁨ج۱ ص۴۹⁩

> ولا يرجع إلى تعديل الرّواة حتّى يعلم أنّ ذلك الخبر في نفسه ممكن أو ممتنع وأمّا إذا كان مستحيلا
> فلا فائدة للنّظر في التّعديل والتّجريح
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/47
> *"One does not turn to vetting the transmitters until it is known whether the report is in itself
> possible or impossible; and if it is impossible, there is no benefit in examining who is sound and
> who is impugned."*

And, on the same page, **the distinction the guardrail is built on** — set out by a classical
authority, in Arabic, which `CLAUDE.md` §1.4 says is free credibility in this room:

> وإنّما كان التّعديل والتّجريح هو المعتبر في صحّة الأخبار الشّرعيّة لأنّ معظمها تكاليف إنشائيّة [١]
> أوجب الشّارع العمل بها حتّى حصل الظّنّ بصدقها وسبيل صحّة الظّنّ الثّقة بالرّواة بالعدالة والضّبط.
> **وأما الأخبار عن الواقعات فلا بدّ في صدقها وصحّتها من اعتبار المطابقة** فلذلك وجب أن ينظر في
> إمكان وقوعه وصار فيها ذلك أهمّ من التّعديل
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/47
> *"Vetting the transmitters is the operative test for the soundness of **legal** reports, because
> most of them are prescriptive obligations the Lawgiver has made binding, so that a sound
> presumption of their truthfulness is needed — and the road to a sound presumption is trust in the
> transmitters through their probity and precision. **But as for reports about events, their truth
> and soundness require testing them against correspondence** — and so one must look into whether
> the thing could have occurred, and in these that becomes more important than vetting."*
>
> *(The «[١]» is the edition's own footnote marker, glossing «⁨إنشائيّة⁩»; it is left in place because
> the block is copied verbatim.)*

> ⭐ **This is the twin of the session-1 guardrail, in classical Arabic.** `CLAUDE.md` §1.6 says the
> vehicle for the guardrail is *"in fiqh a report must be authentic to bind; in ⁨تاریخ⁩ we record what
> was reported in order to fix sequence — so a ⁨تاریخ⁩ report is neither a ruling nor a creed."*
> ⁨ابن خلدون رحمہ اللہ⁩ says precisely that, and `tarikh-definition-and-the-guardrail.md` §4.2
> currently anchors the same point on the **Urdu** muqaddima alone. **This upgrades that section
> from an Urdu paraphrase to a page-cited classical Arabic authority. It is the most valuable single
> find in this pass.**

## 2.5 His positive test, and who he says already practised it — ⁨ج۱ ص۳۷⁩

> وحينئذ يعرض خبر المنقول على ما عنده من القواعد والأصول فإن وافقها وجرى على مقتضاها كان صحيحا
> وإلّا زيّفه واستغنى عنه
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۳۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/35
> *"Then he sets the transmitted report against the rules and principles he holds: if it agrees with
> them and runs according to them, it is sound; and if not, he rejects it as counterfeit and does
> without it."*

Immediately after, on the same page, the sentence that keeps this **inside** the room's frame rather
than importing an outsider's scepticism:

> وما استكبر القدماء علم التّاريخ إلّا لذلك حتّى انتحله الطّبريّ والبخاريّ وابن إسحاق من قبلهما
> وأمثالهم من علماء الأمّة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۳۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/35
> *"The ancients held the science of history in such high regard only for this reason — so much so
> that al-Ṭabarī and al-Bukhārī, and Ibn Isḥāq before them, and their like among the scholars of the
> umma, took it up."*

**He names ⁨البخاري رحمہ اللہ⁩ as a historian.** For an audience that trusts ⁨البخاري⁩ absolutely and is
wary of "history", that one clause does a great deal of work.

## 2.6 The disease that hides — judging the past by the present, ⁨ج۱ ص۳۷⁩

> ومن الغلط الخفيّ في التّاريخ الذّهول عن تبدّل الأحوال في الأمم والأجيال بتبدّل الأعصار ومرور
> الأيّام وهو داء دوي شديد الخفاء إذ لا يقع إلّا بعد أحقاب متطاولة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۳۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/35
> *"Among the hidden errors in history is inattention to the fact that the conditions of peoples and
> generations change as the ages change and the days pass. It is a deep-seated disease, extremely
> hard to detect, because it only occurs after long stretches of time."*

His worked example is clean and entirely non-political, which makes it safe for this room. People
read that **⁨الحجاج بن يوسف⁩'s father was a schoolteacher** and conclude the family were nobodies who
clawed their way up — because *in our day* teaching is a poor man's trade. His answer, ⁨ج۱ ص۴۰⁩:

> والحجّاج بن يوسف كان أبوه من سادات ثقيف وأشرافهم ومكانهم من عصبيّة العرب ومناهضة قريش في الشّرف
> ما علمت ولم يكن تعليمه للقرآن على ما هو الأمر عليه لهذا العهد من أنّه حرفة للمعاش
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/38
> *"As for al-Ḥajjāj ibn Yūsuf, his father was among the chiefs and nobles of Thaqīf, and their
> standing in the ⁨عصبية⁩ of the Arabs and their rivalry with Quraysh in honour is what you know; and
> his teaching of the Qurʾān was not what the matter is in our own day, that it is a trade for a
> living."*

The reader was not wrong about the fact. He was wrong about what the fact **meant**, because he read
a later century's meaning back into an earlier one.

> ⭐ **For a series whose whole purpose is to give people a usable map of a distant past, this is the
> most useful warning in the book, and it costs nothing to say.**

# 3. METHOD, PART TWO — THE NUMBERS PROBLEM

The strand asked for this specifically, because "armies of a hundred thousand" is a live problem in
this session. He is better on it than any other authority we hold.

## 3.1 The headline clause — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۳⁩

> ولا سيّما في إحصاء الأعداد من الأموال والعساكر إذا عرضت في الحكايات إذ هي مظنّة الكذب ومطيّة
> الهذر ولا بدّ من ردّها إلى الأصول وعرضها على القواعد.
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۳⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/11
> *"— and especially in the counting of sums of money and of armies when they appear in the
> narratives, for these are the place where falsehood is to be expected and the mount that idle talk
> rides. They must be referred back to first principles and set against the rules."*

**«⁨مظنّة الكذب ومطيّة الهذر⁩» is the whole caption.** Four words, and it licenses every "transmitted,
not audited" note in the series, from a source the room respects.

## 3.2 The full frame, from the opening paragraph of the book — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۳⁩

> لأنّ الأخبار إذا اعتمد فيها على مجرّد النّقل ولم تحكم أصول العادة وقواعد السّياسة وطبيعة العمران
> والأحوال في الاجتماع الإنسانيّ ولا قيس الغائب منها بالشّاهد والحاضر بالذّاهب فربّما لم يؤمن فيها
> من العثور ومزلّة القدم والحيد عن جادّة الصّدق
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۳⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/11
> *"Because when reports are relied on by bare transmission alone, without the principles of custom,
> the rules of politics, the nature of civilisation and the conditions of human society being
> brought to bear on them, and without the absent among them being measured against the witnessed
> and the present against what has passed — then one may not be safe in them from stumbling, from
> the slip of the foot, and from straying off the road of truth."*

## 3.3 His worked example, and why it is safe to use

He does **not** demonstrate this on an Islamic army. He demonstrates it on **⁨بنو إسرائيل⁩**: ⁨المسعودي⁩
and many historians report that ⁨موسیٰ عليه السلام⁩ counted 600,000 men of fighting age in the
wilderness. His objections, ⁨ج۱ ص۱۴⁩–۱۵:

- **Logistics:** Egypt and ⁨الشام⁩ could not garrison, feed or hold such a number.
- **The battlefield itself:** «⁨يبعد أن يقع بينها زحف أو قتال لضيق ساحة الأرض عنها⁩» — armies that
  size, drawn up, would stretch two or three times beyond eyesight; one wing could not know what the
  other was doing.
- **The comparison:** Persia was far larger than Israel and **never** fielded such a number.
- **The genealogy:** only four generations separate ⁨موسیٰ عليه السلام⁩ from ⁨إسرائيل⁩, and eleven
  separate ⁨سليمان عليه السلام⁩ from him; a line does not multiply to six hundred thousand in four.

And the sentence he closes the argument with — the best one-line statement of his whole method:

> والحاضر يشهد لذلك فالماضي أشبه بالآتي من الماء بالماء.
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/12
> *"And the present bears witness to this — for the past resembles what is to come as water
> resembles water."*

> ✅ **Why this example and no other.** He is testing an Israelite figure carried by ⁨المسعودي⁩, not an
> Islamic one. Nobody in the room has any stake in it, so the *method* can be seen clearly before it
> is ever pointed anywhere sensitive. **Teach the method on this example. Do not teach it on a
> Muslim army.**

## 3.4 ⭐ The demonstration he did not intend — two Persian figures, one book

**This is the strongest "numbers are transmitted, not audited" item in the whole repo, and it is
airtight because it is entirely internal to one author.**

At **⁨ج۱ ص۱۴⁩** he reports, without adjudicating between them, two figures for the Persian host at
القادسیہ:

> وأعظم ما كانت جموعهم بالقادسيّة مائة وعشرين ألفا كلّهم متبوع على ما نقله سيف [١] قال وكانوا في
> أتباعهم أكثر من مائتي ألف وعن عائشة والزّهريّ فإنّ جموع رستم الّذين زحف بهم سعد بالقادسيّة إنّما
> كانوا ستّين ألفا كلّهم متبوع
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/12
> *"The largest their host ever was at al-Qādisiyya was one hundred and twenty thousand, every one
> of them a man with followers, according to what Sayf transmitted — he said that with their
> followers they were more than two hundred thousand. And from ʿĀʾisha ؓ and al-Zuhrī: the host of
> Rustam against which Saʿd ؓ advanced at al-Qādisiyya was only sixty thousand, every one of them a
> man with followers."*
>
> *(«[١]» is the edition's footnote marker on «⁨سيف⁩», identifying ⁨سيف بن عمر الأسدي⁩.)*
>
> ⚠ **The edition prints «⁨زحف بهم سعد⁩» — literally "with whom Saʿd ؓ advanced", which cannot be the
> sense, since the host is ⁨رستم⁩'s.** The reading required is "against whom", and the printed text
> appears to have dropped a preposition. The rendering above supplies that sense. **Another instance
> of §1.3: read this off a page image before it reaches a slide, and if only the figures are wanted,
> quote the numbers and not this clause.**

Then, at **⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩**, arguing that a religious call multiplies strength, **he uses the larger
figure** — see §5.1 for the full block: the Muslims «⁨بضعة وثلاثين ألفا في كلّ معسكر⁩», the Persians
120,000, and ⁨هرقل⁩'s host 400,000 «⁨على ما قاله الواقديّ⁩».

> **Say it plainly and without accusation:** the man who warned us that army-counts are «⁨مظنّة⁩
> ⁨الكذب⁩» records two different figures for the same army in the same book, and reaches for the
> larger one when the larger one makes his point. **That is not a flaw in him. It is exactly what he
> told us to expect of transmitted numbers, illustrated in his own hand.**
>
> ⚠ Everything in this section is a claim about **what one author's book contains**, verifiable by
> anyone in the room from two page numbers. It is **not** a claim about how many Persians were at
> ⁨القادسیہ⁩. **Never let the slide slide into the second thing.**

## 3.5 Why exaggeration happens at all — his psychology of it, ⁨ج۱ ص۱۶⁩

> وما ذلك إلّا لولوع النّفس بالغرائب وسهولة التّجاوز على اللسان والغفلة على المتعقّب والمنتقد حتّى
> لا يحاسب نفسه على خطإ ولا عمد
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/14
> *"And that is nothing but the soul's infatuation with strange things, the ease with which the
> tongue oversteps, and heedlessness of anyone who might follow up or criticise — until a man does
> not hold himself to account for error or for deliberate falsehood."*

His test for this is entirely practical (⁨ج۱ ص۱۵⁩–۱۶): people talk up the armies and treasuries of
**their own age**, and when the registrars actually open the books, «⁨لم تجد معشار ما يعدّونه⁩» — *you
do not find a tenth of what they were counting.* **If men exaggerate about a thing that could be
checked today, what has happened to a figure that has travelled eight centuries?**

# 4. ⁨عصبية⁩ — WHAT IT IS, IN HIS OWN WORDS

`arabian-tribes-and-the-ridda-setup.md` establishes **empirically** who broke and who held in ⁨۱۱ھ⁩.
This section supplies the vocabulary for saying **why kinship had that kind of grip at all** — and
nothing more than that. See §7.2 for the hard limit.

## 4.1 It is not tribal chauvinism; it is protectiveness over kin — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩

> وذلك أنّ صلة الرّحم طبيعيّ في البشر إلّا في الأقلّ ومن صلتها النّعرة على ذوي القربى وأهل الأرحام
> أن ينالهم ضيم أو تصيبهم هلكة فإنّ القريب يجد في نفسه غضاضة من ظلم قريبه أو العداء عليه
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/158
> *"For the bond of kinship is natural in human beings, save in the fewest of cases; and part of that
> bond is the surge of feeling on behalf of close relatives and kinsfolk, that no wrong should reach
> them and no ruin befall them — for a kinsman feels it as an injury in himself when his relative is
> wronged or set upon."*

> ⚠ **The word ⁨عصبية⁩ has two different senses and the room may know only the bad one.** The Shamela
> edition's own editorial footnote at ⁨ج۱ ص۴۱⁩ makes the split explicit: ⁨عصبية⁩ as *defending your
> kinsman's honour* is **⁨ممدوحة⁩**; the ⁨عصبية⁩ condemned in the ḥadīth «⁨ليس منا من دعا إلى عصبية⁩» is
> partisanship *for* one tribe *against* another «⁨لغير ديانة⁩». **Say this out loud before using the
> word, or half the room will hear the condemned sense and stop listening.**
> ⚠ That footnote also carries the ḥadīth. **It is a modern editor's note, not ⁨ابن خلدون⁩'s text, and
> we have not page-cited it from any ḥadīth source. `(to verify)` — the distinction may be stated;
> the ḥadīth may not be quoted or given a takhrīj.**

## 4.2 The Qurʾānic anchor he chooses — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩

He grounds the point on the brothers of ⁨یوسف عليه السلام⁩ saying «⁨لئن أكله الذّئب ونحن عصبة إنّا إذا⁩
⁨لخاسرون⁩», and glosses it:

> والمعنى أنّه لا يتوهّم العدوان على أحد مع وجود العصبة له
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/158
> *"The meaning is that aggression against a man is not even to be imagined while he has a kin-group
> behind him."*

## 4.3 The generalisation that makes it structural, not sentimental — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩

> وإذا تبيّن ذلك في السّكنى الّتي تحتاج للمدافعة والحماية فبمثله يتبيّن لك في كلّ أمر يحمل النّاس
> عليه من نبوة أو إقامة ملك أو دعوة إذ بلوغ الغرض من ذلك كلّه إنّما يتمّ بالقتال عليه لما في طبائع
> البشر من الاستعصاء ولا بدّ في القتال من العصبيّة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/158
> *"And once this is clear in the matter of dwelling, which needs defence and protection, the like
> becomes clear to you in every undertaking men are called to — prophethood, the founding of a
> realm, or a summons — since attaining the aim in any of these is only completed by fighting for
> it, given what is in human nature of resistance; and in fighting there is no doing without
> عصبية."*

## 4.4 The isolated man, and what happens to him on the day of battle — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩

> وأمّا المتفرّدون في أنسابهم فقلّ أن تصيب أحدا منهم نعرة على صاحبه فإذا أظلم الجوّ بالشّرّ يوم
> الحرب تسلّل كلّ واحد منهم يبغي النّجاة لنفسه
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/158
> *"As for men standing alone in their lineage, seldom does any of them feel that surge on his
> comrade's behalf; so when the air darkens with evil on the day of battle, each of them slips away
> seeking his own escape."*

## 4.5 Leadership does not leave the lineage — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۴⁩ and ⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۵⁩

Two chapters, one claim in two steps.

**Step one — within a kin-group, leadership sits in one branch and moves only to the strongest branch
of that same stock** (⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۴⁩):

> والرّئاسة فيهم إنّما تكون في نصاب واحد منهم ولا تكون في الكلّ
> … فلا تزال في ذلك النّصاب متناقلة من فرع منهم إلى فرع ولا تنتقل إلّا إلى الأقوى من فروعه
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/162
> *"Leadership among them lies only in one specific stock and not in the whole … it goes on being
> handed within that stock from one branch to another, and passes only to the strongest of its
> branches."*

**Step two — an outsider, however long attached, does not acquire it** (⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۵⁩):

> والسّاقط في نسبهم بالجملة لا تكون له عصبيّة فيهم بالنّسب إنّما هو ملصق لزيق وغاية التّعصّب له
> بالولاء والحلف وذلك لا يوجب له غلبا عليهم البتّة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۵⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/163
> *"One who has no standing at all in their lineage has no ⁨عصبية⁩ among them by descent; he is merely
> an attached, adhering outsider, and the most that can be felt for him is by clientage or alliance
> — and that gives him no ascendancy over them whatsoever."*

> ⚠ **HANDLING — read this before letting it near ⁨سقيفة⁩.** This is a **structural** claim about how
> kin-groups behave, and it is the reason the room will understand why «⁨منا أمير ومنكم أمير⁩» was even
> a thinkable proposal. It is **not** an argument about the merits of what happened at ⁨سقيفة⁩, and
> **it is not evidence for or against any position taken there.** ⁨ابن خلدون رحمہ اللہ⁩ himself never
> applies it to ⁨سقيفة⁩ (§7.3). Use it to explain the *shape* of the argument the men in that shed were
> having. **Do not use it to say who was right.**

## 4.6 Where ⁨عصبية⁩ ends — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۷۴⁩

> فالتّغلّب الملكيّ غاية للعصبيّة كما رأيت
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۷۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/172
> *"Royal ascendancy is the end-point towards which ⁨عصبية⁩ runs, as you have seen."*

And, on the same page, the fusion rule that a session on the ⁨ردة⁩ actually needs:

> ثمّ إنّ القبيل الواحد وإن كانت فيه بيوتات مفترقة وعصبيّات متعدّدة فلا بدّ من عصبيّة تكون أقوى من
> جميعها تغلبها وتستتبعها وتلتحم جميع العصبيّات فيها وتصير كأنّها عصبيّة واحدة كبرى وإلّا وقع
> الافتراق المفضي إلى الاختلاف والتّنازع
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۷۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/172
> *"Then, though a single tribe may contain separate houses and several ⁨عصبيات⁩, there must be one
> ⁨عصبية⁩ stronger than them all, which overcomes them and draws them after it, so that all the
> ⁨عصبيات⁩ fuse into it and become as if one single great ⁨عصبية⁩ — otherwise there follows the
> fragmentation that leads on to disagreement and strife."*

> ⚠ **«⁨الغاية التي تجري إليها العصبية هي الملك⁩» is the seed of the ⁨خلافت⁩ → ⁨ملوکیت⁩ theme, which
> `CLAUDE.md` §1.5 flags as one of the two evenings that will carry real charge with this
> organisation (session 4).** It belongs there, framed as ⁨ابن خلدون⁩'s reading. **Do not deploy it in
> session 1.** Recorded here so session 4 does not re-research it.

# 5. THE PAYOFF — WHY A CALL MULTIPLIES A PEOPLE'S STRENGTH

This is what the strand was sent for, and the pages deliver.

## 5.1 ⭐ ⁨الفصل الخامس⁩, **⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩** — the chapter title is itself the thesis

> [الفصل الخامس في أن الدعوة الدينية تزيد الدولة في أصلها قوة على قوة العصبية التي كانت لها من عددها]
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/196
> *"Chapter Five: that a religious call adds to a dynasty, at its root, strength over and above the
> strength of the ⁨عصبية⁩ it already had from its numbers."*
>
> *(The square brackets are the edition's own, marking a chapter heading supplied from the
> manuscript's table of contents.)*

The mechanism, in the body of that chapter on the same page:

> والسّبب في ذلك كما قدّمناه أنّ الصّبغة الدّينيّة تذهب بالتنافس والتّحاسد الّذي في أهل العصبيّة
> وتفرد الوجهة إلى الحقّ فإذا حصل لهم الاستبصار في أمرهم لم يقف لهم شيء لأنّ الوجهة واحدة والمطلوب
> متساو عندهم وهم مستميتون عليه
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/196
> *"The reason for it, as we have said, is that the religious colouring takes away the rivalry and
> mutual envy that are in people of ⁨عصبية⁩, and makes their direction single, towards the truth. Then,
> once clear sight in their affair is theirs, nothing stands in their way — because the direction is
> one, the object is equal in all their eyes, and they will die for it."*

**And he names our own period as the worked example**, same page:

> وهذا كما وقع للعرب صدر الإسلام في الفتوحات فكانت جيوش المسلمين بالقادسيّة واليرموك بضعة وثلاثين
> ألفا في كلّ معسكر وجموع فارس مائة وعشرين ألفا بالقادسيّة وجموع هرقل على ما قاله الواقديّ أربعمائة
> ألف فلم يقف للعرب أحد من الجانبين
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/196
> *"And this is what happened to the Arabs at the outset of Islam, in the conquests: the armies of
> the Muslims at al-Qādisiyya and al-Yarmūk were thirty-odd thousand in each camp, and the hosts of
> Persia one hundred and twenty thousand at al-Qādisiyya, and the hosts of Heraclius — according to
> what al-Wāqidī said — four hundred thousand; and nobody on either side stood before the Arabs."*

> ⭐ **This is the superb slide the strand predicted.** A peninsula that had just fragmented — the
> ⁨ردة⁩ — moves against two empires within two years. ⁨ابن خلدون رحمہ اللہ⁩ does not explain that by
> multiplying the Muslims' numbers. He explains it by **subtracting their internal rivalry**. The
> ⁨عصبية⁩ was already there; what the call did was stop it pointing inward.
> ⚠ **The figures in this block are his and are governed by §3.4 and §8. Quote the mechanism; caption
> the numbers as transmitted, and give the other figure he himself records at ⁨ج۱ ص۱۴⁩.**

## 5.2 The Qurʾānic anchor of the mechanism — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۷⁩–۱۹۸

The preceding chapter («⁨الفصل الرابع في أن الدول العامة الاستيلاء العظيمة الملك أصلها الدين اما من⁩
⁨نبوة أو دعوة حق⁩») grounds the same claim on ⁨الأنفال ۶۳⁩:

> وجمع القلوب وتأليفها إنّما يكون بمعونة من الله في إقامة دينه قال تعالى «لَوْ أَنْفَقْتَ ما في
> الْأَرْضِ جَمِيعاً ما أَلَّفْتَ بَيْنَ قُلُوبِهِمْ ٨: ٦٣»
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/195
> *"And the gathering of hearts and their joining comes only by a help from Allah in the
> establishment of His religion. He said, exalted be He: 'Had you spent all that is in the earth, you
> could not have joined their hearts.'"*
>
> *(«٨: ٦٣» is the edition's own inline sūra:āya reference, printed inside the quotation.)*

And the completion of it at the top of ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩:

> إذا انصرفت إلى الحقّ ورفضت الدّنيا والباطل وأقبلت على الله اتّحدت وجهتها فذهب التّنافس وقلّ الخلاف
> وحسن التّعاون والتّعاضد واتّسع نطاق الكلمة لذلك فعظمت الدّولة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/196
> *"When [hearts] turn to the truth, renounce the world and falsehood, and turn towards Allah, their
> direction becomes one; rivalry departs, disagreement grows scarce, cooperation and mutual support
> become good, and the compass of the word widens — so the dynasty becomes great."*

> ✅ **The verse is the safest possible carrier for the whole idea in this room.** It puts the cause
> where the audience already puts it, and it lets the analytical point be made without the speaker
> appearing to explain the ⁨فتوحات⁩ in purely worldly terms.
> ⚠ Qurʾānic text on any slide goes in `Traditional Arabic` Naskh and must be eyeballed against a
> muṣḥaf, per `CLAUDE.md` §1.3.

## 5.3 The reverse test — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۹⁩

He immediately supplies the falsification condition, which is what makes it an argument rather than a
slogan:

> واعتبر ذلك إذا حالت صبغة الدّين وفسدت كيف ينتقض الأمر ويصير الغلب على نسبة العصبيّة وحدها دون
> زيادة الدّين
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/197
> *"And take note of this: when the colouring of religion alters and decays, how the whole matter
> unravels, and ascendancy reverts to the measure of ⁨عصبية⁩ alone, without the increment of
> religion."*

## 5.4 The other half of the same law — a call without a people does not carry, ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۹⁩

> [الفصل السادس في أن الدعوة الدينية من غير عصبية لا تتم]
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/197
> *"Chapter Six: that a religious call without ⁨عصبية⁩ does not come to completion."*

> ⚠ On that page he cites «⁨ما بعث الله نبيّا إلّا في منعة من قومه⁩» and calls it «⁨الحديث الصحيح⁩».
> **We have not verified that ḥadīth from any ḥadīth source, and ⁨ابن خلدون⁩ is not a ḥadīth
> authority. `(to verify)` — do not put it on a slide, do not give it a takhrīj, do not paraphrase it
> as "the Prophet ﷺ said."** The *chapter's own claim* is his and may be used; the ḥadīth may not,
> until it is page-cited from a ḥadīth work.

## 5.5 Why the Arabs specifically needed it — ⁨ج۱ ص۱۸۹⁩–۱۹۰

This chapter does most of the ⁨ردة⁩ work, and it is his, not ours.

> والسّبب في ذلك أنّهم لخلق التّوحّش الّذي فيهم أصعب الأمم انقيادا بعضهم لبعض للغلظة والأنفة وبعد
> الهمّة والمنافسة في الرّئاسة فقلّما تجتمع أهواؤهم فإذا كان الدّين بالنّبوءة أو الولاية كان الوازع
> لهم من أنفسهم وذهب خلق الكبر والمنافسة منهم فسهل انقيادهم واجتماعهم
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۸۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/187
> *"The reason is that, because of the wildness of temper in them, they are the hardest of peoples to
> make yield one to another — from harshness, from pride, from far-reaching ambition, and from
> rivalry over leadership; so their inclinations seldom converge. But when religion comes by
> prophethood or by sanctity, the restrainer over them is from within themselves, and the trait of
> arrogance and rivalry departs from them, so their yielding and their coming together become easy."*

And, on the same page, the sentence that keeps this from reading as contempt:

> وهم مع ذلك أسرع النّاس قبولا للحقّ والهدى لسلامة طباعهم من عوج الملكات وبراءتها من ذميم الأخلاق
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۸۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/187
> *"And they are, with all that, the quickest of people to accept truth and guidance, because of the
> soundness of their natures from the crookedness of acquired habits and their freedom from
> blameworthy character."*

The completion at ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۰⁩:

> وإنّما يصيرون إليها بعد انقلاب طباعهم وتبدّلها بصبغة دينيّة تمحو ذلك منهم وتجعل الوازع لهم من
> أنفسهم
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/188
> *"They come to it only after their natures are overturned and changed by a religious colouring that
> erases that from them and makes the restrainer over them come from within themselves."*

> ⚠ **The saying about ⁨رستم⁩ on ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۰⁩.** Immediately after the above he records:
> «كان رستم [٢] إذا رأى المسلمين يجتمعون للصّلاة يقول أكل عمر كبدي يعلّم الكلاب الآداب».
> It is a striking line — the Persian commander watching prayer ranks form and recognising that what
> he faces is discipline rather than numbers.
>
> ❗ **It is worse than uncorroborated: in the approved works «⁨أكل عمر كبدي⁩» is a different man's
> line entirely.** ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۴⁩ (https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3380) and ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۳۹۹⁩
> (https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1090) both give it to **⁨أبو لؤلؤة فيروز غلام المغيرة بن شعبة⁩**,
> weeping over the captives of ⁨نهاوند⁩. ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۴⁩ prints «⁨جعل أبو لؤلؤة⁩ -⁨فيروز غلام المغيرة بن⁩
> ⁨شعبة⁩- ⁨لا يلقى منهم صغيرًا إلا مسح رأسه وبكى وقال⁩: **⁨آكل⁩** ⁨عُمر كبدي⁩»; ⁨الکامل ج۲ ص۳۹۹⁩ prints the
> same report with «⁨وَقَالَ لَهُ⁩: **⁨أَكَلَ⁩** ⁨عُمَرُ كَبِدِي⁩». *(The ⁨آكل⁩ / ⁨أكل⁩ variant is the two
> editions', and is recorded rather than silently harmonised.)*
> **And ⁨ابن خلدون⁩ himself gives it to ⁨أبو لؤلؤة⁩ in volume 2:**
>
> فكان إذا لقي سبّي نهاوند بالمدينة يبكي ويقول: أكل عمر كبدي.
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۵۵۸⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1355
> *English:* "And when he met the captives of Nahawand in Medina he would weep and say: ʿUmar has

>
> Only «⁨يعلّم الكلاب الآداب⁩» is peculiar to ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۰⁩, and it is found on no other page in any cached
> tree.
>
> **So the ⁨رستم⁩ version is a third internal contradiction inside his own book (cf. §3.4), not merely
> an unverified saying.** Add to that: it is a saying, and `DECISIONS.md` #29 forbids him as the
> authority for a wording; and «⁨الكلاب⁩» is contempt for the Muslims that will land badly however it
> is framed. Recorded so nobody re-researches it. **CUT** — see card E-IKX3.

# 6. HIS NARRATIVE OF ۱۱–⁨۲۳ھ⁩ — WHAT IS ACTUALLY THERE

## 6.1 ⭐ The negative finding, and it is the most important thing in this section

All 95 cached pages of indices **1276–1370** (⁨ج۲ ص۴۷۹⁩–۵۷۳) were searched for every analytical marker
he uses in volume 1 — «⁨والسبب في ذلك⁩»، «⁨وإنما كان ذلك⁩»، «⁨قلت⁩:»، «⁨والذي عندي⁩»، «⁨فاعلم أن⁩»، «⁨وهذا من⁩»،
«والصحيح»، «إن صح».

**Two hits in ninety-five pages.** One is a textual preference about where ⁨النعمان ؓ⁩ was killed
(«⁨والصحيح أنّ النعمان قتل بنهاوند⁩», ⁨ج۲ ص۵۵۹⁩); the other is §6.2 below.

**Conclusion, and it must govern how he is used in this session:**

> **His narrative of ۱۱–⁨۲۳ھ⁩ is narration, not analysis. It is a compression of what ⁨الطبری⁩ carries,
> written faster and shorter. The judgement lives in volume 1, four hundred pages away, and it never
> once names the ⁨ردة⁩. Joining the two is our editorial act, and the slide must say so.**

That is a genuinely useful result: nobody needs to re-read volume 2 hoping for judgements, and it
draws the boundary of §7.2 in the sharpest possible way.

## 6.2 The one real judgement in the narrative — on the ⁨بيعة⁩, ⁨ج۲ ص۴۸۹⁩

> أجمع المهاجرون والأنصار على بيعة أبي بكر ولم يخالف إلا سعد إن صحّ خلافه فلم يلتفت إليه لشذوذه.
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۴۸۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1286
> *"The Muhājirūn and the Anṣār were of one mind on the pledge to Abū Bakr ؓ, and none dissented but
> Saʿd ؓ — if his dissent is even established — and no notice was taken of it, it standing alone."*

**What is useful here is the constitutional point, not the naming.** He is saying that an ⁨إجماع⁩ is
not broken by a single abstention, and he is careful enough to insert **«⁨إن صحّ خلافه⁩»** — *if the
dissent is even established* — before drawing any conclusion. **That double caution is itself worth
showing the room.**

> ⚠ **HANDLING.** ⁨سعد بن عبادة ؓ⁩ is a Companion, and `CLAUDE.md` §1.6 tells us to handle this
> territory warmly and without adjudication. **Do not put the word «⁨لشذوذه⁩» on a slide, and do not
> put ⁨سعد ؓ⁩'s name beside it.** If the point is used at all, use the *principle* — a settled ⁨بيعة⁩ is
> not unsettled by one man's abstention, and even that is stated conditionally by ⁨ابن خلدون⁩ — and
> leave the name out. If asked from the floor, answer from ⁨البدایہ⁩ (which `saqifah-bani-saida.md`
> covers in full) and not from him.
> ⚠ **He also repeats, immediately before that sentence on ⁨ج۲ ص۴۸۹⁩, the two lines of verse claiming
> the jinn killed ⁨سعد ؓ⁩.** That is precisely the sort of report §2 of this note teaches us to reject.
> **Never repeat it, in any form.**

## 6.3 The one conceptual gift the narrative does contain — ⁨ج۲ ص۴۸۹⁩ and ⁨ج۲ ص۴۹۰⁩

He states as a **general rule** what ⁨ابن كثير رحمہ اللہ⁩ shows in a single case (⁨بنو تميم⁩, ⁨البدایہ⁩
⁨ج۷ ص۲۸⁩ — see `arabian-tribes-and-the-ridda-setup.md` §3.5):

> وقد ارتدت [١] العرب إمّا القبيلة مستوعبة وإمّا بعض منها
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۴۸۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1286
> *"The Arabs had turned back — either the whole tribe entire, or a part of it."*
>
> *(«[١]» is the edition's footnote marker recording a variant reading, «⁨أرادت⁩», in a second
> manuscript — itself a small reminder of §1.3.)*

And, listing the fronts at ⁨ج۲ ص۴۹۰⁩, he records a **third** state beyond broke/held:

> واستغلظ أمر مسيلمة واجتمع على طليحة عوام طيِّئ وأسد وارتدت غطفان **وتوقفت هوازن فأمسكوا الصدقة**،
> وارتد خواص من بني سليم
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۲ ص۴۹۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1287
> *"The affair of Musaylima grew formidable; the general body of Ṭayyiʾ and Asad gathered around
> Ṭulayḥa; Ghaṭafān turned back; **and Hawāzin held off, withholding the ṣadaqa**; and particular men
> of Banū Sulaym turned back."*

> ✅ **This corroborates the existing note's central map argument from a second author.** No honest
> map of ⁨۱۱ھ⁩ shades a tribe one colour, because the sources themselves record whole-tribe,
> part-tribe and hold-off as three different things. **Two authors now say so.**

## 6.4 A contradiction, recorded and NOT resolved — who did not break

| Author | The formula | Page |
|---|---|---|
| **⁨ابن كثير رحمہ اللہ⁩**, quoting ⁨ابن إسحاق⁩ | «⁨ارتدَّتِ العربُ⁩ … **⁨ما خلا أهل المسجدين، مكة، والمدينة⁩**» — with **⁨ثقيف⁩ at ⁨الطائف⁩** added separately as having held | ⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۹؛ ج۷ ص۹⁩ |
| **⁨ابن خلدون رحمہ اللہ⁩** | «⁨وقد جاء الخبر بارتداد العرب عامّة وخاصة⁩ **⁨إلا قريشا وثقيفا⁩**» | ⁨ج۲ ص۴۹۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/1287 |

**These are not the same statement.** ⁨ابن كثير⁩ exempts two *cities*; ⁨ابن خلدون⁩ exempts two *tribes*
— a formula which, read strictly, leaves the ⁨أنصار⁩ unexempted, which nobody holds. It is very
probably a compression rather than a claim. **Record it; do not resolve it; and do not use ⁨ابن⁩
⁨خلدون⁩'s formula on any artifact.** The map and the script take ⁨ابن كثير⁩'s wording, which the existing
note already establishes.

## 6.5 The sentence everyone quotes — and it is not his

At ⁨ج۲ ص۴۸۹⁩ he writes «⁨ونجم النفاق والمسلمون كالغنم في الليلة الممطرة لقلّتهم وكثرة عدوّهم وإظلام⁩
⁨الجوّ بفقد نبيّهم⁩». It is a famous image and it is **not his**. ⁨البدایہ⁩ carries it twice, from two
different chains:

> ولما تُوفِّي رسولُ اللَّه ﷺ ارتدَّتِ العَرب، واشْرأبَّتِ اليَهوديةُ والنَّصْرانيّةُ ونَجَمَ
> النّفاقُ، وصارَ المُسْلِمون كالغنم المَطيرةِ في اللَّيلة الشّاتية، لفقدِ نَبيّهم ﷺ، حتى جَمَعَهم
> اللَّهُ على أبي بكر ﵁.
> — ⁨البدایہ والنہایہ ج۵ ص۳۹۷، عن ابن إسحاق⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/2581
> *"When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ died the Arabs turned back, Judaism and Christianity stretched out
> their necks, and hypocrisy showed its head; and the Muslims became like sheep out in the rain on a
> winter's night, for the loss of their Prophet ﷺ — until Allah gathered them upon Abū Bakr ؓ."*

The same image again at **⁨البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰⁩**, through ⁨سيف بن عمر عن هشام بن عروة عن أبيه⁩, in the
context of ⁨أبو بكر ؓ⁩ insisting on sending ⁨جيش أسامة ؓ⁩ («⁨والمسلمون كالغنم المَطِيرة⁩ (٣) ⁨في الليلة⁩
الشاتيةِ، لفقد نبيهم ﷺ، وقلَّتهم وكثرة عدوّهم»).

> ✅ **Use ⁨البدایہ ج۵ ص۳۹۷⁩ as the citation.** It is on the safe list, it is the earlier source, and it
> names ⁨ابن إسحاق⁩. **Never credit the image to ⁨ابن خلدون⁩.** This is the model for how to handle
> anything vivid found in his narrative.

## 6.6 Explanations he records — one of them usable, two not

Three places in volume 2 where he records **an explanation rather than an event**. All three are
excellent for a lecture. **(c) is corroborated almost verbatim in ⁨الکامل⁩ and is therefore usable —
cited to ⁨الکامل⁩, never to him. (a) and (b) are not.**

> ⚠ **A correction to an earlier pass, and the reason matters.** A previous sweep reported all three
> as "not found in ⁨البدایہ، سیر، الکامل⁩". That sweep grepped the bare consonantal phrases against a

**(a) Why the frontier could not simply be held** — ⁨الهرمزان⁩'s advice to ⁨عمر ؓ⁩ after his capture,

> إنّ الحجاز ليس لكم بدار إلّا على النّجعة ولا يقوى عليه أهله إلّا بذلك أين القرّاء المهاجرون عن

# 7. WHAT COULD **NOT** BE ESTABLISHED

**7.1 · No judgement by him on the ⁨ردة⁩, anywhere.** He never writes a sentence explaining why the ⁨ردة⁩
took the shape it did. The two chapters that would explain it (⁨ج۱ ص۱۸۹⁩ on the Arabs needing a ⁨صبغة⁩
⁨دينية⁩; ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩ on the call multiplying ⁨عصبية⁩) **never mention the ⁨ردة⁩**, and the ⁨ردة⁩ narrative
(⁨ج۲ ص۴۸۹⁩–۵۰۶) **never mentions ⁨عصبية⁩**. *Where looked:* all of indices 11–51, 155–200 and 1276–1370,
plus a marker search across all 95 narrative pages (§6.1).

**7.2 · Therefore: the ⁨ردة⁩/⁨عصبية⁩ connection is OURS, not his — and every artifact must say so.** The
honest slide sentence is: *"Ibn Khaldūn does not write about the ridda. He writes about how kin-groups
hold together and what a religious call does to them. Setting his account beside what the narrative
works record about ⁨۱۱ھ⁩ is our doing, not his."* **Anything stronger than that misrepresents him.**
This is the single most important limit in this note.

**7.3 · He never applies the "leadership stays in the lineage" principle to ⁨سقيفة⁩.** The principle is
at ⁨ج۱ ص۱۶۴⁩–۱۶۵; the ⁨سقيفة⁩ narrative is at ⁨ج۲ ص۴۸۷⁩–۴۸۹; **he does not join them.** Neither may we,
beyond explaining the *shape* of the argument (§4.5 handling note).

**7.4 · The ḥadīth «⁨ما بعث الله نبيّا إلّا في منعة من قومه⁩»** — he calls it ⁨صحيح⁩ (⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۹⁩). Not
verified from any ḥadīth source in this pass. `(to verify)` — unusable.

**7.5 · The ḥadīth «⁨ليس منا من دعا إلى عصبية⁩»** — appears only in the **modern editor's** footnote at
⁨ج۱ ص۴۱⁩, not in ⁨ابن خلدون⁩'s text, and referred there to ⁨الجامع الصغير⁩. Not verified. `(to verify)` —
unusable as a citation, though the *distinction* it draws (praiseworthy vs blameworthy ⁨عصبية⁩) is
sound and can be stated without it.

# EVENT CARDS

**Read this before using any card.** Every card below is a card about **a judgement**, not about an
event. **Map** is `n/a` for nearly all of them, because the ideas are not located anywhere. The rule
that governs all of them without exception: **his name goes on the claim.** *"Ibn Khaldūn reads it
this way"* is the sentence; *"this is what happened"* never is.

### E-IKM1 · The count is the weakest part of the story
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** n/a — a claim about method, not a dated event · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn opens his book with a warning, and it is not about lying. It is about
counting. He says that when historians report sums of money and the sizes of armies, that is exactly
where a report is likeliest to be false. He does not say the battles did not happen. He says the
numbers travelled differently from the events. His reason is simple. Nobody was ever going to check.

**The statement:**
> ولا سيّما في إحصاء الأعداد من الأموال والعساكر إذا عرضت في الحكايات إذ هي مظنّة الكذب ومطيّة الهذر
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۳⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/11
> *English:* "— and especially in the counting of sums of money and of armies when they appear in the
> narratives, for these are the place where falsehood is to be expected and the mount that idle talk
> rides."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** A number nobody could check is the part of a story that travels least well.
**Hands-up?** Yes — *"When a report says an army was a hundred thousand men, who counted them?"*

### E-IKM2 · Two figures for one army, in one book
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn warned that army-counts are the likeliest thing in a history to be
wrong. Then he wrote his own history. On page fourteen he records the Persian host at al-Qādisiyya
twice: a hundred and twenty thousand on one transmission, sixty thousand on another. He does not
choose between them. A hundred and eighty-four pages later, arguing a different point, he uses the
larger figure. The man is not being careless. He is showing us, in his own hand, exactly what he told us to
expect.

**The statement:**
> وأعظم ما كانت جموعهم بالقادسيّة مائة وعشرين ألفا كلّهم متبوع على ما نقله سيف … وعن عائشة والزّهريّ
> فإنّ جموع رستم الّذين زحف بهم سعد بالقادسيّة إنّما كانوا ستّين ألفا كلّهم متبوع
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/12
> *English:* "The largest their host ever was at al-Qādisiyya was one hundred and twenty thousand,
> every one of them a man with followers, according to what Sayf transmitted … And from ʿĀʾisha ؓ and
> al-Zuhrī: the host of Rustam against which Saʿd ؓ advanced at al-Qādisiyya was only sixty thousand,
> every one of them a man with followers."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** The honest historian is not the one whose numbers agree; he is the one who shows you that
they do not.
**Hands-up?** No. Put ⁨ج۱ ص۱۴⁩ and ⁨ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩ side by side and let the room read them.

### E-IKM3 · Seven ways a report goes wrong, and partisanship is the first
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn does not treat falsehood in history as the moral failure of individual
liars. He treats it as a thing with causes, and he lists them. Seven. The first is partisanship for an
opinion or a school. His point is unsettling and exact. It is not that a partisan lies. It is that a
partisan stops checking, because the report already agrees with him. The scrutiny he would give
anything else never gets given at all.

**The statement:**
> وإذا خامرها تشيّع لرأي أو نحلة قبلت ما يوافقها من الأخبار لأوّل وهلة وكان ذلك الميل والتّشيّع غطاء
> على عين بصيرتها عن الانتقاد والتّمحيص
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۶⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/44
> *English:* "When partisanship for some opinion or creed has got into it, it accepts whatever agrees
> with it at first hearing — and that inclination and partisanship become a covering over the eye of
> its insight, keeping it from criticism and scrutiny."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** The report you are least able to test is the one that already agrees with you.
**Hands-up?** No. This one lands better read plainly than asked about.

### E-IKM4 · A fiqh test and a history test are not the same test
**Tier:** CORE — **this is the guardrail** · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn draws a line that this whole course rests on, and he draws it in one
sentence. Checking the chain of transmitters, he says, is the right test for a legal report — because
a legal report imposes an obligation, and an obligation must be secure before it binds. But a report
about an event needs something else first. It needs to be possible. If a thing could not have
happened, examining who narrated it is wasted effort.

**The statement:**
> وإنّما كان التّعديل والتّجريح هو المعتبر في صحّة الأخبار الشّرعيّة لأنّ معظمها تكاليف إنشائيّة
> أوجب الشّارع العمل بها … وأما الأخبار عن الواقعات فلا بدّ في صدقها وصحّتها من اعتبار المطابقة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/47
> *English:* "Vetting the transmitters is the operative test for the soundness of legal reports,
> because most of them are prescriptive obligations the Lawgiver has made binding … But as for
> reports about events, their truth and soundness require testing them against correspondence."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** A report of an event is neither a ruling nor a creed; it is a record, and it is weighed as
one.
**Hands-up?** No. Deliver it flat and move on.

### E-IKM5 · The disease that only shows itself after centuries
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn names an error he calls hard to catch, and he is right. It is the habit
of reading a later century's meanings back into an earlier one. He calls it a deep-seated disease,
extremely well hidden, because it only becomes visible after long stretches of time have passed. A man
is not wrong about the fact. He is wrong about what the fact meant to the people it happened to.

**The statement:**
> ومن الغلط الخفيّ في التّاريخ الذّهول عن تبدّل الأحوال في الأمم والأجيال بتبدّل الأعصار ومرور
> الأيّام وهو داء دوي شديد الخفاء
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۳۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/35
> *English:* "Among the hidden errors in history is inattention to the fact that the conditions of
> peoples and generations change as the ages change and the days pass. It is a deep-seated disease,
> extremely hard to detect."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** Distance in time is not only distance in dates; the words themselves have moved.
**Hands-up?** Yes — *"If someone in the year 2500 read that a man taught school, what would they
assume about him?"*

### E-IKM6 · The schoolteacher's son
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** the point concerns ⁨الحجاج بن يوسف⁩, d. ⁨۹۵ھ⁩ `[STANDARD]` (to verify) ·
**Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn gives an example of that error, and it is a small, clean one. People
read that al-Ḥajjāj's father taught the Qurʾān and conclude the family were nobodies. He answers that

century wrong.

**The statement:**
> والحجّاج بن يوسف كان أبوه من سادات ثقيف وأشرافهم … ولم يكن تعليمه للقرآن على ما هو الأمر عليه لهذا
> العهد من أنّه حرفة للمعاش
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۴۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/38
> *English:* "As for al-Ḥajjāj ibn Yūsuf, his father was among the chiefs and nobles of Thaqīf … and
> his teaching of the Qurʾān was not what the matter is in our own day, that it is a trade for a
> living."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** The same word does not carry the same weight in two different centuries.
**Hands-up?** No.

### E-IKM7 · He tests the method on a figure nobody is attached to
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn wanted to show how to test a number, so he picked one nobody in his
audience had a stake in. Historians reported six hundred thousand fighting men counted in the
wilderness. He asked four questions. Could that land feed them. Could a field hold them. Did any
empire we know of ever put such a number on a battlefield. And could one family line reach that size
in four generations. The answers ruled the figure out. He never had to accuse anyone of lying.

**The statement:**
> والحاضر يشهد لذلك فالماضي أشبه بالآتي من الماء بالماء.
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/12
> *English:* "And the present bears witness to this — for the past resembles what is to come as water
> resembles water."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** What you can see today is the only measuring stick you have for what you cannot.
**Hands-up?** Yes — *"How would you check a number nobody alive ever counted?"*

### E-IKM8 · He calls al-Bukhārī a historian
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** A room that trusts the Ṣaḥīḥ absolutely can still be wary of the word history. Ibn
Khaldūn closes that gap in one clause. He says the ancients held history in the highest regard, and
then names the men who took it up. Al-Ṭabarī. Al-Bukhārī. And Ibn Isḥāq before both of them. These are
not three kinds of men. They are scholars of the umma working one discipline with different aims.

**The statement:**
> وما استكبر القدماء علم التّاريخ إلّا لذلك حتّى انتحله الطّبريّ والبخاريّ وابن إسحاق من قبلهما

> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۳۷⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/35
> *English:* "The ancients held the science of history in such high regard only for this reason —
> so much so that al-Ṭabarī and al-Bukhārī, and Ibn Isḥāq before them, and their like among the
> scholars of the umma, took it up."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** History was never outside the scholarship of this umma; it was one of its instruments.
**Hands-up?** No.

### E-IKA1 · What actually holds a people together
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a — but this is the concept the ⁨۱۱ھ⁩ tribal map rests on

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn gives kinship a name and a job. He does not call it a virtue and he
does not call it a vice. He says the bond of kin is simply natural in people, and that part of that
bond is the surge of feeling when a relative is wronged. That surge is what makes a group defensible.
He anchors it on the brothers of Yūsuf, peace be upon him, saying that nobody would dare touch a man
who has a kin-group behind him.

**The statement:**
> وذلك أنّ صلة الرّحم طبيعيّ في البشر إلّا في الأقلّ ومن صلتها النّعرة على ذوي القربى وأهل الأرحام
> أن ينالهم ضيم أو تصيبهم هلكة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/158
> *English:* "For the bond of kinship is natural in human beings, save in the fewest of cases; and
> part of that bond is the surge of feeling on behalf of close relatives and kinsfolk, that no wrong
> should reach them and no ruin befall them."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** Before there were states in Arabia there were kin, and kin were the only protection anybody
had.
**Hands-up?** Yes — *"In a land with no police and no courts, who protects you?"*
⚠ Say the two senses of ⁨عصبية⁩ out loud before this card (§4.1), or half the room hears the condemned
sense.

### E-IKA2 · The man standing alone slips away in the dark
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn explains what happens to a man with no kin behind him. Not in peace —
in battle. He writes that a man alone in his lineage feels no surge on anyone's behalf, and nobody
feels one on his. So when the air darkens with evil on the day of fighting, each such man slips away
looking to save himself. It is not an accusation of cowardice. It is an observation that there was
nothing holding him there.

**The statement:**
> وأمّا المتفرّدون في أنسابهم فقلّ أن تصيب أحدا منهم نعرة على صاحبه فإذا أظلم الجوّ بالشّرّ يوم
> الحرب تسلّل كلّ واحد منهم يبغي النّجاة لنفسه
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/158
> *English:* "As for men standing alone in their lineage, seldom does any of them feel that surge on
> his comrade's behalf; so when the air darkens with evil on the day of battle, each of them slips
> away seeking his own escape."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** Men hold a line for the people beside them before they hold it for anything else.
**Hands-up?** No.

### E-IKA3 · Nothing gets carried without a people to carry it
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn takes the point about defending a camp and widens it as far as it will
go. Anything that has to be carried against resistance, he says, needs a group behind it. He names
three things in one breath: prophethood, the founding of a realm, and a call. Human beings resist;
resistance has to be met; and meeting it takes people who will stand together. He states it as a rule
with no exceptions.

**The statement:**
> فبمثله يتبيّن لك في كلّ أمر يحمل النّاس عليه من نبوة أو إقامة ملك أو دعوة إذ بلوغ الغرض من ذلك
> كلّه إنّما يتمّ بالقتال عليه لما في طبائع البشر من الاستعصاء ولا بدّ في القتال من العصبيّة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۰⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/158
> *English:* "The like becomes clear to you in every undertaking men are called to — prophethood, the
> founding of a realm, or a summons — since attaining the aim in any of these is only completed by
> fighting for it, given what is in human nature of resistance; and in fighting there is no doing
> without ⁨عصبية⁩."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** A truth still needs people willing to stand for it before it moves anything in the world.
**Hands-up?** No.

### E-IKA4 · Leadership does not leave the line it started in
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn makes a claim about how leadership moves inside a kin-group, and it
explains the shape of a great many arguments in early Arabia. Leadership, he says, sits in one branch
of a stock, not in the whole of it, and it passes only to the strongest branch of that same stock. An
outsider, however long attached and however trusted, does not acquire it. He is an attached man, and
attachment gives no ascendancy.

**The statement:**
> والسّاقط في نسبهم بالجملة لا تكون له عصبيّة فيهم بالنّسب إنّما هو ملصق لزيق وغاية التّعصّب له
> بالولاء والحلف وذلك لا يوجب له غلبا عليهم البتّة
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۶۵⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/163
> *English:* "One who has no standing at all in their lineage has no ⁨عصبية⁩ among them by descent; he
> is merely an attached, adhering outsider, and the most that can be felt for him is by clientage or
> alliance — and that gives him no ascendancy over them whatsoever."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** In a world organised by kin, the first question about any leader was not what he had done
but whose he was.
**Hands-up?** No.
⚠ **HANDLING — this card explains the *shape* of the argument at ⁨سقيفة⁩, never its merits.** Ibn
Khaldūn himself never applies it there (§7.3). Do not let it drift into adjudication.

### E-IKA5 · Where kinship-strength ends up
**Tier:** GOOD — **for session 4, not session 1** · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn does not leave group strength as a description. He gives it a
destination. It runs, he says, towards one end, and that end is kingship. Leadership is being
followed; kingship is being obeyed. And a man who has reached the first and can see the road to the
second does not stop, because the human soul wants it. He states this as a natural tendency, not as a
judgement on any particular man.

**The statement:**
> فالتّغلّب الملكيّ غاية للعصبيّة كما رأيت
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۷۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/172
> *English:* "Royal ascendancy is the end-point towards which ⁨عصبية⁩ runs, as you have seen."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** Group strength has a direction of its own, and it does not stop where its holders intended.
**Hands-up?** No.
⚠ **This is the seed of the ⁨خلافت⁩ → ⁨ملوکیت⁩ theme. `CLAUDE.md` §1.5 flags session 4 as one of the two
evenings that carry real charge with this organisation. Deploy it there, attributed to him, and not
in session 1.**

### E-IKA6 · Many small loyalties, or one big one
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn describes what happens inside a single tribe that contains several
houses. Each house has its own loyalty. Unless one of them is strong enough to draw the rest after it
until they fuse into a single great loyalty, the tribe splits, and the splitting leads to disagreement
and then to strife. It is the plainest possible account of why a confederation is either led or is
nothing.

**The statement:**
> فلا بدّ من عصبيّة تكون أقوى من جميعها تغلبها وتستتبعها وتلتحم جميع العصبيّات فيها وتصير كأنّها
> عصبيّة واحدة كبرى وإلّا وقع الافتراق المفضي إلى الاختلاف والتّنازع
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۷۴⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/172
> *English:* "There must be one ⁨عصبية⁩ stronger than them all, which overcomes them and draws them
> after it, so that all the ⁨عصبيات⁩ fuse into it and become as if one single great ⁨عصبية⁩ — otherwise
> there follows the fragmentation that leads on to disagreement and strife."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** A gathering of loyalties either becomes one loyalty or becomes a quarrel.
**Hands-up?** No.

### E-IKR1 · ⭐ The call does not add numbers; it removes rivalry
**Tier:** CORE — **the headline slide of this strand** · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn asks the question anyone looking at the map of the year eleven would
ask. How does a peninsula that has just fragmented move against two empires within two years? His
answer does not multiply anyone's numbers. He says a religious call takes away the rivalry and the
mutual envy that exist among people bound by kin, and points them all one way. And once that happens
nothing stands in front of them — because the direction is one, the object is equal in every eye, and
they will die for it. He names the conquests as his own example.

**The statement:**
> والسّبب في ذلك … أنّ الصّبغة الدّينيّة تذهب بالتنافس والتّحاسد الّذي في أهل العصبيّة وتفرد الوجهة
> إلى الحقّ فإذا حصل لهم الاستبصار في أمرهم لم يقف لهم شيء لأنّ الوجهة واحدة والمطلوب متساو عندهم
> وهم مستميتون عليه
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۸⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/196
> *English:* "The reason for it … is that the religious colouring takes away the rivalry and mutual
> envy that are in people of ⁨عصبية⁩, and makes their direction single, towards the truth. Then, once
> clear sight in their affair is theirs, nothing stands in their way — because the direction is one,
> the object is equal in all their eyes, and they will die for it."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** What changed in Arabia was not how many men there were, but which way they were all facing.
**Hands-up?** Yes — *"Two years after the tribes broke apart, they were moving on two empires. What
had changed?"*
⚠ **The slide must carry his name and the words "as he reads it".** He never wrote this about the
ridda; joining his chapter to the year eleven is our doing (§7.2).

### E-IKR3 · The test that runs the other way
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn does not stop at the claim. He supplies the condition under which it
would fail, which is what makes it an argument and not a slogan. Watch, he says, what happens when the
religious colouring alters and decays. The whole thing unravels, and the outcome falls back to the

same numbers, a different result.

**The statement:**
> واعتبر ذلك إذا حالت صبغة الدّين وفسدت كيف ينتقض الأمر ويصير الغلب على نسبة العصبيّة وحدها دون

> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/197

> matter unravels, and ascendancy reverts to the measure of ⁨عصبية⁩ alone, without the increment of
> religion."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** The increment was never owned; it was only ever held while it was being lived.
**Hands-up?** No.

### E-IKR4 · Why it had to be a religious call and could not have been a political one
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn explains why the peninsula could not have been unified on any other
basis. The Arabs, he writes, were the hardest of peoples to make yield one to another — from
harshness, from pride, from far-reaching ambition, and above all from rivalry over leadership. Their
inclinations, he says, seldom converge. Only when religion came did the restraint come from inside
them instead of being imposed from outside, and then yielding and gathering became easy. And he adds
at once that they were also the quickest of peoples to accept guidance, because their natures were
sound.

**The statement:**
> فقلّما تجتمع أهواؤهم فإذا كان الدّين بالنّبوءة أو الولاية كان الوازع لهم من أنفسهم وذهب خلق الكبر
> والمنافسة منهم فسهل انقيادهم واجتماعهم
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۸۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/187
> *English:* "Their inclinations seldom converge. But when religion comes by prophethood or by
> sanctity, the restrainer over them is from within themselves, and the trait of arrogance and
> rivalry departs from them, so their yielding and their coming together become easy."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** A restraint a man carries inside himself holds where one imposed from outside does not.
**Hands-up?** Yes — *"What would it have taken to make these tribes obey one man?"*
⚠ **Always pair it with the sentence on the same page** — «⁨وهم مع ذلك أسرع النّاس قبولا للحقّ⁩
⁨والهدى⁩» — or the first half reads as contempt for the Arabs and the room will bristle.

### E-IKR5 · A call with no people behind it
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn writes the other half of his own law, and it is the half that keeps him
honest. A religious call without a group behind it, he says, does not come to completion. He means it
structurally, not spiritually: a summons still has to be carried by people who will stand together for
it, and where there is no such body, the summons does not travel. He gives a worked example from his

**The statement:**
> [الفصل السادس في أن الدعوة الدينية من غير عصبية لا تتم]
> — ⁨تاریخ ابن خلدون ج۱ ص۱۹۹⁩ · https://shamela.ws/book/12320/197
> *English:* "Chapter Six: that a religious call without ⁨عصبية⁩ does not come to completion."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** A call still needs a community willing to stand under it.
**Hands-up?** No.
⚠ On that page he cites a ḥadīth and calls it ṣaḥīḥ. **We have not verified it. Use the chapter's
claim, never the ḥadīth (§7.4).**

### E-IKN2 · The sheep in the rain — and who really said it
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** ⁨۱۱ھ⁩ `[SOURCED]` · **Map:** n/a

**What happened:** There is a famous sentence about the state of the Muslims when the Prophet ﷺ died:
that they became like sheep out in the rain on a winter's night. Ibn Khaldūn uses it. It is not his.
Ibn Kathīr carries it twice, once from Ibn Isḥāq and once through a second chain, and Ibn Isḥāq is
the older source by centuries. This is worth saying aloud, because it shows the audience exactly how
a phrase travels, and why the citation has to follow it back.

**The statement:**

> *English:* "And the Muslims became like sheep out in the rain on a winter's night, for the loss of
> their Prophet ﷺ — until Allah gathered them upon Abū Bakr ؓ."

**⁨عبرت⁩:** A sentence worth repeating is worth tracing back to the man who first said it.
**Hands-up?** No.

### E-IKN3 · One dissent does not unmake a consensus — and even that is said conditionally

**What happened:** Ibn Khaldūn's account of the pledge to Abū Bakr ؓ ends with a short constitutional

**Hands-up?** No.

### E-IKX1 · The chapters that must be read whole before they are quoted at all
**Tier:** CUT — recorded so it is never re-researched · **When:** n/a · **Map:** n/a

**What it is:** Two chapters of the Muqaddima are routinely quoted against the Arabs:
«[⁨الفصل الخامس والعشرون في أن العرب لا يتغلبون إلا على البسائط⁩]» (⁨ج۱ ص۱۸۶⁩) and
