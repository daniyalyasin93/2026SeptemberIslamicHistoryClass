# The first conquests — Iraq, the desert march, Syria and اليرموک (12–15 AH)

**Question this note answers:** What can be said, from a page actually read, about
سیدنا خالد بن الولید ؓ in Iraq (12 AH); the crossing from Iraq to Syria; the four commanders
سیدنا ابوبکر ؓ sent to الشام and their sectors; أجنادين, the taking of دمشق, فحل; the battle of
اليرموك — where, who commanded, what numbers the sources give, what was actually said; and the
removal of خالد ؓ from command by سیدنا عمر ؓ — when, why, and in whose words.

## 0. The sentences this note licenses

Each may be said aloud; each carries its page.

1. **The Iraq campaign began as an order, not an ambition.** أبو بكر ؓ wrote to خالد ؓ after
   اليمامة telling him to enter Iraq from its lower end, to call people to God first, then to the
   jizya, and only then to fight — and **not to compel anyone to march with him, and not to use any
   man who had apostatised even if he had returned** — البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۲.
2. **الحيرة surrendered on terms and paid the first jizya taken out of Iraq** — 90,000 dirhams in
   one report, 200,000 in another — البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۳.
3. **خالد ؓ wrote to the Persian marzubāns at المدائن, and the letter survives verbatim** —
   البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۴; a second, shorter form of the same message in سیر ج۱ ص۳۷۴.
4. **He crossed the waterless السماوة desert from Iraq into Syria by a route no army had used**,
   guided by **رافع بن عُمَيْرة الطائي**, watering the army from the bellies of camels prepared for
   the purpose — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۷; the fullest version الکامل ج۲ ص۲۵۳; the summary سیر ج۱ ص۳۶۶.
   **Nine days** in البدایہ; **five nights** in الکامل and in سیر. Give both.
5. **أبو بكر ؓ appointed four commanders to Syria with four sectors** — يزيد بن أبي سفيان ؓ
   (دمشق), أبو عبيدة ؓ (حمص), عمرو بن العاص ؓ (فلسطين), شرحبيل بن حسنة ؓ (الأردن) — and sent each
   by a different road, following يوسف ۶۷ — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۳–۸۴.
6. **بُصرى was the first city in Syria to be taken** — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۸; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۵۵.
7. **The date of اليرموك is disputed inside our own sources**: سيف بن عمر and ابن جرير put it in
   **13 AH, before Damascus fell**; ابن عساكر, ابن إسحاق and ابن الكلبي put it **Monday 5 Rajab 15
   AH, after Damascus fell**, and ابن عساكر says «وَهَذَا هُوَ الْمَحْفُوظُ» — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۵.
   **Say the disagreement out loud; do not pick.**
8. **دمشق was entered at two gates at once** — خالد ؓ storming the Eastern Gate by rope ladders
   while the other commanders accepted a surrender at their gates — and the commanders met in the
   middle of the city, one side taking it by force and the other by treaty. The Companions
   **treated the whole city as صلح out of caution** — البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۵–۱۰۹; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۶۹–۲۷۰.
9. **The news of أبو بكر ؓ's death and of خالد ؓ's removal reached the field during اليرموك, and
   خالد ؓ concealed it until the battle ended** — البدایہ ج۷ ص۹۵, ص۹۷.
10. **عمر ؓ gave his reason for the removal in writing, twice, and it was never a charge of
    misconduct in war**: «لأَنْزِعَنَّ خَالِداً، حَتَّى يُعْلَمَ أَنَّ اللهَ إِنَّمَا يَنْصُرُ دِيْنَهُ» (سیر ج۱ ص۳۷۸) and
    «إِنِّي لَمْ أَعْزِلْ خَالِدًا عَنْ سُخْطَةٍ وَلَا خِيَانَةٍ» (الکامل ج۲ ص۳۶۰; البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۹۱).
11. **At the handover the two men each quoted a ḥadīth in the other's praise** — البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۶,
    citing مسند أحمد ٤/٩٠, graded حسن by the editor.

# 1. IRAQ, 12 AH

## 1.1 The order that started it — البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۲

Ibn Kathīr's whole account of the Iraq year is set inside a chapter titled **بعْثُ خالدِ بن الوليدِ
إلى العراق**, and it opens not with a battle but with a brief:

> لمَّا فَرَغ خالدُ بن الوليد من اليمامة، بعثَ إليه الصِّدِّيقُ أن يسيرَ إلى العراقِ، وأن يبدأ بفَرْجِ الهند،
> وهي الأُبُلَّة، ويأتي العراقَ من أسافلها، وأن يَتألَّفَ الناسَ ويدعوهم إلى الله ﷿، فإن أجابوا وإلا أخذ
> منهم الجزيةَ فإن امتنعوا عن ذلك قاتلهم، وأمره أن لا يُكْرِه أحدًا على المسير معه، ولا يستعينُ بمنِ
> ارتدّ عن الإسلام وإن كان [قد] عاد إليه.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۲ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3208
> *"When Khālid b. al-Walīd had finished with al-Yamāma, al-Ṣiddīq sent word that he should march
> to Iraq, and begin with the frontier of India — that is al-Ubulla — and come at Iraq from its
> lower end; and that he should win the people over and call them to God ﷿; if they answered, well;
> and if not, take the jizya from them; and if they refused that, fight them. And he ordered him
> not to compel anyone to march with him, and not to make use of any man who had apostatised from
> Islam, even if he had returned to it."*

Three orders in one sentence, and two of them are restraints. Ibn Kathīr also records **a
disagreement about the route** on the same page: الواقدي says some hold he went straight on from
اليمامة, others that he returned to المدینہ first and came by the Kūfa road to الحيرة —
«قلت: والمشهور الأول» (ج۷ ص۶۲).

## 1.2 الحيرة and its terms

خالد ؓ's demand to the notables of الحيرة, put to قَبِيصة بن إياس الطائي — Chosroes' governor there
after النعمان بن المنذر — البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۳:

> أدعوكم إلى الله وإلى الإسلام، فإن أجبتم إليه فأنتم من المسلمين، لكم ما لهم وعليكم ما عليهم، فإن
> أبيتم فالجزية فإن أبيتم [الجزية] فقد أتيتكم بأقوامٍ هم أحرصُ على الموتِ منكم على الحياة، جاهدناكم
> حتى يحكم الله بيننا وبينكم.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3209
> *"I call you to God and to Islam. If you answer, you are of the Muslims: yours is what is theirs
> and upon you is what is upon them. If you refuse, then the jizya. And if you refuse the jizya,
> then I have brought you men more eager for death than you are for life — we shall fight you until
> God judges between us."*

قبيصة's answer, same page: «ما لنا بحربك من حاجةٍ بل نُقيم على ديننا ونعطيكم الجزية».

**The terms — two figures, and Ibn Kathīr gives both.** «ثم صالحهم على تسعين ألفًا، (وفي رواية مئتي
ألف درهم)، **فكانت أول جزيةٍ أخذت من العراق**، وحُملت إلى المدينة» (ج۷ ص۶۳). A later page, in the
sequel to the same negotiation, records **400,000 dirhams paid up front** (ج۷ ص۷۰). Report the
first pair as the terms of الحيرة; do not silently merge the third figure into them.

## 1.3 The letter to the Persian commanders — the most quotable document of the Iraq year

Ibn Kathīr gives it with an isnād running through الشعبي, who says he was read the letter itself by
the بنو بُقَيْلة of الحيرة — البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۴:

> مِن خالد بن الوليد إلى مرازبة أهل فارس: سلامٌ على من اتَّبع الهُدى، أما بعد فالحمد لله الذي فض
> خدمتكم وسلبَ مُلْككم ووهنَ كيدكَم، وإنَّ منْ صلَّى صلاتنا واستقبل قبلتنا وأكلَ ذبيحتنا فذلك المسلمُ
> الذي له ما لنا وعليه ما علينا، أما بعد فإذا جاءكم كتابي فابعثوا إليَّ بالرُّهن واعتقدوا مني الذِّمة،
> و (إلا) فوالذي لا إله غيره لأبعثن إليكم قومًا يحبّون الموتَ كما تحبّون أنتم الحياة.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3210
> *"From Khālid b. al-Walīd to the marzubāns of Persia. Peace be upon whoever follows guidance. To
> proceed: praise be to God who has broken up your company, stripped away your kingdom and weakened
> your stratagem. Whoever prays our prayer, faces our qibla and eats what we slaughter — he is the
> Muslim: for him is what is for us, and upon him is what is upon us. To proceed: when my letter
> reaches you, send me hostages and take a covenant of protection from me. Otherwise — by Him
> beside whom there is no god — I shall send against you a people who love death as you love life."*

Ibn Kathīr's closing line on the page is the one that makes it tellable: **«فلما قرؤوا الكتاب أخذوا
يتعجبون»** — when they read it they were left marvelling.

**A second, much shorter form of the same message, from a different route** — الذہبی, from أبو وائل:

> إِنَّ مَعِي جُنْداً يُحِبُّوْنَ القَتْلَ كَمَا تُحِبُّ فَارِسٌ الخَمْرَ.
> — سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۱ ص۳۷۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/1800
> *"With me is an army that loves killing as Persia loves wine."*

⚠ These are two different transmissions of the same letter, not one text. Use one or the other on a
slide, captioned to its own work. Do not stitch them together.

## 1.5 The two exchanges from Iraq that ARE fit to tell

**(a) أُكيدر of دومة الجندل, arguing for surrender — البدایہ ج۷ ص۷۵.** An enemy's assessment of
خالد ؓ, which is worth more than any assessment of our own:

> أنا أعلم الناس بخالد، لا أحد أيمن طائرًا منه في حرب، ولا أحدُّ منه، ولا يرى وجه خالد قوم أبدًا،
> قلوا أم كثروا إلا انهزموا عنه، فأطيعوني وصالحوا القومَ.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۷۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3221
> *"I know Khālid better than anyone. No man in war has a luckier omen than he, and none is
> sharper. No people ever set eyes on Khālid's face — few or many — but they were routed from
> before him. So listen to me and make peace with these people."*

**(b) عمرو بن عبد المسيح بن بُقَيْلة and the poison — البدایہ ج۷ ص۷۰.** The Christian Arab negotiator
of الحيرة carries a pouch; خالد ؓ asks what it is; the man says it is a poison that kills within the
hour, carried so that he can take it if he sees his people ruined. خالد ؓ takes it in his hand and
says:

> إنه لن تموت نفس حتى تأتي على أجلها، ثم قال: بسم الله خير الأسماء، رب الأرض والسماء، الذي ليس
> يضرُ مع اسمه داء، الرحمن الرحيم … فبادرهم فابتلعه.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۷۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3216
> *"No soul dies until it reaches its appointed term." Then he said: "In the name of God, best of
> names, Lord of earth and sky, with whose name no sickness can harm — the Merciful, the
> Compassionate" … and he swallowed it before they could stop him.*

And ابن بُقَيْلة's reaction, same page: «والله يا معشر العرب لتملكن ما أردتم ما دام منكم أحد».
The same incident is in سیر ج۱ ص۳۷۶ by a different route (يونس بن أبي إسحاق عن أبي السَّفَر), where
the editor notes it is **مرسل** — أبو السفر did not hear it from خالد ؓ. **Tell it as a report the
books carry, not as a proven event.**

## 1.6 The secret ḥajj, and the first reprimand — البدایہ ج۷ ص۷۸

This is the page that makes the dismissal in §5 intelligible, four years early. After الفراض,
خالد ؓ sent the army back to الحيرة under two officers, gave out that he was riding in the rear —
and instead rode to Mecca by a road never travelled before, performed the ḥajj of that year, and
rejoined the rearguard before it reached الحيرة. Almost nobody knew, **and أبو بكر ؓ only learned of
it when the pilgrims came home.**

> فبعث يعتب عليه في مفارقته الجيش **وكانت عقوبته عنده أن صرفه من غزو العراق إلى غزو الشام** … وقال له
> فيما كتب إليه: … فليهنئك أبا سليمان النية والحظوة، فأتممْ يُتمِّمِ الله لكَ، **ولا يدخلنك عُجْبٌ فتخسر
> وتخذل، وإياكَ أن تُدلَّ بعمل فإنَّ اللهَ له المنُّ وهو وليّ الجزاء**.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۷۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3224
> *He sent to reproach him for leaving the army — and the punishment, in his judgement, was to turn
> him from the Iraq campaign to the Syrian one … And he wrote to him: "May the intention and the
> favour bring you joy, Abū Sulaymān; finish, and God will finish for you. **But let no
> self-admiration enter you, or you will lose and be abandoned; and beware of being made proud by
> a deed, for the bounty is God's and He is the master of reward.**"*

خالد ؓ's own reaction, on the same page: **«هذا من عمل الأعيسر — يعني عمر بن الخطاب — حَسَدني أن يكون
فتح العراق على يدي»**. Ibn Kathīr's own footnote sources that remark to تاريخ الطبري ٣/٤١٥.

⚠ **Handling.** This sentence is خالد ؓ's, reported, in a moment of anger, about a matter he was in
the wrong on. It is in the book. **Do not read it aloud as a characterisation of سیدنا عمر ؓ.** If
the strand of the two men's relations is told at all, the honest frame is the one the sources
themselves supply at the end: عمر ؓ's own «إنك عليّ لكريم وإنك إليّ لحبيب» (§5) and his weeping at
the news of خالد ؓ's death (سیر ج۱ ص۳۸۳). See §5.6.

# 2. THE MARCH FROM IRAQ TO SYRIA

This is the most vivid single passage in the whole period, and all three of our works carry it.

## 2.1 البدایہ's version — ج۷ ص۸۷

> فاستناب المثنى بن حارثة على العراق، **وسار خالد مسرعًا في تسعة آلاف وخمسمئة، ودليله رافع بن عُمَيْرة
> الطّائي**، فأخذ به على السماوة حتى انتهى إلى قُراقِر، **وسلك به أراضي لم يسلكها قبله أحدٌ، فاجتابَ
> البراري والقفار، وقطعَ الأوديةَ، وتصعَّد على الجبال، وسار في غير مهيع** … وهو في مفاوز مُعْطشة،
> **وعَطَّشَ النوقَ وسقاها الماء عللًا بعد نهل، وقطع مشافرها وكعمها حتى لا تجترّ وخلَّى أدبارها،
> واستاقها معه، فلما فقدوا [ذلك] الماء نحرها، فشربوا ما في أجوافها من الماء** … **ووصل ولله الحمد
> والمنة في تسعة أيام**، فخرج على الروم من ناحية تدمر.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3233
> *"He left al-Muthannā b. Ḥāritha in charge of Iraq and set off in haste with nine thousand five
> hundred, his guide being Rāfiʿ b. ʿUmayra al-Ṭāʾī. He took him by way of al-Samāwa until they
> reached Qurāqir, and led him through country nobody had crossed before him: he cut through open
> desert and waste, crossed the wadis, climbed the hills, and travelled by no road at all … in
> waterless wilderness. He had the she-camels made thirsty and then watered twice over, cut their
> lips and muzzled them so they could not chew the cud, and drove them along; and when the men ran
> out of water he slaughtered them and they drank what was in their bellies … and he arrived —
> praise and thanks be to God — in nine days, coming out upon the Romans from the direction of
> Tadmur."*

## 2.2 الکامل's version — the fuller one, and better for telling — ج۲ ص۲۵۳

ابن الاثیر gives the guide his own voice. خالد ؓ asks him for the crossing; **رافع ؓ tries to talk
him out of it**:

> فَقَالَ لَهُ رَافِعٌ: إِنَّكَ لَنْ تُطِيقَ ذَلِكَ بِالْخَيْلِ وَالْأَثْقَالِ، فَوَاللَّهِ إِنَّ الرَّاكِبَ الْمُفْرَدَ يَخَافُهُ عَلَى
> نَفْسِهِ. فَقَالَ: إِنَّهُ لَابُدَّ لِي مِنْ ذَلِكَ لِأَخْرُجَ مِنْ وَرَاءِ جُمُوعِ الرُّومِ؛ لِئَلَّا يَحْبِسَنِي عَنْ غِيَاثِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ.
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۵۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/944
> *Rāfiʿ said to him: "You will never manage it with cavalry and baggage. By God, a lone rider
> fears for himself on that road." He said: "I have no choice — I must come out behind the Roman
> masses, so that nothing holds me back from the relief of the Muslims."*

Then the mechanism, in detail: each company was to carry water for five days; the strongest camels
were made thirsty, then given the first drink and the second (**النَّهَل** and **العَلَل** — the
words are glossed in the text), their ears tied and lips bound so they could not chew the cud. After
a day and a night they opened the bellies of ten camels for the horses, mixing the water in the
camels' stomachs with the milk, and watered the horses from it — **«فَفَعَلُوا ذَلِكَ أَرْبَعَةَ
أَيَّامٍ»**.

And the moment the whole march turns on — رافع ؓ, whose eyes were inflamed, near the two waymarks:

> قَالَ لِلنَّاسِ: انْظُرُوا هَلْ تَرَوْنَ شَجَرَةَ عَوْسَجٍ كَقَعْدَةِ الرَّجُلِ؟ فَقَالُوا: مَا نَرَاهَا. فَقَالَ: إِنَّا لِلَّهِ
> وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ، **هَلَكْتُمْ وَاللَّهِ وَهَلَكْتُ مَعَكُمْ!** … فَنَظَرُوا فَرَأَوْهَا قَدْ قُطِعَتْ وَبَقِيَ مِنْهَا
> بَقِيَّةٌ. فَلَمَّا رَأَوْهَا كَبَّرُوا، فَقَالَ رَافِعٌ: احْفِرُوا فِي أَصْلِهَا. فَحَفَرُوا وَاسْتَخْرَجُوا عَيْنًا، فَشَرِبُوا حَتَّى
> رُوِيَ النَّاسُ. فَقَالَ رَافِعٌ: **وَاللَّهِ مَا وَرَدْتُ هَذَا الْمَاءَ قَطُّ إِلَّا مَرَّةً وَاحِدَةً مَعَ أَبِي وَأَنَا غُلَامٌ.**
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۵۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/944
> *He said to the men: "Look — do you see a box-thorn bush the height of a seated man?" They said:
> "We do not see it." He said: "Innā li-Llāh wa innā ilayhi rājiʿūn — **by God, you are dead men and
> I am dead with you.**" … They looked again and saw it, cut down, with a stump left. When they saw
> it they cried Allāhu akbar, and Rāfiʿ said: "Dig at its root." They dug, and brought out a spring,
> and the men drank their fill. Rāfiʿ said: **"By God, I have come to this water only once in my
> life, with my father, when I was a boy."***

## 2.3 The verses, and the proverb

The rajaz a Muslim said about the crossing — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۸ and الکامل ج۲ ص۲۵۳ both carry it, with
small variants in the wording (الکامل has «سارَ الجيشُ» and «إنسيٌّ يُرى» where البدایہ has «سارَها
الجيشُ» and «إنسيٌّ أرى»). The البدایہ text is the one printed here:

> لله عَيْنَا رافِعٍ أنَّى اهْتدى … فَوّزَ منْ قُراقِرٍ إلى سُوى
> خمسًا إذا ما سارها الجيشُ بكى … ما سارَها قَبلكَ إنسيٌّ أرى
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3234
> *"God bless the eyes of Rāfiʿ — how did he find the way! He crossed from Qurāqir to Suwā: five
> days that any army would weep to march. No mortal I know of ever marched them before you."*

And, on the same page, the proverb — Ibn Kathīr says خالد ؓ was **the first man to say it**:

> فقال خالد: **عندَ الصَّباح يحمد القوم السُّرى.** فأرسلها مثلًا، وهو أول من قالها ﵁.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3234
> *Khālid said: "At daybreak the people praise the night march." He sent it out as a proverb, and
> he was the first to say it, may God be pleased with him.*

# 3. SYRIA — THE FOUR COMMANDERS, أجنادين, دمشق, فحل

## 3.1 The decision and the four sectors — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۲–۸۴

The year opens with أبو بكر ؓ resolved on Syria after his ḥajj, and Ibn Kathīr grounds the decision
in two verses (التوبة ۱۲۳ and التوبة ۲۹) and in the Prophet's ﷺ own precedent at تبوك and in
جيش أسامہ ؓ (ج۷ ص۸۲).

The exchange with عمرو بن العاص ؓ, whom أبو بكر ؓ called away from the ṣadaqāt of قُضاعة — ج۷ ص۸۳:

> إنّي سهمٌ من سهام الإسلام، وأنت عبدُ الله الرامي بها، والجامع لها، فانظرْ أشدَّها وأخشاها فارم (بي)
> فيها.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3229
> *"I am one arrow among the arrows of Islam, and you are God's servant who shoots them and gathers
> them. So look for the hardest and most feared of them, and shoot me into it."*

⚠ **Variant on the page itself:** the manuscript (أ) has «فعبد الله» and تاريخ الطبري has «**وأنت
بعد الله** الرامي بها». Ibn Kathīr's printed text is «وأنت عبد الله». Quote the printed text.

Then the appointments, on the same page and the next:

| Commander | Sector he was given | Page |
|---|---|---|
| **يزيد بن أبي سفيان ؓ** — with «جمهور الناس», and سهيل بن عمرو ؓ and the Meccans with him | **دمشق**; his road lay through **تبوك** | ج۷ ص۸۳–۸۴ |
| **أبو عبيدة بن الجراح ؓ** | **حمص**; he camped at **الجابية** | ج۷ ص۸۳–۸۴ |
| **عمرو بن العاص ؓ** | **فلسطين** | ج۷ ص۸۳ |

| **خالد بن سعيد بن العاص ؓ** — first given a banner, then moved | **تيماء**, holding | ج۷ ص۸۳ |
| **معاوية بن أبي سفيان ؓ** — sent later, behind his brother | reinforcement | ج۷ ص۸۵ |

**أبو بكر ؓ walked out with each of them on foot while they rode** — «وخرج معه ماشيًا يوصيه» (ج۷ ص۸۳);

الله» (ج۷ ص۸۴).

**Why four roads and not one — the reason Ibn Kathīr gives:**

> وأمر كلَّ أميرٍ أن يسلكَ طريقًا غير طريق الآخر، لما لحظ في ذلك من المصالح. **وكان الصدِّيقُ اقتدى في
> ذلك بنبيِّ الله يعقوب حين قال لبنيه:** ﴿يَابَنِيَّ لَا تَدْخُلُوا مِنْ بَابٍ وَاحِدٍ وَادْخُلُوا مِنْ أَبْوَابٍ
> مُتَفَرِّقَةٍ﴾ [يوسف: ٦٧].
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۳–۸۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3229 (the quotation begins here and
> the verse runs on to https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3230)
> *"And he ordered each commander to take a road other than the others', for benefits he saw in
> that. In this al-Ṣiddīq was following the example of God's prophet Yaʿqūb when he said to his
> sons: 'O my sons, do not enter by one gate; enter by separate gates.'"*

## 3.2 أجنادين — and a real chronological problem, stated plainly

**Ibn Kathīr carries أجنادين twice, under two different years, from two different sources, and does
not reconcile them.**

**(a) Under 13 AH**, inside the اليرموك chapter, on the authority of **ابن إسحاق and المدائني** —
البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۹: «وكانت وقعةُ أجنادين **لليلتين بقيتا من جمادى الأولى سنة ثلاث عشرة**، وقتل بها بشر
كثير من الصحابة، وهزم الروم وقتل أميرهم القيقلان.» الکامل agrees exactly, adding the day and the
place — ج۲ ص۲۶۱: «وَالْتَقَوْا **يَوْمَ السَّبْتِ لِلَيْلَتَيْنِ بَقِيَتَا مِنْ جُمَادَى الْأُولَى سَنَةَ ثَلَاثَ عَشْرَةَ**»,
and «**أَجْنَادَيْنِ بَيْنَ الرَّمْلَةِ وَبَيْتِ جِبْرِينَ مِنْ أَرْضِ فِلَسْطِينَ**» (ج۲ ص۲۶۰).

**(b) Under 15 AH**, inside the year-15 narrative (the وقعة قيسارية chapter) — البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۵۵ — the battle عمرو بن العاص ؓ fought at
أجنادين against **الأرطبون**, on his way to إيلياء. This is where عمر ؓ's famous line belongs:

> فلما جاءهُ كتابُ عمرو قال: **قد رَمَيْنا أرْطَبونَ الرُّومِ بأرْطبون العَرَبِ، فانظروا عما تنفرج.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۵۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3301
> *When ʿAmr's letter reached him he said: "We have thrown the Arṭabūn of the Arabs against the
> Arṭabūn of the Romans. Let us see what it comes to."*

**How to handle it on stage:** name أجنادين **once**, at 13 AH, as the first pitched battle in
Palestine, and say plainly that a second engagement at the same place under عمرو ؓ is placed by the
books two years later. Do not try to make them one battle, and do not silently pick a year.

**The spy's report — the single best-attested sentence in the Syrian strand, and it is in both
books.** A Christian Arab sent by the Roman commander to look at the Muslim camp, and his answer:

> بِاللَّيْلِ رُهْبَانٌ وَبِالنَّهَارِ فُرْسَانٌ، وَلَوْ سَرَقَ ابْنُ مَلِكِهِمْ قَطَعُوهُ، وَلَوْ زَنَى رُجِمَ لِإِقَامَةِ الْحَقِّ فِيهِمْ.
> فَقَالَ: **إِنْ كُنْتَ صَدَقْتَنِي لَبَطْنُ الْأَرْضِ خَيْرٌ مِنْ لِقَاءِ هَؤُلَاءِ عَلَى ظَهْرِهَا.**
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۶۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/952
> *"By night monks, by day horsemen. If their king's son stole they would cut off his hand, and if
> he committed adultery he would be stoned — so that the truth stands among them." He said: **"If
> you have told me the truth, then the belly of the earth is better than meeting these men on its
> back."***

البدایہ carries the same, ج۷ ص۸۹: «وجدتُ قومًا رهبانًا بالليل فرسانًا بالنهار، والله لو سرقَ فيهم
ابنُ ملكهم لقطعوه، أو زنى لرجموه. فقال له القيقلان: والله لئن كنت صادقًا **لبَطْنُ الأرض خيرٌ من
ظهرها**.»

## 3.3 دمشق — one city taken two ways at once

**The siege.** أبو عبيدة ؓ on باب الجابية الكبير, خالد ؓ on **the Eastern Gate** and باب كيسان,
يزيد ؓ on باب الجابية الصغير, عمرو ؓ and شرحبيل ؓ on the rest; mangonels and siege-engines set up;
أبو الدرداء ؓ posted at بَرْزَة as a screen, and ذو الكَلاع between دمشق and حمص to stop the relief
coming from Heraclius (البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۴–۱۰۵). **The length of the siege is disputed on the page
itself:** «سبعين ليلة، وقيل أربعة أشهر، وقيل ستة أشهر، وقيل أربعة عشر شهرًا، فالله أعلم» (ج۷ ص۱۰۵).
الکامل gives seventy nights (ج۲ ص۲۶۹).

**The night it fell.** Winter, hard cold, the fighting stalled — and a child was born to the
Byzantine commander of the city, who made a feast; the garrison ate, drank, tired and slept away
from their posts.

> وفطن لذلك أميرُ الحرب خالد بن الوليد، **فإنه كان لا ينام ولا يترك أحدًا ينام**، بل مراصد لهم ليلًا
> ونهارًا، وله عيون وقُصّاد يرفعون إليه أحوال المقاتلة صباحًا ومساء … كان قد أعدَّ سلالم من حبال … فقطعوا
> الخندق سباحة بقِرب في أعناقهم، فنصبوا تلك السلالم … فلما استووا على السور رفعوا أصواتهم بالتكبير.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3251
> *"Khālid b. al-Walīd, the commander of the fighting, noticed it — **for he neither slept nor let
> anyone sleep**, but watched them night and day, with scouts and informers bringing him the state
> of the garrison morning and evening … He had prepared ladders of rope … They crossed the moat
> swimming, with waterskins at their necks, and set up those ladders … and when they stood level on
> the wall they raised their voices in takbīr."*

**And the moment that made it a legal problem for centuries** — الکامل's sentence is the cleanest:

> وَدَخَلَ خَالِدٌ عَنْوَةً، **فَالْتَقَى خَالِدٌ وَالْقُوَّادُ فِي وَسَطِهَا، هَذَا قَتْلًا وَنَهْبًا، وَهَذَا صَفْحًا وَتَسْكِينًا،
> فَأَجْرَوْا نَاحِيَةَ خَالِدٍ مَجْرَى الصُّلْحِ**، وَكَانَ صُلْحُهُمْ عَلَى الْمُقَاسَمَةِ.
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۷۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/961
> *"Khālid entered by force. **Khālid and the other commanders met in the middle of the city — this
> one killing and plundering, that one pardoning and calming — so they applied the terms of the
> treaty to Khālid's side as well**, and their treaty was on a half-share."*

**The Companions' own reasoning, in Ibn Kathīr's words** — البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۹:

> فأكثرُ العلماء على أنَّه استقرَّ أمرُها على الصلح، لأنهم شكُّوا في المتقدم على الآخر … **فلما شكُّوا في
> ذلك جعلوها صلحًا احتياطًا.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3255
> *"Most of the scholars hold that its case settled as a treaty, because they were uncertain which
> of the two came first … **and since they were uncertain, they made it a treaty, out of caution.**"*

**The terms, concretely** (ج۷ ص۱۰۶): half the great church of **يوحنا** taken — the eastern side made
a mosque, the western left as a church — and **fourteen further churches left to the people**, with
a document written by خالد ؓ and **witnessed by أبو عبيدة ؓ، عمرو بن العاص ؓ، يزيد ؓ and شرحبيل ؓ**.
Ibn Kathīr then lists the fourteen, one by one, from ابن عساكر, and says which were still standing in
his own day (ج۷ ص۱۰۶–۱۰۸). ⚠ Whether the treaty was written by خالد ؓ or by أبو عبيدة ؓ is itself
disputed on ج۷ ص۱۰۹.

**When دمشق fell — the majority against سيف.** Ibn Kathīr: سيف's narrative sequence implies 13 AH,
but «نص سيف على ما نص عليه الجمهور من أنها فتحت **في نصف رجب سنة أربع عشرة**», and he then lists
سعيد بن عبد العزيز، أبو معشر، محمد بن إسحاق، معمر، الأموي، ابن الكلبي، خليفة بن خياط and أبو عبيد القاسم
بن سلّام all for **14 AH** (ج۷ ص۱۰۸–۱۰۹).

## 3.4 فحل — «ذات الرَّدَغة», the battle of the mud

Ibn Kathīr flags the sequencing problem before he narrates: «وقد ذكرها كثيرٌ من علماء السِّيَر **قبل
فتحِ دمشق**، وإنما ذكرها الإمام أبو جعفر بن جرير **بعد فتح دمشق**» (ج۷ ص۱۱۲).

The Romans, some eighty thousand, gathered at **فحل** in the Ghawr near بيسان and **released the
irrigation water over the ground** so that it turned to deep mud between them and the Muslims —
which is why the Arabs called that campaign **ذات الرَّدَغة** (ج۷ ص۱۰۴, ص۱۱۳; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۷۰). The
command was **شرحبيل بن حسنة ؓ**, with خالد ؓ in the van, أبو عبيدة ؓ and عمرو ؓ on the wings,
ضرار بن الأزور ؓ over the cavalry and عياض بن غنم ؓ over the foot (ج۷ ص۱۱۳).

The Romans attacked by night expecting to catch the camp asleep:

> وأمير هذا الحرب شرحبيل بن حسنة **وهو لا يبيت ولا يصبح إلا على تعبئة** … فهجموا على المسلمين فنهضوا
> إليهم **نهضة رجل واحد لأنهم على أُهبة دائمًا** … **وأسلمتهم هزيمتهم إلى ذلك الوحل الذي كانوا قد كادوا
> به المسلمين فغرَّقهم الله فيه.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۱۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3259
> *"The commander of this war was Shuraḥbīl b. Ḥasana, **and he never spent a night or a morning
> except in battle order** … They fell upon the Muslims, who rose against them **as one man,
> because they were always in readiness** … **and their rout delivered them into the very mud with
> which they had meant to trap the Muslims, and God drowned them in it.**"*

That is the whole moral of the engagement, and the book supplies it without any comment from us.

## 3.5 حمص, قنسرين, and Heraclius leaving Syria (15 AH)

- **حمص** fell after a winter siege, on the same terms as دمشق — half the houses, kharāj on the
  land, jizya on persons **«بحسب الغنى والفقر»** (ج۷ ص۱۵۳). Ibn Kathīr records that the besiegers
  stood in the snow **in sandals**, while Roman soldiers came back with a foot fallen off inside the
  boot, «ومع هذا لم يُصَبْ منهم قدَمٌ ولا أصبعٌ» (ج۷ ص۱۵۲–۱۵۳).
- **قنسرين** — خالد ؓ's line to the garrison who shut themselves in:

  > إنكم لو كنتم في السَّحاب لحَمَلنا اللّهُ إليكم أو لأنزلكم إلينا.
  > — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۵۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3299
  > *"If you were in the clouds, God would carry us up to you, or bring you down to us."*

- **Heraclius leaves Syria** (البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۵۳–۱۵۴), and the farewell the books put in his mouth:

  > عليكِ السلامُ يا سوريةُ سلامًا لا اجتماع بعده … ولا يعودُ إليكِ روميٌّ أبدًا إلا خائفًا.
  > — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۵۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3300
  > *"Peace be upon you, Syria — a farewell with no meeting after it … no Roman will ever come back
  > to you except in fear."*

  ⚠ Ibn Kathīr transmits this from ابن جرير عن ابن إسحاق and it is the kind of set-piece speech that
  historians build. It is on the page; **narrate it as what the book reports Heraclius to have
  said**, not as a transcript.

# 4. اليرموک

## 4.1 The date — the disagreement, stated in Ibn Kathīr's own words

This is the first thing on the page, before a word of narrative. **Read it as it stands:**

> **على ما ذكره سيف بن عمر في هذه السنة قبل فتح دمشق، وتبعه على ذلك أبو جعفر بن جرير ﵀.**
> **وأما الحافظ ابن عساكر ﵀ فإنه نقل عن يزيد بن أبي عبيدة والوليد وابن لهيعة والليث وأبي معشر: أنها
> كانت في سنة خمس عشرة بعد فتح دمشق.** وقال محمد بن إسحاق: كانت في رجب سنة خمس عشرة. وقال خليفة بن
> خياط: قال ابن الكلبي: **كانت وقعة اليرموك يوم الإثنين لخمسٍ مَضَيْن من رجب سنة خمس عشرة.**
> **قال ابن عساكر: وهذا هو المحفوظ، وما قاله سيف من أنها قبل فتح دمشق سنة ثلاث عشرة فلم يتابع عليه.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3231
> *"…according to what Sayf b. ʿUmar recorded, in this year, before the conquest of Damascus, and
> Abū Jaʿfar b. Jarīr followed him in that. But al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn ʿAsākir transmitted from Yazīd b. Abī
> ʿUbayda, al-Walīd, Ibn Lahīʿa, al-Layth and Abū Maʿshar that it was in the year 15, after the
> conquest of Damascus. Muḥammad b. Isḥāq said: it was in Rajab of the year 15. Khalīfa b. Khayyāṭ
> said, on the authority of Ibn al-Kalbī: the battle of al-Yarmūk was on Monday, five nights into
> Rajab, in the year 15. Ibn ʿAsākir said: **this is what is preserved**, and what Sayf said — that
> it was before the conquest of Damascus in the year 13 — has not been corroborated."*

**Label:** `[SOURCED]` for the disagreement itself. **13 AH `[SOURCED — سيف/ابن جرير]` · 15 AH,
Monday 5 Rajab `[SOURCED — ابن الكلبي via خليفة, and ابن عساكر calls it المحفوظ]`.** The narrative
sequence in البدایہ, in الکامل and in this note follows سيف — because that is the sequence the books
narrate in — **while the date most likely to be right is the other one.** Say that; it is a better
lesson about history than any lesson we could add.

## 4.2 Where it was fought

The Romans camped at **الواقوصة near the اليرموک**, «وصار الوادي خندقًا عليهم» — the ravine served
them as a moat (ج۷ ص۸۷). Another route: «أن الروم نزلوا فيما بين **دير أيوب واليرموك**، ونزل المسلمون
من وراء النهر من الجانب الآخر، **وأذرعات خلفهم ليصل إليهم المدد من المدينة**» (ج۷ ص۸۸). The Muslims
deliberately sat across the Romans' only road out — hence عمرو ؓ's remark:

> أبشروا أيها الناس، فقد حصرت (والله) الروم، **وقلما جاء محصور بخير.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3234
> *"Rejoice, people — by God, the Romans are besieged, **and seldom did a besieged man come to any
> good.**"*

## 4.3 The command arrangement — خالد ؓ's own proposal, and it is the best thing he said

The armies came in **متساندين** — each commander over his own men, nobody over all of them
(الکامل ج۲ ص۲۵۵). خالد ؓ stood up and offered a rotation:

> إن هذا يوم من أيام الله، **لا ينبغي فيه الفخر ولا البغي**، أخلصوا جهادكم وأريدوا الله بعملكم، وإن هذا
> يوم له ما بعده لو رددناهم اليوم إلى خندقهم فلا نزال نردهم، وإن هزمونا لا نفلح بعدها أبدًا، **فتعالوا
> فلنتعاور الإمارة فليكن عليها بعضُنا اليوم والآخر غدًا والآخر بعد غد، حتى يتأمَّر كلكم، ودعوني اليوم
> أليكم**، فأمّروه عليهم وهم يظنون أن الأمرَ يطول جدًّا.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3235
> *"This is a day of God's days. **There should be no boasting in it and no aggression.** Make your
> jihād sincere and intend God by your work. This is a day with everything after it in it: if we
> push them back to their ditch today we shall keep pushing them back, and if they rout us we shall
> never prosper afterwards. **So come — let us take the command in turn: one of us over it today,
> another tomorrow, another the day after, until every one of you has commanded. And let me have
> charge of you today.**" So they made him commander over them, thinking the affair would go on a
> long time.*

The disposition that followed (ج۷ ص۸۹–۹۰): **36 to 40 كراديس**, each of a thousand men with its own
officer; أبو عبيدة ؓ in the centre — then moved by خالد ؓ's advice **behind the whole army, so that
any man who broke would see him and turn back out of shame**, with سعيد بن زيد ؓ (one of the ten) put
in the centre in his place; عمرو ؓ and شرحبيل ؓ on the right, يزيد ؓ on the left; the cavalry split
into two bodies posted behind each wing; **قُباث بن أشيم ؓ over the scouts, عبد الله بن مسعود ؓ over
the spoils, أبو الدرداء ؓ as qāḍī of the day, أبو سفيان ؓ as the man who went along the ranks
exhorting, and المقداد ؓ as the man who rode round reciting سورة الأنفال and the verses of jihād**.

That last pair of appointments is worth a slide on its own: an army that appointed **a judge and a
reciter** before it appointed a rearguard.

## 4.4 What خالد ؓ said when told the Romans were too many

The single most usable sentence of the battle, and it is a real recorded exchange — a Christian Arab
speaking to him as he came in from Iraq:

> قال رجل من نصارى العرب لخالد بن الوليد: **ما أكثر الرومَ وأقلَّ المسلمينَ!!** فقال خالد: **ويلك،
> أتخوّفني بالروم؟ وإنما تكثر الجنود بالنصر، وتقل بالخذلان لا بعدد الرجال.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۱–۹۲ · the question is at https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3237 (ص۹۱) and
> خالد ؓ's answer at https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3238 (ص۹۲)
> *A man of the Christian Arabs said to Khālid b. al-Walīd: **"How many the Romans are, and how few
> the Muslims!"** Khālid said: **"Woe to you — are you frightening me with the Romans? Armies are
> made many by victory and few by abandonment, not by the number of men."***

## 4.5 جرجة — the Roman commander who asked خالد ؓ a question and did not go back

This is the most valuable single passage in the whole strand: a genuine recorded dialogue, at
length, between two commanders between the lines. البدایہ ج۷ ص۹۵–۹۶.

جرجة rode out and called خالد ؓ to him «حتى اختلفت أعناق فرسيهما» — until their horses' necks
crossed — and said:

> يا خالد أخبرني فاصدقني ولا تكذبني، **فإنَّ الحُرَّ لا يكذب**، ولا تخادعني **فإنّ الكريمَ لا يُخادع
> المسترسل بالله**، هل أنزل (الله) على نبيكم سيفًا من السماء فأعطاكه فلا تسلّه على أحد إلا هزمتهم؟
> **قال: لا! قال: فبم سُمِّيت سيفَ الله؟**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3241
> *"Khālid, tell me and be truthful with me and do not lie to me — **for a free man does not lie** —
> and do not deceive me, **for a noble man does not deceive one who trusts himself to him in God's
> name**. Did God send down a sword from heaven upon your Prophet and give it to you, so that you
> never draw it against anyone but you rout them?" **He said: "No." He said: "Then why are you
> called the Sword of God?"***

خالد ؓ's answer, on the same page — and note that it is an answer about **himself**, not about the
sword:

> إن الله بعثَ فينا نبيَّه فدعانا فنفرنا منه ونأينا عنه جميعًا، ثم إن بعضنا صدقه وتابعه، وبعضنا كذبه
> وباعده، **فكنت فيمن كذَّبه وباعدَه**، ثم إنّ اللهَ أخذَ بقلوبنا ونواصينا فهدانا به وبايعناه، فقال لي:
> **أنت سيفٌ من سيوفِ الله سله (الله) على المشركين**.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3241
> *"God sent His Prophet among us, and he called us, and we fled from him and drew away from him,
> all of us. Then some of us believed him and followed him, and some called him a liar and kept
> away — **and I was one of those who called him a liar and kept away**. Then God took hold of our
> hearts and our forelocks and guided us by him, and we gave him our allegiance. And he said to me:
> **'You are a sword among the swords of God; God has drawn you against the idolaters.'**"*

The dialogue continues through what the Muslims call people to, what happens to one who refuses,
what jizya means — and then the question that turns it:

> قال جرجة: **فلمن دخل فيكم اليوم من الأجر مثل ما لكم من الأجر والذخر؟** قال: **نعم وأفضل.** قال: وكيف
> يساويكم وقد سبقتموه؟ فقال خالد: إنا قبلنا هذا الأمر عنوة وبايعنا نبينا وهو حيّ بين أظهرنا … **وإنكم
> أنتم لم تروا ما رأينا، ولم تسمعوا ما سمعنا من العجائب والحجج، فمن دخل في هذا الأمر منكم بحقيقةٍ ونيَّةٍ
> كان أفضلَ منا**.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۵–۹۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3241
> *Jirja said: **"Has the man who enters your religion today the same reward and treasure as you?"**
> He said: **"Yes — and better."** He said: "How can he equal you when you went before him?" Khālid
> said: "We took this matter up when we could see it, and gave our Prophet allegiance while he was
> alive among us … **but you did not see what we saw, and did not hear what we heard of the wonders
> and the proofs. So whoever among you enters this matter in truth and sincerity is better than
> us.**"*

And the ending, ج۷ ص۹۶:

> فعند ذلك قلبَ جرجة الترسَ ومال مع خالد. وقال: **علّمْني الإسلامَ**، فمال به خالدٌ إلى فسطاطه فسنّ عليه
> قربةً من ماءٍ ثم صلَّى به ركعتين … **وأُصيب جرجة ﵀ ولم يصل لله إلا تلك الركعتين مع خالد.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3242
> *At that Jirja turned his shield over and came across to Khālid, and said: **"Teach me Islam."**
> Khālid took him aside to his tent, poured a waterskin over him, then prayed two rakʿas with him …
> **and Jirja was killed, and he never prayed to God but those two rakʿas with Khālid.**

## 4.6 The courier during the battle

> وبينما هُمْ في جولةِ الحرب وحومةِ الوَغى … إذ قدم البريدُ من نحو الحجاز فدُفع إلى خالد بن الوليد فقال
> له: ما الخبرُ؟ فقال له — فيما بينه وبينه —: **إن الصدِّيقَ ﵁ قد توفي واستخلفَ عمرَ، واستنابَ على
> الجيوش أبا عبيدةَ عامرَ بن الجرّاح. فأسرّها خالدٌ ولم يُبْد ذلك للناس لئلا يحصل ضعفٌ ووهنٌ في تلك
> الحال**، وقال له والناسُ يسمعون: **أحسنتَ**، وأخذ منه الكتابَ فوضعه في كنانته واشتغل بما كان فيه من
> تدبير الحرب.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3241
> *And while they were in the swirl of the fighting … a courier arrived from the direction of the
> Ḥijāz and was brought to Khālid b. al-Walīd. He said to him: "What is the news?" He said —
> privately, between the two of them — **"al-Ṣiddīq has died and appointed ʿUmar after him, and has
> put Abū ʿUbayda ʿĀmir b. al-Jarrāḥ over the armies." Khālid kept it secret and did not show it to
> the men, lest weakness and faltering set in at such a moment**, and said to him, with the men
> listening: **"You have done well"** — and took the letter from him and put it in his quiver, and
> went back to the ordering of the battle.*

And afterwards, when the spoil was gathered — البدایہ ج۷ ص۹۷: «**وما فرحوا بما وجدوا بقدر حزنهم على
الصدِّيق حين أعلمهم خالد بذلك، ولكن عوضهم الله بالفاروق ﵁**.»

## 4.7 The outcome for Syria

The rout ended in the الواقوصة ravine, the chained ranks pulling one another down (ج۷ ص۹۶). خالد ؓ
pursued to دمشق, whose people came out saying «نحن على عهدنا وصلحنا؟ قال: نعم», then on through
ثنية العقاب to حمص, which he took on the same terms as دمشق (ج۷ ص۹۷). Heraclius, at حمص, withdrew:

> وقال هرقل: **أما الشامُ فلا شام، وويلٌ للرومِ من المولودِ المشؤوم.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3244
> *And Heraclius said: **"As for Syria — there is no Syria. And woe to the Romans from the
> ill-omened newborn."***

**Two Roman explanations of the defeat, from two separate routes, and both are usable:**

> **ويْلَكُم أخبروني عن هؤلاء القوم الذين يقاتلونكم أليسوا بشرًا مثلكم؟** قالوا: بلى … قال: **فما بالكم
> تنهزمون؟** فقال شيخٌ من عظمائهم: **من أجل أنهم يقومون الليل ويصومون النهار، ويوفون بالعهد، ويأمرون
> بالمعروف، وينهون عن المنكر، ويتناصفون بينهم، ومن أجل أنا نشرب الخمر، ونزني، ونركب الحرام، وننقض
> العهد، ونغْصب ونظلم** … فقال: **أنت صدقتني.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3245
> *"Woe to you — tell me about these people who fight you: are they not men like you?" They said:
> "Yes" … He said: "Then why do you get routed?" One of their elders said: **"Because they stand at
> night and fast by day, keep their covenants, command good and forbid wrong, and deal justly among
> themselves — and because we drink wine, commit adultery, ride into the forbidden, break our
> covenants, seize and oppress"** … He said: **"You have told me the truth."***

⚠ **isnād note:** Ibn Kathīr transmits this from **أحمد بن مروان المالكي in "المجالسة"** — outside the
Six, and a work with a known reputation for weak material. **It is in البدایہ; cite it to البدایہ, and
present it as a report the book carries.** The second, on the same page (via يحيى بن يحيى الغساني),
is the better-known wording and is the one to prefer:

> جئتكَ من عند رجالٍ دقاق يركبون خيولًا عتاقًا، **أما الليل فرهبان، وأما النهار ففرسان** … لو حدَّثت
> جليسك حديثًا ما فهمه عنك **لما علا من أصواتهم بالقرآن والذكر**.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3245
> *"I have come to you from lean men riding thoroughbred horses. **By night they are monks and by
> day they are horsemen** … If you spoke to the man next to you he would not make out a word,
> **for the rising of their voices with the Qurʾān and the remembrance of God.**"*

# 5. THE DISMISSAL — NARRATED, NEVER ADJUDICATED

**Handling rule for this section, before anything else.** Both men are Companions. The sources
record عمر ؓ's reason **in his own words**, in writing, twice, and it is not a charge of misconduct.
They also record خالد ؓ's compliance in one sentence, and the two men's mutual regard in the
Prophet's ﷺ own words. **Tell the facts and the quotations; draw no conclusion; if pressed, defer to
the session that handles اصول.** Everything below is chosen so that it can be delivered without
taking a side.

## 5.1 There were TWO removals, four years apart. Keep them separate.

| | When | Scope | Reason the sources give |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | On عمر ؓ's accession, **13 AH** — the news reaching the field at اليرموك | Removed from **command of Syria**; أبو عبيدة ؓ made amīr; خالد ؓ stayed and fought on under him | ابن إسحاق: «لكلام بلغه عنه، ولما كان من أمر مالك بن نُويرة» (البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۳); ابن عون: so that people would know God gives the victory (سیر ج۱ ص۳۷۸) |
| **Second** | **17 AH** | Removed from **قنسرين**, and from any command thereafter | A gift of **ten thousand** to الأشعث بن قيس ؓ out of the campaign's spoil; عمر ؓ had it audited (البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۹۰–۱۹۱; الکامل ج۲ ص۳۵۹–۳۶۰) |

## 5.2 The first removal — the letter and the sandal

> فلما ولي عمر كان أولَ ما تكلّم به أن عزل خالدًا، وقال: **لا يلي لي عملًا أبدًا**. وكتب عمر إلى أبي
> عبيدة: **إن أكذب خالد نفسه فهو أمير على ما كان عليه، وإن لم يكذب نفسه فهو معزول، فانزع عمامته عن
> رأسه وقاسمه ماله نصفين**. … فقاسمه أبو عبيدة حتى أخذ [إحدى] نعليه وترك له الأخرى، **وخالد يقول سمعًا
> وطاعة لأمير المؤمنين.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3249
> *When ʿUmar took office, the first thing he spoke of was removing Khālid, and he said: **"He will
> never hold a post for me."** And ʿUmar wrote to Abū ʿUbayda: **"If Khālid gives himself the lie,
> he is commander over what he was commander of. If he does not give himself the lie, he is
> removed: take his turban off his head and divide his property with him in halves."** … So Abū
> ʿUbayda divided with him, until he took one of his two sandals and left him the other — **and
> Khālid was saying, "I hear and obey the Commander of the Faithful."***

الکامل carries the same at ج۲ ص۲۶۸, adding خالد ؓ's consultation with his sister فاطمة and — a
detail البدایہ does not have — **«فَقَبَّلَ رَأْسَهَا وَقَالَ: صَدَقْتِ»**, he kissed her head and said "you
are right."

## 5.3 The reason, in عمر ؓ's own words — the two texts to put on the slide

> لأَنْزِعَنَّ خَالِداً، حَتَّى يُعْلَمَ أَنَّ اللهَ إِنَّمَا يَنْصُرُ دِيْنَهُ.
> — سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۱ ص۳۷۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/1804
> *"I shall certainly remove Khālid, so that it may be known that it is God who gives His religion
> the victory."*

And the public letter of 17 AH, sent to every garrison city — الکامل's wording is the fullest:

> إِنِّي لَمْ أَعْزِلْ خَالِدًا عَنْ سُخْطَةٍ وَلَا خِيَانَةٍ، **وَلَكِنَّ النَّاسَ فَخَّمُوهُ وَفُتِنُوا بِهِ، فَخِفْتُ أَنْ يُوكَلُوا
> إِلَيْهِ، فَأَحْبَبْتُ أَنْ يَعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ الصَّانِعُ، وَأَنْ لَا يَكُونُوا بِعَرَضِ فِتْنَةٍ.**
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۳۶۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1051
> *"I did not remove Khālid out of displeasure, nor for any dishonesty — **but the people magnified
> him and were entranced by him, and I feared they would be left to rely on him. So I wanted them
> to know that God is the Doer, and that they should not stand exposed to a trial.**"*

البدایہ carries the same letter more briefly at ج۷ ص۱۹۱: «إني لم أعزل خالدًا عن سخطة ولا خيانة،
ولكن الناس فتنوا به فأحببت أن يعلموا أن الله هو الصانع»; and a parallel remark covering both خالد ؓ
and المثنى ؓ at ج۷ ص۲۳۸: «إنما عزلتهما ليعلم الناسُ أنَّ اللهَ نصرَ الدِّين لا بنصرهما، وأن القوةَ لله
جميعًا».

## 5.4 عمر ؓ's own later verdict on خالد ؓ — the sentence that closes the subject

Written after قنسرين, when the reports of what خالد ؓ had done there reached المدینہ:

> يرحمُ الله أبا بكر، **كان أعلمَ بالرجالِ مني**، واللّهِ إنِّي **لم أعزلْهُ عن ريبةٍ** ولكن خشيتُ أن يُوكلَ
> الناسُ إليه.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۵۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3299
> *"May God have mercy on Abū Bakr — **he knew men better than I do**. By God, **I did not remove
> him for any suspicion**, but I feared that people would come to rely on him."*

## 5.5 The handover itself — each man quoting a ḥadīth in the other's praise

**This is the material that makes the whole episode safe to tell in this room.** Ibn Kathīr gives it
in خالد ؓ's obituary, sourced to مسند أحمد ٤/٩٠, which his editor grades **حسن**:

> استعمل عمر بن الخطاب أبا عبيدة على الشام وعزل خالد بن الوليد، فقال خالد: **بعث إليكم أمين هذه
> الأمة**، سمعتُ رسول الله ﷺ يقول: **"أمينُ هذه الأمة أبو عبيدة بن الجراح"**. فقال أبو عبيدة: سمعت رسول
> الله ﷺ يقول: **"خالدٌ سيفٌ من سيوف الله، نِعْمَ فَتى العشيرة"**.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3382
> *ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb appointed Abū ʿUbayda over Syria and removed Khālid b. al-Walīd. Khālid said:
> **"He has sent you the trustee of this community"** — I heard the Messenger of God ﷺ say: **"The
> trustee of this community is Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ."** And Abū ʿUbayda said: I heard the
> Messenger of God ﷺ say: **"Khālid is a sword among the swords of God — what an excellent young man
> of the clan."***

The same exchange is in سیر ج۱ ص۳۷۳ (https://shamela.ws/book/10906/1799).

**And how أبو عبيدة ؓ handled the letter when it reached him.** البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۱۰ — he kept it from
خالد ؓ for about twenty nights, until دمشق had fallen:

> فقال له خالدٌ: يرحمك الله، ما منعك أن تعلمني حين جاءك؟ فقال: **إني كرهتُ أن أكسر عليك حربكَ، وما
> سلطان الدنيا أريدُ، ولا للدنيا أعملُ … وإنما نحن أخَوان، وما يضرُّ الرجل أن يليه أخوه في دينه ودنياه.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۱۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3256
> *Khālid said to him: "God have mercy on you — what kept you from telling me when it reached you?"
> He said: **"I hated to break your war upon you. It is not worldly authority I want, nor for the
> world that I work … We are only two brothers, and it does a man no harm to be governed by his
> brother, in his religion and in his world."***

سیر ج۱ ص۳۸۰ gives أبو عبيدة ؓ's reason in three words: **«كَرِهْتُ أَنْ أُرَوِّعَكَ»** — "I hated to
alarm you."

## 5.6 The complications the sources also record — kept here so nobody re-researches them

- **A Companion objected to the removal, publicly, to عمر ؓ's face, at الجابية** — and the report has
  a sound chain (أحمد ٣/٤٧٥; البخاري, التاريخ الأوسط ١/٥٧). البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۸ gives عمر ؓ's own stated
  ground first — «أمرتُه أن يحبسَ هذا المال على ضعَفَةِ المهاجرين، فأعطاه ذا البأس، وذا الشرف واللسان» —
  and then the objection of أبو عمرو بن حفص بن المغيرة ؓ: «**ما اعتذرت يا عمر، لقد نزعتَ عاملًا استعمله
  رسول الله ﷺ، ووضعتَ لواء رفعه رسول الله ﷺ، وأغمدت سيفًا سلَّه الله**» — the page then carries a
  further clause of the objection, «ولقد قطعتَ الرحمَ وحسدتَ ابنَ العم», which is **not** for delivery —
  and عمر ؓ's reply: «**إنك قريبُ
  القرابة، حديثُ السن، مغضبٌ في ابن عمك**». سیر ج۱ ص۳۷۹ has the same with a shorter text.
  ⚠ **This can be told**, and it shows a caliph being argued with in the open. It must be told with
  عمر ؓ's own reason given **first**, and it must stop where the sources stop.
- **The wrestling story** — that خالد ؓ broke عمر ؓ's leg when both were boys, «وكان ذلك سببَ العداوة
  بينهما» (البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۸) — is transmitted through **مجالد بن سعيد**, whom Ibn Kathīr's own editor
  marks **ضعيف** on that page. **Do not use it.** It is the single likeliest thing a hostile question
  will be built from; the answer is that the book carries it with a weak chain and that the
  caliph's own written reason is on the record.
- **خالد ؓ's line over عكرمة ؓ's body at اليرموك** — «زَعَمَ ابْنُ حَنْتَمَةَ — يَعْنِي عُمَرَ — أَنَّا لَا
  نُسْتَشْهَدُ!» (الکامل ج۲ ص۲۵۸) — is on the page and is **not** for delivery. Recorded here only.
- **عمر ؓ knew that أبو بكر ؓ knew.** الکامل ج۲ ص۲۶۰: «قَدْ عَلِمَ أَبُو بَكْرٍ أَنَّهُ يَسُوءُنِي أَنْ أُؤَمِّرَ
  خَالِدًا، فَلِهَذَا أَمَرَنِي أَنْ أَرُدَّ أَصْحَابَ خَالِدٍ، وَتَرَكَ ذِكْرَهُ مَعَهُمْ» — Abū Bakr, dying, told ʿUmar
  to send Khālid's *men* back to Iraq and pointedly did not name Khālid among them.

## 5.7 The second removal, 17 AH — and how it ended

عمر ؓ's written order to أبو عبيدة ؓ, البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۹۰: stand خالد ؓ up, unwind his turban, take off
his cap, and ask where the ten thousand came from — **«إن كان أجازها الأشعث من ماله فهو سَرَفٌ، وإن
كان من مال الصائفة فهي خيانة، ثم اعزله عن عمله»**. It was done publicly, from the minbar, by
**بلال ؓ**, with أبو عبيدة ؓ sitting silent (ج۷ ص۱۹۰–۱۹۱). خالد ؓ's answer, in الکامل ج۲ ص۳۶۰:

> فَلَمْ يَمْنَعْهُ سَمْعًا وَطَاعَةً … ثُمَّ قَالَ: **نَسْمَعُ وَنُطِيعُ لِوُلَاتِنَا وَنُفَخِّمُ وَنَخْدِمُ مَوَالِيَنَا.**
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۳۶۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/1051
> *He did not withhold hearing and obedience from him … then he said: **"We hear and we obey our
> governors; we honour and we serve those set over us."***

Then المدینہ. He said his property came «من الأنفال والسهمان» and offered عمر ؓ everything above
sixty thousand; the audit produced twenty thousand, which went into بيت المال — and then:

> يَا خَالِدُ! **وَاللهِ إِنَّكَ لَكَرِيْمٌ عَلَيَّ، وَإِنَّكَ لَحَبِيْبٌ إِلَيَّ**، وَلَنْ تُعَاتِبَنِي بَعْدَ اليَوْمِ عَلَى شَيْءٍ.
> — سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۱ ص۳۸۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/1806
> *"Khālid — **by God, you are noble in my sight and beloved to me**, and after today you will have
> nothing to reproach me for."*

## 5.8 The end — خالد ؓ's death (21 AH), and عمر ؓ's last words about him

His dying words, البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۹ and سیر ج۱ ص۳۸۱ (isnād **حسن** per the سیر editor, via ابن المبارك
in *الجهاد*):

> لقد طلبتُ القتلَ في مظانّه فلم يُقَدَّر لي إلا أن أموتَ على فراشي. **وما من عملي شيءٌ أرْجَى عندي بعد
> لا إله إلا الله من ليلة بتُّها وأنا مُتَترّسٌ والسماء تهلّني ننتظر الصبح حتى نُغير على الكفار.** ثم
> قال: إذا أنا متُّ فانظروا إلى سلاحي وفرسي فاجعلوه عدّة في سبيل الله.
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3385
> *"I sought death in the places where it is found, and it was not decreed for me to die anywhere
> but on my bed. **And there is nothing of my deeds I hope in more, after 'there is no god but
> God', than one night I passed under my shield with the rain pouring on me, waiting for the dawn
> to raid the unbelievers.**" Then he said: "When I die, look to my weapons and my horse and make
> them equipment in the way of God."*

The other version, البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۷ and سیر ج۱ ص۳۸۲, via الواقدي عن أبي الزناد:

> ما في جسدي شبرٌ إلا وفيه ضربةُ سيفٍ، أو طعنةٌ برمحٍ، أو رميةٌ بسهمٍ، **وها أنا أموت على فراشي حتفَ
> أنفي كما يموت البعيرُ، فلا نامت أعينُ الجبناء.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۷ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3383
> *"There is not a hand's breadth of my body but on it is the stroke of a sword, or the thrust of a
> spear, or the shot of an arrow — **and here I am dying on my bed, of my own nose, as a camel dies.
> So may the eyes of cowards never sleep.**"*

⚠ **الواقدي is in that chain.** The version at §5.8 first (البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۹ / سیر ج۱ ص۳۸۱) has the
better isnād; prefer it for the slide, and keep the second as the wording الذہبی himself echoes at
سیر ج۱ ص۳۶۷: «**وَمَاتَ عَلَى فِرَاشِهِ، فَلاَ قَرَّتْ أَعْيُنُ الجُبَنَاءِ**».

عمر ؓ at the funeral, البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۹, and **البخاري carries it in a taʿlīq**:

> وقال عمر: **دعهن يبكين على أبي سليمان ما لم يكن نقعٌ أو لقلقةٌ.**
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3385
> *ʿUmar said: **"Let them weep for Abū Sulaymān, so long as there is no dust-throwing and no
> wailing."*** (نقع = throwing dust on the head; لقلقة = shrieking.)

And when the news reached him on the Mecca road — سیر ج۱ ص۳۸۳:

> فَاسْتَرْجَعَ عُمَرُ مِرَاراً، وَنَكَسَ، وَأَكْثَرَ التَّرَحُّمَ عَلَيْهِ، وَقَالَ: **كَانَ — وَاللهِ — سَدَّاداً لِنَحْرِ
> العَدُوِّ، مَيْمُوْنَ النَّقِيْبَةِ.**
> — سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۱ ص۳۸۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/10906/1809
> *ʿUmar said "innā li-Llāh…" over and over, bowed his head, and asked God's mercy on him again and
> again, and said: **"By God, he was a stopper set against the enemy's throat, blessed in
> outcome."***

**And what he left.** سیر ج۱ ص۳۸۳, via جويرية عن نافع: «لَمَّا مَاتَ خَالِدٌ، **لَمْ يَدَعْ إِلاَّ فَرَسَهُ
وَسِلاَحَهُ وَغُلاَمَهُ**. فَقَالَ عُمَرُ: **رَحِمَ اللهُ أَبَا سُلَيْمَانَ، كَانَ عَلَى مَا ظَنَنَّاهُ بِهِ**.» — *When
Khālid died he left nothing but his horse, his weapons and his servant. ʿUmar said: "God have mercy
on Abū Sulaymān; he was as we had thought him."*

# X. WHAT COULD **NOT** BE ESTABLISHED

1. **The title «سیف اللہ المسلول».** The word **المسلول does not appear on a single page fetched into
   this repository** — not in خالد ؓ's ترجمة in سیر (ج۱ ص۳۶۶–۳۸۳), not in his obituary in البدایہ
   (ج۷ ص۲۳۵–۲۴۰), not anywhere in the اليرموك or Iraq chapters. What the sources give is
   **«سَيْفٌ مِنْ سُيُوْفِ اللهِ»** (البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۶ from مسند أحمد ١/٨ and ٤/٩٠; سیر ج۱ ص۳۷۲–۳۷۴), the
   verb **«سَلَّهُ اللهُ عَلَى الكُفَّارِ وَالمُنَافِقِيْنَ»** in the same ḥadīth, and الذہبی's own **«وَسَمَّاهُ
   النَّبِيُّ ﷺ: سَيْفَ اللهِ»** (سیر ج۱ ص۳۶۶). ⚠ **`CLAUDE.md` §1.2 currently lists «سیف اللہ المسلول»
   among the القاب the tradition gave.** On the evidence fetched, the page-cited form is **«سیف الله»
   / «سیف من سیوف الله»**. Use that on slides. The longer form should carry `(to verify)` until a page
   is produced for it.
2. **A total for the Muslim dead across the whole Syrian campaign.** Only اليرموك is given a figure
   (three thousand — البدایہ ج۷ ص۹۶; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۵۸). No source fetched totals the campaign.
3. **Any claim that خالد ؓ was never defeated.** No page fetched says it. The nearest thing is
   **أُكيدر's** assessment, which is an enemy's opinion offered to persuade his own side to
   surrender (البدایہ ج۷ ص۷۵). **Do not upgrade it into a historian's verdict.**
4. **The size of the Muslim force at أجنادين.** Neither البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۹ nor الکامل ج۲ ص۲۶۰–۲۶۱ gives
   one. Only اليرموك has numbers.
5. **Which of خالد ؓ or أبو عبيدة ؓ actually wrote the Damascus treaty.** البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۹ records
   both views and settles neither: «ثم قيل: إن أبا عبيدة هو الذي كتب لهم كتابَ الصلح، وهذا هو
   الأنسبُ والأشهرُ … وقيل: بل الذي كتب لهم الصلحَ خالد بن الوليد … فالله أعلم». Meanwhile ج۷ ص۱۰۶
   says the document was خالد ؓ's and the other four witnessed it. **State the disagreement.**
6. **Whether خالد ؓ went to المدینہ immediately after the first removal or stayed in Syria.**
   البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۳ implies he went; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۶۸ records both and says **«وَقِيلَ: بَلْ هُوَ أَقَامَ
   بِالشَّامِ مَعَ الْمُسْلِمِينَ. وَهُوَ أَصَحُّ.»**
7. **The place of خالد ؓ's death.** حمص in the majority view (البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۸; سیر ج۱ ص۳۶۷، ص۳۸۳);
   المدینہ per دُحيم (سیر ج۱ ص۳۸۳). The سیر editor devotes a long footnote at ج۱ ص۳۶۷ to the problem
   without settling it. **Say حمص, and say the other view exists.**
8. **A page for the popular story that خالد ؓ fought at اليرموك with the Prophet's ﷺ hair in his
   cap and that this was why he was never beaten.** What *is* on the page (البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۶, with the
   editor citing البوصيري «رواه أبو يعلى بسند صحيح») is narrower and better: he lost the cap during
   the fighting and searched hard for it, and when reproached said «إن فيها شيئًا من شعر ناصية رسول
   الله ﷺ، **وإنها ما كانت معي في موقف إلا نُصرتُ بها**». **Quote the narrow version.**

# Y. NUMBERS, AND WHY THEY ARE UNRELIABLE

Every figure below is given **exactly as its source gives it**. None of it is audited. Early Muslim
historiography counted armies the way it counted anything else — by report, through a chain, often
generations later — and our own books show they knew it, because **they print two or three
incompatible figures side by side on the same page and leave them there.** That habit is the honest
one, and it is the one to imitate on the slide.

# EVENT CARDS

### E-C1 · The order to Iraq
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** المحرم 12 AH `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۲]` · **Map:** first arrow leaves
Arabia — from اليمامة east to the lower Euphrates. Arabia stops being the whole board.
**What happened:** Khālid ؓ had just finished al-Yamāma. A letter came from Abū Bakr ؓ in Medina. It
did not say "conquer Iraq." It said: come at Iraq from its lower end. Call the people to God. If
they answer, they are Muslims — what is yours is theirs. If they refuse, take the jizya. If they
refuse that, fight. And then two restrictions that are easy to miss. Do not compel any man to march
with you. And do not use any man who apostatised, even if he has come back.
**The statement:**
> وأن يَتألَّفَ الناسَ ويدعوهم إلى الله ﷿، فإن أجابوا وإلا أخذ منهم الجزيةَ فإن امتنعوا عن ذلك قاتلهم،
> وأمره أن لا يُكْرِه أحدًا على المسير معه، ولا يستعينُ بمنِ ارتدّ عن الإسلام وإن كان [قد] عاد إليه
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۲ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3208
> *English:* "…that he should win the people over and call them to God; if they answered, well, and
> if not, take the jizya from them; and if they refused that, fight them. And he ordered him not to
> compel anyone to march with him, and not to make use of any man who had apostatised from Islam,
> even if he had returned to it."
**عبرت:** A state that has just won a civil war and still refuses to conscript the defeated is
telling you what it thinks it is for.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-C2 · The letter to the marzubāns of Persia
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** 12 AH `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۴]` · **Map:** a line drawn from الحيرة to
المدائن — the first time Medina addresses the Persian court directly.
**What happened:** Khālid ؓ had taken al-Ḥīra. Upriver from it stood al-Madāʾin — the throne of
Persia, the iwān of Chosroes. He did not march on it. He wrote to it. The letter was short and it
survives, because al-Shaʿbī says the family of Buqayla in al-Ḥīra read it out to him. It sets out
one offer, one alternative, and one warning. Ibn Kathīr رحمہ اللہ ends the page on their reaction:
when they read it, they were left marvelling.
**The statement:**
> فابعثوا إليَّ بالرُّهن واعتقدوا مني الذِّمة، و (إلا) فوالذي لا إله غيره لأبعثن إليكم قومًا يحبّون الموتَ
> كما تحبّون أنتم الحياة
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3210
> *English:* "…so send me hostages and take a covenant of protection from me. Otherwise — by Him
> beside whom there is no god — I shall send against you a people who love death as you love life."
**عبرت:** A man who has made peace with his own death is very difficult to bargain with.
**Hands-up?** Ask the room: how far is Medina from the Persian capital? Then tell them a letter went,
before an army did.

### E-C3 · The poison cup at al-Ḥīra
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** 12 AH `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۷۰; also سیر ج۱ ص۳۷۶, where the chain is
مرسل]` · **Map:** al-Ḥīra pinned — the old Arab-Christian capital of the Lakhmids, now negotiating.
**What happened:** The man who negotiated for al-Ḥīra was ʿAmr b. ʿAbd al-Masīḥ b. Buqayla, a
Christian Arab, very old. Khālid ؓ noticed a pouch on him and asked what was in it. Poison, the man
said — enough to kill within the hour. And why do you carry it? So that if I see ruin coming on my
people, I can take it. Khālid ؓ took the pouch out of his hand, said the name of God over it, and —
as the commanders reached out to stop him — swallowed it. Ibn Buqayla, watching, said that he had
never seen a day of plainer good fortune than this; and he turned to the people of al-Ḥīra, who then
asked Khālid ؓ for terms. (The page does not add a sentence about the poison's effect; it moves
straight to the treaty. Narrate only that far.)
**The statement:**
> إنه لن تموت نفس حتى تأتي على أجلها … بسم الله خير الأسماء، رب الأرض والسماء، الذي ليس يضرُ مع اسمه
> داء، الرحمن الرحيم
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۷۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3216
> *English:* "No soul dies until it reaches its appointed term … In the name of God, best of names,
> Lord of earth and sky, with whose name no sickness can harm — the Merciful, the Compassionate."
**عبرت:** Certainty about the appointed hour is not recklessness; it is what makes a man usable.
**Hands-up?** no
*(Note for the speaker: al-Dhahabī رحمہ اللہ carries the same incident by a different route where the
chain is broken — the narrator did not hear it from Khālid ؓ. Tell it as a report the books carry.)*

### E-C4 · The secret ḥajj and the letter of reproof
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** ذو القعدة–ذو الحجة 12 AH `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۷۸]` · **Map:** a thin
line looping from al-Firāḍ on the Roman frontier all the way down to Mecca and back — a route
"nobody had travelled before."
**What happened:** After the fighting at al-Firāḍ, Khālid ؓ sent the army back to al-Ḥīra and let
them believe he was riding in the rear. Instead he turned south with a few companions and rode to
Mecca by a road nobody had used, performed the pilgrimage, and caught up with the rearguard before
it got home. Almost nobody knew. Abū Bakr ؓ only found out when the pilgrims came back to Medina and
mentioned it. He wrote to him. The punishment was to be moved from the Iraq front to the Syrian one.
And with the reprimand came a warning about something other than absence.
**The statement:**
> فليهنئك أبا سليمان النية والحظوة، فأتممْ يُتمِّمِ الله لكَ، ولا يدخلنك عُجْبٌ فتخسر وتخذل، وإياكَ أن
> تُدلَّ بعمل فإنَّ اللهَ له المنُّ وهو وليّ الجزاء
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۷۸ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3224
> *English:* "May the intention and the favour bring you joy, Abū Sulaymān; finish, and God will
> finish for you. But let no self-admiration enter you, or you will lose and be abandoned; and
> beware of being made proud by a deed, for the bounty is God's and He is the master of reward."
**عبرت:** The reprimand a commander needs is rarely about the rule he broke.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-C5 · The march across the waterless desert
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** 13 AH `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۷; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۵۳; سیر ج۱ ص۳۶۶]` ·
**Map:** THE map moment of the evening. A line strikes north-west out of Iraq at Qurāqir, crosses
blank desert with no road on it, and comes out at Suwā and Tadmur — behind the Roman armies.
**What happened:** The commanders in Syria wrote to Abū Bakr ؓ asking for reinforcement and telling
him what the Romans had gathered at the Yarmūk. He wrote back to Khālid ؓ in Iraq: put a deputy over
Iraq, bring the men you have to Syria, and when you reach them you are the commander over them. The
letter reached Iraq and Khālid ؓ set off at once — but not by any road. He chose the
Samāwa, five days of waterless country. His guide was Rāfiʿ b. ʿUmayra al-Ṭāʾī, and Rāfiʿ told him
plainly it could not be done with cavalry and baggage: a lone rider fears that road. Khālid ؓ said he
had no choice; he had to come out behind the Romans. So they prepared. The strongest camels were made
thirsty, then watered twice over, their lips cut and their mouths bound so they could not chew and
lose the water. Every day they opened the bellies of ten of them and watered the horses from what was
inside. On the last stretch Rāfiʿ, whose eyes were bad, asked the men whether they could see a
box-thorn bush the height of a seated man. They said no. He said: then by God you are dead, and I am
dead with you. They looked again. The bush was there, cut down to a stump. They dug at its root and
found the spring.
**The statement:**
> وَاللَّهِ مَا وَرَدْتُ هَذَا الْمَاءَ قَطُّ إِلَّا مَرَّةً وَاحِدَةً مَعَ أَبِي وَأَنَا غُلَامٌ
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۵۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/944
> *English:* "By God, I have come to this water only once in my life — with my father, when I was a
> boy."
**عبرت:** The march was survived on preparation and on one man's memory; neither was an accident.
**Hands-up?** Ask: how do you carry water for an army across five days of desert, with no wells? Take
two answers. Then tell them.
*(Length: البدایہ says nine days, الکامل and سیر say five nights. Say both.)*

### E-C6 · "At daybreak the people praise the night march"

**What happened:** On that crossing an Arab of the country had told Khālid ؓ that if he reached a

> عندَ الصَّباح يحمد القوم السُّرى

**عبرت:** The night the effort is made and the morning it is judged are never the same morning.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-C7 · Four commanders, four roads
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** early 13 AH `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۳–۸۴]` · **Map:** four arrows leave
Medina at once, on four separate roads, and four sectors of Syria light up — Damascus, Homs,
Palestine, Jordan.
**What happened:** Abū Bakr ؓ finished with Arabia and turned to Syria. He did not send one army. He
appointed four commanders and gave each of them a province before it was taken: Yazīd b. Abī
Sufyān ؓ Damascus, Abū ʿUbayda ؓ Homs, ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ ؓ Palestine, Shuraḥbīl b. Ḥasana ؓ Jordan. He
ordered each to take a different road. And he walked out of Medina on foot beside each of them while
they rode, giving his instructions. Ibn Kathīr رحمہ اللہ says the four roads were not a military
trick: the caliph was following a verse.
**The statement:**
> ﴿يَابَنِيَّ لَا تَدْخُلُوا مِنْ بَابٍ وَاحِدٍ وَادْخُلُوا مِنْ أَبْوَابٍ مُتَفَرِّقَةٍ﴾ [يوسف: ٦٧]
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۳–۸۴ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3229 (opening words) and
> https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3230 (the rest of the verse)
> *English:* "O my sons, do not enter by one gate; enter by separate gates." — Yūsuf 67, quoted by
> Ibn Kathīr as the precedent Abū Bakr ؓ was following.
**عبرت:** A plan is safer when it is not one plan.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-C8 · The spy's report
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** أجنادين, 2 nights remaining of جمادى الأولى 13 AH `[SOURCED — الکامل
ج۲ ص۲۶۱; البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۹]` · **Map:** Ajnādayn pinned, between al-Ramla and Bayt Jibrīn in Palestine.
**What happened:** Before the battle at Ajnādayn the Roman commander wanted to know what he was
facing, so he sent a Christian Arab into the Muslim camp to look. The man stayed a day and a night
and came back. He did not report numbers or weapons. He reported a description. And the commander,
hearing it, gave the reply of a man who has understood something before his own army has.
**The statement:**
> بِاللَّيْلِ رُهْبَانٌ وَبِالنَّهَارِ فُرْسَانٌ، وَلَوْ سَرَقَ ابْنُ مَلِكِهِمْ قَطَعُوهُ، وَلَوْ زَنَى رُجِمَ … فَقَالَ: إِنْ كُنْتَ
> صَدَقْتَنِي لَبَطْنُ الْأَرْضِ خَيْرٌ مِنْ لِقَاءِ هَؤُلَاءِ عَلَى ظَهْرِهَا
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۶۱ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/952
> *English:* "By night monks, by day horsemen; if their king's son stole they would cut off his
> hand, and if he committed adultery he would be stoned." He said: "If you have told me the truth,
> then the belly of the earth is better than meeting these men on its back."
**عبرت:** What frightened the enemy was not the cavalry; it was that the law was the same for the
ruler's son.
**Hands-up?** Ask: if you sent a man to look at an army, what would you want him to count? Then read
what this one actually reported.

### E-C9 · Khālid ؓ hands the command round
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** the morning of اليرموك `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۹; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۵۵]` ·
**Map:** the Yarmūk. Roman camp at al-Wāqūṣa with the ravine at its back; the Muslim line sitting
across the only road out.
**What happened:** The Muslim armies had come to the Yarmūk still divided — four commanders, each
over his own men, nobody over all of them. On the morning of the battle Khālid ؓ stood up and made a
proposal. He did not ask for the command. He proposed that they pass it round: one of us today,
another tomorrow, another the day after, until every one of us has held it — and let me have today.
They made him commander, expecting the thing to last a long time. It lasted one day.
**The statement:**
> إن هذا يوم من أيام الله، لا ينبغي فيه الفخر ولا البغي … فتعالوا فلنتعاور الإمارة فليكن عليها بعضُنا
> اليوم والآخر غدًا والآخر بعد غد، حتى يتأمَّر كلكم، ودعوني اليوم أليكم
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3235
> *English:* "This is a day of God's days — there should be no boasting in it and no aggression …
> So come, let us take the command in turn: one of us today, another tomorrow, another the day
> after, until every one of you has commanded. And let me have charge of you today."
**عبرت:** The man who asked for one day got the day; a man who had demanded the whole command might
have got neither.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-C10 · "Armies are made many by victory"
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** اليرموك `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۹۱–۹۲]` · **Map:** the numbers panel goes
up beside the map — with the caveat line under it.
**What happened:** Khālid ؓ had come in from Iraq and was looking at the Roman lines. A Christian Arab
standing near him said out loud what everyone could see: how many the Romans are, and how few the
Muslims. He got an answer that had nothing to do with counting.
**The statement:**
> ويلك، أتخوّفني بالروم؟ وإنما تكثر الجنود بالنصر، وتقل بالخذلان لا بعدد الرجال
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۲ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3238
> *English:* "Woe to you — are you frightening me with the Romans? Armies are made many by victory
> and few by abandonment, not by the number of men."
**عبرت:** Counting the enemy is useful; being governed by the count is not.
**Hands-up?** Ask the room to guess the two army sizes. Take three guesses. Then show that the books
themselves give three different answers, and say why.

### E-C11 · Jirja and the two rakʿas
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** اليرموك `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۹۵–۹۶]` · **Map:** no change; a single
point between the two lines.
**What happened:** One of the senior Roman commanders, Jirja, rode out from his own line and called
Khālid ؓ to him. They met between the armies, close enough that their horses' necks crossed. He asked
one question: is it true God sent your Prophet a sword from heaven, and gave it to you, so that you
never draw it without routing them? Khālid ؓ said: no. Then why are you called the Sword of God? And
Khālid ؓ answered by telling him what he had been — one of the men who called the Prophet ﷺ a liar and
kept away from him. Jirja kept asking. What do you call people to. What if a man refuses. What does a
man get who joins you today, having missed everything. Khālid ؓ said: the same reward — and better,
because you did not see what we saw and you still came. At that Jirja turned his shield over and
crossed to the Muslim side. Khālid ؓ took him to his tent, poured a waterskin over him, and prayed two
rakʿas with him. Jirja was killed later that same day.
**The statement:**
> وأُصيب جرجة ﵀ ولم يصل لله إلا تلك الركعتين مع خالد
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3242
> *English:* "And Jirja was killed, and he never prayed to God but those two rakʿas with Khālid."
**عبرت:** A whole life can turn on one honest question asked of an opponent instead of about him.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-C12 · The courier in the middle of the battle
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** اليرموك, and the death of أبو بكر ؓ on Monday, 8 nights remaining of
جمادى الآخرة 13 AH `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۹۵، ص۱۰۲]` · **Map:** a small arrow from Medina to the
Yarmūk — the news travelling while the battle is being fought.
**What happened:** In the middle of the fighting a courier arrived from the Ḥijāz and was brought to
Khālid ؓ. Khālid ؓ asked him what the news was. The man told him quietly: Abū Bakr ؓ is dead. ʿUmar ؓ
is caliph. And Abū ʿUbayda ؓ has been put over the armies in your place. Khālid ؓ heard both pieces of
news at once. He said, loudly enough for the men to hear, "You have done well." Then he took the
letter, put it in his quiver, and went back to running the battle. The army learned neither thing
until it was over. Ibn Kathīr رحمہ اللہ records what happened when they did: they did not rejoice at
the spoil as much as they grieved for Abū Bakr ؓ.
**The statement:**
> فأسرّها خالدٌ ولم يُبْد ذلك للناس لئلا يحصل ضعفٌ ووهنٌ في تلك الحال، وقال له والناسُ يسمعون: أحسنتَ
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3241
> *English:* "Khālid kept it secret and did not show it to the men, lest weakness and faltering set
> in at such a moment — and he said to him, with the men listening: 'You have done well.'"
**عبرت:** He was told he had been replaced and finished the day's work anyway; that is what obedience
looks like before anyone is watching.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-C13 · Damascus, taken two ways at once
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** mid رجب 14 AH per the majority `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۸]`; the storming
`[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۵; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۶۹–۲۷۰]` · **Map:** Damascus falls. Colour it **half and
half** — that is the whole point of the card.
**What happened:** The siege had gone through the winter and stalled. Then a son was born to the
Byzantine commander of the city, and he made a feast; the men on the wall ate, drank and slept. One
man noticed. Khālid ؓ, Ibn Kathīr رحمہ اللہ says, neither slept nor let anyone else sleep, and had
scouts reporting the state of the garrison morning and evening. He had already made ladders out of
rope. That night his party swam the moat with waterskins at their necks, fixed the ladders to the
battlements, climbed, and shouted the takbīr from the top of the wall. He came down inside, killed the
gatekeepers and cut the bolts. At the same hour, at the other gates, the townspeople — hearing the
noise and not knowing what it was — accepted the terms the Muslims had been offering all along and
opened their own gates. The commanders met in the middle of the city: one side had come in by force,
the other by treaty.
**The statement:**
> فَالْتَقَى خَالِدٌ وَالْقُوَّادُ فِي وَسَطِهَا، هَذَا قَتْلًا وَنَهْبًا، وَهَذَا صَفْحًا وَتَسْكِينًا، فَأَجْرَوْا نَاحِيَةَ خَالِدٍ
> مَجْرَى الصُّلْحِ
> — الکامل فی التاریخ ج۲ ص۲۷۰ · https://shamela.ws/book/21712/961
> *English:* "Khālid and the other commanders met in the middle of the city — this one killing and
> plundering, that one pardoning and calming — so they applied the terms of the treaty to Khālid's
> side as well."
**عبرت:** Where they could not tell which had come first, they gave the city the gentler ruling — and
called it caution.
**Hands-up?** Ask: if half a city surrenders and half is stormed on the same night, what are the
terms? Take answers. Then give the Companions' own answer.

### E-C14 · Fiḥl — the mud they laid themselves
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** 13 or 14 AH; the sequence relative to Damascus is disputed `[SOURCED —
البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۱۲–۱۱۳; الکامل ج۲ ص۲۷۰]` · **Map:** Fiḥl / Baysān in the Ghawr, below the Jordan.
**What happened:** The Romans who had gathered at Fiḥl did something clever. They opened the
irrigation channels and flooded the ground between themselves and the Muslim camp until it was deep
mud, so that no charge could reach them. The Arabs afterwards called that whole campaign "the muddy
one." Then, thinking the Muslims must be off their guard, they attacked in the night. But the
commander there was Shuraḥbīl b. Ḥasana ؓ, who never spent a night or a morning out of battle order.
The camp came up as one man. The fighting ran through the night and the whole of the next day. When
the Romans finally broke, the rout carried them straight back into their own mud.
**The statement:**
> وأسلمتهم هزيمتهم إلى ذلك الوحل الذي كانوا قد كادوا به المسلمين فغرَّقهم الله فيه
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۱۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3259
> *English:* "And their rout delivered them into the very mud with which they had meant to trap the
> Muslims, and God drowned them in it."
**عبرت:** The trap a man builds is still there when he is the one running.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-C15 · The removal, and what the two men said
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** 13 AH, on ʿUmar ؓ's accession `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۰۳، ص۲۳۶; سیر
ج۱ ص۳۷۳، ص۳۷۸]` · **Map:** no change. The name over Syria changes; nothing else does.
**What happened:** The first thing ʿUmar ؓ did as caliph was to remove Khālid ؓ from the command of
Syria and put Abū ʿUbayda ؓ in his place. The letter was blunt: take his turban off his head and
divide his property with him. Abū ʿUbayda ؓ carried it out — the books say he divided even the pair of
sandals, taking one and leaving him the other. Khālid ؓ said one sentence while it was being done: I
hear and obey the Commander of the Faithful. And then, at the handover, each of the two men said
something about the other — and neither of them said it in his own words. Each quoted the Prophet ﷺ.
**The statement:**
> فقال خالد: بعث إليكم أمين هذه الأمة، سمعتُ رسول الله ﷺ يقول: "أمينُ هذه الأمة أبو عبيدة بن الجراح".
> فقال أبو عبيدة: سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يقول: "خالدٌ سيفٌ من سيوف الله، نِعْمَ فَتى العشيرة"
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۶ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3382
> *English:* Khālid said: "He has sent you the trustee of this community — I heard the Messenger of
> God ﷺ say: 'The trustee of this community is Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ.'" And Abū ʿUbayda said: "I
> heard the Messenger of God ﷺ say: 'Khālid is a sword among the swords of God — what an excellent
> young man of the clan.'"
**عبرت:** Each of them met the other's promotion by quoting the Prophet ﷺ in the other's favour; that
is what settled it, and it is what should settle it for us.
**Hands-up?** no
*(Speaker note: narrate, do not judge. If the room pushes on why, go straight to E-C16 and stop
there.)*

### E-C16 · "I did not remove him for any suspicion"
**Tier:** CORE · **When:** 15 AH (after Qinnasrīn), and again in writing in 17 AH `[SOURCED —
البدایہ ج۷ ص۱۵۳; الکامل ج۲ ص۳۶۰; سیر ج۱ ص۳۷۸]` · **Map:** no change.
**What happened:** After Khālid ؓ had been removed from the command he stayed in Syria and went on
fighting under Abū ʿUbayda ؓ. At Qinnasrīn he took the town, and when the reports reached Medina,
ʿUmar ؓ said something about Abū Bakr ؓ, and about himself. Two years later, when he removed Khālid ؓ
a second time, he did not leave the matter to talk: he wrote a circular to every garrison city
setting out his reason. Both times the reason is the same, and neither time is it a charge of
wrongdoing.
**The statement:**
> يرحمُ الله أبا بكر، كان أعلمَ بالرجالِ مني، واللّهِ إنِّي لم أعزلْهُ عن ريبةٍ ولكن خشيتُ أن يُوكلَ
> الناسُ إليه
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۱۵۳ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3299
> *English:* "May God have mercy on Abū Bakr — he knew men better than I do. By God, I did not
> remove him for any suspicion, but I feared that people would come to rely on him."
**عبرت:** A commander can be removed for being too successful, and both men can be right.
**Hands-up?** no

### E-C17 · The death on the bed
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** 21 AH, at Homs `[SOURCED — سیر ج۱ ص۳۶۷، ص۳۸۱، ص۳۸۳; البدایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۷
(the «ما في جسدي شبر» version)، ص۲۳۹ (the statement quoted below, and the women weeping)]` ·
**Map:** a last pin at Homs — where the man who crossed the Samāwa is buried.
**What happened:** He died in bed, and he minded. He had looked for death in the places death is
found, he said, and it had not been decreed for him. He was asked what of his own deeds he hoped in
most, and he named neither Yarmūk nor Damascus nor Iraq — he named one cold night spent under his
shield in the rain, waiting for the light. Then he gave his instructions: when I am dead, take my
weapons and my horse and make them equipment in the way of God. He left nothing else — a horse, his
weapons, and a servant. When ʿUmar ؓ heard, on the Mecca road, he said "innā li-Llāh" over and over
and lowered his head. And when the women of Banū al-Mughīra gathered in his house to weep and someone
suggested the caliph forbid them, ʿUmar ؓ refused.
**The statement:**
> وما من عملي شيءٌ أرْجَى عندي بعد لا إله إلا الله من ليلة بتُّها وأنا مُتَترّسٌ والسماء تهلّني ننتظر
> الصبح حتى نُغير على الكفار
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۲۳۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3385
> *English:* "There is nothing of my deeds I hope in more, after 'there is no god but God', than one
> night I passed under my shield with the rain pouring on me, waiting for the dawn."
**عبرت:** The deed he hoped in was the one no one had watched.
**Hands-up?** Ask: of everything this man did, which act do you think he hoped in most? Take three
answers. Then read his own.

### E-C18 · What the Romans said about why they lost
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** after اليرموك `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۹۹]` · **Map:** Heraclius withdraws
from Homs, then Antioch, then out of Syria altogether — the Roman line pulls back off the board.
**What happened:** The defeated troops came back to Heraclius. He asked them a plain question: are
these men not human beings like you? They said yes. Are you not more numerous? They said many times
over. Then why do you keep getting routed? One of their elders answered, and the answer was a list —
half of it about the Muslims and half of it about themselves. Heraclius did not argue with him.
**The statement:**
> من أجل أنهم يقومون الليل ويصومون النهار، ويوفون بالعهد، ويأمرون بالمعروف، وينهون عن المنكر،
> ويتناصفون بينهم، ومن أجل أنا نشرب الخمر، ونزني، ونركب الحرام، وننقض العهد، ونغْصب ونظلم … فقال: أنت
> صدقتني
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۹۹ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3245
> *English:* "Because they stand at night and fast by day, keep their covenants, command good and
> forbid wrong, and deal justly among themselves — and because we drink wine, commit adultery, ride
> into the forbidden, break our covenants, seize and oppress." … He said: "You have told me the
> truth."
**عبرت:** They were beaten, and they knew exactly why; knowing it is not the same as changing it.
**Hands-up?** no
*(Speaker note: Ibn Kathīr رحمہ اللہ transmits this from a collection outside the six books. Cite it
to al-Bidāya, and say "the book records." The narrower, better-known line on the same page — "by
night monks, by day horsemen" — is the safer one if only one is used.)*

### E-C19 · The Romans in chains
**Tier:** CUT · **When:** ذات السلاسل 12 AH, and again at اليرموك `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۶۵، ص۸۹]` ·
**Map:** none.
**What happened:** Twice the sources describe an enemy army chaining its own ranks together so that
no man could run. At Kāẓima the Persians did it, and Ibn Kathīr records that the Muslims took it as a
bad omen against them and said so out loud. At the Yarmūk the figure given is tens of thousands
chained in tens — though on the same page Sayf gives a much smaller figure and Ibn Kathīr writes
"God knows best" after it. At the end, when the Roman line broke, the chains did what chains do.
**The statement:**
> فَتيمَّنَ المسلمون بذلك وقالوا هذا طائر مشؤوم قيدوا نفوسهم بالحديد
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۶۵ · https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3211
> *English:* "The Muslims took it as a good omen for themselves and said: this is an ill-omened
> bird — they have shackled their own souls with iron."
**عبرت:** An army that has to chain its men in place has already told you what it thinks of them.
**Hands-up?** no
*(CUT because the chained-ranks detail is the part of the Yarmūk numbers most likely to be
challenged, and because it adds nothing the evening needs. Recorded so it is not re-researched.)*

### E-C20 · The judge and the reciter
**Tier:** GOOD · **When:** the morning of اليرموك `[SOURCED — البدایہ ج۷ ص۸۹–۹۰]` · **Map:** the
battle-order diagram — centre, wings, cavalry behind each wing, Abū ʿUbayda ؓ behind everyone.
**What happened:** When the line was drawn up at the Yarmūk, the appointments made that morning are
worth reading in full. Qubāth b. Ashyam ؓ over the scouts. ʿAbd Allāh b. Masʿūd ؓ over the spoils.
Abū Sufyān ؓ to walk the ranks exhorting. And two more that no army list would think to include: Abū
al-Dardāʾ ؓ as the judge of the day, and al-Miqdād ؓ to ride round the ranks reciting Sūrat al-Anfāl
and the verses of jihād. Abū ʿUbayda ؓ, on Khālid ؓ's advice, moved himself out of the centre and
stood behind the whole army — so that any man who broke would see him and turn back out of shame.
**The statement:**
> والقاضي يومئذ أبو الدرداء، وقاصّهم الذي يعظُهم ويحثُّهم على القتال أبو سفيان بن حرب، وقارؤهم الذي
> يدور على الناس فيقرأ سورة الأنفال وآياتِ الجهاد المِقْداد بن الأسود
> — البدایہ والنہایہ ج۷ ص۸۹–۹۰ · the list begins at https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3235 (ص۸۹) and
> runs on to https://shamela.ws/book/30097/3236 (ص۹۰)
> *English:* "And the judge that day was Abū al-Dardāʾ; the one who preached to them and urged them
> to fight was Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb; and their reciter, who went round the men reading Sūrat al-Anfāl
> and the verses of jihād, was al-Miqdād b. al-Aswad."
**عبرت:** They appointed a judge before the battle, not after it.
**Hands-up?** Ask: what posts would you fill before a battle? Take answers. Then read the list.
