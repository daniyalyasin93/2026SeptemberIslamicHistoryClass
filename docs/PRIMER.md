## 0 · The frame you are teaching inside

Before any of the story, three ideas from your own مقدمہ do more work than any date:

- **تاریخِ اسلام = تاریخِ مسلمین** (ص۶۱, its own section). We study the history of the *Muslims* —
  who are human, with rise and decline — not of the *religion*, which is complete. Every ugly
  episode in ten weeks is filed here. This is the single most useful sentence you own.
- **The fiqh grading of studying history** (ص۵۲–۵۳). Some of it فرضِ عین, some مکروہ, some حرام —
  and studying the مشاجرات without need is مکروہ, while answering propaganda against the صحابہ ؓ
  becomes واجب. Both halves matter: it restrains session 3 *and* authorises it.
- **روایہ and درایہ** (ص۶۳–۶۴). A chain of transmission, and weighing the content by reason. The
  Muslim tradition uniquely uses both. This is why "there's no historical basis for that" often
  means "it doesn't fit our conjecture."

## 1 · ۱۱–۴۰ھ [v] · خلافتِ راشدہ — thirty years

The Prophet ﷺ dies in ۱۱ھ [v]. Three things happen almost at once: the succession is settled at
سقیفہ; much of Arabia stops paying زکوٰۃ and has to be brought back (**حروبِ الردہ**); and after heavy
losses among the قرّاء at **یمامہ** [v], the Qur'ān is gathered into one written collection.

Then, within roughly a decade, two empires that had exhausted each other in a long war are broken:
**Byzantium** loses Syria at **یرموک** [v] and then Egypt; **Sasanian Persia** is ended after
**قادسیہ** [v] and the fall of its capital. Under سیدنا عمر ؓ this is not only conquest but
administration — the **دیوان** (a register and stipend system) and the **Hijri calendar** itself.

Under سیدنا عثمان ؓ expansion continues and the **مصحف** is standardised. Then strain: distance,
new wealth, a generation that did not see the Prophet ﷺ. His killing opens the **first فتنہ**, and
سیدنا علی ؓ's caliphate is spent inside it.

> **Where it comes from:** البدایہ (30097) narrates all of this and applies criticism.
> سیر (10906) for the people. **For anything touching the فتنہ, use Rehan sahib's framing, not raw
> طبری** — طبری collects everything with its chain and does not sift for you, which is exactly what
> ص۷۱ of your مقدمہ warns about.
>
> **Watch for:** the temptation to explain *why* the فتنہ happened. You narrate what the book
> narrates and stop. That is not evasion — it is ص۵۲–۵۳.

## 2 · ۴۰–۱۳۲ھ [v] · بنو امیہ — the office becomes a throne

سیدنا حسن ؓ hands over to سیدنا معاویہ ؓ and the fighting stops — the year is remembered as
**عامُ الجماعۃ** [v] (*the year of unity — a name for the year, not a title for a man*). The capital
moves to **دمشق**. Succession becomes hereditary, and that change is the substance of session 4.

**کربلا** [v ۶۱ھ] belongs here. Handle it only from the page.

Then the widest reach of the whole story: in roughly the same decade [v ۹۲–۹۳ھ] Muslim armies enter
**الأندلس** in the west and **سندھ** in the east. Look at a map when you read that sentence — it is
the most striking single fact in the course.

**عمر بن عبد العزیز رحمہ اللہ** is the Umayyad caliph remembered for returning what his family had
taken. He is the moral centre of the evening.

> **Where:** البدایہ (30097). سیر (10906) for عمر بن عبد العزیز رحمہ اللہ.

## 3 · ۱۳۲–۶۵۶ھ [v] · بنو عباس — the century of books

The Abbasids take power and build **بغداد** [v]. Paper arrives from Samarqand and changes
everything — a scholar can now own books rather than memorise alone. Translation, mathematics,
medicine, and above all the maturing of the Islamic sciences themselves: this is when حدیث, فقہ and
تاریخ are put into the forms we still use.

The evening's moral centre is **امام احمد بن حنبل رحمہ اللہ** and the **محنہ** — the state trying
to compel a doctrinal position, and a scholar refusing. That is what "a civilisation is measured by
whether its scholars can say no to its rulers" actually refers to.

Meanwhile in the west, **قرطبہ** under the Umayyads of الأندلس builds a parallel capital — the two
run alongside each other for centuries, which is why session 5 pairs them.

> **Where:** البدایہ (30097) · الکامل (21712) for the year-by-year · سیر (10906) for امام احمد
> رحمہ اللہ.

## 4 · ۴۹۲–۶۵۸ھ [v] · the near-death

Crusader armies take **بیت المقدس** [v ۴۹۲ھ/۱۰۹۹]. Nearly a century later **صلاح الدین ایوبی رحمہ
اللہ** retakes it [v ۵۸۳ھ/۱۱۸۷].

Then the far worse blow, from the east. The Mongols reach **بغداد** in **۶۵۶ھ/۱۲۵۸** [v]: the city
falls, the caliph is killed, the libraries are destroyed. This is the lowest point in the whole
fourteen centuries.

And then it turns. At **عین جالوت** [v ۶۵۸ھ/۱۲۶۰] the Mongol advance is stopped for the first time.
**قاہرہ** becomes where the scholars and the books had gone — which is *why* the civilisation
survived losing its capital.

> **Where:** البدایہ (30097) reaches ۷۷۴ھ so it covers all of this and is the natural spine.
> الکامل (21712) stops at ۶۲۸ھ — it has the Crusades but **not** the fall of بغداد.
>
> **For session 6, use the whole البخاری ۳۶۱۲ hadith, not the first half.** It goes from iron combs
> to «وَاللَّهِ لَيُتِمَّنَّ هَذَا الْأَمْرَ» — torture to a promise of completion. That turn is the shape of
> the evening. ✅ verified.

## 5 · ۶۹۹–۱۳۴۲ھ [v] · عثمانیہ — the last empire

A frontier principality in Anatolia grows into the longest-lived Muslim state. **قسطنطنیہ** falls
to سلطان محمد الفاتح in **۸۵۷ھ/۱۴۵۳** [v] — thirty-nine years before **غرناطہ**, the last of
الأندلس, is lost in **۱۴۹۲** [v]. One door closing as another opens; worth showing on the same
slide.

Expansion peaks under سلطان سلیمان [v], reaches Vienna, and then two centuries of retreat follow.
The caliphate is abolished in **۱۹۲۴** [v].

> **Where:** ⚠ **none of your standing shelf reaches this.** البدایہ ends ~۷۷۴ھ. This era needs
> either a later volume of *تاریخِ امت* or a separate named work. **Do not draft session 7 until
> that is decided** — it is where the review found a scene had been composed rather than sourced.

## 6 · ۹۳–۱۹۴۷ [v] · the subcontinent — how it reached us

**سندھ** [v ۹۳ھ], then centuries later the Ghaznavid and Ghurid campaigns, then a Muslim sultanate
ruling from **دہلی** for roughly six centuries [v], then the Mughals. Then decline, **۱۸۵۷** [v],
the scholarly and educational responses to it, and **۱۹۴۷** [v].

**شاہ ولی اللہ رحمہ اللہ** sits before 1857, not in it — he is the intellectual root of the
responses that come after, which is how you position him.

> **Where:** ⚠ **nothing on your shelf covers this either.** First check whether *تاریخِ امت*
> reaches South Asia at all. If not, the candidates are **تاریخ اسلام** (اکبر شاہ خان نجیب آبادی
> رحمہ اللہ) and **تاریخ دعوت و عزیمت** (ابو الحسن علی ندوی رحمہ اللہ). Run the choice past a
> scholar before naming it to the room.

## The five works you should be able to name and rank

From your own مقدمہ, ص۶۹–۷۸. Note that the book gives **خوبیاں and کمزوریاں for each** — that
candour is itself worth showing the room.

1. **تاریخ الطبری** — the raw well. Everything with its chain, sifted for nobody.
2. **الکامل** — ابن الاثیر رحمہ اللہ. Distilled, year by year, to ۶۲۸ھ.
3. **تاریخ الإسلام** — الذہبی رحمہ اللہ. The most carefully sifted.
4. **البدایہ والنہایہ** — ابن کثیر رحمہ اللہ. Reason plus chains, and readable.
5. **تاریخ ابن خلدون** — the مقدمہ is a theory of why states rise and fall.

**The two Ṭabarīs** (ص۷۱): two writers share almost the same name. Yours — ابو جعفر محمد بن جریر
الطبری رحمہ اللہ — is the Sunni imam, مفسر و محدث. The confusion with the other is common enough
that your own book devotes a section to answering it. **Learn that section**; it is the commonest
objection you will ever face about sources.

## Ten things worth knowing before you stand up

1. The whole thing is only ~۱۴۰۰ years. On a human-history scale that is recent.
2. Two exhausted empires, not one strong one, is why the ۷th-century expansion moved so fast.
3. Sindh and Spain in the same decade. Say it slowly; nobody expects it.
4. Baghdad falling did **not** end the civilisation — because the books and the scholars had moved.
5. Constantinople gained and Granada lost are 39 years apart [v].
6. The Ottomans lasted longer than any rival and still hollowed out. Length is not health.
7. The صحابہ ؓ disputes are graded مکروہ to study without need — *and* واجب to answer when needed.
8. "History of the Muslims, not of the religion" resolves nine-tenths of hard questions.
9. Dates catch forgers. That is the whole of session 1's method segment.
10. You are narrating a book. Say so in week 1, and say it again whenever the room gets hot.
