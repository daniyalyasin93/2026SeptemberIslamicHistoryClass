## What the room is for

AS WRITTEN BEFORE 2026-10-08. The series is to give non-specialists 'a usable mental map of Islamic history: where events happened, in what order, how we know them, and what civilizational lessons follow' (CLAUDE.md, head), to mature, religiously committed, Qur'an-literate adults under Tanzeem-e-Islami, by a speaker who is 'reading a book, not authoring history', delivering 'mostly in urdu with mix of english' (#68) from the deck and its notes alone (#60).

IN HIS OWN WORDS, WHAT AN EVENING SHOULD BE. Full: 'more and more events, maps, great statements and dialogues, and i will filter myself' (#20). A story: reviewed 'from story telling perspective', with no 'narrative or story discontinuity' (#45); the room and a manager asked to 'proceed calmly… teach history like a canvas of stories' and said 'it shouldn't feel like a history speed-run' (#20, #27). Seen, not read: 'If we had more maps and animated moving of troops as I speak it might have been more interesting' (#56) — now 'like total war type animation', 'kings and generals youtube channel style', 'maybe a close up map' (2026-10-08). Never boring and never thin: 'the too many slides with sometimes repeated text bored the audience' (#39); 'we will not be able to keep audience interest' (#62); 'i think i will run out of material' (#47). Nothing researched thrown away: 'we dont want to loose well researched details' (#65); 'we must not miss any detail or gloss over' (#70). With a plain lesson from the events themselves (the floor: 'derive lessons or relatability from at least 25-50% of the events', #26). Safe: Sunni frame, the quoted authorities carry every conclusion, no adjudication between Companions, 'no qa like which could de rail the lecture' (#24). Credible: Arabic from the sources on the face, references airtight. Deliverable by a man with stage fright: he must find his next line at a glance ('i will get lost trying to find what to say').

WHAT 2026-10-08 ADDS — THE ROOM IS ALSO FOR FAITH. Of al-Ala's three questions to his men at al-Dahna, the fajr prayer and the dua, he wrote: 'the duas and the manner of prayer as well should be described, this is good stuff for galvanizing the imaan of audience and getting them closer to deen… i guess this is so important that the three arabic questiosn should have their own slide.' And reverence is part of the purpose, not a point of style: ''we followed one man', this doesn't sit well with sahaba'. Until now the repo stated the purpose as a mental map plus 'civilizational lessons' and treated exactly this material as a hazard to hedge — the runsheet's only word on it was a warning that it is 'a karama narrative… narrate as the book narrates' (RUNSHEET.md:104), and the questions and the dua were filed in BACKGROUND while the face carried a logistics sentence. The purpose, corrected: a mental map AND a strengthening of iman. The moments where Companions pray, trust and speak of Allah are the high points of an evening and get the face, the Arabic, the speaker's name and the time; the Companions are always named and honoured; and it is still told as the named book tells it, with no doctrine built on a karama and no verdict between Companions (CLAUDE.md 1.6).

AND THE SYSTEM ITSELF IS NOW PART OF THE BRIEF: 'more importantly we need to make the system that generates our content always within the vision given my feedback. Making that system is important, be it for animations, closeup maps, framing, word choices etc.' Read with the record above, that means: his feedback is not a list of fixes to one deck but a standard every later deck must meet without his having to say it again.

## Rules that pull against each other, and how each is resolved

- DO NOT LOSE RESEARCHED DETAIL (#35 'the speaker notes carry the whole card', #65, #70) versus TOO MUCH IN THE NOTES (#22, #40, 2026-10-08 x5). Resolve by tiers with a hard boundary, in one pane: a lectern block (one warning line, SAY as short cues with blank lines, the quotation, the lesson) under a word budget; then a ruled divider; then BACKGROUND. Completeness is audited against the notes BOOK and the research note, not the pane — he said 'the arabic background in the notes is good' and 'notes are good but maybe increase spacing', so background stays, below the line.
- MORE SCRIPT (#56.3 'the notes should carry more script to explain to me') versus CANNOT FIND WHAT TO SAY (10-08). Both are true of different slides: checkpoints, bridges and closes had no words and need a script; card slides need cues. Keep full sentences only on checkpoint, bridge and close slides.
- HONORIFICS ALWAYS (#4) versus THE MARK RENDERING AS A DOTTED CIRCLE on English faces (Slide14/17/19.PNG) versus HIS OWN TYPING 'R.A'. His call, but put it to him with two rendered examples; the evidence favours one Latin form on English faces (he types it, any font draws it) and the Arabic ligature only inside Arabic text. Whatever he picks, the gate enforces one form, and separately WHO gets it (W4).
- THE 20-WORD FACE CAP (#21) versus 'MORE DETAIL ON THE SLIDE? ONE OR TWO SMALL LINES?' (10-08) and versus renderings of 25-31 words already on faces. Resolve: the cap governs authored body text (context lines, keys, captions); a statement slide may carry two context lines within those 20 words at 24pt; the quotation pair has its own cap (about 24 English words, by an authored excerpt).
- NAMES, NOT SENTENCES (#68) versus HIS 10-08 REMARK ON SLIDE 11 'i did tell you to put text like this. Sure. but a map should go first'. No real clash: the headline is a name; the face may carry short lines; and a front opens on a map before any words slide.
- OVER-BUILD AND 'WE MUST NOT MISS ANY DETAIL OR GLOSS OVER' (#20, #70) versus '6-8 ARE UNNECESSARY' and 'TOO MUCH BACKSTORY' (#62). Resolve: 'detail' means the detail of the story being told (Bahrayn whole, the dua, the uncle); side actions that move nothing stay in the pool. Over-build goes behind the story, never in front of it.
- CERTAINTY LABELS NEVER ON A FACE (#30; FORBIDDEN, series/deck2.py:109) versus '[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]… must be labelled as such on the slide itself' (CLAUDE.md 0.1, #16, RUNSHEET.md:255). As written the build fails on what the other rule demands. Resolve: plain words on the face ('an estimate — our books do not fix this'), never the bracket; the audit requires the plain phrase when the card carries the tag.
- NO BUILD NOTES IN THE NOTES PANE (#59, #60) versus THE IMAGE BRIEF BELONGS IN THE NOTES (#21, #30, deck2.py:345-348) and his 10-08 question. Resolve: the brief is the one permitted production block — last in the pane under its own ruled marker, also in IMAGE_BRIEFS.md, removed once the picture is placed.
- NO EMBLEMS, ICONS OR ASSIGNED OBJECTS FOR PEOPLE (#4) versus his own requests 'al jarud moving out with greenish icon', 'the enemy army should also have an icon', 'camel images', 'background image of horse'. Resolve: a map banner marks a FORCE at a place (cartography; uniform per side, the commander's name beside it) and a picture shows an object, animal or landscape of the scene — both allowed; what stays banned is a personal emblem that stands for a man, and any picture of a person.
- THE BOOKEND IS RE-USED VERBATIM (#23) versus things since struck or newly ruled: the bookend Line on slide 2 still shows 'Ikrima's vow' (#70.4), has six strands (L1) and agentless keys (M4). Resolve: tonight's bookend stays as the room saw it; the new rules bind every closing pair built from now, so the problem does not carry forward.
- ABOUT ONE SLIDE A MINUTE, 40-55 SLIDES (#39) versus new slides he is asking for (a situation map before words, a three-questions slide, a dua slide, close-ups, a closing map per front). Resolve: pace is counted in cards (1.36 minutes each), not slides; map and faith slides ARE the telling and replace narration; the room for them comes from P2 (slides 6-8 leave).
- A WORDS SLIDE AFTER EVERY MAP (#63) versus CHRONOLOGICAL ORDER and NO SPOILERS (#46; slide 21 'didn't this happen before slide 20?'). The words slide of one card quotes the outcome before the next cards tell how. Resolve: a words slide may not quote past the point where the next card begins; splinter cards of one event (RC73, RC74) become clicks and beats of the one map card.
- WARNING FIRST (#60's order) versus SAY FIRST (N1). The card format lets a researcher write any number of warnings and the build hoists them all. Resolve: one warning line of about 20 words above SAY, only for 'do not say X'; every other caution is folded into the wording of the beat it concerns or goes below the divider.
- 'NO NOTICE NEEDED' IS A VALID ANSWER (#43) versus 'where or what is al Khatt??'. An answered row can be wrong. Resolve: an answer that points at something ('the map names it', 'beat 1 is his notice', 'the tree') must name an object the gate can look up; 'no notice needed' remains valid only for a name that is on no face.
- BRIDGE SLIDES OF THREE TEXT CARDS (#45) versus A MAP SHOULD GO FIRST (10-08) and NO BARE-TEXT SLIDES (#10). Resolve: at the opening of a front the bridge IS the situation map, its three steps as clicks (evening 5's MAP_BRIDGE_BEFORE); a text bridge only where nothing moves in space.
- A KARAMA IS A HAZARD TO HEDGE (RUNSHEET.md:104; the caveat as an instruction-beat) versus IT IS THE POINT ('good stuff for galvanizing the imaan of audience'). Resolve: both — it gets the face, the Arabic and the time, told as the named book tells it, with one quiet sentence of attribution and no doctrine built on it (CLAUDE.md 1.6).
- CLAUDE WRITES NO ARABIC OR URDU (#19, CLAUDE.md 1.4) versus 'maybe use arabic al yamin wash shimal' for the title. No clash if the title is the source's own words, verbatim and cited (al-Bidaya 7:61, cached 30097/3207), in Naskh with a plain English place-and-year line beneath; composing a title in Arabic or Urdu remains forbidden.
- THREE HANDS-UP AND A HANDWRITING RITUAL (#24, #28; still described in CLAUDE.md section 2) versus THE WORKSHEET DROPPED (#60, 'assumed… and not objected to') and twelve hands-up prompts in evening 6's notes. Resolve: say plainly in CLAUDE.md that the worksheet is gone; default every card's hands-up to off and name the three in the runsheet.
- EACH HOUSE GETS ITS OWN LANE ON THE LINE (#57) versus ONE OR TWO STRANDS (10-08). The newer statement wins: houses and looks-ahead are marks on one strand, or stay off the Line and on their tree.
