# Lecture 1 — Delivery Advice

## Before you walk in
- **Drop a real map onto Slide 15.** Best free option: Wikimedia "Sasanian Empire 620 CE" or a clean Byzantine-vs-Sasanian map; or screenshot a frame from a Kings & Generals "Muslim Conquests" video. Keep it simple — two empires + the Hijaz.
- **Make the wall banner** (optional but high-impact): a long printed strip of the 6–8 timeline pegs you pin up and physically extend each week. People remember what they watch grow.
- **Rehearse the 3 stories out loud** until you can tell them without the script. If you own the 3 stories, you own the lecture — everything else is connective tissue.
- Open the `.pptx` once in PowerPoint and read the **speaker-notes pane** (View → Notes) — every slide already has its cue + reference there.

## Managing the stage-fright / running-dry fear
- **You will not run dry** — this deck has ~2× more material than 40 min needs. Your risk is *over*-running, not under. So watch the clock at Slide 12 (should be ≈25 min).
- Each of the 3 stories is a self-contained 3–5 min unit. If your mind goes blank, jump to the nearest story and tell it; it always loops back to the thread.
- Put the **Cue Card** on the lectern. One glance restores the whole structure.
- The audience-interaction prompts (`[PROMPT]`) are deliberate breathers — when you ask a question and let them answer, you get 20–30 seconds to reset.

## Pacing map (40 min)
`Cold open 5 · What-is-history 2.5 · Two-lenses 9 · Timeline 6 · Calendar 3 · Map 5 · Why+rules 4 · Close 2` → ~36.5 + slack.
If short on time, **cut the map detail first** (Slide 15 talking points), never the guardrail (Slide 17).

## Handling controversy (management's brief)
- Say the guardrail (Slide 17) **before** anything sensitive — you're setting the contract with the room early, so Lessons 5 & 7 feel principled, not evasive.
- If a heated mushājarāt/Karbala question comes up now, defer warmly: *"بہت اچھا سوال — اسے ہم درس ۵ میں اصول کے ساتھ دیکھیں گے ان شاء اللہ۔"* Never improvise a position on the Sahaba's disputes from the floor.
- Keep to **Sunni positions, present them as such, make no novel claims.** When in doubt, narrate what the book narrates and stop.

## Tech checklist
- Arabic renders best in PowerPoint on Windows; if a term looks broken, set that run's font to **Traditional Arabic** or **Arial** (the deck already requests Traditional Arabic for Arabic runs).
- Carry the deck as **.pptx AND a PDF export** (File → Export → PDF) as a backup in case the venue laptop mangles fonts.
- Test the projector aspect ratio — the deck is **16:9**.

## After the lecture
- Leave the banner up. Note which story landed best — lean into that style for Lesson 2.
- Lesson 2 ("The Rāshida & Abu Bakr ؓ") reuses this same timeline and map, so you're already half-prepared.
