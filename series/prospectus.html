تاریخ سے سبق

Lessons from History

Weekly · 45 minutes Begins 2 September 2026 DHA Islamabad Tanzeem-e-Islami Delivered in Urdu Adult audience
A ten-session course that gives non-specialists a usable mental map of Islamic history — where events happened, in what order, how we know them, and what lessons the scholars have drawn from them. Not a survey to be sat through: participants leave each evening having written on their own timeline and drawn on their own map.

How this course is kept safe

The commitments that govern every slide, every handout and every answer from the floor.

One named Sunni source

The course is built on Tareekh-e-Ummat by Maulana Muhammad Ismail Rehan, rather than self-authored. Where a period falls outside it, a second named work is chosen openly and stated to the audience.

Every claim traceable

Each source volume is indexed to its printed page numbers, so any date, name or quotation on screen can be traced to a page a listener could open themselves. Nothing uncertain is stated as fact.

No lesson is the speaker’s opinion

Every conclusion is presented as a quotation with its author and page shown on the slide — the Qur’ān, a hadith, or a named scholar. The speaker narrates; he does not adjudicate.

The sensitive material is fenced first

Before any disputed episode, session 3 installs the fuqahā’s own ruling from the source: studying the مشاجرات without need is مکروہ, and the Companions ؓ are met with حُسنِ ظن and ادب.

No live questions, no positions from the floor

No questions are taken during the 45 minutes. Written slips go into a box at the door and are answered the following week from prepared, sourced replies; the speaker stays fifteen minutes afterwards for one-to-one. Nothing is ever improvised on the Companions’ ؓ disputes.

Nothing is recorded

No filming and no clips, at any session. The material of sessions 4, 7 and 9 is exactly the kind that travels badly as a two-minute excerpt without the framing that preceded it.

Reviewed before delivery

Tanzeem-e-Islami reviews the sensitive material ahead of the evening it is delivered — specifically session 3 (the مشاجرات framing) and session 4 (کربلا).

Calendar checked

None of the ten dates falls in محرم. Session 4, which carries کربلا, is not being delivered into محرم.

Prepared in writing, in advance

Each session carries a written brief, a full Urdu script with timings, a one-page lectern card, and printed handouts — completed before the evening, not assembled on the day.

The whole span, and how the ten evenings sit on it

Every session drawn to scale against ۶۲۲–۱۹۴۷ عیسوی.

Sessions overlap in time because they follow different regions: Baghdad and Córdoba run alongside the Sultanate of Delhi, and the Ottomans alongside the Mughals. The course keeps them in narrative order, not in strict chronological order.

The ten sessions

Each evening: one story, three people, one movement on the map, one sourced lesson.

01۱۱ھ – ۱۹۴۷

ایک نظر میں — پورا نقشہ

The whole span in a single evening.

Fourteen centuries told fast and in order — from a valley in Arabia to the gates of France, to a caliph killed by a Mongol in ۶۵۶ھ, to the last empire ending in 1924, to this city. The timeline and the map are built live and handed over completed, so every later evening has somewhere to attach.

02۱۱ – ۲۳ھ

بارہ سال

Twelve years: from a threatened city to two broken empires.

The Prophet ﷺ has died and Arabia is in revolt. Twelve years later Persia has fallen and Byzantium has lost Syria and Egypt. How the succession held, how the Qur’ān was gathered, and how an administration was built while the fighting was still on.

The first test — approached with the scholars’ own rules in place.

Baghdad and Córdoba — the same centuries, two capitals, two endings.

Paper arrives from Samarqand and a city starts translating the world; at the other end of the Mediterranean a second capital builds a library. What each achieved, what each cost, and what happened when a scholar had to say no to a ruler.

06۴۹۲ – ۶۵۸ھ

قریبِ مرگ

Two centuries in which it nearly ended.

Jerusalem lost and retaken; then a catastrophe from the east that took Baghdad itself in ۶۵۶ھ. Why the civilisation survived losing its capital — where the books went, where the scholars went, and where the counter-attack finally came from.

A frontier principality becomes the longest-lived Muslim state in history, takes Constantinople in ۸۵۷ھ, reaches Vienna — and then spends two centuries retreating. The evening asks what length actually proves.

What each participant receives

Printed, A4, and theirs to keep.

The workbook

A stapled booklet given at session 1. It opens with a timeline they mark in their own handwriting every week, and a base map they draw on. Then two pages per session: a map worksheet, and three profile pages where the incident is left blank for them to write.

A catch-up sheet

A single page reissued weekly for anyone joining late — the timeline, the map, where the course has reached, and one line on each session so far.

The تعارف نامہ

At session 10, a bound directory of all thirty people met during the course, each with their dates, what they did, and one incident — a reference they keep afterwards.
