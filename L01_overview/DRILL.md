# Session 1 — DRILL

**Fifteen questions. Say the answer out loud before you look.** Confidence against stage fright
comes from being tested, not from re-reading. Do the whole set twice: once seated at the desk,
once standing, at speaking volume, with the deck running behind you.

**Scoring that matters:** a question is *passed* only if you said the **page** as well as the fact.
If you knew the story but not the page, that is a fail — the page is the whole authority of this
course.

Sources are **البدایہ والنہایہ** — ط دار ابن كثير — unless the answer says otherwise. Full working:
`docs/research/saqifah-bani-saida.md` · `docs/research/wafat-and-medina-11h.md` ·
`docs/research/muhajirun-quraysh-structure.md` · `docs/catalogue/QA_BANK.md`.

**Honorifics — said aloud every time, on this sheet as on the podium.** Every Companion named here
carries **رضي الله عنه**, the Prophet carries **ﷺ**, and the later scholars carry **رحمہ اللہ**.
Where a name is written in Latin letters the رضي الله عنه sign is left off the spelling only
because a combining mark cannot sit on a Latin letter. **It is never left off the speech.**

*Typesetting note for whoever prints this: every page citation is set off by a spaced em-dash and
never wrapped in Latin brackets, so that the Urdu-Indic digits are not reordered against Latin
punctuation. Keep that convention if you edit the sheet.*

## A · Sequence — what happened, in what order

### 1. Put these four in order and give the day of the week for each: the death · the Saqīfa · the general bayʿah · the burial.

Monday, when the forenoon grew hot — the death. There is no disagreement at all about the **day**:

<div dir="rtl">«لا خلافَ أنّه ﵊، توفّيَ يومَ الإثنين» — ج۵ ص۳۵۸<br>«وتُوفِّي رسولُ اللَّه ﷺ. حين اشتدَّ الضُّحَى من ذلك اليوم. وقيل: عندَ زوالِ الشمسِ. واللَّه أعلم» — ج۵ ص۳۴۳</div>

The rest of **Monday** — the Saqīfa and the private bayʿah. **Tuesday morning** — the general bayʿah
in the mosque, from the minbar. The rest of **Tuesday** — the washing, the shrouding, the prayer.
**The night of Wednesday** — the burial.

Ibn Kathir gives the whole sequence in one paragraph — **ج۷ ص۵** — and states plainly that all of it
was *before* the burial:

<div dir="rtl">«كان هذا في بقيّةِ يوم الإثنين فلما كان الغدُ صَبيحةَ يومِ الثلاثاء اجْتَمَعَ الناسُ في المسجد فَتُمِّمَت البيعةُ من المهاجرين والأنْصار قاطبةً، وكان ذلك قبل تَجْهيز رسول اللَّه ﷺ» — ج۵ ص۳۴۸</div>

**Two cautions.** For the day of the *month*, Ibn Kathir records the disagreement and then names the
12th of Rabīʿ al-Awwal only as **المشهور** — the well-known view — not as established, at
**ج۵ ص۳۶۰**; the 2nd is also reported. And Ibn Saʿd reports the burial on **Tuesday** —
**ج۵ ص۳۵۹**. So never put a single burial day on a slide.

### 2. Before anyone went to the Saqīfa, what had already happened in the mosque?

This is the one most people get wrong, and it is the hinge of the whole evening. All of it on
**ج۵ ص۳۴۳** — Abū Bakr was not in Medina. He had judged the Prophet ﷺ recovering after fajr and
ridden home to **al-Sunḥ**, a mile east of the city. **Sālim b. ʿUbayd** went out to fetch him. He
came, uncovered the blessed face, kissed it, confirmed the death, then went out and addressed the
people **beside the minbar** — «وأزاح الجدالَ وأزال الإشكال» — and **a group of the Companions gave
him bayʿah in the mosque there and then**.

**Only after that** did the doubt arise among some of the Anṣār:

<div dir="rtl">«وبايعَه في المسجد جماعةٌ من الصَّحابة، ووقعت شُبْهَةٌ لبعضِ الأنصارِ، وقام في أذْهان بعضِهم جوازُ اسْتِخْلافِ خليفةٍ من الأنصارِ» — ج۵ ص۳۴۳</div>

So the Saqīfa is not the beginning of the story. It is the second move.

### 3. What did Abū Bakr say from beside the minbar, and which verse did he recite?

The Bukhārī chain runs الزهري ← أبو سلمة ← ابن عباس ؓ

<div dir="rtl">«أما بعد، فمَنْ كانَ منكم يَعْبُدُ محمدًا فإِنَّ محمدًا قد مات، ومن كان يَعْبُدُ اللَّهَ فإنَّ اللَّه حيٌّ لا يموتُ» — ج۵ ص۳۴۱ · البخاري ٤٤٥٢–٤٤٥٤</div>

The verse is **آل عمران ۱۴۴**. The edition prints that reference three times on three consecutive
pages — ج۵ ص۳۴۰، ص۳۴۱، ص۳۴۲ — so the sūrah and the āyah number are established from the source,
not assumed. **But the edition stops mid-āyah** and writes «الآية». If the whole verse is to go on a
slide, take it from a muṣḥaf, not from that page.

Say the two words **«أما بعد، فـ»** and **«منكم»**. Both are in the printed edition, and the version
circulating without them is not what Ibn Kathir prints.

And ʿUmar's own account of the moment it landed on him — same page, and the line to have by heart:

<div dir="rtl">«فعَقِرْتُ حتى ما تُقِلُّني رِجْلاي، وحتى هَوَيْتُ إلى الأرض، وَعَرَفْتُ حينَ سَمِعْتُهُ تلاها أنّ رسولَ اللَّه ﷺ قد مات» — ج۵ ص۳۴۱</div>

### 4. Give the order of the speeches in the courtyard, from the first word to the hand being struck.

Six moves, all of them on **ج۵ ص۳۴۵–۳۴۷**:

1. The Anṣār's **khaṭīb — not named in either narration** — opens. — ج۵ ص۳۴۵

   <div dir="rtl">«أما بَعْدُ فنحنُ أنصارُ اللَّه وكتيبةُ الإسلام» — ج۵ ص۳۴۵</div>

2. ʿUmar had prepared a speech and **never gave it**. Abū Bakr pre-empted him and said every good
   thing that was in it. — ج۵ ص۳۴۵–۳۴۶

   <div dir="rtl">«واللَّهِ ما تركَ من كَلمةٍ أعْجَبَتني في تَزْويري إِلَّا قالها في بديهته وأفضل» — ج۵ ص۳۴۶</div>

3. **Abū Bakr's speech**, and the offer of two other men in his place. — ج۵ ص۳۴۶
4. **al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir** counters — «منا أمير، ومنكم أمير» — ج۵ ص۳۴۶
5. The voices rose, and ʿUmar called for the hand. — ج۵ ص۳۴۶

   <div dir="rtl">«فكَثُرَ اللَّغَطُ وارتَفَعَت الأصْواتُ حتى خشينا الاختلاف. فقلت: ابسُطْ يَدَكَ يا أبا بكرٍ» — ج۵ ص۳۴۶</div>

6. **An Anṣārī struck it before ʿUmar could.** — ج۵ ص۳۴۷

Separately — and this is the argument that actually settles it in Ibn Kathir's telling — ʿUmar's
appeal to **the prayer**, from the report of Ibn Masʿūd

<div dir="rtl">«يا معشرَ الأنْصار ألستم تعلمون أن رسولَ اللَّه ﷺ قد أمر أبا بكر أن يَؤُمّ النَّاسَ؟ فأيُّكم تطيبُ نفسُه أن يتقدم أبا بكر؟ فقالت الأنصار: نعوذُ باللَّه أن نتقدم أبا بكر» — ج۵ ص۳۴۷</div>

## B · Who was who

### 5. Which clan was Bashīr b. Saʿd from, and what does the book actually say he did?

**al-Khazraj**, of the Anṣār. He is the father of **al-Nuʿmān b. Bashīr**. He is named as the man
who struck Abū Bakr's hand **before** ʿUmar — the first bayʿah of the day. He is given **no speech**
in these pages. His whole part is an action, and it is reported in ʿUmar's own words:

<div dir="rtl">«ثم أخذتُ بيده، وبَدَرَني رجلٌ من الأنصار، فضربَ على يده قبلَ أن أضربَ على يده، ثم ضَرَبْتُ على يده وَتَتَابع الناس» — ج۵ ص۳۴۷<br>«وسمَّى هذا الرجل الذي بايع الصدّيقَ قبلَ عمَرَ بن الخطاب، فقال: هو بَشير بن سَعْدٍ، والد النعمان بن بَشير» — ج۵ ص۳۴۷</div>

**His sub-clan within al-Khazraj is not established from any page in hand — do not name one.**

### 6. Abū Bakr and ʿUmar: which clan each, and which of the two is further from the Prophet ﷺ by descent?

**Abū Bakr — Banū Taym.** **ʿUmar — Banū ʿAdī.** Both of Quraysh.

<div dir="rtl">أبو بكر ؓ · بنو تيم — البدایہ ج۳ ص۸۳ · سیر أعلام النبلاء، سیر الخلفاء الراشدین ص۷<br>عمر ؓ · بنو عدي — البدایہ ج۲ ص۴۸۵ · ج۲ ص۴۹۲ · ج۳ ص۸۳ · سیر الخلفاء الراشدین ص۷۱</div>

**ʿUmar is the further of the two.** Banū ʿAdī branches off a full generation above Murra: Abū Bakr's
line meets the Prophet ﷺ at **Murra b. Kaʿb**, ʿUmar's at **Kaʿb b. Luʾayy**.

⚠️ Those two meeting-points are **arithmetic on chains the sources print, not statements the sources
make.** Only one line in the whole set is stated outright — and it happens to make the point better
than any of the others. **Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ, of Banū al-Ḥārith b. Fihr**, meets the Prophet ﷺ
at **Fihr**, further out than all of them:

<div dir="rtl">«يَجْتَمِعُ فِي النَّسَبِ هُوَ وَالنَّبِيُّ -صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ- فِي فِهْرٍ» — سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۱ ص۶</div>

And it was Abū ʿUbayda together with ʿUmar whom Abū Bakr put forward at the Saqīfa —
**ج۵ ص۳۴۶**. Nearness of blood was not the currency.

### 7. Saʿd b. ʿUbāda: his clan, his condition that day, and the one thing البدایہ records him saying.

**Banū Sāʿida, of al-Khazraj** — the courtyard was his clan's, and al-Dhahabī رحمہ اللہ calls him
**the chief of al-Khazraj**. He was **ill and wrapped up**, and Ibn Kathir gives him **no speech at
the Saqīfa at all**:

<div dir="rtl">«وإذا بينَ ظَهرانيهم رجلٌ مُزَمَّلٌ، فقلت: منْ هذا؟ قالوا: سعدُ بن عُبادة. فقلت: ما له؟ قالوا: وجعٌ» — ج۵ ص۳۴۵</div>

He speaks only **afterwards**, to accept — and Ibn Kathir gives that its own section heading,
«ذكر اعتراف سعد بن عبادة بصحة ما قاله الصديق يوم السقيفة» — **ج۵ ص۳۴۷**:

<div dir="rtl">«فقال له سعد: صدقتَ، نحن الوزراءُ وأنتم الأمراءُ» — ج۵ ص۳۴۸</div>

That sentence is **the whole of what البدایہ reports him as saying.** Ibn Kathir does not say on
these pages that he gave bayʿah, and does not say that he refused. Do not fill the gap.

### 8. Who was *not* in that courtyard?

**ʿAlī and al-Zubayr** — they and those with them stayed behind in the house of فاطمہ ؓ.
Both gave bayʿah when Abū Bakr summoned them from the minbar, each answering «لا تَثْريبَ يا خليفةَ
رسولِ اللَّه ﷺ» — *no reproach*.

<div dir="rtl">«تَخَلَّفوا في بَيْت فاطمة بنتِ رسول اللَّه ﷺ» — ج۵ ص۳۴۵ · بیعت: ج۵ ص۳۵۰</div>

And the correction that is easy to get wrong: **Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr and Thābit b. Qays are not
mentioned at the Saqīfa at all** — not in ج۵ ص۳۴۳–۳۵۷، not in ج۷ ص۵–۸. They stand on the clan chart
as chiefs of their people. **Do not put them in that courtyard.**

## C · Citations

### 9. Which page carries Abū Bakr's inaugural khuṭba, how does Ibn Kathir grade it, and what is the trap?

**ج۵ ص۳۴۹** — the chain runs محمد بن إسحاق ← الزهري ← أنس بن مالك ؓ and Ibn Kathir's grading in his
own words is **«وهذا إسنادٌ صحيحٌ»**. Delivered on the **Tuesday**, after ʿUmar had called the people to
the general bayʿah.

<div dir="rtl">«أيُّها الناسُ، فإِنّي قد وُلِّيتُ عَلَيْكم ولستُ بخَيْرِكُم … أطيعوني ما أطَعْتُ اللَّهَ ورسولَه، فإذا عَصَيْتُ اللَّهَ ورسوله فلا طاعة لي عليكم» — ج۵ ص۳۴۹</div>

**The trap is that the same khuṭba appears again at ج۷ ص۶ with different wording** — «حتى أُرْجعَ
عليه حقَّه» for «أُريحَ», and «إلا خذلهم اللهُ بالذلّ» for «ضَرَبَهُم». Both are Ibn Kathir, both the
same edition. **Pick one page, cite that page, and never silently blend the two.**

If you use «ولستُ بخَيْرِكُم», carry Ibn Kathir's own gloss **in the script, not on the slide**:

<div dir="rtl">«من باب الهَضْم والتَّواضعِ، فإنَّهم مُجْمِعون على أنه أفْضلُهم» — ج۵ ص۳۴۹</div>

And the single best line in the strand for the two-stage structure of the bayʿah — ʿUmar speaking,
same page:

<div dir="rtl">«وكانت طائفة قد بايَعوه قبل ذلك في سَقيفة بني ساعدة، وكانت بيعة العامة على المِنْبَرِ» — ج۵ ص۳۴۹ · البخاري ٧٢١٩</div>

### 10. Which page carries «منا أمير ومنكم أمير», and on whose authority is the speaker named?

**ج۵ ص۳۴۶** — both the line and the naming. The line is quoted anonymously in the Mālik/Zuhrī
narration as "a man of the Anṣār"; the identification comes at the end of the same page, from
**سعيد بن المسيّب**, reported by **ابن شهاب**:

<div dir="rtl">«قال ابنُ شهاب: وأخبرني سعيدُ بن المُسَيّب أنَّ الذي قال: أنا جُذَيْلُها المُحَكَّكُ وعُذَيْقُها المُرَجَّب. هو الحباب بن المنذر» — ج۵ ص۳۴۶</div>

The Anṣār say the same thing **collectively** in the Ibn Masʿūd report at **ج۵ ص۳۴۷**. And the
longer al-Ḥubāb speech familiar from سيرة ابن ہشام / الطبری is **not carried in these pages** — do
not import it.

## D · Hostile — rehearse these **aloud**, quietly, as if to one person after the lecture

Remember the delivery conditions: no live questions, slips in the box, and these are answered
**one-to-one, off the record, in the 15 minutes afterwards.** Conversational, never defensive. The
shape of every answer is the same — *the page is open · here is what it says · here is what the
author himself says about it.*

### 11. «عمر ؓ خود کہتے ہیں کہ ابوبکر ؓ کی بیعت ایک "فلتہ" تھی»

**Yes, and he confirms it in the very next breath — ج۵ ص۳۴۵.** The full sentence is the answer, so
never quote the word without the two clauses that follow it:

<div dir="rtl">«فلا يغترَّنَّ امرؤٌ أن يقولَ: إنَّ بيعةَ أبي بكر كانَتْ فَلْتَة ألا وإنها كانَتْ كذلك، ألا إن اللَّه وَقَى شَرَّها، وليسَ فيكم اليومَ منْ تُقْطَعُ إليه الأعناقُ مثلَ أبي بكرٍ» — ج۵ ص۳۴۵</div>

**فلتہ means *sudden, without prior arrangement* — not *illegitimate*.** And the stated purpose of the
whole sermon is on the facing page. ʿUmar is legislating against improvising, not against Abū Bakr:

<div dir="rtl">«فمن بايعَ أميرًا عن غير مَشورة المُسلمين فلا بيعةَ له، ولا بيعةَ للذي بايَعه تَغِرَّةَ أن يُقْتَلا» — ج۵ ص۳۴۶</div>

### 12. «سعد بن عبادہ ؓ کے ساتھ کیا ہوا؟ عمر ؓ نے خود کہا "نزونا على سعد"»

**It is in the book — ج۵ ص۳۴۶ — and it is in البخاري ٦٨٣٠ and مسلم ١٦٩١.** It cannot be denied and
it must not be softened. Three things, in this order.

These are **ʿUmar's own words, reported by ʿUmar against himself, in public**, in a sermon whose
entire point was that the day had been dangerous and must never be repeated. Ibn Kathir's own answer
comes **one page later**, first as a section heading at **ج۵ ص۳۴۷** and then as Saʿd's own
«صدقتَ، نحن الوزراءُ وأنتم الأمراءُ» at **ج۵ ص۳۴۸**. And on whether Saʿd ever gave bayʿah —
**state the acceptance, say the rest is disputed on weak chains, and do not adjudicate.** البدایہ
reports only the agreement; سیر carries a refusal report which al-Dhahabī رحمہ اللہ brands in three
words:

<div dir="rtl">«إِسْنَادُهَا كَمَا تَرَى» — سیر أعلام النبلاء ج۱ ص۲۷۷</div>

⚠️ **Do not supply a gloss for «نزونا عليه» and attribute it to Ibn Kathir — he does not gloss it on
these pages.**

### 13. «آپ نے سعد بن عبادہ ؓ کی تقریر کیوں نہیں سنائی؟»

The famous long speech is from **سیرت ابن ہشام / طبری — outside the course's safe list.** In البدایہ
he is present but ill and wrapped up at **ج۵ ص۳۴۵**, and is given no speech that day. Say it
straight:

<div dir="rtl">جو تقریر مشہور ہے وہ اِس کتاب میں نہیں ہے۔ میں وہی سناتا ہوں جو اِس کتاب میں ہے۔</div>

**This is a strength, not an embarrassment.** It is the method of the whole course, made visible.

### 14. «آپ نے انبیاء ؑ کی تاریخیں کہاں سے لیں؟»

Answer it the honest way, because the slide already says it. Those dates are labelled
**`[CONVENTIONAL-ESTIMATE]`** on the slide itself — approximations from general scholarship, drawn
**hollow** and marked *approx.*, shown only to convey distance. Our sources do not fix them, and
**آدم ؑ and نوح ؑ carry no number at all.**

<div dir="rtl">ترتیب وحی سے معلوم ہے۔ تاریخیں معلوم نہیں — اور جو معلوم نہ ہو، اُسے ہم معلوم نہیں کہتے۔</div>

This is the strongest single moment in the evening for your credibility. Use it.

### 15. Someone has read البدایہ and reads out the polemical passage at ج۵ ص۳۵۵. What do you do?

**You know that page exists so that you are not ambushed by it — that is the only reason.** It is Ibn
Kathir رحمہ اللہ refuting a theological claim, not narrating an event. **Do not quote it, do not
paraphrase it, do not defend it.** The answer is short, and then you move:

<div dir="rtl">یہ ابن کثیر رحمہ اللہ ایک عقیدے کے دعوے کا رد کر رہے ہیں — کوئی واقعہ بیان نہیں کر رہے۔<br>ہم واقعات بیان کر رہے ہیں، اور واقعات سامنے والے صفحے پر ہیں۔</div>

Then move on. Do not elaborate.

The same rule governs two other passages you must know of and must never use — the clause about
فاطمہ ؓ in the middle of **ج۵ ص۳۵۱**, and the second inaugural khuṭba at **ج۷ ص۸**, which is
transmitted by سيف بن عمر and which Ibn Kathir's own footnote refers to الطبری. Under the standing
rule, الطبری is corroboration only, never alone.
