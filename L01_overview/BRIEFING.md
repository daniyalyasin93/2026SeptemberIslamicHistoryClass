## 2. The method block — what تاریخ is, and why the guardrail is worded as it is

### 2.1 The definition — three of them, all from the مقدمہ

The book gives the لغوی sense first: تاریخ in Arabic is **الاعلام بالوقت** — the announcing of
time (مقدمہ ص۳۲).

Then three اصطلاحی definitions, in this order on the page:

| Whose | The definition, as the مقدمہ prints it | Page |
|---|---|---|
| ابن خلدون رحمہ اللہ | «إِخْبَارٌ عَنِ الْأَيَّامِ وَالدُّوَلِ وَالسَّوَابِقِ مِنَ الْقُرُونِ الْأُوَل» | مقدمہ ص۳۲ |
| The general definition of علم التاریخ | «هُوَ عِلْمٌ يُبْحَثُ فِيهِ عَنِ الزَّمَانِ وَأَحْوَالِهِ وَعَنْ أَحْوَالِ مَا يَتَعَلَّقُ بِهِ مِنْ حَيْثُ تَعْيِينِ ذَلِكَ وَتَوْقِيتِهِ» | مقدمہ ص۳۲ |
| السخاوي رحمہ اللہ | «الْبَحْثُ عَنْ وَقَائِعِ الزَّمَانِ بِالتَّوْقِيتِ» | مقدمہ ص۳۳ |
| السخاوي رحمہ اللہ — the subject-matter | «مَوْضُوعُهُ الْإِنْسَانُ وَالزَّمَانُ» | مقدمہ ص۳۳ |

⚠ **All four are eyeball-before-use.** The OCR mangles the vowelling badly (it renders the third
as «الْبَحْث عَن وَقَائِعِ الزَّمَانِ بِالتوقيت»). The *sense* is secure; the *pointing* is not.
The مقدمہ footnotes them to الإعلان بالتوبيخ and to المختصر في علم التاريخ للكافيجي, but **the
footnote page numbers are OCR-garbled and must not be reproduced.**

**The one to put on the slide is السخاوي's**, because it is four words long and it contains the
whole argument: *the investigation of the events of time — by their timing.* `CLAUDE.md` §4
records that الإعلان بالتوبيخ is Shamela book **34** and that its **فوائد التاريخ** chapter begins
at printed p.۱۱۱, if the Arabic is ever wanted from the source rather than through the مقدمہ.

**Working English for the room:** تاریخ is not "what happened" — every discipline asks that.
Its distinguishing question is **"when, and in what order."**

### 2.2 The حدیث / تاریخ distinction — this is on the page, and it is the load-bearing part

The مقدمہ has a titled section, **«تاریخ اور دوسرے فنون میں فرق»** (مقدمہ ص۳۳), and it makes
exactly the distinction the guardrail needs. In the book's own reasoning:

- Events appear in **ادب** too, and in **حدیث** in large numbers — but those are not called books
  of تاریخ, because in حدیث and ادب the events **are not arranged by time**, and often the month
  or even the year is not stated at all.
- In **ادب**, the purpose is that one incident comes before the reader and he takes a lesson from it.
- In **حدیث**, events are gathered **according to فقہی احکام و نکات, the standing of the سند, or
  the names of the رواۃ** — each compiler with his own aims.
- **تاریخ is a separate discipline**: its whole attention is on arranging events so that what
  happened first comes first. Most تواریخ proceed محرم-by-month, year by هجری year.

> **This is the sourced spine of the guardrail.** The book itself says that حدیث collects an event
> for its *ruling*, its *chain*, or its *narrator*; تاریخ collects the same event for its *place
> in the sequence*. Different disciplines, different questions, different burdens.

### 2.3 The fiqh of studying تاریخ — the scale, in the book's own order

Titled section **«تاریخ کا حکم فقہاء کے نزدیک»**, مقدمہ ص۵۲–۵۳. The book states that the فقہاء
graded this science across the whole scale: **فرض عین → فرض کفایہ → واجب → مندوب → مباح → مکروہ →
حرام**, and then works down it:

| Grade | What it covers, per مقدمہ ص۵۲–۵۳ |
|---|---|
| **فرض عین** | Enough of the سیرت of the Prophet ﷺ that a Muslim knows who he ﷺ is |
| **واجب** | Events on which عقیدہ, فقہی مسائل and the مصالح of the Muslims depend |
| **فرض کفایہ** | On one class of the ummah: that quantity of history — because rulings rest on hadith, and hadith rests on knowing whether a narrator met his shaykh, on اتصالِ سند, on ناسخ و منسوخ, and on نسب و وراثت, none of which can be settled without تاریخ |
| **مندوب** | The lives of the صحابہ ؓ, the أولیاء and the righteous, in so far as they move a man toward good |
| **مباح** | Kings, ministers, poets, littérateurs and the rest — no harm to دین, some worldly benefit |
| **مکروہ** | Idle events with no benefit in this life or the next |
| **حرام** | Tales of عاشقی معشوقی, degraded stories, and the doings of the فاسق و فاجر where there is fear of falling into corruption of belief or of being drawn to evil |

**And then the clause the whole guardrail hangs on, same section, مقدمہ ص۵۲–۵۳:**

> Studying **مشاجراتِ صحابہ ؓ without a specific need is مکروہ** — because there is no benefit in
> it worldly or otherworldly, and there is the possibility of a loss of ادب و احترام toward those
> figures. **If there is a danger of خلل in عقیدہ, it is حرام.**
>
> **However** — studying the subject, and debating it, **in order to know the answer to the
> propaganda of أہلِ باطل against the صحابہ ؓ and to inform others of the reality, is جائز, and at
> the time of need becomes واجب.**

The same section carries a footnote (مقدمہ ص۵۳) reporting ابن حجر الہیتمی رحمہ اللہ on why he
himself narrated مشاجرات reports: that the ائمہ have specified in اصول that these should not be
mentioned; that his own purpose was to state the sound events and draw the sound conclusions from
them; and that such mention, done for اظہارِ حق, is among the important obligations, because it is
what makes the purity of the صحابہ ؓ apparent.

### 2.4 تاریخِ اسلام = تاریخِ مسلمین — the second half of the guardrail

Its own titled section, **«تاریخ اسلام یا تاریخ مسلمین»**, **مقدمہ ص۶۱**. The book's argument, in
its own order:

1. تاریخِ اسلام is in reality **تاریخِ مسلمین** — it is not the history of a religion, as the
   phrase "تاریخِ اسلام" makes it sound.
2. A *history of a religion* would be its origin, its spread, the life of its founder and of the
   followers who carried it, and the account of people turning away from it, its acceptance or
   rejection, and its sects — as شہرستانی's الملل والنحل is.
3. **سیرت النبی ﷺ and the سیر of the صحابہ ؓ can without doubt be called the history of the
   religion.** The condition of Muslims in later ages cannot.
4. Much in later ages was done *as religion* — spreading Islam, تبلیغ, مدارس, مساجد, خانقاہیں,
   scholarly work. But the remaining affairs of **سلطنت و سیاست were worldly**, and we should look
   at them in history in exactly that capacity.
5. So the mutual wars of Muslims, political upheavals, dynastic quarrels and the doings of فاسق و
   جابر kings **cannot be included in the history of the religion**. In fairness, this is the
   history of the *people who believed in Islam* — with both عروج and زوال in it, both closeness
   to religion and, in places, distance from it.
6. **Therefore** — and this is the sentence the course is built on — the general books of Islamic
   history, **«تاریخ طبری، البدایہ والنہایہ، الکامل فی التاریخ»** and the like, **should be read as
   the history of a people (قوم), not as the history of the religion.**

That is not the speaker's framing. It is the framing of the very book the course is named after,
and it names البدایہ والنہایہ explicitly — the book tonight is read from.

### 2.5 Why order is a weapon — the four moves that make the timeline the point

**(a) The Qur'ānic basis** — مقدمہ ص۵۱, titled «تاریخ کی اہمیت قرآن مجید کی نظر میں»:

> ﴿وَكُلًّا نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ مِنْ أَنْبَاءِ الرُّسُلِ مَا نُثَبِّتُ بِهِ فُؤَادَكَ﴾ — سورہ ھود، آیت ۱۲۰

> ﴿لَقَدْ كَانَ فِي قَصَصِهِمْ عِبْرَةٌ لِأُولِي الْأَلْبَابِ﴾ — سورہ یوسف، آیت ۱۱۱

The سورہ names and آیت numbers are printed on that page. ⚠ **Take the Qur'ānic text itself from a
مصحف, not from the OCR.** The page also carries its own guard, which is worth saying aloud:
**the Qur'an is not a book of history — it is a نوشتۂ ہدایت**, and it treats history only in the
way that gives people guidance.

**(b) The three sayings — the heart of the block.** All three are on **مقدمہ ص۵۷**, in the titled
section «تاریخ کے ذریعے جھوٹے راویوں کی پکڑ». Wordings below are the settled forms recorded in
`CLAUDE.md` §1.4; **the OCR on the page differs in pointing and must not be used.**
⚠ **eyeball-before-use, all three.**

> <div dir="rtl">لَمَّا اسْتَعْمَلَ الرُّوَاةُ الْكَذِبَ اسْتَعْمَلْنَاهُمُ التَّارِيخَ</div>
>
> — سفیان الثوری رحمہ اللہ · مقدمہ ص۵۷
> *When the narrators began to deal in lies, we dealt with them by means of chronology.*

> <div dir="rtl">إِذَا اتَّهَمْتُمْ فَحَاسِبُوهُ بِالسِّنِينَ</div>
>
> — حفص بن غیاث رحمہ اللہ · مقدمہ ص۵۷

>

And the proverb the same strand uses, which `CLAUDE.md` §1.4 records from the same pages

> <div dir="rtl">مَا أَشْبَهَ اللَّيْلَةَ بِالْبَارِحَةِ</div>
>
> — مقدمہ ص۵۳ · *how like tonight is to last night.* The مقدمہ notes on the same page that

**(c) The worked example — the forged Khaybar document.** مقدمہ ص۵۸, titled «تاریخ میں مہارت کے
ذریعے یہودیوں کی سازش ناکام». The story, exactly as the book has it:

In the **fifth century AH**, the Jews of بغداد produced to the government an ancient-looking
document under which the Prophet ﷺ had remitted the جزیہ after the conquest of خیبر. It carried
the signatures of **علی ؓ, سعد بن معاذ ؓ and معاویہ ؓ**. The document looked genuinely old, and
the Muslims had settled on releasing them from the جزیہ. Before the decision was executed it was
shown to **الإمام أبو بكر الخطیب البغدادی رحمہ اللہ**, who declared it forged **on one look**, and
gave two grounds:

1. It bears **معاویہ ؓ**'s signature — and he accepted Islam **after** the conquest of خیبر.
2. It bears **سعد بن معاذ ؓ**'s signature — and he had been martyred at **غزوۂ خندق**, long
   **before** the conquest of خیبر.

The book adds, in its own footnote on the same page, that on the alternative view — that معاویہ ؓ
had accepted Islam secretly earlier but could not emigrate from Mecca — **the argument is
unaffected**, because معاویہ ؓ by agreement was not present at خیبر but was in Mecca at that time.
The footnote refers the whole incident to **المنتظم لابن الجوزی**, and adds a closely parallel
case involving الإمام ابن تیمیہ رحمہ اللہ, reported by ابن القیم رحمہ اللہ in **زاد المعاد**.
⚠ The OCR mangles all the reference numbers on that page; **do not reproduce them.**

**The line that makes it a slide:** the document was old, it was plausible, and it was *dated*.
**Two dates killed it.** That is why this course is built on a timeline.

**(d) The second worked example, if there is room** — مقدمہ ص۵۷: a report ascribed to
الإمام الشافعی رحمہ اللہ that المأمون once had him drink something that intoxicates an ordinary
man and it had no effect on him. **الحافظ ابن حجر رحمہ اللہ, in لسان المیزان, proved it forged
from historical evidence** — الشافعی's meeting المأمون is not established — and reinforced it with
درایت: this was the man who said that if he feared cold water would disturb his understanding and
his dignity he would drink hot water for the rest of his life. `docs/catalogue/SOURCES.md` marks
this anchor ✅ read in source.

### 2.6 The best method passage in the whole evening — and it is ابن کثیر's own

At the exact seam between the death of the Prophet ﷺ and the سقيفہ, ابن کثیر stops narrating and
tells you what he threw away and why. **البدایہ ج۵ ص۳۴۳:**

> <div dir="rtl">وقد ذكر الواقديّ وغيرُه في الوفاة أخبارًا كثيرة فيها نكارات وغرابة شديدة أضْرَبْنا عن أكثرها صَفْحًا؛ لضَعْفِ أسانيدها، ونكارةِ متونِها، ولا سيّما ما يوردُهُ كثيرٌ من القُصّاصِ المُتْأخِّرين، وغيرهم، فكثيرٌ منه موضوع لا محالة. وفي الأحاديث الصحيحة والحسنة المرويَّة في الكتب المشهورة غنيةٌ عن الأكاذيب وما لا يعرف سنده، واللَّه أعلم.</div>

*(working translation — from the research note, ours, not the source's: "al-Wāqidī and others
mention many reports about the death containing gross oddities; we have passed over most of them
because of the weakness of their chains and the strangeness of their texts — especially what many
of the later storytellers bring, much of which is without doubt fabricated. In the sound and good
hadiths transmitted in the well-known books there is enough to make one independent of falsehoods
and of what has no known chain.")*

And the worked example two lines later, same page — الواقدی's report that أسماء بنت عميس ؓ
established the death because the seal between his ﷺ shoulders had been lifted (ج۵ ص۳۴۲).
ابن کثیر's verdict, **ج۵ ص۳۴۳**:

> <div dir="rtl">وهو ضعيف، وشيوخه لم يُسَمَّوْا ثم هو منقطع بكل حال، ومخالفٌ لما صحَّ، وفيه غرابة شديدةٌ، وهو رفع الخاتم. فاللَّه أعلم بالصواب.</div>

**Four named grounds for rejection in one sentence** — a weak transmitter, unnamed shaykhs, a
broken chain, and contradiction of what is authentic. That is a complete method statement with
nothing invented, and it is by the author of the book you are reading from all evening.

### 2.7 THE GUARDRAIL — the words, and why each clause is there

The block as it is delivered, per the spec:

> **حدیث** — a report must be authentic before it can bind. It carries دین.
> **فقہ** — a report is weighed for what it obliges. It carries حکم.
> **تاریخ** — a report is recorded to establish *what happened, and in what order*.
>
> So **a تاریخ report is not a ruling and it is not a creed.**
> **تاریخِ اسلام = تاریخِ مسلمین** — this is the history of Muslims, not a source of the دین.
> We narrate what the books narrate. **We do not judge between the Companions ؓ.**

Urdu form, for the podium — its own block, never mixed into an English line
(this is also QA line ۱ in `docs/catalogue/QA_BANK.md` §0):

> <div dir="rtl">تاریخِ اسلام = تاریخِ مسلمین۔ یہ مسلمانوں کی تاریخ ہے، دین کا ماخذ نہیں۔<br>ہم وہی بیان کرتے ہیں جو کتاب بیان کرتی ہے — اور صحابہ ؓ کے درمیان فیصلہ نہیں کرتے۔</div>

**Why it is phrased that way and not another way — four reasons, and you should be able to give
any of them if pressed:**

1. **Because the مقدمہ says it.** «تاریخ اسلام یا تاریخ مسلمین» is a titled section at
   مقدمہ ص۶۱, and it ends by naming البدایہ والنہایہ as a book to be read as the history of a
   قوم. The framing is the course book's, not the speaker's. That is the whole point of putting
   it in these words.
2. **Because the fiqh says it.** مقدمہ ص۵۲–۵۳: مشاجرات without need is **مکروہ**, and حرام where
   عقیدہ is at risk. A speaker who adjudicates from the floor is doing the thing the book grades
   مکروہ, in front of a room, without need.
3. **Because the discipline-distinction is on the page** (مقدمہ ص۳۳, §2.2 above). حدیث gathers a
   report for its ruling, its chain, its narrator; تاریخ gathers it for its place in the sequence.
   So a report can be historically well-attested and still be carrying **no حکم and no عقیدہ**.
   That is not a dodge — it is what the disciplines actually are.
4. **Because of this room.** The venue is Tanzeem-e-Islami, the audience is religiously committed
   and Qur'an-literate, and سقيفہ is the most contested episode after کربلا. There are **no live
   questions** — slips into a box, and fifteen minutes one-to-one afterwards. Sixty seconds at the
   top of the evening converts a safety problem into the evening's intellectual spine.

### 4.3 The Muhājirūn — this half *is* page-cited, in depth

Source: `docs/research/muhajirun-quraysh-structure.md`. Root label on the chart:
**THE MUHĀJIRŪN — of Quraysh, عدنان, northern.**

**(a) Where "Quraysh" starts.** ابن کثیر reports two views on the ancestor from whom Quraysh is
reckoned — **النضر بن كنانة** (the majority) or **فهر بن مالك** — and settles it for النضر on the
ḥadīth of الأشعث بن قيس ؓ, calling that chain «هذا إسناد جيد قوي، وهو فَيْصل في هذه المسألة»
(البدایہ ج۲ ص۴۷۷–۴۷۹). Practically: **every بطن on the chart traces to فهر بن مالك**, and فهر is
where the outermost Companion of the evening — أبو عبيدہ ؓ — meets the Prophet ﷺ.

The اصطفاء ḥadīth, which ابن کثیر cites from مسلم at **ج۲ ص۴۹۴** (his own reference: مسلم ٢٢٧٦):

> <div dir="rtl">إن الله اصْطفى كِنانةَ من ولد إسماعيل، واصْطَفى قُريشًا من كِنانةَ، واصطفى هاشمًا من قُريش، واصطفاني من بني هاشم</div>

**(b) The skeleton, in one sentence of ابن إسحاق — البدایہ ج۲ ص۴۸۵.** This single sentence places
four of tonight's men on the tree in one stroke:

> **كعب بن لؤي → مرة · هُصَيص · عدي** ; **مرة → كلاب · تيم · يقظة** ; **كلاب → قصي · زهرة**

So أبو بكر ؓ is **تيم بن مرة**; عمر ؓ is **عدي بن كعب**; خالد ؓ is **مخزوم بن يقظة بن مرة**;
عبد الرحمن ؓ and سعد ؓ are **زهرة بن كلاب**. The descent lines downward from قصي are at
**ج۲ ص۴۹۳**, and that page also records that the Prophet's ﷺ mother is **آمنة بنت وهب بن عبد مناف
بن زُهرة** and his paternal grandmother **فاطمة بنت عمرو … بن مخزوم** — i.e. **بنو زهرة are his
أخوال and بنو مخزوم his grandmother's house.**

**(c) What "standing" in Mecca actually consisted of — the five offices, البدایہ ج۲ ص۴۸۸.**
قصي بن كلاب took the Bayt back from خزاعة, gathered Quraysh into Mecca, and held all five. The
definitions below are the **edition's own footnote on that same printed page**, not ours:

| Office | What it was (per the ج۲ ص۴۸۸ gloss) |
|---|---|
| **الحِجابة** | Custody of the keys of the Kaʿba; no one enters but by his leave |
| **السقاية** | Watering the pilgrims |
| **الرِّفادة** | The food Quraysh levied on itself each year to feed the pilgrims |
| **الندوة** | The assembly for counsel and decision — دار الندوة |
| **اللواء** | The war banner, carried only by designated men |

**ج۲ ص۴۸۹** adds that دار الندوة was built for settling disputes, that no banner was tied and no
marriage contracted except in it, and that it later passed to **حكيم بن حزام ؓ**, who sold it in
معاویہ's time for 100,000 dirhams and gave the whole price away in صدقہ, saying:

> <div dir="rtl">إنما الشرف اليوم بالتقوى</div>

*(Spare material. Excellent if the clan block runs short; the first thing to drop if it does not.)*

**(d) The split that shaped everything — المطيَّبون vs الأحلاف, البدایہ ج۲ ص۴۹۲.** قصي gave all
five offices to his eldest son عبد الدار; the cousins later disputed it; Quraysh divided into two
sworn blocs. The party of **بنو عبد مناف** put their hands into a bowl of perfume (طيب) and then
wiped them on the corners of the Kaʿba — hence **حلف المطيَّبين**.

| | Clans, as ج۲ ص۴۹۲ names them |
|---|---|
| **With بنو عبد مناف — المطيَّبون** | بنو أسد بن عبد العزى · بنو زهرة · **بنو تيم** *(see the caveat)* · بنو الحارث بن فهر |
| **With بنو عبد الدار — الأحلاف** | بنو مخزوم · بنو سهم · بنو جُمح · بنو عدي |
| **Neutral** | بنو عامر بن لؤي · محارب بن فهر |
| **The settlement they reached** | الرفادة + السقاية → بنو عبد مناف · الحجابة + اللواء + الندوة → بنو عبد الدار |

⚠ **Reading caveat, and it must be checked against a page image before it reaches a slide.** The
Shamela text of ج۲ ص۴۹۲ prints **«وبنو تميم»** among the المطيَّبون. تميم is not a بطن of قريش;
the clan in that position is **تيم بن مرة** — أبو بكر's ؓ house — and ابن کثیر's own parallel
narration at **ج۳ ص۸۳** names **تَيْم بن مُرَّة** in exactly that place. **Build off ج۳ ص۸۳, which
is unambiguous, until the ج۲ ص۴۹۲ page image has been eyeballed.**

**(e) حلف الفضول — البدایہ ج۳ ص۸۳.** Sworn in دار عبد الله بن جُدْعان by **هاشم · زهرة · تيم بن
مرة**, in ذو القعدة, twenty years before the بعثة, four months after حرب الفجار; first called for
by **الزبير بن عبد المطلب**, the Prophet's ﷺ uncle; ابن کثیر calls it «أكرمَ حلفٍ سُمع به،
وأشرفَهُ في العرب». The Prophet ﷺ, in the ḥadīth ابن کثیر gives on that page from الحُميدي ←
سفيان بن عيينة ← محمد وعبد الرحمن ابني أبي بكر:

> <div dir="rtl">لقد شهدتُ في دار عبد الله بن جُدْعان حِلْفًا لو دُعيت به في الإسلام لأجبت</div>

⛔ **Do not use the «هاشم وأمية وزهرة ومخزوم» gloss** that appears at ج۳ ص۸۲ — **البیہقی رحمہ اللہ
himself flags it as مُدْرَج** («كذا روي هذا التفسير مُدْرَجًا في الحديث، ولا أدري قائله»), and
ابن کثیر resolves the whole report to حلف الفضول. The book disowns it; so do we.

**(f) The بطون at 11 AH — the table.** *(From `muhajirun-quraysh-structure.md` §4. "Standing" is
only what a fetched page states.)*

| بطن | Leading figure(s) at 11 AH | Standing, as the sources give it | Page |

**(h) السابقون الأولون vs الطلقاء — the distinction that does the real work.**

الذہبی's named roster of **السابقون الأولون** is at **سیر ج۱ ص۱۴۴**. From tonight's people it names
outright: **علي ؓ · أبو بكر ؓ · عثمان ؓ · الزبير ؓ · سعد بن أبي وقاص ؓ · طلحة ؓ · عبد الرحمن بن
عوف ؓ · أبو عبيدة ؓ**, in that order after خديجة ؓ and زيد بن حارثة ؓ. **خالد ؓ, أبو سفيان ؓ,
سهيل ؓ and العباس ؓ are not in the roster.**

The roster also carries something the chart should not lose: several of the earliest Muslims are
**حلفاء, not Qurashīs by blood** — خباب بن الأرت ؓ «حَلِيْفُ بَنِي زُهْرَةَ», عبد الله بن مسعود ؓ
«مِنْ حُلَفَاءِ بَنِي زُهْرَةَ», عامر بن ربيعة ؓ «حَلِيْفُ آلِ الخَطَّابِ», عبد الله بن جحش ؓ
«حَلِيْفُ بَنِي أُمَيَّةَ». **Clan membership in Mecca was partly contractual, not only
genealogical.** One sentence, and it inoculates the room against reading the chart as a race chart.

**The word الطلقاء** is fixed by the Prophet's ﷺ address at the door of the Kaʿba on the day of
the فتح, البدایہ **ج۴ ص۵۵۳** — «اذهبوا فأنتم الطُلقاء». ⚠ **Isnād honesty is required here:** the
edition's own footnote on that page says **«وإسناده معضل»**, and notes العراقي's تخريج finding
weakness. **Report it as what the سیرہ narrates, name ابن إسحاق as the reporter, and do not present
it as an authenticated ḥadīth.** The *term* is independently in use as a technical category —
الذہبی applies it as an established class at سیر ج۲ ص۷۹.

**(j) Where this lands — state it, do not adjudicate it.** Three observations the pages support,
offered as observations only (`muhajirun-quraysh-structure.md` §8):

1. At the سقيفہ the man الصدیق ؓ was minded to name was from **the outermost clan of all**
   (سیر ج۱ ص۵–۶). The choice was not tracking proximity of blood.
2. The Muhājirūn present at the سقيفہ **spanned the old المطيَّبون / الأحلاف divide** — أبو بكر ؓ
   (تيم), عمر ؓ (عدي, of **الأحلاف**), أبو عبيدة ؓ (الحارث بن فهر, of **المطيَّبون**). All three
   positions of the pre-Islamic quarrel are in that courtyard.
3. The ردة command structure did not run along clan lines either — the field commander is خالد ؓ
   of مخزوم (الأحلاف, and not a سابق), while هاشم, زهرة and تيم supplied the السابقون.

**Say what the books say; leave the inference to the room.**
