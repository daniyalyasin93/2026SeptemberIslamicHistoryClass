Reading the map

    Arrow keys also step through the sequence.
    Sources and their limits. The narrative behind this map comes from al-Tabari, al-Baladhuri, Ibn Sa'd and the Sayf b. Umar material they transmit — written 150–250 years after the events, often in conflict on chronology, and shaped by later Iraqi and tribal claims. Sayf in particular is treated as unreliable by many modern scholars. Boundaries here are feathered because tribal territory had no lines; battle sites like Buzakha, Butah and Aqraba are located only approximately, and several are unidentified on the ground. Use the map to teach structure, not precision. For the scholarly baseline see Fred Donner, The Early Islamic Conquests (1981); Ella Landau-Tasseron on the Ridda traditions; Hugh Kennedy, The Prophet and the Age of the Caliphates.
